\begin{abstract}
Difficult open-ended differential diagnosis requires recalling enough disease hypotheses \emph{and} keeping candidate representations and belief states consistent across stages of hierarchical reasoning.
Simply enlarging the candidate space creates synonym fragmentation, local evidence that never updates the global decision state, and output names that drift from canonical concepts.
We retain the \emph{Recall--Discrimination Dilemma} as motivation, but upgrade the research object to \textbf{alignment-preserving hierarchical hypothesis management (APHHM)}: case-adaptive organization, candidate-relative evidence updating, equivalence-class-aware candidate compression, local posterior write-back, and bounded global decoding.
All reported scores use an unmodified scoring interface: we deliberately exclude synonym bind-repair, because that interface-level lever raises baseline scores at least as much as ours and therefore measures protocol alignment rather than reasoning quality.
On frozen 100-case slices of DiagnosisArena (DA)~\cite{diagnosisarena}, MedCaseReasoning (MCR)~\cite{medcasereasoning}, and Open-XDDx (OX)~\cite{dualinf2025}, the hierarchical stack reaches DA option @1/@2 of $0.71/0.78$ versus $0.62/0.71$ for the strongest external baseline.
A case-irrelevant random L1 axis costs $0.34$ option @1 on DA and collapses candidate-relative evidence selection (mean selected facts $4.94\rightarrow0.13$).
State-consistent write-back at locked budget raises OX micro-F1 from $0.576$ (cold) to $0.651$ (paired bootstrap $p{=}4.0{\times}10^{-4}$); budget alone moves at most $0.008$, and per-family caps are not load-bearing.
Pre-registered ablations locate the compression contribution in the structural requirement rather than in our heuristic: removing equivalence handling costs $0.102$ accuracy on MCR (about a fifth of the deployed configuration's), a candidate-count-matched semantics-blind control is significantly worse, yet the gate predicate, its timing and its composition are equivalent within $\pm 3$~pp---and we retract an earlier closed-set option-accuracy gain that an audit shows to be a scoring artifact.
A genuinely budget-matched flat control (ten-sample self-consistent retrieve--rerank~\cite{wang2023selfconsistency,rrf2009}, roughly $90$ model calls per case) does not close these gaps.
We insist on stage-attributable errors---\texttt{TREE\_PARENT\_ABSENT}, \texttt{L2\_MISS}, \texttt{LOCAL\_ELIMINATION}, \texttt{GLOBAL\_MISRANK}, \texttt{MAPPER\_UNBIND}---because our audits show that conflating scoring-interface failures with recall failures recommends interventions that measurably reduce accuracy (full-tree leaf injection: option @1 $0.72\rightarrow0.42$, paired $39/9$).
Candidate-relative evidence selector \emph{strategies} (including anti-prototype anchoring) remain open components; what the ablations support is the case-adaptive axis that feeds them, not a particular selection rule.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Terminology at a Glance}
\textbf{LLM}: large language model;
\textbf{RAG}: retrieval-augmented generation;
\textbf{CoT}: chain-of-thought;
\textbf{L1/L2}: Level-1 diagnostic family / Level-2 concrete disease leaf;
\textbf{DA/MCR/OX}: DiagnosisArena / MedCaseReasoning / Open-XDDx;
\textbf{APHHM}: Alignment-Preserving Hierarchical Hypothesis Management (this paper);
\textbf{RRF}: reciprocal rank fusion;
\textbf{Fine crowding}: multiple near-synonym L2 leaves occupying top frontier slots;
\textbf{soft prior}: overturnable L1 relative score used in global arbitration;
\textbf{SC}: self-consistency (multi-sample decoding with rank fusion);
\textbf{A1/A2/A3}: the representation, state, and interface alignment invariants defined in Section~\ref{sec:dilemma}.
Failure-stage labels are defined in Section~\ref{sec:taxonomy}.
