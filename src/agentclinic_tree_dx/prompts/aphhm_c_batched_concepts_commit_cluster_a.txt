Role: AphhmCBatchedConcepts (C3, axis-free contract with per-candidate evidence, committing)

Build the differential for this case in one pass, and for each candidate write
down the evidence that argues for it and against it. The payload gives the
vignette, the observed facts, and any gap_obligation_fact_ids that nothing yet
explains.

Start from the wrong end. Do not write the differential you would teach for this
presentation. Find the one or two findings in this vignette that are strange —
the ones that would not appear in a typical case of whatever this superficially
looks like — and ask what disease those findings belong to. That disease is your
first candidate, however rare it is. Published cases exist because something in
them was unusual; the unusual finding is the whole point of the case.

Hard rules on what earns a slot:
- Every candidate must be driven by findings that are UNUSUAL for it, not by the
  presentation its whole family shares. If a candidate's support_spans are only
  the common findings that every candidate in the differential explains, it has
  not earned a slot — cut it.
- NEVER list a candidate "for completeness", "to be safe", or because it is the
  common thing that must be excluded. A defensible generic differential is
  exactly what this contract forbids. Prefer the specific entity the case is
  about over the common entity the case resembles.
- Do NOT enumerate the subtypes of one family. At most 2 candidates may share a
  family (same head noun: two kinds of lymphoma, two kinds of pemphigoid). If the
  case does not tell you which subtype it is, name the family once and spend the
  other slots on genuinely different diseases.
- Emit 5 to unique_budget concepts, GLOBALLY UNIQUE, never the same disease twice.
- preferred_label must be a specific, diagnosable disease entity. Use the standard
  clinical name; put abbreviations and synonyms in aliases. Never spend a slot on
  a symptom, sign, imaging descriptor or bare pathogen category.
- Cover the decisive findings: every high-specificity fact, and every
  gap_obligation_fact_id, should be explained by at least one candidate.
- support_fact_ids must reference fact_ids from the payload only; at least one.

Per-candidate evidence — this is the part that decides the case:
- support_spans: quote VERBATIM from the vignette the findings that specifically
  argue for THIS candidate over its rivals. Pick the discriminating ones, not the
  findings every candidate shares. 1-4 spans.
- A candidate's support_spans must rest on at least TWO DISTINCT UNDERLYING
  OBSERVATIONS. Two spans that restate the same observation — the same lesion
  described twice, a lab value and its named abnormality, a symptom and the
  patient's own words for it, a finding and the imaging that shows it — count as
  ONE observation, not two. Three restatements of one observation do not earn a
  slot. If a candidate genuinely rests on a single observation, cut it and spend
  the slot on a candidate with wider support.
- observation_groups: for each candidate, label every support_span with the index
  of the underlying observation it reports, in the same order as support_spans.
  Spans that restate one observation share an index. The number of distinct
  indices is the number of independent observations backing that candidate.
- contradict_spans: quote VERBATIM the findings that argue AGAINST this
  candidate. Leave empty only if you genuinely find none. 0-3 spans.
- Spans must be exact substrings of the vignette. Do not paraphrase, do not
  summarise, do not merge two separate findings into one span.
- Two candidates must not end up with the same support_spans; if they would, you
  have not identified what distinguishes them, and one of them is filler.

Return strict JSON only:
{
  "concepts": [
    {
      "preferred_label": "specific disease entity",
      "aliases": ["synonym or abbreviation"],
      "support_fact_ids": ["F09", "F12"],
      "support_spans": ["verbatim span that is unusual and points here"],
      "observation_groups": [1],
      "contradict_spans": ["verbatim span that argues against it"],
      "recall_provenance": ["case_report|cpg|textbook"],
      "why": "one sentence naming the unusual finding that earned this slot"
    }
  ]
}
