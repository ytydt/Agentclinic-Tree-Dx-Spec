Key Points

  • •

    LEO-MER real-world prospective cohort study shows that systemic PTCLs remain highly heterogeneous, with poor outcomes in most subtypes.

  • •

    Minimal change in survival rates in the last 2 decades emphasizes the unmet clinical need for novel therapeutic approaches in PTCL.

Visual Abstract

Abstract

Few prospective benchmark studies exist to characterize the evolving contemporary real-world practice for peripheral T-cell lymphoma (PTCL). We report the patterns of first-line care and outcomes for 720 patients with systemic PTCL enrolled in 2 related prospective cohort studies, Lymphoma Epidemiology of Outcomes (LEO) from 2015 to 2020 and Molecular Epidemiology Resource (MER) from 2002 to 2015, both followed to 2024. The primary end points were event-free survival (EFS) and overall survival (OS) using Kaplan-Meier estimator and Cox regression model. Secondary end points included correlations of clinical and treatment factors with survival. The most common induction regimens were CHOP (cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vincristine, prednisone) based (70%), given as CHOP (36%), CHOP plus etoposide (23%), or CHOP-like plus novel agents (11.5%, including 5% BV-CHP [brentuximab plus cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, and prednisone]). Consolidative autologous stem cell transplant was performed in 102 patients (14%). Within nodal PTCL, EFS and OS were adversely associated with International Prognostic Index 2 to 5, prognostic index for T-cell lymphoma score 1 to 4, and non–anaplastic large-cell lymphoma (ALCL) subtypes. Within LEO, which captured increasing first-line etoposide and brentuximab vedotin, adding etoposide to CHOP was associated with better OS in anaplastic lymphoma kinase-negative ALCL. BV-CHP showed a trend toward OS improvement in ALCL. Patients failing EFS6 and EFS24 had 5-year subsequent OS of 12% and 17%, respectively. The inferior outcomes in non-ALCL subtypes and patients failing EFS6 and EFS24 highlight unmet needs with CHOP-based induction, where clinical trials with targeted therapy should be prioritized. This trial was registered at www.clinicaltrials.gov as NCT02736357.

Introduction

Peripheral T-cell lymphomas (PTCL) are a heterogeneous group of non-Hodgkin lymphomas (NHL) of mature T-cell origin, encompassing ∼10% of NHL in most Western countries and 15% to 20% in Asia and South America.1, 2, 3, 4, 5 Despite divergent cells of origin and mechanisms of lymphomagenesis, CHOP (cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vincristine, prednisone)–based regimens, including CHOEP (cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vincristine, etoposide and prednisone), are the most common initial treatment for systemic PTCL. With the exception of anaplastic large-cell lymphoma (ALCL), CHOP is generally not curative for PTCL and provides 5-year overall survival (OS) rates in the range of 20% to 30% for non-ALCL subtypes.2,6 Although consolidative autologous stem cell transplant (SCT) may extend progression-free survival (PFS) for some patients,7, 8, 9, 10 its benefit remains untested in randomized studies.

Novel agents have been gradually introduced into frontline therapies in the last decade, most often in combination with a chemotherapy backbone. There are 4 US Food and Drug Administration–approved targeted agents for relapsed/refractory (R/R) PTCL, including pralatrexate (antifolate, 2009),11 romidepsin (histone deacetylase inhibitor, 2011; removed in 2022),12 brentuximab vedotin (BV, anti-CD30 antibody-drug conjugate, 2011),13 and belinostat (histone deacetylase inhibitor, 2014).14 There is also a growing list of novel agents in clinical development.15 To date, however, BV-CHD (brentuximab plus cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, and prednisone) is the only novel agent combination that has demonstrated survival benefit (ECHELON-2).16,17 The impact of novel agents and combinations in the real-world setting is an area of active investigation.

A paucity of prospective studies on treatment and outcomes of PTCL in the real-world setting has hampered comprehensive understanding of the heterogeneity of PTCL and, accordingly, improvement in therapeutics and clinical trial designs. Previously published retrospective studies included the International T-cell Lymphoma Project, which evaluated 1314 previously untreated cases of PTCL from 22 centers diagnosed between 1990 and 20022; the Swedish Lymphoma Registry study of 755 patients with PTCL diagnosed between 2000 and 200918; the Netherlands Cancer Registry, which reported 1427 patients with PTCL between 1989 and 201819; and the Latin America PTCL retrospective registry, which included 1979 patients diagnosed between 2000 and 2023.20 In recent years, prospective registries have been created, including the T-Cell Project 1.0, a global prospective PTCL registry of 1553 patients from Europe, North America, and South American between 2006 and 2018,21, 22, 23 and its successor, the T-Cell Project 2.0. The COMPLETE study prospectively enrolled 499 newly diagnosed patients with PTCL in the United States from 2010 to 2014, but only 275 patients had complete treatment records, and the study had limited follow-up of 2 years.24

Few benchmark studies exist to characterize the contemporary real-world landscape of clinical and pathologic practice, capturing the evolving landscape of molecular diagnosis and the development of novel therapeutics. We report the patterns of care and outcomes for systemic patients with PTCL prospectively enrolled in the Lymphoma Epidemiology of Outcomes (LEO) cohort (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT02736357) and its predecessor, the Molecular Epidemiology Resource (MER) cohort, harmonized into a single LEO-MER cohort analysis.

Patients and methods

Study population

Patients aged >18 years with newly diagnosed PTCL were prospectively enrolled in 2 closely related cohort studies, LEO and the MER of the University of Iowa/Mayo Clinic Lymphoma Specialized Programs of Research Excellence. MER, the predecessor cohort to LEO, enrolled 6972 consecutive patients with lymphoma between September 2002 and June 2015.25 LEO, initiated in 2015, expanded the MER cohort to include a total of 8 US academic centers (Mayo Clinic, University of Iowa, MD Anderson Cancer Center, Emory University, Weill Cornell Medicine, University of Miami, University of Rochester, and Washington University in St. Louis) and enrolled 7735 patients with NHL between July 2015 and May 2020, with LEO phase 2 continuing to accrue.26

Expert pathology review was performed prospectively through the LEO (or previously MER) Pathology and Biospecimens core. Diagnostic tissue slides were reviewed by a dedicated LEO pathologist at each study site and coded into the database using the World Health Organization (WHO) classification for tumors of hematopoietic and lymphoid tissues. In keeping with the real-world nature of the study, classification followed the WHO version contemporaneous with each patient’s diagnosis, because this formed the basis for treatment decisions. Accordingly, the fourth edition of the WHO classification was used until 2016, after which the revised fourth edition was used. All patients were diagnosed before the introduction of the WHO fifth edition or International Consensus Classification. Clinical diagnosis was abstracted separately from the medical record, and any discrepancy between pathologic diagnosis and clinical diagnosis or other need for clinical correlation was flagged for joint review between the LEO pathologist and LEO clinician at each study site to adjudicate the final LEO diagnosis.

For this study, demographic, clinical, pathology, treatment, and outcome data from 1107 patients enrolled between 2002 and 2020 with a diagnosis of PTCL were abstracted and harmonized across the 2 cohorts including 439 patients from MER and 668 patients from LEO, with the combined cohort referred to as LEO-MER. Of these, 859 patients had systemic PTCL and 248 had cutaneous T-cell lymphoma (to be reported separately). Among patients with systemic PTCL, 78 were excluded due to inadequate information for classification and 61 were excluded with leukemic and/or rare systemic PTCL subtypes not often managed with initial systemic chemotherapy (27 T-cell large granular lymphocytic leukemia, 24 T-cell prolymphocytic leukemia, 4 natural likker (NK) cell leukemia, 4 breast implant–associated ALCL, 1 indolent T-cell lymphoproliferative disease of the gastrointestinal tract, and 1 systemic Epstein-Barr virus–positive T-cell lymphoma of childhood). Thus, this study included 720 patients with systemic PTCL subtypes historically managed with conventional combination chemotherapy (including CHOP-like regimens) for initial treatment (Figure 1).

Statistical analysis

Diagnosis-to-treatment interval (DTI) was defined as the number of days from date of diagnosis to the initiation of therapy. Data on treatment responses were not collected. OS was calculated from date of diagnosis to date of death or last follow-up. Event-free survival (EFS) was calculated from date of diagnosis to disease progression, initiation of second-line therapy, or death from any cause. EFS24 (or EFS6) was defined as being alive and event free 24 months (or 6 months) after diagnosis and initiation of treatment.27 Subsequent OS (sOS) was measured after achieving EFS24 (or EFS6) or from time of progression if it occurred within 24 months (or 6 months). Association of clinical factors with EFS and OS was evaluated using the Kaplan-Meier estimator, and the log-rank test was used to test the significance of differences between groups. Proportional hazard Cox regression was used to estimate hazard ratios (HR) and 95% confidence intervals (CI) from univariate and multivariate models. Landmark analysis at 6 months from diagnosis was performed to evaluate the impact of consolidative autologous SCT on survival in patients aged <70 years.

Study oversight

The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and good clinical practice guidelines. Institutional Review Boards approved the study protocol at the respective sites, and all participants provided written informed consent. The LEO cohort was registered at ClinicalTrials.gov as NCT02736357.

Results

Patient enrollment and characteristics

PTCL subtypes of the 720 patients were categorized as nodal, extranodal, or disseminated. Nodal PTCL (N = 603; 242 MER and 361 LEO) included PTCL-not otherwise specified (NOS) (n = 252 [35%]); nodal T-follicular helper cell lymphomas (nTFHL; n = 173 [23.8%]), which were predominantly angioimmunoblastic T-cell lymphoma (AITL; n = 164 [22.7%]); and ALCL (n = 180 [24.9%]), which included anaplastic lymphoma kinase (ALK)-positive ALCL (n = 75 [10.4%]) and ALK-negative ALCL (n = 103 [14.3%]). Extranodal PTCL (n = 84; 49 LEO and 35 MER) included extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma (ENKTCL; n = 50 [6.9%]), hepatosplenic T-cell lymphoma (HSTCL; n = 13 [1.8%]), enteropathy-associated T-cell lymphoma (EATL; n = 19 [2.6%]), and monomorphic epitheliotropic intestinal T-cell lymphoma (n = 2 [0.3%]). The disseminated category included adult T-cell leukemia/lymphoma (ATLL; n = 33 [4.6%]; Table 1).

Baseline clinical characteristics of the 720 patients were comparable between MER and LEO (Table 1). The median age at diagnosis in the combined cohort was 60 years (range, 18-91), and the male-to-female ratio was 1:5. Furthermore, 21% of patients had performance status ≥2, 51% had elevated lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), 68% had stage III to IV disease, 62% had International Prognostic Index (IPI) scores of 2% to 5%, and 49% had prognostic index for T-cell lymphoma (PIT) score of 2 to 4. There was more demographic diversity in LEO than in MER (African American, 15.7% vs 0.7%; Hispanic, 10% vs 2.2%; and Asian, 3.6% vs 1.1%, respectively).

Initial treatment

Of the 720 patients, 618 (85.8%) received lymphoma-specific systemic therapy (Table 2), with a median DTI of 23 days (range, 0-363). The remaining 102 patients (14.2%) received palliative options, including observation/no treatment in 35 (4.9%), radiation only in 30 (4.2%), surgery in 7 (1.0%), and steroids only in 6 (0.8%).

Induction regimens

The most common first-line induction chemotherapy regimens were CHOP based across both MER and LEO cohorts (n = 508 [70.8%]). Of these, 425 patients (59%) received CHOP-like chemotherapy alone, including CHOP (n = 261 [36%]), CHOEP (n = 91 [13%]), or EPOCH (infusional etoposide, prednisone, vincristine, cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin; n = 73 [10%]), whereas 83 (11%) received CHOP-like chemotherapy in combination with novel agents (Table 2; supplemental Figure 1). More patients received etoposide-containing CHOP-like regimens in LEO (34%) than in MER (11%). There was also more use of novel agents in LEO than in MER, including BV, pralatrexate, romidepsin, lenalidomide, or azacitidine added to a CHOP-like backbone (17% in LEO vs 2% in MER), as well as novel agent combinations without CHOP-like chemotherapy (2.5% in LEO vs 1% in MER). Extranodal subtypes were treated more frequently with non–CHOP-based chemotherapy regimens than nodal PTCL subtypes, including SMILE (dexamethasone, methotrexate, ifosfamide, L-asparaginase, and etoposide) or ICE (ifosfamide, carboplatin, etoposide) for ENKTCL and ICE or IVAC (ifosfamide, etoposide, cytarabine) for HSTCL (supplemental Table 1; supplemental Figure 1). In addition, 65 patients (49 in LEO and 16 in MER) received initial treatment on clinical trials.

Consolidative SCT

A total of 110 patients (15.4% overall: 16.1% in LEO and 14.5% in MER) received consolidative SCT (102 autologous, 8 allogeneic) after induction treatment. SCT recipients had a median age of 57 years (range, 26-77) and male-to-female ratio of 1:9, 84% had stage III to IV disease, 57% had elevated LDH, and 84% received CHOP-based induction regimens (supplemental Table 2). Most autologous SCT recipients had nodal PTCL subtypes, including PTCL-NOS (33%), AITL (41%), ALK-negative ALCL (15%), and ALK-positive ALCL (3%), compared with 7% with extranodal subtypes (ENKTCL, HSTCL, and EATL). For allogeneic SCT recipients (n = 8), 4 (50%) had extranodal subtypes (ENKTCL and HSTCL), 3 (37.5%) had nodal subtypes (PTCL-NOS and AITL), and 1 (12.5%) had ATLL.

Survival outcomes and prognostic factors

Summary of EFS and OS

At a median follow-up of 9.3 years (112 months) in MER and 5.2 years (62 months) in LEO, 177 (MER) and 269 (LEO) events and 140 (MER) and 206 (LEO) deaths were observed, respectively. The 5-year EFS and 5-year OS estimates for all patients (n = 720) were 44% (95% CI, 38-50) and 56% (95% CI, 51-63) in MER, and 34% (95% CI, 29-39) and 47% (95% CI, 42-52) in LEO, respectively (Figure 2; supplemental Table 3). ALCL had superior survival, with 5-year EFS and OS of 78% (95% CI, 70-88) and 91% (95% CI, 84-97) for ALK-positive ALCL, and 60% (95% CI, 51-71) and 73% (95% CI, 65-83) for ALK-negative ALCL, respectively. Non-ALCL nodal subtypes had inferior outcomes, with 5-year EFS and OS of 26% (95% CI, 21-33) and 39% (95% CI, 33-45) for PTCL-NOS, and 28% (95% CI, 21-36) and 45% (95% CI, 38-54) for AITL, respectively. There were insufficient nTFHL of non-AITL types for outcome analysis. The extranodal subtypes of HSTCL and EATL and the disseminated subtype of ATLL had the worst outcomes, with 5-year EFS and OS of 29% (95% CI, 12-74) and 38% (95% CI, 18-81) for HSTCL, 32% (95% CI, 16-61) and 34% (95% CI, 17-64) for EATL, and 19% (95% CI, 8.4-45) and 34% (95% CI, 21-57) for ATLL, respectively.

Association of survival with clinical risk factors

In nodal PTCL, univariate Cox regression analysis showed that EFS and OS were adversely associated (P < .001) with age >60 years, stages III to IV, performance status >1, elevated LDH, non-ALCL subtypes, and DTI ≤14 days. In multivariate analysis, EFS and OS remained adversely associated with age >60 years, elevated LDH (P < .001), and stages III to IV (P = .001) (Table 3). Associations of survival with addition of etoposide to CHOP induction

Most patients (n = 508 [70%]), including 92% of those with nodal subtypes, received CHOP-like induction therapy (Table 2; supplemental Table 1). The association between survival and the addition of etoposide to CHOP in the form of CHOEP and EPOCH was analyzed across cohorts and subtypes. There was no difference in OS or EFS comparing CHOP plus etoposide with CHOP only in MER (not shown) or LEO (Figure 3A-B; P = .11 and P = .34, respectively). Subtype-specific survival analysis was conducted in LEO, which had increased first-line incorporation of etoposide with CHOP across nodal subtypes. In ALK-negative ALCL, CHOP plus etoposide was associated with significantly better OS (Figure 3C; log-rank P = .0011; HR, 0.14; 95% CI, 0.03-0.69, P = .015 by multivariate Cox adjusting for IPI) but not EFS (Figure 3D; P = .12). There was no significant difference in OS or EFS for ALK-positive ALCL (Figure 3E-F) or non-ALCL subtypes of AITL and PTCL-NOS (Figure 3G-H).

Associations of survival with addition of novel agents to CHOP induction

Novel agents were incorporated more frequently in LEO than in MER (Table 2), reflecting the increasing availability of clinical trials and the new treatment standard in CD30+ PTCL after the approval of BV-CHP. On the basis of the ECHELON-2 study,16 we compared preliminary outcomes between BV-CHP (n = 34) and CHOP in patients with ALCL and non-ALCL subtypes in LEO (supplemental Figure 2). In ALCL (ALK-positive and ALK-negative combined), BV-CHP (n = 16) trended toward better OS (log-rank P = .041; HR, 0.15; 95% CI, 0.02-1.19, P = .073 by multivariate Cox adjusting for IPI) but not EFS (log-rank P = .33). There was no difference between BV-CHP (n = 18) and CHOP in OS and EFS for non-ALCL subtypes irrespective of CD30 expression status (not revealed).

Associations of survival with consolidative autologous SCT

To adjust for the fact that MER and LEO did not collect data on treatment response to induction regimens or intent to transplant, a landmark analysis of 6 months from diagnosis was conducted for nodal PTCL treated with CHOP-based chemotherapy to explore the potential association of consolidative autologous SCT with survival. The 6-month landmark analysis included 303 patients with nodal PTCL subtypes aged ≤70 years who received and completed CHOP-based induction chemotherapy, with 85 receiving transplant. Survival analysis (supplemental Table 2; supplemental Figure 3) showed that consolidative autologous SCT was not associated with a significant difference in OS or EFS.

Relapse and progression

At a median follow-up time of 6.4 years (77 months) for the LEO-MER cohort, 454 EFS events (63%) occurred, including 277 (63%) in LEO and 177 (63%) in MER; 372 patients (52%) died, including 222 (51%) in LEO and 150 (53%) in MER. To date, 194 patients (27%) failed to achieve EFS6, including 136 (31%) in LEO and 58 (21%) in MER, whereas 378 patients (55%) failed to achieve EFS24, including 243 (59%) in LEO and 135 (49%) in MER. Consistent with previous report that EFS24 stratifies sOS in PTCL,27 the median sOS was 5.7 months (95% CI, 4.6-8.1) with 5-year sOS of 17% (95% CI, 13-22) in patients who failed to achieve EFS24 compared with median sOS not reached and 5-year sOS at 80% (95% CI, 75-86) in patients achieving EFS24 (HR, 8.29; 95% CI, 6.29-10.9; P < .001). EFS6 further highlighted the end point of primary refractory disease, where median sOS and 5-year sOS were 5.2 months (95% CI, 3.2-6.0) and 12% (95% CI, 7.8-19), respectively, for patients who failed to achieve EFS6, compared with median sOS at 110 months (95% CI, 89 to not achieved) and 5-year sOS at 62% (95% CI, 58-67) in patients achieving EFS6 (HR, 5.23; 95% CI, 4.23-6.47; P < .001). Similar trends were observed in LEO, MER, and the combined cohorts (Figure 4).

Discussion

To our knowledge, this is one of the largest prospective cohort studies of systemic PTCL reported in the contemporary era. We present data on patterns of care and clinical outcomes in a series of 720 patients managed in real-world practice settings at 8 major US centers as part of the LEO cohort study and its predecessor, the MER cohort. The study period, 2002-2020, allows unique insight into the evolving landscape of the PTCL therapeutic armamentarium, including the use of BV in frontline therapy, shifting practice patterns with regard to consolidative SCT, and modifications to standard induction regimens such as the addition of etoposide. In addition to its prospective design, the LEO-MER cohort incorporates expert pathologic diagnostic review and active, longitudinal follow-up.

LEO-MER enrollment showed a pattern of PTCL subtype distribution similar to other North American and European PTCL consortia2,5,28,29 and captured an increased percentage of AITL than a previous Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results report (24% vs 11%).30 Nine non-AITL nTFHL were diagnosed in LEO based on the revised version of the 4th edition of the WHO classification criteria. Most patients (84%) in LEO-MER have nodal PTCL subtypes, whereas extranodal subtypes and ATLL represented 12% and 4%, respectively.

Most patients with PTCL (86%) in the LEO-MER cohort received systemic treatment with curative intent. CHOP-based combinations were the most common induction chemotherapy in 70% of patients. Optimization of induction treatment began with adding etoposide to the CHOP backbone in the forms of CHOEP and infusional EPOCH (11% in MER and 34% in LEO), followed by increasing incorporation of novel agents such as BV, pralatrexate, romidepsin, lenalidomide, and azacitidine in the form of CH(O)P+X in settings of clinical trials or routine practice (2% in MER and 17% in LEO). Consolidative autologous SCT was provided to 14% patients after induction treatment.

The benefit of adding etoposide to the CHOP backbone as induction therapy remains controversial.15 A combined analysis of patients with PTCL enrolled in studies of the German High-Grade Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma Study Group found that the addition of etoposide was associated with improved EFS in patients aged 18 to 60 years with normal LDH level, especially in patients with ALK-positive ALCL.31 Other retrospective population-based studies reported variable PFS and OS associations.18,19 The use of CHOEP or EPOCH was associated with improved OS compared with CHOP in the Latin America T-cell lymphoma study.20 However, data from 1933 patients with PTCL in the Korean National Cancer Registry found that etoposide was not associated with improvement in PFS or OS and was associated with longer hospitalization and cytopenia requiring more transfusion.32 Here, we also found no benefit associated with addition of etoposide to CHOP in the whole cohort, with the exception of improved OS in ALK-negative ALCL in subgroup analysis. It remains unclear from our data set whether the relatively increased toxicity associated with the etoposide regimen biased the selection of younger and fitter patients.

The LEO-MER cohort captured the dynamic evolution of novel therapeutic development in the first-line setting. Although rarely available in MER, the LEO era began to witness a steady increase of novel combinations made available by clinical trials as novel agents matured from the R/R setting toward initial therapy. The approval of BV based on the ECHELON-2 study further promoted practice-changing incorporation of BV into the first-line setting for CD30+ PTCL. Furthermore, although the sample size for BV-CHP was small in LEO (n = 34 [8%]), limiting the power to detect definitive survival differences, accrual is ongoing in LEO phase 2 to increase the sample size and statistical power to capture the survival trend of BV in patients with ALCL in the real-world setting. However, neither the addition of etoposide across cohorts nor the incorporation of novel agents such as BV has changed the overall adverse outcomes of non-ALCL subtypes, including PTCL-NOS and AITL. Our data underscore the ongoing unmet need for therapeutic breakthroughs in PTCL, particularly through clinical trials. One such example is the ongoing frontline randomized phase 2 trial A059102 which explores targeted strategies with epigenetic agent azacitidine or PI3K inhibitor duvelisib in CD30− PTCL.

The practice of consolidative autologous SCT after induction is largely based on feasibility and efficacy in phase 2 studies.7,8 Although retrospective analyses have suggested favorable impact on survival in physically fit patients with chemotherapy-sensitive disease after CHOP-/CHOEP-based induction or novel combination, such as BV-CHP,18,19,33 clinical implementation of SCT varies in the community. Because LEO-MER did not collect data on treatment response or intent to transplant, we performed a landmark analysis using the cutoff of 6 months after diagnosis, when most patients would have completed first-line treatment. We did not observe a survival difference in patients aged ≤70 years who received autologous SCT, regardless of whether ALK-positive ALCL was included in the analysis. Furthermore, although the clinical parameters largely matched between transplant and nontransplant groups, the nontransplant group appeared to have higher proportion of patients with limited-stage disease (40% vs 20%), low-risk IPI of 0 to 1 (47% vs 27%), and low-risk PIT of 0 (40% vs 18%), potentially confounding the survival analysis. Given the heterogeneity of disease subtypes and induction and conditioning regimens, the prospective randomized phase 3 trials including EA4232 and the LYSA TRANSCRIPT trial aim to properly assess the impact of autologous SCT in the novel agent era.

Our outcome data also emphasize the utility of EFS6 and EFS24 as prognosticators that dichotomize patients into groups with markedly distinct sOS, including primary refractory disease and early relapse, which can be used for further investigation of pathogenic and potentially targetable pathways contributing to these disparate outcomes in PTCL subtypes. Additional analyses are ongoing to delineate subsequent treatment and survival beyond first line for patients with R/R diseases and compare outcomes to data accumulated from retrospective series such as the PETAL study.34 Continuing maturation of outcome data from the LEO-MER cohort, as well as ongoing accrual, suggests that this combined cohort is poised to serve as a benchmark for understanding the clinical heterogeneity and outcomes of PTCL in the contemporary era.

The real-world nature of the LEO-MER prospective cohort study confers both strengths and limitations with regard to the pathologic subclassification of the PTCL in this study. All cases underwent expert pathology review, ensuring that the coded pathologic diagnosis was accurate within the classification system used at the time of diagnosis. This real-world approach did not incorporate central or consensus review to attempt to reclassify cases retrospectively nor to harmonize diagnoses across changes in the classification, such as the recent transition to the fifth edition WHO classification.3,4,35 In addition, molecular and epigenetic biomarkers such as TBX21/GATA3 molecular subtypes and TFH markers were not consistently available, which limits the applicability of findings to contemporary, biomarker-driven treatment strategies and clinical trial design. Therefore, additional pathologic study of the LEO-MER cohort will be of great interest to more fully understand the clinical implications of newer evolving concepts in PTCL classification, such as TFH marker expression, Epstein-Barr virus in nodal PTCL, and molecular subgroups associated with prognosis and distinct targetable biology, such as the TBX21 and GATA3 subtypes of PTCL, NOS,36,37 and new genetic subtypes of ALCL.38

Recent advances in molecular and genomic profiling have provide unprecedented insight into disease pathogenesis driven by distinct cells of origin and molecular pathways. Emerging novel agents and combinations targeting the epigenome, proliferative signaling pathways, and the tumor microenvironment continue to advance the therapeutic landscape in both frontline and relapsed diseases. The overall stability of clinical outcomes within the consecutive MER and LEO cohorts underscores the unmet need of therapeutic breakthrough on a real-world population basis within the past 2 decades, during which clinical trials with biomarker-driven targeted approach for non-ALCL frontline treatment are being evaluated and remain in great need.
