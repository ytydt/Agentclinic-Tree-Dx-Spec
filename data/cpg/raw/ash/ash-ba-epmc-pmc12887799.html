Visual Abstract

TO THE EDITOR:

Myelodysplastic syndromes (MDS) are clonal bone marrow disorders characterized by somatic mutations in hematopoietic stem cells. Within higher-risk MDS, patients who harbor heterozygous deletions of chromosome 17 and/or mutations in TP53 have shorter survival and higher relapse rates after a hematopoietic stem cell transplant (HSCT).1, 2, 3, 4

In 2020, the US Food and Drug Administration approved decitabine-cedazuridine (DEC-C), an oral combination, for the treatment of MDS based on the results of the ASCERTAIN trial.5,6 The activity of DEC-C in patients with TP53mut MDS and the role of HSCT in these patients are not well defined.7, 8, 9 In this pooled analysis, we described the response dynamics and survival of patients with TP53mut MDS who were treated with DEC-C in the phase 2/3 clinical trials. We also performed propensity score matching to compare the outcomes of patients treated with DEC-C with those of patients treated with parenteral hypomethylating agents (HMA).

The patients were participants in the phase 2/3 trials of DEC-C (ClinicalTrials.gov identifiers: NCT02103478 and NCT03306264). The studies were conducted in accordance with Good Clinical Practice guidelines. All patients provided written informed consent. Institutional review board approval was obtained from each participating institution.

We defined 3 groups using the World Health Organization (WHO) 2022 classification10 as follows: (1) TP53wild-type, absence of TP53 mutations; (2) TP53single-hit, a single TP53 mutation with a variant allele fraction (VAF) of <50% and no deletion of 17p or chromosome 17 (−17); and (3) TP53multihit, a TP53 mutation with a VAF of ≥50%, a TP53 mutation with deletion of 17p/−17, or ≥2 TP53 mutations. No copy-neutral loss of heterozygosity testing for chromosome 17 was performed. Using propensity scores, we matched an institutional cohort (supplemental Table 1) that was treated with parenteral HMA at the MD Anderson Cancer Center. We report the revised International Prognostic Scoring System11 and the International Working Group 2006 criteria responses.12 Cytogenetic abnormalities were parsed using an automatic tool.13 Molecular testing was conducted in a central laboratory with a minimum VAF detection of 1%. Survival was estimated using Kaplan-Meier analyses, and intergroup testing was calculated using the Mantel-Cox log-rank test. Data analyses were performed using SPSS (Armonk, NY) and Python version 3.12 (Wilmington, DE).

Of the 180 patients, 73 (40.5%) had TP53 mutations. Patients received a median of 7.5 cycles of DEC-C. The median follow-up time was 21.5 months (interquartile range [IQR], 10.4-28.8). A total of 50 patients (27.7%) were classified as TP53single-hit and 23 (12.7%) were TP53multihit (Table 1; supplemental Figure 11). In total, 36 patients (20%) received an HSCT at the time of the analysis. Poor-risk cytogenetics were uncommon among TP53wt patients, with complex karyotype reported in 10.3% of these patients compared with 34% of patients with TP53single-hit and of 69.6% of patients with TP53multihit (P < .0001). In total, 74% of those with TP53multihit MDS were classified as very high risk according to the revised International Prognostic Scoring System scoring system. Frequent comutations included TET2 (39.2%), ASXL1 (35.7%), and DNMT3A (33.7%) (supplemental Figures 1 and 12); TP53 state did not affect the distribution of these mutations (P = .8; supplemental Figure 12). Patients with TP53multihit mutations rarely had comutations in the spliceosome genes (P = .023; supplemental Figures 1 and 5), but had an increased frequency of PPM1D mutations (30.4%; P = .001). The mutational burden was inversely correlated with the TP53mut state; TP53wild-type patients had a median of 4 mutations (IQR, 3-6), TP53single-hit patients had 3 mutations (IQR, 1-5), and TP53multihit patients had 2.5 mutations (IQR, 2-3; P = .002).

A total of 36 patients (20%) achieved complete remission (CR). The CR rates were 22.4% in patients with TP53wild-type, 14% in patients with TP53single-hit, and 21.7% in patients with TP53multhit (P = .215; Figure 1A). The median time to response was 94 days (IQR, 57-162), and the median time to loss of response (n = 63/105) was 407 days (IQR, 260-531). Figure 1B-D show the response and relapse times by TP53 state. Patients with TP53multihit MDS demonstrated a shorter time to response loss, with a median of 249 days (IQR, 183-371), compared with 401 days (IQR 302-514) for TP53single-hit and 459 days (IQR, 271-551) for TP53wild-type (P = .1). These results suggest that the TP53 status does not impact the response rate or cadence, but being classified as TP53multihit led to earlier relapse.

Survival decreased linearly by TP53 state (Figure 1E). Patients with TP53wild-type MDS had a median overall survival (mOS) of 31.7 months (95% confidence interval [CI], 19.5-51.1), compared with 22.1 months (95% CI, 14.6-35.9) for TP53single-hit and 11.5 months (95% CI, 8.6-19.1) for those with TP53multihit (multivariate log-rank P < .001). The mOS for the whole cohort was 39.3 months for those who underwent an HSCT and 29.0 months for those who did not (log-rank P = .94). Figure 1G-I show the survival at an HSCT landmark time of 4 months by TP53 state (supplemental Figures 3-9).

We then evaluated an institutional cohort of 102 patients with TP53mut MDS (supplemental Table 1; supplemental Figure 10) and matched them to the 73 patients with TP53mut MDS who were treated in the DEC-C trials. These patients were treated with either azacitidine for 5 to 7 days (47.1%) or decitabine for 5 days (52.9%). Propensity matching balanced 47 from each group (supplemental Table 2). In the matched cohort, the OS was 13.1 months (95% CI, 8.4-21.3) for DEC-C, and 8.0 months (95% CI, 5.2-13.0) for parenteral single-agent HMA therapy (Figure 1K; log-rank P = .047).

HMA are the only therapies that have led to improved survival in patients with higher-risk MDS.14, 15, 16, 17 Patients with TP53 mutations have demonstrated resistance to therapy, early relapse after HMA or transplant, and early mortality.1,2,18,19 To our knowledge, this study is the first to present the outcomes of oral DEC-C in TP53mut MDS in a prospective setting.

Significant variability in the survival outcomes has been reported among patients with TP53 mutations based on TP53 status inference using VAF cutoffs and a lack of clinical copy-neutral loss of heterozygosity testing.1,2,18 These inference methods remain inaccurate.20 The International Consensus Criteria and the WHO have provided diverging definitions of this state. In this study, when using the WHO criteria, there was a gradual decrease in survival with higher TP53 burden (Figure 1E). Classification according to the International Consensus Criteria did not predict outcomes (supplemental Table 3; supplemental Figure 13).

The CR rates with HMA therapy have ranged from 22% to 35% in studies with single-agent HMA or when combined with investigational agents.19,21,22 In our study, the CR rates were not affected by the TP53 status. The reported survival of patients with TP53mut MDS ranges from 7 to 12 months without considering the TP53 statuis.2,16,19 In the study4 that defined the multihit status, patients with TP53multihit MDS who were treated with HMA had an mOS of 8.7 months. A phase 2 study of guadecitabine reported an mOS of 6.8 months among 21 patients with TP53multihit MDS.22 Magrolimab and APR-246 reached an OS of 9.8 months and 10.8 months, respectively.7,23,24 In this study, patients with TP53single-hit MDS had an mOS of 22 months, and those with TP53multihit MDS had an mOS of 11.5 months. HSCT did not lead to significant survival benefit in patients with TP53 mutations, confirming the findings of previous studies.25

Our study is limited by the inference of TP53muli-hit using VAF cutoffs. In addition, the institutional cohort included patients who were treated with azacitidine or decitabine, drugs that have different biologic effects. There is no clear explanation why DEC-C performed better than parenteral HMA in the matched analysis. Patients who were treated with DEC-C have higher rates of myelosuppression.6 It is possible that flat oral dosing may lead to higher intracellular concentrations of decitabine, similar to longer dosing schedules with IV decitabine.16 This study directly compared the activity of DEC-C in TP53mut MDS with parenteral HMA and this is particularly important, especially when HSCT eligibility and benefit is questionable.

The proportion of patients with TP53mut MDS will continue to increase as patients live longer after hematopoietic injury. HMA remain the only approved therapy for patients with higher-risk MDS. This study suggests that in TP53mut MDS, DEC-C led to improved OS when compared with parenteral therapy.
