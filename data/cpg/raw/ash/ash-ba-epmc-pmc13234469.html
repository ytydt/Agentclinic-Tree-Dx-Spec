Abstract

Idecabtagene vicleucel (ide-cel) is a chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T-cell therapy approved for relapsed and refractory multiple myeloma (RRMM). Out-of-specification (OOS) CAR T cells not meeting criteria for commercial release are considered an investigational product; the expanded access protocol (EAP) allows release of OOS product for administration. The manufacturing analysis of capability, reliability, and timely delivery of ide-cel in the United States over >3 years since its approval included patients with RRMM who underwent leukapheresis for ide-cel treatment after ≥2 prior lines of therapy between 23 February 2021 and 1 November 2024. Additionally, the BB2121-EAP-001 study evaluated safety and efficacy of OOS ide-cel and included patients who had undergone leukapheresis for commercial ide-cel treatment not meeting commercial release specifications, most commonly due to high CD137 activation or low-infused dose. Primary end point was safety. Secondary end points were overall response rate (ORR) and complete response rate (CRR). At data cutoff (1 November 2024), the manufacturing success rate of ide-cel improved from 95.3% in 2021 to 96.8% in 2024. Turnaround time also improved from 31 days in 2021 to 24 days in 2024. In BB2121-EAP-001, 68 (72.3%) patients experienced ≥1 cytokine release syndrome event (mostly grade 1/2) and 15 (16.0%) had neurological toxicities (2 grade 3, 13 grade 1/2). ORR was 75.4% (95% confidence interval [CI], 63.1-85.2), and CRR was 35.4% (95% CI, 23.9-48.2). The manufacturing success of ide-cel is reliable and consistently high, and when OOS ide-cel products emerged, they demonstrated consistent clinical efficacy and safety. The BB2121-EAP-001 study was registered at www.clinicaltrials.gov as NCT04771078.

Introduction

Idecabtagene vicleucel (ide-cel; BB2121), a B-cell maturation antigen–directed chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T-cell therapy, is currently approved in the United States, Europe, and Japan for patients with relapsed and refractory multiple myeloma (RRMM) after ≥2 prior lines of therapy.1, 2, 3, 4 Ide-cel previously demonstrated frequent, deep, and durable responses in patients with triple-class–exposed RRMM in the phase 2 KarMMa study (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT03361748).5 In the pivotal KarMMa-3 study (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT03651128), ide-cel significantly improved median progression-free survival (PFS; 13.8 vs 4.4 months; hazard ratio, 0.49; 95% confidence interval [CI], 0.38-0.63; P < .001) and overall response rate (ORR; 71% vs 42%; P < .001) vs standard regimens in patients with triple-class−exposed RRMM who had disease refractory to the last regimen.1

Patients receiving CAR T-cell therapy must undergo leukapheresis, after which the product is manufactured and infused after lymphodepletion.6 For optimal treatment, patient experience, and outcomes in RRMM, CAR T-cell therapies such as ide-cel must be delivered in a timely and reliable manner; considerations at each step can reduce the time between patient selection and infusion.6,7 Turnaround time (TAT) for CAR T-cell manufacturing can vary due to differences in logistical processes; therefore, a reliable and predictable TAT is crucial for effective planning for bridging therapy and ensuring patients can benefit from CAR T-cell therapy.6

Out-of-specification (OOS) CAR T cell is a unique postmarketing issue for commercial autologous cell therapies. The US Food and Drug Administration determines and sets the commercial specifications and, historically, these specifications have been narrower than clinical trial specifications. OOS is a situation in which at least 1 predefined release criterion for final commercial product release is not met.8 The BB2121 expanded access protocol (EAP) was developed to allow access to certain products that do not meet commercial specifications. Within the EAP, an OOS product may be infused if it meets the EAP criteria and if deemed to be appropriate by the manufacturer, the patient’s physician, and after a collaborative discussion between the patient and the prescriber based on the available data. Release through the EAP may provide more immediate access to therapy rather than attempting reapheresis; however, there are limited data on the safety and efficacy of OOS products.9

In this analysis, we report the manufacturing capability, reliability, and timely delivery of ide-cel in the United States over >3 years since its initial market entry. We also report the safety and efficacy of ide-cel not meeting commercial release specification criteria but instead released under the most common OOS attributes (high CD137 activation or low-infused dose) per the BB2121-EAP-001 study, in the United States and Japan (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT04771078).

Methods

Inclusion criteria for the manufacturing analysis

The manufacturing analysis included patients with RRMM, as captured on the Cell Therapy 360 portal, who underwent leukapheresis for ide-cel treatment after ≥2 prior lines of therapy between 23 February 2021 and 1 November 2024 and had final manufacturing outcomes available. The portal provides health care professionals with real-time access to key information throughout the CAR T-cell therapy process, ensures efficient communication between treatment centers and manufacturers, and helps facilitate timely delivery of the final CAR T-cell product, and effective patient care.10,11 The analysis of TAT, defined as the number of days from leukapheresis to product release, included all products meeting specification with a leukapheresis and product release date that did not undergo any kind of material review board process. At the time of the analysis, 121 centers in the United States were qualified for treating patients with commercially available ide-cel and a manufacturing site in Summit, New Jersey, was used to supply these centers.

Definitions of ide-cel manufacturing and EAP terms

Ide-cel manufacturing and EAP terms and definitions used in the analysis are summarized in supplemental Table 1.

The BB2121-EAP-001 study included patients aged ≥18 years who had undergone leukapheresis for commercial treatment with ide-cel where the product did not meet approved commercial release specifications. This analysis included 2 subgroups based on the most common attributes for OOS products: OOS by high CD137 activation and OOS product by low-infused dose; the material review board process had deemed both subgroups to be suitable for release as investigational products under the EAP, and they have been supplied at physicians’ discretion upon request. OOS product by high CD137 activation was defined as CD137 activation >85% (ie, above the threshold required for release as a commercial product). OOS product by low-infused dose was defined as infused dose ≥150 × 106 to < 300 × 106 CAR+ T cells for patients in the United States and ≥150 × 106 to <280 × 106 CAR+ T cells for Japanese patients (ie, below the threshold required for release as a commercial product). The study completer analysis set, defined as total OOS study cohort, included patients who received OOS ide-cel and completed 3 months of follow-up, died, or discontinued early in the study. Patients were required to be clinically stable and to have recovered from any prior toxicities before receiving lymphodepleting chemotherapy. Patients were excluded if they experienced a significant worsening in clinical status that would, in the opinion of the treating physician, either increase the risk of adverse events (AEs) associated with lymphodepleting chemotherapy or exclude them from treatment with OOS ide-cel. The study was performed in accordance with the International Council for Harmonisation and the Declaration of Helsinki. Independent review board/ethics committee approval was obtained before study initiation. All patients provided written informed consent.

BB2121-EAP-001 study treatment

Patients were managed in accordance with ide-cel prescribing information,3,4 local routine care, and as per the treating physician’s discretion. Patients received ide-cel per the approved prescribing label. Patients were treated with lymphodepleting chemotherapy (fludarabine 30 mg/m2 per day and cyclophosphamide 300 mg/m2 per day for 3 days), followed by CAR T-cell administration (2-7 days after completion of lymphodepleting chemotherapy). For posttreatment follow-up, routine clinical practice was followed for up to 3 months following CAR T-cell infusion.

BB2121-EAP-001 study end points

The primary end point in the BB2121-EAP-001 study was safety, defined as the type, frequency, and severity of AEs and laboratory abnormalities for 3 months after the date of OOS ide-cel infusion.

Secondary end points were ORR and complete response (CR) rate (CRR) per International Myeloma Working Group criteria for 3 months after the date of OOS ide-cel infusion.

BB2121-EAP-001 statistical analysis

Reporting of AEs was based on the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities and Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events, version 5.0. Cytokine release syndrome (CRS) and neurological toxicities, including immune effector cell–associated neurotoxicity syndrome (ICANS), were reported using Lee 2014 criteria.12 The efficacy analysis was conducted using the total OOS study cohort, defined as treated patients who received OOS ide-cel and completed 3 months of follow-up, died, or discontinued early in the study, using the investigator’s assessments of disease status per International Myeloma Working Group Uniform Response Criteria for Multiple Myeloma.13 PFS was evaluated at 3 months, corresponding to the follow-up time for the BB2121-EAP-001 efficacy analysis. ORR was defined as the percentage of patients with a best overall response of either stringent CR, CR, very good partial response (PR; VGPR), or PR. CRR was defined as the percentage of patients with a best overall response of either stringent CR or CR.

Results

Manufacturing success rate

Between February 2021 and November 2024, with a data cutoff of 1 November 2024, patients who had undergone leukapheresis for ide-cel treatment after ≥2 prior lines of therapy and had manufacturing outcomes available were evaluated (Figure 1). The manufacturing success rate (MSR) improved over 3 years; 95.3% in 2021, 95.5% in 2022, 96.4% in 2023, and 96.8% in 2024 (at data cutoff of 1 November 2024; Table 1). In total, 8.7% of products were OOS; 7.8% in 2021, 12.3% in 2022, 6.9% in 2023, and 5.8% in 2024. In 2022, more patients had an OOS product (12.3%) compared with other years; however, most OOS products were supplied at the physician’s discretion upon request (7.8%). Overall, no drug product could be produced in 0.8% of patients (ie, terminations).

TAT

The current commercial TAT demonstrated year-on-year improvements (Figure 2). From 2021 to 2024, the median commercial TAT improved from 31 days (interquartile range, 28-35) to 24 days (interquartile range, 23-25) in the United States.

BB2121-EAP-001: patient baseline characteristics

At 3 months of follow-up, with a data extraction date of 16 September 2024, 94 patients were included in the total OOS study cohort (80 from the United States and 14 from Japan). The most common reasons for OOS were high CD137 activation (40.4%), which is a surrogate marker of T-cell activation, low-infused dose (31.9%), followed by CAR+ T-cell percentage (13.8%) and vector copy number (10.6%); all OOS reasons for patients enrolled in the BB2121-EAP-001 study are listed in supplemental Table 2. Median age was 68 years (range, 39-79) with 56 (59.6%) patients aged ≥65 years. Seventy-six (80.9%) patients had prior stem cell transplantation, and 91 (96.8%) had received prior anti-MM regimens (Table 2; supplemental Table 3). Median absolute lymphocyte count was 0.03 × 109/L (range, 0.00 × 109/L to 50.00 × 109/L). The median for low CAR T-cell dose was 207.64 × 106 (range, 151.21 × 106 to 290.38 × 106), and the median for high CD137 activation was 93.6 (85.9-146.7); 4 patients fell under both low-infused dose and high CD137 activation subgroups.

BB2121-EAP-001: safety of OOS ide-cel

Among patients in the total OOS study cohort, 68 (72.3%) experienced ≥1 CRS event (Table 3). Most events were grade 1/2 and no grade 5 events were reported. The median time to first onset was 2.0 days (range, 1-13), and the median duration was days 4.0 days (1-14). Among patients receiving OOS product by high CD137 activation, 30 (78.9%) had ≥1 CRS event; the median time to first onset was 2.0 days (range, 1-4), and the median duration was 4.0 days (range, 1-10). Among patients receiving OOS product by low-infused dose, 20 (66.7%) had ≥1 CRS event; the median time to first onset was 2.0 days (range, 1-13), and the median duration was 3.0 days (range, 1-12) (Table 3). Among patients in the total OOS study cohort, 51 (54.3%) and 17 (18.1%) patients used tocilizumab and steroids, respectively, for management of CRS.

BB2121-EAP-001: efficacy of OOS ide-cel

Among the 94 patients in the total OOS study cohort who were evaluable for response at 3 months of follow-up, the ORR was 75.4% (95% CI, 63.1-85.2), the CRR was 35.4% (95% CI, 23.9-48.2), and the VGPR rate or better was 56.9% (95% CI, 44.0-69.2; Figure 3). Among patients receiving OOS product by high CD137 activation (n = 38), the ORR was 72.4% (95% CI, 52.8-87.3), the CRR was 34.5% (95% CI, 17.9-54.3), the VGPR rate or better was 58.6% (95% CI, 38.9-76.5), and the PFS rate at 3 months was 75.4% (95% CI, 59.5-91.4). Among patients receiving OOS product by low-infused dose (n = 30), the ORR was 86.4% (95% CI, 65.1-97.1), the CRR was 45.5 (95% CI, 24.4-67.8), the VGPR rate or better was 68.2% (95% CI, 45.1-86.1), and the PFS rate at 3 months was 81.3% (95% CI, 75.1-87.4).

Discussion

The timely and reliable delivery of CAR T-cell products is essential for effective patient planning and optimizing treatment outcomes in RRMM. A reliable and predictable TAT is crucial for effective treatment plans and to ensure that patients can benefit from CAR T-cell therapy.6 This analysis has shown that the commercial manufacturing reliability of ide-cel has been notably high since 2021 and that there were consistent year-on-year improvements in MSR and TAT for patients treated with ide-cel over the past 3 years, with the most current MSR of 96.8% in 2024 and a median TAT of 24 days. There continues to be an ongoing effort to shorten CAR T-cell therapy TAT so that patients can get timely access to treatment. In this manufacturing analysis, TAT for commercial ide-cel has been significantly reduced over the past 3 years in the United States.

Compared with manufacturing CAR T-cell products for clinical trials with a finite number of patients, commercial manufacturing must meet much higher demands, introducing a greater complexity and inherent challenges of scale. The commercial MSR of ide-cel closely approached those of the KarMMa and KarMMa-3 clinical trials.1,5 The median commercial TAT for ide-cel in 2024 was largely decreased to that previously reported in previous ide-cel clinical trials (∼34-35 days).4,14 Additionally, commercial ide-cel MSR is higher, and TAT is shorter than that reported for the CAR T-cell therapy ciltacabtagene autoleucel (cilta-cel). In the phase 1/2b CARTITUDE-1 trial (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT03548207), in which heavily pretreated patients with RRMM received cilta-cel, 18% of patients experienced manufacturing failures.15 In the phase 3 CARTITUDE-4 trial (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT04181827), in which patients with lenalidomide-refractory multiple myeloma were randomized to receive either cilta-cel or the physician’s choice of standard of care, the median TAT from leukapheresis to product release was 44 days.15 The manufacturing success of ide-cel is reliable and consistently high, and OOS ide-cel products were rare.

When OOS products did occur, many of these products were still deemed viable and were accessible for release and requested by physicians via the EAP. The BB2121-EAP-001 data demonstrate that these products, in particular those that were OOS for high CD137 activation and low-infused dose, have clinical efficacy and safety data that are consistent with those of commercial grade drug products. Overall, for OOS products that were deemed to be appropriate for release via the BB2121-EAP-001, ORR was 75.4% (95% CI, 63.1-85.2) and CRR was 35.4% (95% CI, 23.9-48.2). ORR rates also remained high for patients receiving ide-cel that was OOS by high CD137 activation and by low-infused dose. Although response rates at defined time points were assessed, the study design did not allow for evaluation of duration of response or time-to-event outcomes at time points beyond 3 months.

Additionally, these OOS products were not associated with any new safety signals. Overall, 68 (72.3%) patients had ≥1 CRS event (mostly grade 1/2) and 15 (16.0%) had neurological toxicities (2 grade 3, others grade 1/2).

For products that are OOS, the BB2121-EAP-001 results showcase response rates and safety data that are consistent with those of commercial grade drug products, especially with the 2 most common types of OOS drug products of high CD137 activation and low-infused dose.16 Despite limited follow-up, OOS, CRR, and PFS rates at 3 months suggest comparable efficacy to commercial grade products, though durability of response remained to be determined. Ide-cel has a robust MSR that sits at ≥96% for commercial supply of ide-cel, demonstrating the ability to deliver products reliably to patients in critical need. Consistent year-on-year improvement in TAT for ide-cel enables provision of drug products to patients in a timely manner across geographical regions. These improvements have enabled the manufacturing of ide-cel to meet the increasing global patient demand effectively; in rare cases where OOS ide-cel emerged, the drug products showed clinical safety and efficacy.
