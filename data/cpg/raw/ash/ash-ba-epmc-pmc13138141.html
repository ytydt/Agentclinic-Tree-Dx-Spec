Visual Abstract

TO THE EDITOR:

Treatment outcomes of relapsed/refractory (R/R) large B-cell lymphoma (LBCL) dramatically improved with the use of chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T-cell therapies.1, 2, 3, 4, 5 Lisocabtagene maraleucel (liso-cel) is an autologous, CD19-directed, 4-1BB CAR T-cell product. In TRANSFORM (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT03575351; supplemental CONSORT checklist), a global, randomized, open-label, phase 3 study, liso-cel demonstrated superior, more durable efficacy vs standard of care (SOC; 3 cycles of protocol-defined immunochemotherapy followed by high-dose chemotherapy and autologous stem cell transplantation in patients with complete response or partial response) as second-line (2L) therapy for R/R LBCL.1, 2, 3 Upon completion of TRANSFORM, patients who received liso-cel, either in the liso-cel arm or after crossing over to receive liso-cel from the SOC arm, could enroll into a separate, long-term follow-up (LTFU) study (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT03435796). Here, we report results after ∼4 years of follow-up in patients from TRANSFORM who consented to the LTFU study.

Patients with 2L R/R LBCL were randomized 1:1 to the liso-cel arm (target dose, 100 × 106 CAR+ T cells) or SOC arm.1, 2, 3 Patients in the liso-cel arm who received liso-cel were followed for up to 3 years after randomization in TRANSFORM (after completion of, or early discontinuation from, TRANSFORM) before enrolling in LTFU; patients in the SOC arm who crossed over to receive liso-cel were followed for up to 12 months after liso-cel infusion before enrolling in LTFU.

In the LTFU, no study treatment was administered, per protocol; subsequent therapies could be provided at the investigator’s discretion. Patients were assessed at least every 6 months for the first 5 years after liso-cel infusion and, thereafter, annually for up to 15 years after infusion, or until study withdrawal or death, whichever occurred first. Selected adverse events (AE) considered related to liso-cel (malignancies, neurologic, hematologic, or rheumatologic/autoimmune disorders), disease status assessed per local institutional practice (progression [yes/no], date of progression, method of assessment [positron emission tomography, computed tomography, magnetic resonance imaging scans, and physical exam]), and overall survival (OS) were collected. Mandated central laboratory evaluations included monitoring of B-cell levels, replication-competent lentivirus presence, and persistence of vector sequence. Local laboratory testing, including hematology (hemoglobin, leukocytes, platelets/thrombocytes, lymphocytes, monocytes, eosinophils, basophils, and neutrophils) and immunology (immunoglobulin G [IgG], IgA, and IgM) assessments, was not mandated; however, it was performed and collected as clinically indicated. OS for the combined TRANSFORM and LTFU studies was defined as the time from randomization to death from any cause. Progression-free survival (PFS) was defined as the time from randomization to disease progression or death from any cause, with PFS assessed by an independent review committee in TRANSFORM and by investigators in LTFU. Censoring rules for OS and PFS followed the TRANSFORM and LTFU statistical analysis plans. For patients in the liso-cel arm or SOC crossover subgroup who entered LTFU and had no event, PFS was censored at the start of subsequent therapy or last known alive date in LTFU (whichever occurred first), and OS at the last known alive date in LTFU. For those who did not enter LTFU, if no event occurred, PFS was censored at the start of subsequent therapy or last known alive date in TRANSFORM (whichever occurred first), and OS at the last known alive date in TRANSFORM. Results are descriptive.

In total, 184 patients were randomized in TRANSFORM (92 per arm). In the liso-cel arm, 89 patients received liso-cel and 43 further entered LTFU, including 42 patients (47%) who completed the posttreatment period (∼36 months) and 1 patient who withdrew from TRANSFORM ∼11 months after infusion; of 46 patients who did not enter LTFU, 16 refused to join, 2 had unknown consent status, and 28 died. The causes of the 28 deaths were lymphoma progression or lymphoma complications (n = 17), AE (n = 5), and other (COVID-19, n = 4; COVID-19 pneumonia, n = 1; pneumonia, n = 1). In the SOC arm, 61 patients were approved for crossover, 57 effectively received liso-cel, and 24 (42%) entered LTFU; all patients who entered LTFU had completed the posttreatment period (∼12 months) in TRANSFORM. Of 33 SOC crossover patients who did not enter LTFU, 18 refused to join and 15 died. The causes of the 15 deaths were lymphoma progression or lymphoma complications (n = 10), other (sepsis, n = 3; severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 acute respiratory disease, n = 1), and unknown (n = 1). Although only a portion of TRANSFORM patients entered LTFU, descriptive statistics of key demographics and baseline characteristics for patients in LTFU were generally consistent with those from TRANSFORM, as previously reported (Table 1).1, 2, 3

The combined TRANSFORM and LTFU studies demonstrated prolonged survival benefit of liso-cel. In the 92 patients randomized to the liso-cel arm, medians for OS and PFS were still not reached (NR); 48-month OS rate was 61.5% (95% confidence interval [CI], 51.2-71.7; Figure 1A) and 48-month PFS rate was 52.2% (95% CI, 41.5-62.8; Figure 1B).

In the subset of liso-cel‒treated patients who were alive and enrolled in LTFU, both those who received liso-cel treatment for LBCL as 2L (liso-cel arm) or 3L (SOC crossover patients) experienced prolonged survival benefit. In the liso-cel arm (n = 43), median OS was NR and 24-month OS rate was 95.1% (95% CI, 88.4-100.0; Figure 1C). In SOC crossover patients (n = 24), median OS was NR and 24-month OS rate was 78.3% (95% CI, 61.4-95.1; Figure 1D).

Results from the LTFU showed no new safety signals compared with previous reports from TRANSFORM (Table 2).1, 2, 3 Any-grade AEs occurred in 2 of 43 patients (5%) in the liso-cel arm (grade 2 hypogammaglobulinemia, grade 3 dyspnea; n = 1 [2%] each) and 3 of 24 (12.5%) SOC crossover patients (grade 4 COVID-19, grade 1 cognitive disorder, invasive ductal breast carcinoma of unknown grade; n = 1 [4%] each). No second primary malignancies or serious infections related to liso-cel were observed.

Seven patients died in the LTFU. Among 43 patients in the liso-cel arm, 2 (5%) died. One patient died due to severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 infection. One patient experienced disease progression after liso-cel, subsequently received another CD19-directed, autologous CAR T-cell therapy, axicabtagene ciloleucel (axi-cel), and then died of mucormycosis. Among 24 SOC crossover patients, 5 (21%) died (lymphoma progression, n = 4 [17%]; cardiac arrest, n = 1 [4%]). No AEs related to liso-cel led to death.

A limitation of this analysis is that patients from TRANSFORM had to consent separately to join the LTFU, resulting in a reduced number of patients who were followed up beyond 3 years in the liso-cel arm and after 12 months after infusion in the SOC crossover subgroup. Nonetheless, 47% of liso-cel–treated patients in the liso-cel arm completed the 3-year follow-up in TRANSFORM and entered LTFU. In addition, the demographics and baseline characteristics were consistent between the TRANSFORM intent-to-treat population and patients in LTFU, suggesting reduced risk of selection bias for the liso-cel arm. After 4 years of follow-up, OS and PFS outcomes observed with liso-cel in TRANSFORM and LTFU are on par with the outcomes reported with axi-cel in a similar 2L LBCL population in ZUMA-7 after a median follow-up of 47 months.6 Median OS was NR for both liso-cel and axi-cel. The 36-month and 48-month OS rates were 62.8% and 61.5%, respectively, for liso-cel, and 55.9% and 54.6%, respectively, for axi-cel. Median PFS was NR for liso-cel and 14.7 months (95% CI, 5.4-43.5) for axi-cel. The 36-month and 48-month PFS rates were 52.2% and 52.2%, respectively, for liso-cel, and 44% and 41.8%, respectively, for axi-cel. LTFU results suggest that patients who achieved durable responses with liso-cel at 3 years continued to demonstrate favorable long-term survival outcomes, with a curative potential for R/R LBCL.

In summary, after a median follow-up of ∼4 years, combining data from the TRANSFORM and LTFU studies, liso-cel continued to demonstrate long-term clinical benefit with high OS and PFS rates in patients with 2L R/R LBCL. The safety profile of liso-cel continued to be favorable and consistent with previous reports,1, 2, 3 with no new safety signals observed during LTFU. These results further support liso-cel as an effective 2L treatment with curative potential for R/R LBCL.
