Background[edit]

Generally classified by GI and Non-GI causes:

Inflammatory[edit]

  • Esophagus:
    • Esophagitis (reflux, ingestion)
  • Stomach:
    • Gastritis (EtOH, NSAID's)
  • Small Bowel:
    • Enteritis (infectious, foodborne)
  • Liver:
    • Hepatitis
  • Pancreas:
    • Pancreatitis

Non-GI Causes[edit]

CNS[edit]

  • Increased Intracranial Pressure (ICP):
    • Meningitis/encephalitis
    • Mass lesion
    • Pseudotumor cerebri
    • CVA/TIA
  • Vestibular:
    • Labyrinthitis
    • Meniere's
  • Other:
    • Migraine
    • Seizure

Toxic/Metabolic[edit]

  • Metabolic:
    • Acidosis
    • Uremia
  • Medications:
    • Opiates
    • Antibiotics
    • Chemotherapy
    • AED
  • Toxins:
    • EtOH
    • Illicit substances

Stressor[edit]

  • Severe Illness:
    • MI
    • Sepsis
  • Other:
    • Pain


Clinical Features[edit]

  • Nausea and/or vomiting
  • Additional features of underlying process

Evaluation[edit]

Varies widely depending on clinical presentation

  • CBC
  • Chemistry
  • Urine pregnancy
  • LFTs
  • Lipase
  • APAP, ASA, digoxin levels
  • Urinalysis
  • ?Abdominal xray
  • ?CT
  • ?Ultrasound


Management[edit]

  • Treat underlying pathology
  • Address electrolyte derangements and dehydration as needed
  • Antiemetics
  • Inhaled isopropyl alcohol (sniffing an alcohol wipe) has been shown to reduce mild to moderate nausea and vomiting[1]


Disposition[edit]

  • Depends on cause
  • Most non-specific episodes of acute nausea and vomiting may be discharged, if:
    • No emergent/urgent cause identified or suspected
    • Patient tolerating fluids after treatment


Complications[edit]

  • Hypovolemia
  • Metabolic alkalosis
  • Hypokalemia
  • Mallory-Weiss (tear)
  • Boerhaave (rupture)
  • Aspiration
