Graphical Abstract 
This editorial refers to ‘Aspirin vs. clopidogrel monotherapy after percutaneous coronary intervention: 1-year follow-up of the STOPDAPT-3 trial’, by H. Watanabe et al ., https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehae617 . 
The balance of ischaemic and bleeding risks after percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) is an ongoing challenge. Primary objectives of dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT) following PCI are to prevent stent thrombosis and, particularly in patients with acute coronary syndromes (ACS), to prevent atherothrombotic events, while minimizing the risk of bleeding. 1 Current European and US guidelines support a default DAPT strategy of aspirin and a P2Y 12 inhibitor for 6 months after PCI in patients with chronic coronary syndromes (CCS) and for 1 year in those with ACS. 2–4 Yet, during the past few years, this strategy has increasingly been challenged due to advances in PCI techniques, stent platforms, and antiplatelet drugs, as well as to the efficacy of other pharmacological therapies for atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (e.g. novel lipid-lowering drugs) that have progressively reduced the residual risk of recurrent ischaemic events. 4 This progress has paved the way for testing a shorter duration of DAPT, in particular in patients at high bleeding risk (HBR). 2–4 
Which single antiplatelet agent should be used as maintenance therapy after DAPT discontinuation? Current guidelines indicate aspirin as the drug of choice (class I, level of evidence A), 2 , 4 supported by at least 30 years of scientific evidence and clinical practice, low cost, predictable pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics, and other longer term health benefits. 5 The 2024 ESC guidelines for the management of CCS also recommend clopidogrel as an alternative to aspirin monotherapy in patients with prior myocardial infarction (MI) or remote PCI (class I, level of evidence A). 2 A number of clinical trials and meta-analyses have compared P2Y 12 inhibitor monotherapy strategies (with either ticagrelor or clopidogrel) after 1–3 months of DAPT with longer DAPT strategies (i.e. 12–15 months) with aspirin and a P2Y 12 inhibitor (either ticagrelor or clopidogrel). 6 These trials consistently showed a reduction of bleeding events, used as the primary endpoint, without any detectable increase in ischaemic risk. Based on these findings, current guidelines suggest potential shorter DAPT strategies with subsequent transition to P2Y 12 inhibitor monotherapy to reduce bleeding risk in selected patients undergoing PCI (class IIa, level of evidence A) and in ACS patients with HBR (class IIb, level of evidence B). 4 However, no previous clinical trial had directly compared aspirin monotherapy with P2Y 12 inhibitor monotherapy following short or very short DAPT after PCI. 
STOPDAPT-3 was designed to test the hypothesis that removing aspirin from a short prasugrel-based DAPT regimen might reduce major bleeding events early after PCI using new-generation drug-eluting stents (DES) without increasing the risk of cardiovascular events. The trial enrolled 6002 Japanese patients with ACS (75%) and/or HBR (54%). 7 The aspirin-free strategy compared with the DAPT strategy using aspirin and prasugrel at the dose conventionally used in Japan (20 mg loading followed by 3.75 mg daily) failed to reduce major bleedings within 1 month after PCI and was associated with a signal suggesting an excess of coronary events, such as unplanned coronary revascularization and subacute definite or probable stent thrombosis. 7 The latter finding is not unexpected given the role of thromboxane (TX)A 2 -dependent platelet activation in coronary atherothrombosis 8 and the clinical efficacy of low-dose aspirin in ACS. 5 Furthermore, the dose of prasugrel conventionally used in Japan (20/3.75 mg) is known to produce less intense peak platelet inhibition than the globally used dose of 60/10 mg, although time to full platelet inhibition is similar. 9 
The study by Watanabe et al . 10 published in the current issue of the European Heart Journal describes the 1-year results of STOPDAPT-3, whereby at ∼1 month after the index PCI, patients who had received DAPT were switched to aspirin (81–100 mg/day) monotherapy (aspirin group), and patients who had received prasugrel monotherapy were switched to clopidogrel (75 mg/day) monotherapy (clopidogrel group). The co-primary endpoints of the study were the cardiovascular endpoint defined as a composite of death from cardiovascular cause, MI, definite stent thrombosis, or ischaemic stroke, and the bleeding endpoint defined as Bleeding Academic Research Consortium (BARC) 3 or 5. The key finding of the current analyses was that aspirin monotherapy compared with clopidogrel monotherapy was associated with very similar cardiovascular and bleeding outcomes beyond 1 month and up to 1 year after PCI ( Graphical Abstract ). 10

The secondary release of ADP and TXA 2 induces further platelet activation and aggregation, thereby amplifying the initial activation signal(s). 8 ( B ) The main efficacy (left graph) and safety (right graph) results of the GLOBAL LEADERS trial, 11 a randomized comparison of ticagrelor plus aspirin for 1 month, followed by ticagrelor monotherapy for 23 months vs. aspirin plus clopidogrel or ticagrelor for 12 months, followed by aspirin monotherapy for 12 months. Is this a surprising finding? It would be if the STOPDAPT-3 results were only compared with the HOST-EXtended Antiplatelet Monotherapy (HOST-EXAM) trial 12 which tested clopidogrel vs. low-dose (100 mg once daily) aspirin after an event-free DAPT period of 6–18 months in 5438 South Korean patients who had undergone PCI with DES. Its results showed the superiority of clopidogrel monotherapy over aspirin monotherapy in preventing a composite of all-cause death, non-fatal MI, stroke, readmission due to ACS, and major bleeding (BARC 3 or higher) complications during the 24-month follow-up period (hazard ratio [HR] 0.73; 95% confidence interval [CI] 0.59–0.90; P = .0035). 12 However, in the recent PANTHER meta-analysis of seven randomized trials (including HOST-EXAM) comparing P2Y 12 inhibitor monotherapy vs. aspirin monotherapy for the prevention of cardiovascular events in 24 325 patients with established coronary artery disease (CAD), HOST-EXAM was clearly an outlier for both the primary vascular endpoint and the key secondary bleeding outcome (BARC 3 or 5). 13 In fact, a statistically significant treatment-by-subgroup interaction with geographical regions ( P interaction = .034) suggested that the difference between P2Y 12 inhibitor and aspirin monotherapy was confined to an Asian population. 13 Moreover, when examining the two largest randomized comparisons between the two antiplatelet strategies in patients with CAD, both CAPRIE (with a post-MI subpopulation of 8446 patients) 14 and GLOBAL LEADERS (with a population of 15 968 patients undergoing PCI with DES for ACS or stable CAD) 11 failed to show superiority of a P2Y 12 inhibitor (either clopidogrel or ticagrelor)-based strategy over an aspirin-based strategy for both ischaemic and bleeding outcomes ( Graphical Abstract ). 
While the results of STOPDAPT-3 are limited to 1-year follow-up, the findings from the STOPDAPT-2 landmark analysis beyond 1 year suggest that clopidogrel and aspirin monotherapies following 12-month DAPT after PCI are similar in terms of net adverse clinical events up to 5 years, without significant differences in cardiovascular and bleedings outcomes. 15 
It should be noted that only one out of four patients enrolled in the STOPDAPT-3 trial underwent a complex PCI, while the use of intravascular imaging for procedural guidance was almost systematic (i.e. 93% of cases), 7 , 10 which might not be representative of routine clinical practice. 
Overall, the previous head-to-head comparisons between aspirin and a P2Y 12 inhibitor and the current STOPDAPT-3 analyses are consistent with the concept that the synthesis and/or release of two potent platelet agonists, i.e. TXA 2 activating its platelet thromboxane receptor to increase Ca 2+ , and ADP activating its platelet P2Y 12 receptor to decrease cAMP ( Graphical Abstract ), represent similarly important and independent amplification mechanisms of platelet activation in response to a variety of physiological and pathophysiological stimuli triggering primary haemostasis and atherothrombosis, respectively. 8 The participation of these amplification mechanisms of platelet activation in protecting against major bleeding and causing coronary occlusion appears largely independent of the clinical presentation, revascularization procedure, and concomitant treatments, as CAPRIE, 14 GLOBAL LEADERS, 11 and STOPDAPT-3 7 , 10 were performed over a three-decade time frame, in quite different management settings. 
In conclusion, the 1-year results of the STOPDAPT-3 trial represent another piece of evidence supporting the similar antithrombotic efficacy and bleeding liability of clopidogrel and low-dose aspirin after PCI. However, in contrast to the highly predictable, high-grade inhibition of platelet TXA 2 production by low-dose aspirin in the virtual totality of compliant patients, 5 the highly variable conversion of clopidogrel to its active metabolite results in large interindividual variability in P2Y 12 inhibition, ranging from a trivial effect to a prasugrel-like antiplatelet response. 1 Under which circumstances one may want to prefer this substantial uncertainty, short of performing pharmacodynamic and/or pharmacogenetic characterization of the clopidogrel response in the individual patient, is currently unclear.
