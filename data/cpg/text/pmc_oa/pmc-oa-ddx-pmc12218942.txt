Efficacy evaluation of allergen-specific immunotherapy in children with asthma: a systematic review and meta-analysis
Background
Asthma is the most common chronic respiratory disease in children. Although conventional treatments such as inhaled corticosteroids and long-acting β2-receptor agonists can effectively control symptoms, some children still face the problem of frequent attacks and drug side effects. As a potential etiological treatment, allergen-specific immunotherapy aims to induce immune tolerance by gradually increasing exposure to specific allergens, thereby relieving asthma symptoms in the long term. This meta-analysis aims to synthesize the existing high-quality evidence and systematically review the efficacy of allergen-specific immunotherapy for childhood asthma.
Methods
We performed literature search, screening, and data extraction according to the Cochrane Collaboration guidelines. A computerized search of PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, Cochrane Central, and CNKI databases was performed, and 10 randomized controlled clinical trials (RCTs) were included. Two independent reviewers screened the literature, extracted data, and assessed the risk of bias according to predefined criteria. The main outcome measures included TASS, TMS, VAS scores, and FEV1%.
Results
A total of 10 RCT studies were included, and all met the inclusion criteria. Meta-analysis results showed that allergen-specific immunotherapy showed a positive effect in improving the symptoms of childhood asthma and reducing the need for medication. Specifically, immunotherapy significantly reduced the total asthma symptom score (TASS, SMD=-1.05, 95% CI [-2.09; -0.01]) and total medication score (TMS, SMD=-1.32, 95%CI [-2.21; -0.43]). However, no significant difference was found in VAS score and FEV1% (SMD=-1.77, 95% CI [-3.76;0.22]; SMD = 0.51, 95% CI [-0.38;1.40]). All indicators had high heterogeneity.
Conclusion
Allergen-specific immunotherapy showed a positive effect in improving the symptoms of childhood asthma and reducing the need for medication, but there was significant heterogeneity among different studies. More high-quality RCTs are needed in the future to further explore the safety and long-term efficacy of immunotherapy to optimize its application in the management of childhood asthma.

Introduction
Asthma is a chronic inflammatory airway hyperresponsiveness and reversible airflow limitation, often accompanied by symptoms such as wheezing, coughing, shortness of breath, and chest tightness. Globally, asthma affects hundreds of millions of people, with a particularly high incidence in children, making it one of the most common chronic diseases in pediatrics. The pathogenesis of asthma is complex and involves the interaction of genetic and environmental factors. Genetic susceptibility makes certain individuals more prone to developing asthma, while environmental triggers such as air pollution, tobacco smoke, viral infections, and allergen exposure further promote the onset and progression of the disease. Particularly for allergic asthma, a type of asthma triggered by specific allergens, which accounts for 60-80% of all asthma cases, common sensitizing agents include pollen, dust mites, pet dander, and mold. These allergens activate T-helper cell type 2 (Th2) responses in the immune system, leading to IgE-mediated allergic reactions that result in airway inflammation and constriction.
Although conventional treatments such as inhaled corticosteroids (ICS) and long-acting beta-agonists (LABA) effectively control symptoms in most patients, some children still experience frequent exacerbations, side effects from medications, and long-term dependency. Moreover, prolonged use of corticosteroids may lead to adverse effects such as growth retardation and decreased bone density, especially in children. Therefore, finding an alternative or supplementary therapy that can reduce medication use while providing long-term symptom improvement is crucial.
In this context, allergen-specific immunotherapy has gained attention as a potential disease-modifying treatment. Allergen-specific immunotherapy aims to induce immune tolerance by gradually increasing exposure to specific allergens, thus achieving long-term relief of asthma symptoms. The therapy includes two main administration routes: subcutaneous injections and sublingual tablets. It has been widely used in the treatment of allergic diseases in adults and has shown good clinical outcomes. However, for children with asthma, especially those triggered by common allergens like pollen and dust mites, the efficacy and safety of immunotherapy remain controversial, with significant variations in results across different studies and a lack of systematic evaluation. Therefore, this meta-analysis aims to synthesize existing high-quality evidence to systematically evaluate the efficacy of allergen-specific immunotherapy for pediatric asthma, providing scientific evidence for clinical decision-making and guiding further research.

Method

Search strategy
A computer-based search was conducted in five databases: PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, Cochrane Central, and China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI), with a search cutoff date of May 15, 2025. A combination of subject terms and free words was used for the search strategy, tailored to the characteristics of each database. The search terms included “immunotherapy,” “asthma,” “children,” “randomized controlled trial,” and other related terms and their synonyms. Additionally, references from the included studies were traced to supplement the search for relevant literature. The complete search strategy can be found in Supplementary Table 1.
This systematic review has been performed and written in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines. The protocol was registered with PROSPERO (number: CRD420251052530; URL: https://www.crd.york.ac.uk/PROSPERO/).

Inclusion and exclusion criteria
Inclusion Criteria: (1) The literature type is a randomized controlled clinical trial. (2) The study population consists of children with asthma. (3) The experimental group receives specific immunotherapy. (4) The control group is treated with standard medication.
Exclusion Criteria: (1) Animal studies. (2) Conference abstracts, academic reports, and review articles. (3) Studies that could not be retrieved in full text, lacked necessary data extraction, or involved combined rehabilitation therapies. (4) Duplication Research.

Literature screening and data extraction
Two independent reviewers screened titles and abstracts of all retrieved records to identify potentially relevant studies. Full texts of eligible articles were then assessed independently by the same reviewers according to the predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria. To evaluate inter-rater reliability, a random sample of 15% of the records was double-screened, and Cohen’s kappa statistic was calculated. The kappa value was 0.87, indicating a high level of agreement between reviewers. Any disagreements were resolved through discussion or consultation with a third reviewer to achieve consensus.
The following data were extracted from the studies: methodological design, sample size, control group, intervention protocols, and outcome measures. The primary outcomes extracted were total asthma symptom scores, total medication scores, visual analog scale (VAS) scores, and FEV1%. If there were inconsistencies in the data, a third researcher re-processed and analyzed the data.

Risk of bias assessment
The same two reviewers used the Cochrane risk of bias tool to assess the risk of bias in the studies. The assessment covered the following areas: randomization method, allocation concealment, blinding of patients and physicians, blinding of outcome assessors, completeness of outcome data, selective reporting, and other potential sources of bias. In cases where there were discrepancies in the assessment, a third reviewer intervened to resolve the issues after discussion.

Statistical methods
Meta-analysis was performed using R Studio software. For dichotomous variables, relative risk (RR) was used as the effect measure, and for continuous variables, the standardized mean difference (SMD) was used as the effect measure, with 95% confidence intervals (95% CI). The heterogeneity of the studies was assessed using the I2 statistic. If there was no significant heterogeneity (P > 0.1, I2 < 50%), a fixed-effects model was used; otherwise, a random-effects model was applied. A P-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Risk of bias assessment for included studies
All 10 included studies were randomized controlled clinical trials. Most of the studies showed good performance in terms of random sequence generation and completeness of outcome reporting. However, there were substantial risks of bias related to allocation concealment, blinding of participants and personnel, blinding of outcome assessors, and selective reporting (Figs. 2 and 3).

Total asthma symptom score (TASS)
TASS is an indicator used to measure the overall symptom control of childhood asthma during immunotherapy. Six studies reported TASS. The meta-analysis showed that immunotherapy significantly reduced the total asthma symptom score (SMD = -1.05, 95% CI [-2.09; -0.01]). However, there was high heterogeneity (I² = 97%, τ² = 1.5808), indicating significant inconsistency across the studies. Immunotherapy may, in most cases, reduce asthma symptoms in children and improve their quality of life. Sensitivity analysis further confirmed the robustness of the conclusion by evaluating the impact of individual studies on the overall results. Despite substantial differences in efficacy among the studies, immunotherapy generally showed a positive effect on improving asthma symptoms in children (Figs. 4 and 5).

Total medication score (TMS)
TMS reflects the medication usage in children receiving immunotherapy, including changes in medication types and dosages. The meta-analysis revealed that immunotherapy reduced the total medication score (SMD = -1.32, 95% CI [-2.21; -0.43]), but the results also showed high heterogeneity (I² = 92%, τ² = 0.6992). Sensitivity analysis showed that excluding specific studies did not affect the overall effect (Figs. 6 and 7).

Visual analog scale (VAS) score
VAS scores indicate the change in symptom severity. The results showed no significant difference in VAS scores between the experimental and control groups (SMD = -1.77, 95% CI [-3.76; 0.22]). The VAS scores exhibited high heterogeneity (I² = 97%), which may indicate inconsistent symptom relief effects of immunotherapy across different children (Fig. 8).

FEV1%
Forced Expiratory Volume in 1 s (FEV1%) is a key indicator of lung function and is used to assess the severity of asthma and the effect of immunotherapy on lung function in children. There were considerable differences in the reports of FEV1% across studies, resulting in high heterogeneity (I² = 96%, τ² = 1.3813). The meta-analysis showed that immunotherapy had no significant effect on FEV1% (SMD = 0.51, 95% CI [-0.38; 1.40]) (Fig. 9).

Discussion
In recent years, allergen-specific immunotherapy has gained widespread attention as one of the effective treatments for allergic asthma. Pediatric asthma is one of the most common chronic diseases worldwide, with its prevalence continuously rising, significantly affecting the quality of life of children and placing a substantial burden on their families. Although conventional drug therapy plays a crucial role in alleviating symptoms and controlling acute exacerbations, long-term use of these medications may lead to adverse side effects, such as growth restriction and decreased bone density. Therefore, immunotherapy, which works by modulating the immune system’s response and alleviating allergic reactions, has gradually become an important complementary strategy in the treatment of pediatric asthma. The core concept of allergen-specific immunotherapy is to promote immune tolerance through long-term, low-dose allergen exposure, thus reducing allergic inflammation, alleviating asthma symptoms, and reducing drug dependence. However, the efficacy and safety of immunotherapy exhibit certain heterogeneity across different studies, making a systematic evaluation of its effects particularly important.
In this meta-analysis, we evaluated the efficacy of allergen-specific immunotherapy for pediatric asthma. Regarding efficacy, the significant improvement in total asthma symptom scores indicates that immunotherapy can effectively alleviate asthma symptoms in children, showing promising clinical potential. The alleviation of asthma symptoms is directly related to the quality of life of affected children. Reducing the occurrence of daily symptoms allows children to participate more fully in school and social activities, thereby improving their overall quality of life. However, the high heterogeneity among studies suggests that immunotherapy does not yield consistent results in all patients. Specifically, the effectiveness of immunotherapy may be influenced by various factors, including the type of allergen, individual differences in immune responses, treatment dosage, and the duration of the therapy.
Furthermore, the reduction in drug dependence, especially the decreased use of corticosteroids, highlights the significant clinical value of immunotherapy. Long-term use of corticosteroids in children may have adverse effects on growth, bone density, and other aspects of development. Immunotherapy offers a new treatment option to reduce drug dependence. Reducing drug usage not only lowers the risk of side effects but may also help prevent the development of drug resistance. Although immunotherapy can effectively reduce drug dependence, the extent of medication reduction varies across studies, which is closely related to the treatment protocols, baseline health conditions of the patients, and whether other treatments are used in combination. Therefore, clinical practice should consider combining immunotherapy with other conventional treatments to optimize efficacy and minimize potential side effects.
However, the effects of immunotherapy on certain key indicators, particularly visual analog scale (VAS) scores and pulmonary function (FEV1%), did not meet expectations. Immunotherapy primarily alleviates allergic symptoms by modulating the immune system’s response but has a relatively limited effect on structural changes in the airways, particularly airway remodeling and resistance. Improvement in pulmonary function typically requires longer treatment durations and follow-up, and the effects of immunotherapy may take several years or even longer to become apparent. Therefore, although immunotherapy can relieve allergic symptoms and acute exacerbations, long-term improvements in pulmonary function still require management through other therapeutic measures, such as inhaled corticosteroids or bronchodilators. Additionally, since the results regarding pulmonary function improvement vary significantly across studies, further research is needed to clarify the impact of immunotherapy on pulmonary function, particularly in different types of asthma patients.
Although this meta-analysis provides strong evidence for the use of immunotherapy in pediatric asthma treatment, there are still certain limitations that may affect the generalizability and clinical applicability of the results. Specifically, the sample sizes of the studies were relatively small, and there were differences in baseline characteristics of the patients across studies. Moreover, some studies showed biases related to blinding and allocation concealment. Although we included multiple high-quality randomized controlled trials, the long-term effects and safety of immunotherapy still require more longitudinal data for further validation. Existing studies mainly focus on short-term efficacy, with a lack of comprehensive evaluation of the long-term impact of immunotherapy on children’s growth, development, immune systems, and other potential long-term risks. Potential side effects of immunotherapy, such as allergic reactions or the development of new allergens, have not been sufficiently addressed in clinical practice. Therefore, future research should focus on the long-term efficacy and safety of immunotherapy, as well as how to optimize its application in different clinical types of pediatric asthma.
Overall, allergen-specific immunotherapy has shown significant clinical potential in the treatment of pediatric asthma, especially in symptom control and reduction of drug dependence. Despite some heterogeneity in efficacy, immunotherapy remains a valuable treatment option for asthma, particularly in reducing drug side effects and improving the quality of life. In clinical practice, physicians should personalize treatment plans based on the specific condition and treatment response of each patient, and combine immunotherapy with other medication strategies to achieve the best therapeutic outcome. As more high-quality, large-scale clinical studies are conducted, the efficacy evaluation of immunotherapy will become more precise, providing stronger evidence for individualized treatment of pediatric asthma.
