Multidisciplinary team approach to pelvic floor disorders: A systematic review
Background
Pelvic floor disorders (PFDs) severely and negatively impact on quality of life, affecting physical, psychological, and social well-being. Historically, PFDs have been managed within single-specialty frameworks, yet the complexity of these conditions often necessitates a comprehensive, multidisciplinary team (MDT) approach. This systematic review assesses the effectiveness of MDT strategies in improving outcomes for individuals with PFDs, aiming to identify the benefits and potential advantages of integrated, multi-specialty care for these complex conditions.
Methods
Following PRISMA guidelines, a systematic search of PubMed, EMBASE, and Cochrane CENTRAL was conducted through September 25, 2024. Inclusion criteria were studies of adult patients with PFDs in which MDT protocols were integrated for diagnosis, treatment, or rehabilitation. Eligible studies included comparisons between MDT and non-MDT care, as well as studies without a comparison group. Outcomes of interest were patient-reported outcomes, therapy adherence, complication rates, and recurrence rates. Three independent reviewers screened titles and abstracts for eligibility, with discrepancies resolved through discussion to reach a consensus. Data extraction was independently performed by two reviewers, and any discrepancies were adjudicated by a third reviewer.
Results
In total, 24 studies conducted across various countries were included, comprising 21 retrospective cohort studies, 2 prospective cohort studies, and 1 randomized controlled trial (RCT), with sample sizes ranging from 36 to 3600 patients. The findings generally indicate favorable outcomes with an MDT approach, including enhanced surgical results, greater symptom relief, reduced recurrence and complication rates following surgical interventions, improved treatment adherence, and high patient satisfaction.
Conclusions
An MDT approach offers benefits for PFD management, improving patient-reported outcomes, treatment adherence, and quality of life. This review supports the broader adoption of MDT approaches for comprehensive PFD management. Further research is warranted to confirm long-term effectiveness and standardize MDT protocols.

Introduction
Pelvic floor disorders (PFDs) encompass a range of conditions, including pelvic organ prolapse, urinary incontinence, fecal incontinence, and chronic pelvic pain, which significantly impact the quality of life of affected individuals. These conditions are prevalent among women, with increasing incidence correlating with aging, childbirth, obesity, and other factors. The prevalence of PFDs ranges from 10 % to 50 % and is largely influenced by age and parity, with urinary incontinence being the most common disorder. Despite their commonality, PFDs are often underdiagnosed and undertreated due to the complex interplay of anatomical, physiological, and psychosocial factors that contribute to their development and persistence.
Given the complexity and multifactorial nature of PFDs, effective management requires strategies that go beyond traditional single-specialty care. Traditional management strategies are often led by a clinician of a single specialty and may fail to provide optimal outcomes due to the limitations in addressing the full spectrum of symptoms and contributing factors. In contrast, a multidisciplinary team (MDT) approach integrates the expertise of various healthcare professionals, including urogynecologists, colorectal surgeons, physiotherapists, urologists, and pain specialists, among others, to deliver personalized and holistic care.
In recent years, MDT care has been shown to improve patient outcomes, enhance the quality of care, and reduce healthcare costs associated with PFDs. By facilitating coordinated care and promoting shared decision-making, MDT care ensures that all aspects of the patient's condition are addressed through collaborative efforts.
Given the growing interest in and implementation of MDT approaches in managing PFDs, it is crucial to systematically evaluate the evidence supporting this model of care. This systematic review aims to synthesize current research on the effectiveness, challenges, and outcomes associated with MDT management of PFDs, providing insights into the benefits and limitations of this approach and guiding future clinical practice and research in this area.

Methods
The study is reported based on Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines 2020. The protocol is registered in PROSPERO (ID: CRD42024598425).

Ethics statement
As a systematic review, the present work did not involve human or animal subjects, and only involved the analysis of de-identified data; therefore, ethics approval was not required.

Search strategy
This systematic review was conducted in adherence to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines. On September 25, 2024, a comprehensive search was conducted across three major databases: PubMed, EMBASE, and Cochrane CENTRAL, from their inception to the search date. To ensure precision and relevance, we used targeted search terms including “multidisciplinary team,” “pelvic floor disorders,” “pelvic organ prolapse,” “urinary incontinence,” “fecal incontinence,” and “multicompartment reconstruction,” along with Boolean operators. Medical Subject Headings (MeSH) terms were included where applicable, and the filters used were abstract available and studies published in English or Chinese.
An example search string used is:
(“Multidisciplin∗” OR “Interdisciplin∗”) AND (“pelvic floor” OR “Pelvic organ prolapse”)
After removing duplicates, titles and abstracts were independently screened by three reviewers (Tzong-Yun Tsai, Chun-Kai Liao, and Shu-Huan Huang) according to predefined eligibility criteria. Full-text articles of potentially eligible studies were then retrieved and independently assessed. Any discrepancies were resolved through discussion until consensus was reached.

Study selection criteria
This systematic review adhered to the PICOS criteria: participants, intervention, comparison, outcomes, and study design. Studies included were those that evaluated adult patients with PFDs, including conditions such as POP, urinary incontinence, fecal incontinence, or multicompartment reconstruction, and specified the integration of MDT protocols for diagnosis, treatment, or rehabilitation (I). Studies comparing MDT care with non-MDT care (C), or with no comparison group, were eligible. The outcomes considered were patient-reported outcomes, adherence to therapy, complications, and recurrence rates (O). Eligible study designs included randomized controlled trials (RCTs), prospective cohort studies, retrospective cohort studies, and case-control studies (S).
Reviews, letters, comments, editorials, conference abstracts, case reports, proceedings, personal communications, and studies involving non-human subjects were excluded.

Main outcomes measures and data extraction
The primary outcome measures included patient-reported outcomes (e.g., symptom scores, satisfaction with care), adherence to therapy, complication rates, and recurrence rates for PFDs. Data extracted from eligible studies included study characteristics (author, year of publication, country, design), patient population, MDT protocols, outcomes, and study limitations. Data extraction was independently performed by two reviewers (Tzong-Yun Tsai and Chun-Kai Liao), and any discrepancies were resolved by consultation with a third reviewer (Shu-Huan Huang).

Quality assessment
The Quality Assessment Tool for Observational Cohort and Cross-Sectional Studies, recommended by the National Institutes of Health, was used to assess the quality and robustness of methodologies in the included studies. Quality assessment was independently performed by 2 reviewers, and any discrepancies were resolved by discussion.

Results

Search results

A total of 1017 studies were identified through the database searches, including 530 from PubMed, 59 from Cochrane CENTRAL, and 428 from EMBASE. After removing duplicates, 528 studies remained for the title and abstract screening. During this process, 501 records were excluded due to irrelevant titles, off-topic studies, and reviews or conference abstracts. Subsequently, 27 full-text articles were retrieved for detailed assessment. Among these, 3 articles were excluded due to duplicate patient populations, and 2 articles were excluded because the study populations were not relevant to PFDs. Finally, 24 studies met the inclusion criteria and were included in the systematic review. A PRISMA flow diagram outlining the study selection process is provided in [Fig. 1].

The characteristics of the included studies are summarized in [Table 1]. The studies were distributed across several countries including the United States (US) (7 studies), the United Kingdom (UK) (8 studies), Germany (2 studies), Canada (3 studies), the Netherlands (3 studies), Ireland (1 study), and Norway (1 study). The included studies were from 2007 to 2024, and most studies were published between 2019 and 2024. The majority of the studies were retrospective (21 studies), 5 were prospective, and 1 was an RCT. The number of patients ranged from 36 to 3600. The mean age of the patients ranged from 38 to 67 years, and the majority of patients were women with a range of PFDs including POP, urinary incontinence, fecal incontinence, and obstructive defecation syndrome (ODS) [Table 1].

Patient disorders
The included studies included a diverse range of patient disorders, with POP being the most commonly reported condition, often accompanied by urinary incontinence or rectal prolapse. Several studies focused on patients with chronic pelvic pain (CPP). Studies also addressed other PFDs including fecal incontinence, stress urinary incontinence, and other complex pelvic floor conditions, reflecting the broad clinical spectrum encountered in multidisciplinary pelvic floor programs.

The purpose of a MDT
The stated purpose of the MDT approach across the included studies was to enhance patient outcomes, optimize treatment plans, and improve care quality. Many MDT programs aimed to strengthen surgical planning and execution, particularly for procedures such as rectopexy and sacrocolpopexy for POP, through collaborative input from multiple specialties. Beyond surgical optimization, MDTs were frequently utilized to ensure comprehensive preoperative assessments, integrate perioperative care, and coordinate postoperative rehabilitation. Improving patient satisfaction and promoting adherence to therapy regimens were also commonly cited goals, achieved by offering streamlined access to multiple specialists in a cohesive care model. Moreover, several studies emphasized the importance of addressing both the physical and psychological dimensions of PFDs, thereby aiming to enhance functional outcomes, reduce symptom burden, and improve overall quality of life through an integrated multidisciplinary strategy.

Type of MDT approaches
The included studies demonstrated various types of MDT approaches. Some focused on joint surgical procedures involving multiple specialties, while others emphasized collaborative preoperative and postoperative decision-making through regular MDT meetings. Non-surgical interventions, such as pelvic floor muscle training (PFMT) and chronic pain management were also highlighted, with integrated psychological and counseling support. The overall distribution showed that MDTs were designed to integrate surgical, medical, and therapeutic expertise to provide comprehensive patient care.

Time of MDT involvement
The timing and continuity of MDT involvement varied considerably across the included studies, reflecting differences in clinical settings and care models. Some MDTs met biweekly or monthly to discuss and coordinate patient management, while others held weekly or remote meetings to ensure timely updates and multidisciplinary input. In terms of care duration, the period over which patients were followed by MDTs ranged from 3 months to over 5 years, depending on the complexity of the case and the nature of interventions required.

MDT team composition
The composition of MDTs across the included studies demonstrated considerable diversity, tailored to the specific needs of patients with PFDs. The most commonly involved specialists were colorectal surgeons, urogynecologists, urologists, and physiotherapists, forming the core of most MDTs. Beyond these primary disciplines, many teams incorporated nurse specialists and psychologists to address perioperative care and psychosocial factors. Additional members in some MDTs included dietitians, radiologists, social workers, and occupational therapists, broadening the scope of support services available to patients. A few studies further expanded their teams to involve specialists in physical medicine and rehabilitation (PMR) and internal medicine.

The outcomes reported in the included studies are summarized in [Table 2]. Across the studies, reported outcomes focused on several key areas. Surgical outcomes, such as operative time, blood loss, complication rates, recurrence rates, and hospital length of stay, were commonly measured, with many studies noting low complication rates, low recurrence of rectal prolapse, and successful POP restoration. Studies reported high success rates in surgical interventions for POP restoration, significant improvements in symptoms like obstructed defecation and fecal incontinence, and low recurrence rates of rectal prolapse when specific techniques, such as middle compartment suspension (MCPS) were used (see [Table 3]).
Many studies also reported favorable outcomes such as low complication rates, short hospital stays, and low readmission rates. Functional and quality-of-life improvements were commonly reported, particularly patient-reported outcomes related to reductions in obstructed defecation and rectal toxicity. Some studies also showed enhancements in pelvic floor functioning and quality of life. Pain reduction and psychological well-being were notable, with reductions in anxiety and improvements in quality of life when psychological support was provided.
High levels of patient satisfaction with MDT care were also reported, with patients expressing confidence in their care and valuing the opportunity to see multiple specialists in a single visit. Studies that included psychological and motivational support or same-day physiotherapy reported better adherence to treatment, with increased attendance and completion rates for PFMT. Overall, the outcomes reflected the benefits of MDT approaches, though many studies were limited by retrospective design and a lack of long-term follow-up.

Symptom relief
Rudroff et al. reported a significant reduction in symptoms of pelvic organ prolapse, with 80 % of patients achieving complete restoration. Similarly, improvements in urinary incontinence were observed in 76 % of patients in the same study. In another study, Kalev et al. reported improvements in obstructed defecation symptoms in 62 % of patients who underwent combined resection rectopexy and sacrocolpopexy, with a recurrence rate of 3 %.

Complications and recurrence
Complication rates varied among the studies, with postoperative complications generally being minor. For example, Devane et al. reported a complication rate of 2.5 % and a readmission rate of 2.5 % within 30 days. Kalev et al. reported a rectal prolapse rate of 7 % in patients who received resection rectopexy and 2.7 % in patients who received combined resection and sacrocolpopexy. Bordeianou et al. reported that the inclusion of middle compartment suspension (MCPS) during rectal prolapse repair significantly reduced the recurrence rate (7 % in the MCPS group vs. 17 % in the non-MCPS group, p = 0.043).

Patient satisfaction and adherence to MDT protocols
High levels of patient satisfaction were consistently reported across the included studies, highlighting one of the key advantages of the MDT approach. For instance, O'Leary et al. reported a 94 % patient satisfaction rate, with 97 % of patients finding it beneficial to consult multiple specialists during a single visit. Brown et al. further demonstrated that attendance to same-day physical therapy increased adherence rates significantly, with 91 % of patients attending therapy sessions compared to 61 % in non-MDT settings.

Quality assessment results
We evaluated the included studies based on 14 distinct quality indicators, systematically assigning ratings of “Yes,” “No,” “Cannot Determine (CD),” “Not Applicable (NA),” or “Not Reported (NR)” and determining an overall quality classification as “Good,” “Fair,” or “Poor.” Fourteen out of 24 received a “Poor” overall rating, indicating significant limitations in methodological quality because these studies commonly lacked essential elements such as sample size justification, multiple assessments of exposure over time, and adjustment for confounding variables. However, these limitations are not “fatal flaws” since these flaws are inherent in observation cohort or cross-section studies. Only one study achieved a “Good” rating, meeting most quality criteria and demonstrating high methodological rigor due to randomized-controlled trial study design. On the other hand, most studies still clearly stated their research question and defined the study population well, with consistent application of inclusion and exclusion criteria. Further, most studies had sufficient timeframes for observing associations between exposure and outcome and clearly defined outcome measures, enhancing the reliability of results. Overall, while a few studies met high standards, the majority were rated as poor to fair, warranting cautious interpretation of results and underscoring areas for improvement in future observational research.

Discussion
The traditional approach to patient care involves a compartmentalized approach where specialists such as gynecologists, urologists, and colorectal surgeons operate independently, often leading to fragmented care and incomplete assessments, which result in suboptimal outcomes. In contrast, the MDT approach emphasizes integrated care through collaboration among specialists. The goal is to provide comprehensive, coordinated care that addresses all aspects of the patient's condition. This systematic review highlights the current landscape and multifaceted benefits of employing an MDT approach in the management of PFDs, which are reflected in enhanced patient-reported outcomes, significant symptom relief, and notable improvements in overall quality of life. The reported outcomes across the studies consistently suggest positive results in both surgical and non-surgical management of PFDs, CPP, and related conditions. Many studies showed high surgical success rates, including significant improvements in obstructed defecation, fecal incontinence, and pelvic organ prolapse restoration, along with low recurrence and complication rates. Additionally, functional outcomes, such as improved pelvic floor functioning, reduced pelvic pain, and enhanced quality of life, were commonly reported. Patient satisfaction was also high. Studies incorporating psychological support or same-day physiotherapy demonstrated improved adherence to treatment plans. Overall, our results reflect a broad international perspective on MDT approaches to PFDs and related conditions.
In the past decade, MDT approaches have been studied and/or applied in almost every field of medicine. A PubMed search of the term “multidisciplinary approach” from 2023 to the present identified over 14,000 relevant citations. This, along with our results provides data on the overwhelming interest in developing MDT methods to improve patient care. We identified many studies regarding an MDT approach to various aspects of PFDs and related conditions that did not meet the criteria for inclusion in our review. For example, Caldwell et al. developed evidence-based pathways for the management of PFDs that optimize shared decision-making between patients and surgeons. A survey of Italian physicians overwhelmingly concluded that an MDT approach is the best way to improve outcomes of patients with PFDs, especially patients with complex conditions. Hainsworth et al. stressed that pelvic floor dysfunction is “multifactorial and multicompartmental” and that a MDT approach is necessary to achieve optimal assessments and patient outcomes. Although the number of studies was small, a recent systematic review and meta-analysis concluded that pelvic floor physical therapy combined with “mindfulness” was the best approach to treat women with chronic pelvic pain.
From a broader perspective, two fields of medicine in which MDT approaches have been examined in depth and applied are the management of patients with cancer and those with cardiovascular and metabolic conditions. A recent meta-analysis of the impact of MDT conferences in the management of patients with various malignancies included about 135,000 patients from 59 studies, with about 48,000 patients managed with an MDT approach and 85,000 with standard care. The primary outcome, overall survival (OS) was significantly greater in patients managed with an MDT approach (30 months vs. 19 months). Two recent meta-analyses of the effect of an MDT approach for the treatment of patients with non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) both reported that the MDT approach was associated with significantly longer OS, as well as increased likelihood of receiving treatment and shorter treatment duration. Some authors, however, have pointed out barriers to MDT approaches to the care of patients with cancers, such as lack of standardized protocols and physician reluctance to embrace sharing the development of a treatment plan.
A recent systematic review and meta-analysis by Tu et al. of the effect of an MDT approach in the primary care of patients with diabetes found that the MDT approach was associated with a significant reduction of cardiovascular risk factors. Another recent review of patients with diabetes and comorbidities concluded that although there was high heterogeneity in the 19 studies included and that MDT protocols varied, an MDT approach improved overall outcomes. A recent expert panel review of the role of the “multidisciplinary heart team in cardiovascular medicine” concluded that “heart teams” play an important role in the care of patients with many different cardiovascular diseases; however, best practices for these teams need to be better defined and more evidence-based support of their effectiveness is needed.
While every medical field cannot be examined in this report, it is notable that studies are suggesting that an MDT approach can improve the care and outcomes of hospitalized older adults, and improve outcomes of pain management for persons with chronic pain.
In summary, the present review adds to the expanding literature on MDT in healthcare by illustrating the significant advantages of a multidisciplinary team approach in managing PFDs. Future research should focus on developing standardized MDT protocols and investigating long-term outcomes to further substantiate the benefits of this approach and guide clinical practice in this area.

Strengths and limitations
The strengths of this review include its comprehensive analysis of current evidence on MDT approaches for PFDs, incorporating diverse outcomes such as surgical success rates, patient-reported improvements, adherence to therapy, and better quality of life. By including studies from various countries and healthcare settings, the review captures an international perspective, enhancing the generalizability of its findings. Additionally, it considers both surgical and non-surgical outcomes, offering a well-rounded view of the impact of an MDT approach. Practical aspects, such as team composition and continuity of care, are also emphasized, providing useful insights for healthcare systems considering developing an MDT program for the treatment of various conditions. However, limitations exist, primarily due to the predominance of retrospective studies, which may introduce bias and limit causal conclusions. The evidence largely relies on observational studies, reducing the generalizability of MDT effectiveness. Language limitations may introduce publication bias, as studies in languages other than English and Chinese were excluded. Variability in MDT protocols, team composition, and follow-up durations across studies further complicated the identification of an optimal MDT model. Additionally, significant heterogeneity among the included studies—such as differences in study design (retrospective versus prospective), patient populations, MDT compositions, interventions, and inconsistently reported outcomes—precluded quantitative synthesis and prevented the performance of a meta-analysis. Similarly, the lack of standardized reporting on MDT structure (e.g., involved specialties, meeting frequency and format, timing of MDT involvement) made it infeasible to conduct meaningful subgroup analyses based on MDT characteristics. Future systematic reviews would benefit from adopting standardized definitions, detailed reporting of MDT protocols, and harmonized outcome measures to facilitate meta-analytic comparisons and enable more robust subgroup analyses to better inform best practices. Despite these limitations, this review provides a valuable foundation for understanding the role of an MDT in the management of PFDs and guiding future research in the field.

Conclusions
This systematic review underscores the value of an MDT approach in managing PFDs, emphasizing its impact on patient outcomes, symptom relief, and quality of life. The integration of diverse specialties, such as urogynecology, colorectal surgery, urology, physiotherapy, and psychological support, enables a holistic care model that addresses the multifaceted nature of PFDs. Evidence from the included studies highlights the success of MDT approaches in reducing complication and recurrence rates, optimizing functional outcomes, and achieving high levels of patient satisfaction and treatment adherence. For patients undergoing surgical interventions within an MDT framework, outcomes were notably successful in restoring pelvic function and alleviating symptoms. Non-surgical strategies, especially when combined with psychological support, further enhanced treatment adherence and overall well-being.
