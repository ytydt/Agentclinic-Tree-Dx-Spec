A sustainable approach in pumpkin seed oil processing line: Recent advances in pumpkin seed oil and oil processing by-products
Pumpkin seed oil (PSO) has gained popularity worldwide for its nutritional profile and biological effects, significantly increasing its market demand and consumption. However, pumpkin seed oil cake (PSOC), as the secondary by-product from oil processing, contains high potential value and is scarcely utilised. With the PSO increase in production, a large amount of PSOC will be generated. The key to achieving a sustainable food system is maximising value from the food supply chain. This review aims to summarise the nutritional profile of PSO and PSOC and highlight the current advance in the biological activity of PSO and the valorisation strategies of PSOC. This review also concludes the current advance in food applications of PSO and PSOC in meat and bakery products, respectively. A better understanding of their value and current advances can help to achieve the maximisimg value from PSO processing line in an effective and sustainable approach.

Introduction
Pumpkin, belonging to the Cucurbitaceae family, is an important vegetable widely cultivated in the world. The most commercially cultivated pumpkin species are Cucurbita pepo, Cucurbita maxima, and Cucurbita moschata. According to the data from FAOSTAT (Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations Statistical Analysis) in 2022, the annual pumpkin worldwide production is around 23 million tons, whereas the largest production region in Asia (50.5 %), followed by Europe (21.2 %), Americas (15.4 %), Africa (11.9 %), and Oceania (1.0 %). The global production of pumpkin seed, which accounts for 4 % to 6 % of total weight of the pumpkin, is estimated to be around 0.92–1.38 million tons. Pumpkin seed is commonly used as food ingredient and for oil production, of this, approximately 4.9 % is used for the production of pumpkin seed oil.

Pumpkin seed oil (PSO) has received much attention due to its nutritional value and biological effects. PSO contains high level of unsaturated fatty acid and other biological compounds, such as phytosterols, squalene, tocopherols, and carotenoids; in addition, many studies have shown that the potential of PSO in intestinal parasites, prostate, and hair loss treatment. The market demand and consumption of PSO are increasing, and the PSO market is expected to reach USD 2.07 billion by 2030, with a GAGR of 10.4 % during period 2024 to 2030. PSO in the market is usually obtained through traditionally pressed method (e.g. cold and hot pressed) and solvent extraction method. Besides, other novel extraction methods, including supercritical fluid extraction, aqueous enzymatic extraction, and ultrasonic assisted extraction have been employed in PSO extraction. Notably, during oil processing, pumpkin seed oil cake (PSOC), the secondary by-product of pumpkin seed oil, is generally discarded or converted into animal feed. However, this utilisation strategy is not the most effective approach, since oil cake contains high potential value, such as protein, fibre, phenolics, and minerals, which can be re-utilised. According to the United States Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) developed the Wasted Food Scale (Fig. 1), if the food by-products cannot be avoided, the most effective approach is to recover and re-utilise them as food ingredients to feed people (the second tier in Fig. 1). Additionally, many studies have reported that oil cake has great for re-introducing into food chain as a promising ingredient to fortify the nutritional profile of food products. On the other hand, the above utilisation approach also can reduce food waste and enhance food system resilience, which is in line with sustainable development goals (SDG), such as SDG 2 (zero hunger) and SDG 12 (ensure sustainable production and consumption). Therefore, to better help to maximise the value of pumpkin seed in the oil production line, it is important to understand the value of PSO and PSOC and their current advances.
In this context, this review aims to summarise and highlight the nutritional profile of PSO and PSOC, the biological effects of PSO, and the current popular valorisation strategy of PSOC. Besides, this review also concludes the current advances in food applications of both. Overall, this review could provide useful information to promote a complete valorisation of pumpkin seed oil processing lines and build a more sustainable and resource-efficient food system.

PSO is rich in unsaturated fatty acid (UFA), where oleic acid (C18:1; 21.0 % - 46.75 %) and linoleic acid (C18:2; 32.57 % - 60.6 %) are major fatty acids. In terms of saturated fatty acid (SFA) in PSO, palmitic acid (C16:0; 10.0 % - 19.08 %) and stearic acid (C18:0; 5.16 % - 10.98 %) are major fatty acids. Besides, PSO is detected to contain low levels of ALA (α-linolenic acid; C18:3 n-3; 0.03 % - 0.7 %) and GLA (γ-linolenic acid; C18:3 n-6; 0.33 % - 0.4 %), indicating that its potential nutritional value. Studies have been indicated that ALA and GLA are bioactive components, which have potential biological benefits for human health, such as cardioprotective and colorectal cancer prevention. On the other hand, the fatty acid of PSO could be influenced by environmental factors (e.g. climate, soil, and cultivation area), genotype (e.g. pumpkin variety), and extraction methods, especially in the content of oleic acid (C18:1) and linoleic acid (C18:2). investigated the effect of extraction methods on fatty acid profile of PSOC, three extraction methods, including cold-pressed, microwave pretreatment, and supercritical fluid) were employed. They found that the PSO obtained from cold-pressed had highest SFA content (27.31 %) and lowest UFA content (72.69 %), while PSO obtained from supercritical fluid extraction had highest UFA content (75.86 %) and lowest SFA content (24.14 %). Similarly, extracted PSO using three extraction methods (solvent extraction, cold-pressed extraction, and aqueous enzymatic extraction) and found that extraction method affected fatty acid profile of PSOC: solvent extraction (oleic acid 21 %, linoleic acid 61.9 %) and cold-pressed extraction (oleic acid 34.1 %, linoleic acid 47.9 %) showed the highest linoleic and oleic acids, respectively. According to and, the properties or structures of fatty acids have different responses to different extraction conditions (e.g. solvent type and temperature). Besides, analysed 928 samples of PSO (Cucurbita pepo L.) from Croatia, Slovenia, Austria, and Hungary, and found that geographical location had a significant effect on the ratio of oleic acid (C18:1) to linoleic acid (C18:2), with the PSO obtained from Austria had a lower ratio than PSO obtained from Croatia and Slovenia. This variation could be attributed to climate conditions. Climate conditions (e.g. temperature) determine fatty acid accumulation during plant growth, the change in climate conditions cause adaptive responses, thereby affecting the fatty acid accumulation Notably, the genotype is also an important factor influencing fatty acid accumulation in plants, since different varieties could respond differently to the same climate conditions.

PSO also contains various bioactive compounds, mainly including phytosterols, tocopherols, squalene, and carotenoids, which have various health benefits and contribute to their biological activities. The bioactive compounds of PSO are listed in Table 2.

Phytosterols
Phytosterols are natural bioactive compounds widely found in plants, which have a variety of health benefits (e.g. cholesterol-lowering, preventing cardiovascular diseases, and anti-inflammatory), and their structural similarity to cholesterol. detected 14 phytosterols in PSO (Cucurbita maxima, obtained from Tunisia), namely campesterol, campestanol, cholesterol, clerosterol, stigmasterol, sitosterol, sitostanol, Δ5-avenasterol, 24-methylenecholesterol, Δ5,23-stigmastadienol, Δ5,24-stigmastadienol, Δ7-campesterol, Δ7-stigmastenol, and Δ7-avenasterol. As shown in Table 2, among the reported phytosterols in PSO, β-sitosterol (49.25–208.2 mg/100 g oil) was the major phytosterol. Notably, according to, Δ7-sterols were specific phytosterols in PSO and could be as identification markers to detect PSO authenticity. Besides, as shown in Table 2, the content and components of phytosterols in PSO can be affected by genotype and environmental factors. Except the above factors, the process of PSO extraction can also influence the phytosterols content. Furthermore, investigated the effect of pretreatment methods (microwave and roasting) on the phytosterols content of cold-pressed PSO, the results showed that the PSO obtained from cold-pressed with pretreatments (microwave for 598.9 mg/100 g oil and roasting for 715.6 mg/100 g oil) had higher phytosterol content than control (473.5 mg/100 g oil; cold-pressed without pretreatment).

Tocopherols
Tocopherols are important lipophilic antioxidants in widely plant oils, which are classified into four types, i.e., α-tocopherol, β-tocopherol, γ-tocopherol, and δ-tocopherol. As can be seen from Table 2, α, γ, and δ are the most common types of tocopherols in PSO. Additionally, Singh & Kumar and detected the presence of β-tocopherol isomer in PSO, ranged from 0.61 to 7.10 (mg/100 g oil). Notably, the content of tocopherols and the ratio of isomers in PSO can be affected by multiple factors, such as pumpkin genotype and environmental conditions (e.g. soil, climate, and location), as shown in Table 2. For example, analysed the tocopherol content in two varieties of PSO (Cucurbita moschata Duchesne and Cucurbita argyrosperma Huber) obtained from Yucatan, Mexico. They reported that δ-tocopherol (32.87–41.78 mg/100 g oil) was most predominant tocopherol in PSO, followed by α-tocopherol (1.29–3.75 mg/100 g oil) and γ-tocopherol (0.54–1.52 mg/100 g oil). In contrast, in study, who detected PSO (Cucurbita pepo L. subsp. pepo var. styriaca Greb.) obtained from Poland and the content of γ-tocopherol (37.74 mg/100 g oil) was found to be the highest among three types of tocopherols identified (α-tocopherol and δ-tocopherol were 0.43 mg/100 g oil and 0.12 mg/100 g oil, respectively).

Squalene
Squalene, a nature polyunsaturated triterpenoid compound widely presents in various oils, has many biological activities, such as anti-aging in skin, anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and cholesterol-lowering). The content of squalene in PSO ranges from 116.0 to 283.58 (mg/100 g oil), depending on pumpkin variety and growing location (Table 2). Compared with other conventional diet oils, PSO is similar to olive oil (234.75–393.2 mg/100 g oil), but higher than sunflower oil (12.56–18.68 mg/100 g oil), rapeseed oil (3.42–5.70 mg/100 g oil), soybean oil (5.84–13.74 mg/100 g oil), and sesame oil (0.99.- 8.76 mg/100 g oil). The above suggests that PSO is a potential good source of squalene.

Carotenoids
Carotenoids are natural lipophilic antioxidants and bioactivity compounds, which are important to human nutrition and health (e.g. preventing neurodegenerative diseases and health of the eyes). and reported that PSO contained β-carotene (0.04–0.38 mg/100 g oil) and lutein (0.04–0.21 mg/100 g oil). Besides, identified 5 carotenoids in PSO, including β-carotene (0.69 mg/100 g oil); α-carotene (0.24 mg/100 g oil); zeaxanthin (0.25 mg/100 g oil); violaxanthin (0.47 mg/100 g oil), and lutein epoxide (1.85 mg/100 g oil), whereas β-carotene content was higher than and studies. This difference could be attributed to the pumpkin variety, growing factors, and extraction methods.

PSO has various health benefits, such as antioxidant capacity, anti-inflammatory, anthelmintic effect, anti-hyperplastic effect, neuroprotective effect, and against alopecia. These biological activities could be attributed to the bioactive compounds in PSO, as mentioned above. Table 3 concludes the current advances in biological activity of PSO. Many studies have demonstrated the antioxidant and anti-inflammatory effects of PSO, which can be attributed to its bioactivity compounds, such as squalene, tocopherols, phytosterols, and among others. evaluated the antioxidant capacity of PSO extracts through in vitro chemical assay (DPPH and ABTS), and shown that PSO extracts had good antioxidant capacity (DPPH assay as 15.25 mg Trolox equivalent/100 mL sample and ABTS as 22.90 mg Trolox equivalent/100 mL sample). There is also evidence to indicate antioxidant capacity of PSO in animal model. reported that PSO can contributed to reduction of alleviate heat stress-induced oxidative damage in rabbit model, in which male rabbits were supplied PSO in their daily diet at 2.0 mL/kg diet for 8 weeks. In terms of anti-inflammatory, indicated the anti-inflammatory activity of PSO (IC50 = 6.25 μg/mL) through in vitro chemical method (by determining the inhibition of serum bovine albumin denaturation), which was higher than control (diclofenac sodium, IC50 = 5.47 μg/mL).
Trichinellosis, also referred to as trichinosis, is a parasitic infection caused by Trichinella spiralis; most of human infections are caused by consuming uncooked meat (common in pork) that carries encysted Trichinella spiralis larvae. The conventional treatment drugs are albendazole and mebendazole, however, these drugs could cause many side effects, such as headaches, fever, anemia, and pancytopenia. Therefore, finding safe and natural alternative drugs are necessary. In a study by, treatment with PSO showed a significant effect on the reduction of parasite in terms of the number of adults and encysted larvae in the infected mice model; in addition, PSO exhibited the potential anti-inflammatory through reducing MMP-9 (matrix metalloproteinase-9) levels in intestinal and muscular tissues from infected mice.
Benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH), a common chronic disease in the aging men population (beginning around 50 years old), results in begin enlargement of prostate gland followed by bladder outlet obstruction; presenting as lower urinary tract symptoms (e.g. weakness of urinary stream and hesitancy initiating flow). In study, who used phytosterol extracts from PSO as treatment to evaluate its effect on inhibition of BPH in the animal model (rats), they found that the PSO phytosterol extracts treatment (1 mL per day per rat) for 4 weeks can ameliorated the pathological prostate enlargement in BPH induced rats, with the prostate weight of the former (treated group using PSO extracts, 875.1 mg) was lower than the latter (BPH induced group, 1072.9 mg). In another single-blind randomised clinical study, treated BPH patients using tamsulosin and PSO. The results showed that PSO treatment (360 mg, twice per day) for 3 months can relieved BPH symptoms assessed by IPSS (international prostate symptom score). Although PSO treatment was not as effective as tamsulosin (alpha blockers), the former treatment did not cause any sides effect.
Besides that, PSO exhibits the effects on neuroprotective effects and against alopecia.
In terms of neuroprotective effect, the animal study shown that PSO exhibited prophylactic effect on neuro-inflammatory interactions in the ovariectomised rat model. In terms of against alopecia, PSO showed promising potential in male mice model and results showed that the PSO orally (1 mL/kg) for 14 days or topically (10 μL) for 7 days can acted in the proliferation of the hair follicles and without toxicity in the liver.

Pumpkin seed oil cake from oil processing
Considering the above context, pumpkin seed is largely used to obtain pumpkin seed oil; during the pumpkin seed oil processing, pumpkin oil processing by-product, known as press cake (i.e. pumpkin seed oil cake; PSOC) is generated after oil extraction, and which also contains potential nutritional value (e.g. protein, fibre, minerals, and phenolics). Valorisation of PSOC into high value-added products, which in line with sustainable food development and circular economy strategies, thus, developing comprehensive utilisation of PSOC has received much attention, such as re-introducing in food supply chain as food ingredient and the extraction of high value-added compounds. In this section, the compositions of PSOC and its current advance in high value-added compounds extraction are concluded.

Application of PSO in food
The recent exploration of PSO-added food prototypes including burger, sausage, and meatball, whereas PSO is mainly as animal fat replacer and antioxidant for above products (Table 7).
The fatty acid composition and bioactivity compounds of PSO make it efficient functional ingredient to improve the fatty composition of meat products and provide potential health effects. developed a novel dried-fermented sausage (‘fuet’) using PSO emulsion as pork fat replacer at 50 %, 75 %, and 100 % replacing level. The sausage made with 75 % of PSO or above can decreased SFA content and increased PUFA (mainly in linoleic acid content) compared to control (100 % pork fat sausage). In terms of colour and texture, PSO addition increased redness (a*), yellowness (b*), and springiness value, but decreased the hardness of the sausage. Finally, the reformulated sausage product had a lower sensory score than control in terms of appearance, taste, odour, and texture. Similarly, in another study that has used PSO emulsion as animal fat replacer in burger, the results shown that an increase in redness (a*) and yellowness (b*), while texture properties (hardness, elasticity, cohesiveness, and chewiness) decreased. In terms of nutritional profile, the protein and vitamin E content of reformulated burger increased, and fat content decreased. Regarding with the fatty acid profile, the content of SFA and MUFA (mainly in oleic acid) decreased, while the PUFA (mainly in linoleic acid) content increased. Besides, in sensory evaluation, although all reformulated burgers decreased the texture and taste scores, the external aspect score increased.
In addition to the above, PSO has also been studied as an antioxidant ingredient to inhibit the formation of heterocyclic aromatic amines (HAAs) in cooked meat products. HAAs are Maillard reaction derivatives and classified as carcinogens, which are produced during thermal processing of high protein products, including baking, roasting, smoking, and frying. One study from, who added PSO in meatballs at 1 % and 2 % of total meatball weight and investigated its inhibitory effects on HAAs formation. The results showed that PSO addition can reduced the HAAs formation in pan-fried (10 min) and roasted meatballs (7 min) at cooking temperature between 150 °C to 250 °C, with the reduction rate reaching 76.25 % in pan-fried meatballs and 19.95 % in roasted meatballs at the highest temperature.

Application of PSOC in food
As mentioned earlier, PSOC, as a valuable ingredient, is a good source of protein, fibre, minerals, and phenolic compounds, offering the opportunity to enrich the nutritional value of these products.
reported that the addition of PSOC improved the pasta nutritional profile in terms of protein content, ash, oil, total phenolic content, and antioxidant capacity. Regarding with physical parameters of pasta, PSOC addition increased hardness and greenness and decreased lightness. Considering all the parameters of pasta, reformulated paste with 9 % PSOC had the overall good sensory scores. Similarly, PSOC was used to fortified bread nutritional value by. They found that PSOC addition increased protein, ash, and fibre content in bread, and 10 % PSOC addition had the highest sensory score among all bread products.
The PSOC was used in gluten-free crackers formulation. used two types of PSOC (cold-pressed and thermal pre-treatment before pressed) replaced chickpea flour at 20 % (w/w) and 35 % (w/w) replacing level and 100 % chickpea cracker as control. The results shown that PSOC addition increased protein and minerals (e.g. magnesium, iron, and zinc) content in crackers. After in vitro digestion, except for cracker made with 35 % cold-pressed PSOC, the protein digestibility of reformulated crackers (59.56 % - 74.86 %) were lower than control (76.97 %). This could be attributed to the phenolics in PSOC, which bind to proteins inhibit access to digestive enzymes.
used PSOC as bioactive ingredient to enrich the nutritional value of wafers. They reported that PSOC addition changed the functional properties of wafers in terms of water holding capacity (WHC) and oil holding capacity (OHC), whereas WHC decreased and OHC increased with increasing PSOC addition. PSOC addition did not significantly changed (p > 0.05) the hardness, but caused wafer colour to darker. Regarding with nutritional changed, the total phenolic content and antioxidant capacity were increased.
Apart from the above, PS protein extracted from PSOC has been used in several food applications. For example, added PS protein (5 %, 10 %, and 20 % of total wheat flour weight) in bread production, the results showed that PS addition did not affect (p > 0.05) the gas holding and shape hold capacity of the dough and increased the protein content of bread; in addition, bread contained up to 10 % PS protein had similar scores compared to control bread (without PS protein addition) in terms of bread quality evaluation (e.g. colour, aroma, taste, chewability of crumb, and appearance). Additionally, developed the low-fat cheddar cheese using PS protein-based double emulsions (25 % wt. protein concentration, water-in-oil-in-water). They found that cheese made with PS protein-based double emulsion had significantly lower (p < 0.05) hardness and oil loss than control (low-fat cheddar cheese) after 14 days of maturation.

The potential application of PSO in cosmetics
Considering the nutritional profile and biological activities of PSO, it also has been used as a promising ingredient for development of novel cosmetics. Investigated the potential of PSO in photoprotection cosmetic application and found that incorporation of PSO in formulation increased the photoprotective properties. developed PSO-based vitamin E cream formulation for topical application to nourish the skin and the results showed that PSO addition improved the antioxidant activity of the formulation, with the formulation containing 20 % PSO had highest antioxidant activity. formulated makeup remover using PSO and results showed that the remover containing PSO did not cause skin irritation and performed better than the comparator in terms of makeup removal ability.

Conclusions and future perspectives
PSO contains high level of UFA (mainly in oleic acid and linoleic acid) and is also rich in other biological compounds such as squalene, phytosterols, tocopherols, and carotenoids, which have been reported to exhibit excellent biological activities (e.g. antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and Inhibition of benign prostatic hyperplasia) in in vitro and in vivo studies. However, it is unclear the mechanism of its various biological activities and the corresponding compounds, the potential concentration limits of its use, and the question of safety. Therefore, more studies are needed to discover this field. Additionally, regarding with PSO in food application, PSO exhibits its potential as an animal fat replacer in the development of healthy meat products. However, it should be emphasized that the use of fat replacer can significantly changes the physical properties of meat products, including texture, colour, and appearance, which could affect sensory properties and consumer acceptance. Therefore, optimising the incorporation level of PSO or combination of PSO with other ingredients to produce meat products with desirable quality is important in further studies.
Besides, PSOC, as a major by-product of PSO processing, contains high potential value. Re-utilising this by-product in food development and recovering its high value-added compounds could greatly contribute to food waste avoidance and achieving sustainable food development. Currently, the utilisation of PSOC as a novel alternative plant-based protein source has exhibited high potential. Nevertheless, to maximise the potential of PSCO in plant-based protein sector, more studies are needed on extraction methods, digestibility, allergenicity, and sensory properties. On the other hand, the nutritional profile of PSOC indicates its potential value as a novel food ingredient and has been successfully applied in the development of bakery products to enrich their nutritional value. Future studies could focus on the expansion of a wider range of PSOC products and the nutrient bioavailability of reformulated foods to satisfy the requirements of specific markets and provide more nutritional information to consumers.
