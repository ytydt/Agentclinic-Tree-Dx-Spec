Herbal Remedies: A Complementary Approach to Diabetes Mellitus Management
ABSTRACT
Diabetes mellitus (DM) is a metabolic disorder characterized by hyperglycemia due to deficiencies in insulin secretion and action, or both, affecting 463 million individuals worldwide and leading to significant health complications. Current therapies focus on symptom relief and delay onset of complications; however, a multitargeted approach is essential to avert complications and achieve optimal glycemic control—utilizing herbal medications used in Ayurveda and Chinese medicine. Herbal remedies have been tested on animal models to assess their efficacy in diabetes prevention and optimize glucose metabolism. A comprehensive search of scientific databases, including Medline, Scopus, EBSCO, and others, was performed. Numerous scientific studies have demonstrated that the phytochemicals found in medicinal plants exhibit antihyperglycemic properties. This research examines the therapeutic properties of Coriandrum sativum, Phyllanthus emblica, Punica granatum, and Zingiber officinale, owing to their prevalent application in the studied region.

INTRODUCTION
Diabetes mellitus (DM) causes hyperglycemia due to abnormal glucose metabolism, leading to acute and chronic complications affecting kidneys, retina, arteries, nerves, and bones, causing irreversible damage. Currently, 463 million adults globally have DM in 2019. Without effective prevention, it will climb to 578 million by 2030 and 700 million by 2045.
DM is categorized as Type 1 (T1DM), Type 2 (T2DM), gestational diabetes, and neonatal diabetes. T1DM and T2DM are common, among which T2DM being more frequent. Currently, T2DM management involves lifestyle changes, oral and injectable medications, surgery, and complementary therapies.

Exploring natural remedies for diabetes management
Ancient medical therapies have significant potential due to cost-effectiveness, accessibility, and little side-effects and were widely employed before the emergence of contemporary medicine. Ayurveda, Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM), and indigenous traditions of Africa and the Americas have recorded the antidiabetic benefits of specific plants. These approaches, refined over millennia, have found numerous plants purported to manage blood sugar readings and prevent diabetic complications. This review analyzes the medicinal attributes of Coriandrum sativum, Phyllanthus emblica, Zingiber officinale, and Punica granatum. The species in this review were selected for their extensive research data and their capability in the management of DM and prevention of complications.

Coriandrum sativum
Coriandrum sativum (L) (C. Sativum) belongs to the Apiaceae family and is known as Coriander. It has been validated by comprehensive animal studies as a medicinal herb in the management of hyperglycemia. C. Sativum has been shown to possess larvicidal, bactericidal, and fungicidal properties and is used to treat anorexia, dyspepsia, flatulence, subacid gastritis, and gastrointestinal motility disorders.
In 2012, the antidiabetic and antioxidant effects of C. Sativum in alloxan-administered DM rats revealed a dramatic decrease in blood glucose and serum total cholesterol (TC), triglycerides (TG), decreased lipid peroxidation (LPO), increased antioxidant capacity of liver, and antioxidant parameters activity related to diabetic rats besides the effects were attributed to phenolics, flavonoids, steroids, and tannins in the extract. These data support C. Sativum for glycemic control.
An experiment conducted by Kajal et al. on the impact of hydroalcoholic extract from C. Sativum seeds in a Streptozotocin (STZ)-induced diabetes model showed lowered blood glucose, pain sensitivity, oxidative stress, and reduced symptoms of diabetic peripheral neuropathy. Various docking studies show the strong affinity of flavonoids for the tumor necrosis factor (TNF-α) binding site, thus lowering the oxidative stress and pro-inflammatory cytokine levels, reducing diabetes-induced neuropathic pain.
C. Sativum research has encouraging results in regulating glucose levels and postponing diabetic complications. Further clinical studies are necessary to ascertain its dosage, safety, and efficacy.

Phyllanthus emblica
Phyllanthus emblica (L) (P. emblica), of the Phyllanthaceae family, is known for its therapeutic and nutritional properties. In TCM, it is used for treating throat pain, hypertension, and hepatitis B, while in Africa, it is used for malaria, jaundice, and renal calculus. Researches have shown that when regularly incorporated in diet, P. emblica enhances insulin action, stimulating the insulin-signaling pathway, safeguarding β cells, decreasing free radicals, easing inflammation, and lowering advanced glycation end products, thus aiding diabetes management.
A clinical study on endothelial dysfunction and oxidative stress involving 80 individuals with T2DM, who were administered aqueous P. emblica extract, atorvastatin, and a placebo for 12 weeks, showed reduction in TC, TG, and low-density lipoprotein (LDL), while there was improvement in high-density lipoprotein (HDL). Furthermore, the combination of atorvastatin and P. emblica showed better lipid profile and HbA1C, improved endothelial function and systemic inflammation, and reduced oxidative stress, as measured by levels of nitric oxide (NO), glutathione (GSH), highly sensitive C-reactive protein (hsCRP), and malondialdehyde (MDA).
To study the in vivo antidiabetic effect of P. emblica stem barks, Quranayati et al. administered ethyl acetate extract from P. emblica (EPE) stem barks orally to STZ-induced DM rats with weekly monitoring of blood glucose levels, which showed reduced blood glucose levels.
The results of a similar study showed that EPE had immunoregulatory effects in nonobese diabetes mice, lower blood glucose and HbA1C levels, higher insulin levels, and increased insulin-producing β cells, suggesting that EPE supplements prevent T1DM and are an immunomodulator.
The results of various studies compliment a 2023 study on STZ-induced T1DM mice showing the superior efficacy of EPE over other extracts in reducing blood glucose, HbA1C, increased insulin levels, and better lipid profile.

Zingiber officinale
Zingiber officinale (L) (Z. officinale), known as Ginger, belongs to the Zingiberaceae family, is native to East and Southern Asia, and has been used worldwide as a spice and medicine since ancient times. In Ayurveda, Chinese, Islamic, African, and Caribbean medicine, it is used to treat throat infections, asthma, inflammation, and more. The American Diabetic Association classifies Z. officinale as a spice and a nutraceutical, and its health benefits include disease prevention and therapy.
In a study by Ayuob et al. using STZ-induced DM rat model, groups administered with ginger plus cinnamon showed significant growth in total antioxidant function and decrease in fasting blood glucose (FBG) levels compared to DM groups with no treatment. Furthermore, this combination boosted the homeostasis model assessment of β-cell function (HOMA-β) cell index, suggesting enhanced insulin secretion in diabetic groups.
A study by Ghoreishi et al., evaluating the effectiveness and safety of administering ginger supplements to patients with T2DM with nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) for three months, showed improved baseline values for blood insulin, HDL cholesterol, and reduced insulin resistance assessed by homeostatic model evaluation of insulin resistance (HOMA-IR). In addition, the mean systolic and diastolic blood pressure were lowered.
The diabetic cardiomyopathy observed in hearts of DM patients is characterized by increased collagen and free radical production, leading to inflammation and fibrosis. Research using ginger extract on STZ-induced DM rats showed reduced inflammation and cardiac fibrosis as observed on histological examination of the left ventricle compared to the untreated group. Additionally, notable decline in serum levels of creatine kinase muscle and brain, lactate dehydrogenase, and aspartate aminotransferase were observed along with downregulation of expression of TGF-β1 and TGF-β3 genes, along with the angiotensin II type 1 receptor in treated groups, thus suggesting the influence extract on the regulation of genes associated with the SMAD/transforming growth factor (TGF)-β pathway.

Punica granatum
Punica granatum (L) (P. granatum) is a prominent member of the Punicaceae family and has been used for centuries in traditional medicine in the Middle East, India, and China to treat various ailments, including inflammation, rheumatism, throat pain, diarrhea, and metrorrhagia. In Unani medicine, flowers are given as dietary supplements to treat DM.
Fruits high in antioxidants and polyphenols, including pomegranates, have been shown to promote the secretion of insulin by beta cells in T1DM patients. Pomegranate juice (PJ) has been shown to lower FBG in diabetic individuals, which could be due to methanolic and punicic acids from the peel extracts. The mechanism involves inhibition or activation of nuclear factor κB and peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor γ while decreasing oxidative stress and lipid LPO.
A study in Iran where 52 obese T2DM patients were given pomegranate seed oil for eight weeks reported significantly reduced FBG with no substantial changes in insulin and HbA1C levels. Additionally, consumption of fresh PJ significantly reduced the FBG, insulin resistance and increased β-cell activity.
In a double-blind placebo-controlled trial by Rahul et al., 40 patients with DM and myocardial infarction observed that administering pomegranate extract of whole fruit (PEWF) significantly reduced FBG, postprandial glucose, and HbA1C levels.
Bagri et al. observed that oral administration of pomegranate blossom aqueous extract for 21 days lowered TC and FBG. While TG, low-density lipo-protein cholesterol (LDL-C), very-low-density lopoprotien-cholesterol (VLDL-C), and tissue LPO were higher than in the diabetic control group, high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C), GSH, and antioxidant enzymes were lower. Thus, it was concluded that the dietary supplements flower aqueous extracts can cure and prevent chronic diseases with a heterogenous lipoprotein profile, lowered antioxidant status, and impaired glucose metabolism. Through routes unknown, P. granatum flower petals may prevent diabetes by attaching to peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor-gamma and producing NO.
Standardization of extracts of P. granatum is important while studying its safety and effectiveness in the treatment of DM and also when replicating or comparing results with other studies to arrive at a final conclusion.

CONCLUSION
Natural remedies complement traditional diabetes treatment. Coriandrum sativum, Phyllanthus emblica, Zingiber officinale, and Punica granatum herbs, along with diet and lifestyle changes, may help people control their blood glucose levels and prevent complications. It could be beneficial for those seeking holistic therapy in addition to regular treatment. It is crucial to recognize that natural remedies, while beneficial, are not medical replacements. Clinical study and testing are needed to evaluate whether these diabetes treatments are safe, effective, and suitable for long-term usage.
