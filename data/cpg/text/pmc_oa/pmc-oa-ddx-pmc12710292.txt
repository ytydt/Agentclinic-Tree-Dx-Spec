PI-RR: A Reporting System for Local Prostate Cancer Recurrence Evaluation After Radiation Therapy or Radical Prostatectomy
Biochemical recurrence, marked by rising prostate specific antigen levels, is a common and challenging clinical scenario following curative treatments for localized prostate cancer (PCa), such as radical prostatectomy (RP) or radiation therapy (RT). An accurate assessment is critical for detecting local recurrence and distinguishing it from distant metastatic disease, thereby guiding salvage therapies. While multiparametric MRI has demonstrated strong performance in detecting local recurrence, the lack of standardized reporting has historically limited its clinical utility and reproducibility. To address this variability, an international consensus-based Prostate Imaging for Recurrence Reporting (PI-RR) system was introduced in 2021. PI-RR adopts a 5-point Likert scale, similar to Prostate Imaging Reporting and Data System, but places greater emphasis on dynamic contrast-enhanced MRI and incorporates knowledge of the prior primary tumor location to grade the likelihood of recurrence. This review article provides a comprehensive overview of the PI-RR system, detailing its imaging acquisition recommendations, specific scoring criteria after RT and RP, clinical implications, and future directions within the evolving landscape of PCa imaging.

INTRODUCTION
Prostate cancer (PCa) is a significant global health concern. Radical prostatectomy (RP) and radiation therapy (RT) are established treatments with curative intent for patients with localized disease. However, 27%–53% of treated patients experience biochemical recurrence (BCR). This presents a diagnostic dilemma: BCR due to treatable local recurrence or the manifestation of distant systemic disease. Management of these patients critically depends on accurate disease localization.
In this post-treatment setting, multiparametric MRI (mpMRI) has emerged as an indispensable tool for noninvasive assessment of the treated prostate bed or remnant gland. Recurrent PCa typically presents as a focus of cellularity (restricted diffusion) and enhanced neoangiogenesis (early contrast enhancement). Despite the proven accuracy of mpMRI, the transition from the Prostate Imaging Reporting and Data System (PI-RADS) v2.1 framework, designed for the native gland, to the post-treatment environment is challenging. Treatment-related changes, such as diffuse fibrosis and volume reduction after RT or scar tissue and architectural distortion after RP, substantially alter the expected T2-weighted imaging (T2WI) signal intensity. This fundamental alteration renders the conventional interpretation highly subjective and variable among readers.
Recognizing the need for standardization in this complex scenario, an international expert panel published the Prostate Imaging for Recurrence Reporting (PI-RR) guidelines in 2021. The PI-RR provides a structured, universally applicable framework to standardize the performance, interpretation, and reporting of mpMRI for local recurrence, thereby facilitating communication among radiologists, urologists, and radiation oncologists. The PI-RR system explicitly shifts the diagnostic focus by prioritizing functional sequences over T2WI to address post-treatment anatomical changes.

MRI ACQUISITION GUIDELINES
The PI-RR system recommends following the technical standards of PI-RADS v2.1 for mpMRI sequences, such as T2WI, diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI), and dynamic contrast-enhanced (DCE)-MRI. However, specific adjustments are crucial for optimizing imaging of post-treatment anatomy and minimizing artifacts.
• T2WI: For post-RP patients, PI-RR specifically recommends obtaining three orthogonal planes (axial, coronal, and sagittal) of T2WI. This is essential for the detailed evaluation of the vesicourethral anastomosis (VUA) and adjacent structures, which are common sites of recurrence.
• Field of view (FOV): In addition to small FOV mpMRI sequences, at least one large FOV sequence (T1WI or DWI) is required to screen for suspicious lymphadenopathy or bone metastases, which signify systemic progression.
• DCE-MRI DCE plays a critical role because focal early enhancement is a hallmark of recurrence. The use of subtracted DCE images is highly recommended to enhance the visualization of subtle early enhancements, particularly in scar tissue or radiation-altered glands.
• Timing of MRI: To avoid confounding inflammatory changes, mpMRI should be performed at least 3 months after RP and at least 3 months after RT.
• Artifact management: Post-surgical clips or brachytherapy seeds can cause susceptibility artifacts, often severely degrading DWI and DCE images. Metal artifact reduction techniques may be helpful in these cases.

PI-RR SCORING SYSTEM: A SHIFT IN DIAGNOSTIC PARADIGM
The PI-RR system employs a 5-point Likert scale, ranging from PI-RR 1 (very low likelihood of recurrence) to PI-RR 5 (very high likelihood), to assess the overall suspicion of local recurrence. A defining feature of PI-RR is the explicit consideration of the primary tumor location, which increases the suspicion score if the new finding corresponds to the site of the original disease. T2WI provides essential anatomical information but does not determine the final PI-RR assessment score after either RT or RP.

POST-RT ASSESSMENT
Following RT, the prostate gland typically exhibits glandular loss and fibrosis as the predominant histopathological changes, resulting in a diffusely low signal intensity on T2-weighted images. Therefore, T2WI has limited utility in differentiating recurrence from post-treatment changes. However, recurrent tumors typically exhibit diffusion restriction due to high cellularity and early hyperenhancement secondary to neovascularization, similar to the imaging features of the original tumor. Therefore, both DWI and DCE are co-dominant after RT. Recurrences are typically confined to the site of the original primary tumor; therefore, lesions that are clearly visualized on DWI or DCE images and correspond to the location of the original tumor are assigned higher scores. POST-RP ASSESSMENT
After RP, recurrence occurs predominantly in the VUA and surrounding soft tissue. Scarring and fibrosis severely limit the utility of T2WI and DWI. Consequently, DCE is the dominant sequence for PI-RR scoring after RP. • Upgrading rule: If the DCE score is 2 or 3 and the DWI score is 4 or 5, the overall PI-RR score is upgraded by one point. Therefore, DWI plays an adjunctive role.

CLINICAL IMPLICATIONS AND THE ROLE OF PI-RR
The PI-RR score offers robust, standardized risk stratification that informs subsequent clinical decisions, although it should be integrated with conventional risk factors.
• PI-RR 1-2 (Very Low to Low Likelihood): The focus shifts to ruling out distant disease using highly sensitive modalities, such as prostate-specific membrane antigen (PSMA) PET/CT. Salvage RT may still be considered after RP based on the clinical guidelines, irrespective of the imaging results.
• PI-RR 3 (Equivocal/Indeterminate): Targeted biopsy is generally indicated to obtain histopathological confirmation, especially for post-RT patients, where definitive salvage therapy carries a risk of morbidity. For post-RP patients, further imaging or biopsy is recommended, particularly if the PSA levels exceed 1 ng/mL.
• PI-RR 4-5 (High to Very High Likelihood): Strong recommendation for a biopsy (for post-RT) or moving towards salvage treatment (for post-RP). Staging with highly sensitive nuclear medicine scans (e.g., PSMA PET/CT) should precede definitive local treatment to confirm the absence of systemic disease.

PI-RR AND PSMA PET/CT INTEGRATION
The emergence of PSMA PET/CT has profoundly affected the evaluation of BCR. PSMA PET offers exceptional sensitivity for low PSA levels but can be limited in the local setting, particularly near the VUA after RP, due to urinary tracer excretion.
Therefore, the PI-RR system plays a crucial, complementary role.
• Targeting biopsy: PI-RR is essential for guiding MRI-US fusion biopsies.
• Addressing PSMA indeterminacy: In cases where PSMA-PET is negative or equivocal, PI-RR provides the necessary local anatomical and functional assessments.
The synergy between the local details provided by PI-RR and the high molecular sensitivity of PSMA-PET is increasingly recognized as the optimal diagnostic approach for BCR.

CONCLUSION AND FUTURE PERSPECTIVES
The introduction of the PI-RR system represents a paradigm shift toward standardized evidence-based reporting of local PCa recurrence. By carefully adapting PI-RADS principles to post-treatment anatomy and prioritizing functional sequences, PI-RR enhances the reliability of mpMRI. Early studies have demonstrated the system's excellent inter-reader reproducibility and promising diagnostic performance, bolstering its utility in clinical practice.
Looking ahead, several key areas require focus:
• Prospective validation: PI-RR requires extensive prospective multi-institutional studies to validate its clinical efficacy across diverse patient populations and levels of reader experience.
• Integration with focal therapy: Currently, the PI-RR does not provide specific guidelines for evaluating recurrence after focal therapy. Developing a PI-RR adapted for this setting is a critical next step.
• Formal PSMA integration: To maximize the diagnostic yield, the next version of the PI-RR is likely to incorporate PSMA PET findings, leading to a comprehensive multimodal reporting system.
In summary, PI-RR is poised to significantly improve patient care by facilitating the precise detection and localization of local recurrence, thereby enabling clinicians to transition from a single-modality approach to an integrated, personalized diagnostic strategy for patients with BCR.
