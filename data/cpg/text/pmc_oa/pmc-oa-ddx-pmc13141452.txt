Safety evaluation of PD-1/PD-L1 therapies for treatment of NSCLC: a systematic review, bayesian network meta-analysis, and cost-effectiveness analysis
Background
Lung cancer remains the foremost cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide. With the expanding use of PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors in its treatment, a comprehensive understanding of their safety profiles and economic implications is essential to guide clinical decision-making.
Objective
This study evaluates the safety and economics of PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors in NSCLC. Our findings indicate that they are a cost-effective option with a favorable benefit-risk profile, offering practical guidance for immunotherapy decisions.
Methods
We have conducted a comprehensive literature search for randomized controlled trials (RCTs) evaluating PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors in PubMed, Web of Science, and the Cochrane Library up to August 2025. Eligible studies were limited to English-language RCTs relevant to network meta-analysis (NMA). This systematic review and NMA was conducted and reported following the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines. Data extraction was carried out independently by two investigators. The data were then synthesized using a Bayesian random-effects model for NMA. The primary outcome was the incidence of immune-related adverse events, which was analyzed and compared across different PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitor regimens.
Results
A total of 60 eligible articles were involved, covering 13 treatments and 23,992participants. The safety evaluation revealed significant differences in toxicity profiles among PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors, highlighting treatment-specific adverse effect patterns. Based on disability-adjusted life years (DALYs), the pharmacoeconomic analysis identified pembrolizumab as a dominant therapeutic strategy.
Conclusions
Among the evaluated regimens, nivolumab plus ipilimumab was associated with the broadest toxicity spectrum. Sintilimab demonstrated a more favorable safety profile than other PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors and ranked highest in the safety assessment.Our pharmacoeconomic analysis identified pembrolizumab as the most cost-effective option across treatment lines for NSCLC.
The protocol was registered in advance in PROSPERO online platform as CRD42023442502.

Introduction
Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) is the most common cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide, as reflected in current epidemiological data. According to GLOBOCAN 2022 estimates, there were 2,480,301 new lung cancer cases (12.4% of all cancers) and 1,817,172 deaths globally in 2022. Patients with NSCLC face a low 5-year relative survival rate, which poses a substantial global health burden.
Immunotherapy is recognized as another potent anti-tumor therapeutic approach following surgery, chemotherapy, targeted therapy and radiotherapy. Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs), a key focus in cancer immunotherapy, block the immune escape mechanisms of cancer cells using antibodies. ICIs have proven effective in treating patients with certain tumors that lack specific molecular targets, and they have significantly transformed the treatment strategy for lung cancer. ICIs consist of three main components: cytotoxic T lymphocyte associated protein-4 (CTLA-4), programmed death ligand-1 (PD-L1) and programmed cell death protein-1 (PD-1). Numerous large clinical trials have demonstrated significantly longer survival for lung cancer patients treated with PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors, offering substantial therapeutic advantages, especially for those who are not sensitive to specific targeted therapies. Toxicities associated with ICIs include immune-related adverse events (irAEs) and infusion reactions. Compared to conventional therapies, irAEs associated with PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors differ in terms of the affected organs, morbidity patterns, and severity. These events can affect multiple organ systems simultaneously and are potentially fatal. Moreover, compared with chemotherapy, the use of PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors significantly increases treatment costs, thereby imposing a substantial economic burden.
Beyond toxicity, the financial burden of immunotherapy is becoming an increasingly important consideration in the treatment of lung cancer. To date, studies examining the costs associated with the safety of PD-1/PD-L1 therapies in lung cancer are limited, primarily focusing on a small number of specific drugs with a finite number of participants and trials. Thus, a well-designed study is needed to investigate the comparative cost-effectiveness and safety of various PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors in patients with NSCLC. We conducted a network meta-analysis (NMA) and cost-effectiveness analysis, integrating both indirect and direct evidence, to compare the safety and cost-utility of PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors for lung cancer treatment from the perspective of U.S. payers.

Method
This systematic review and NMA was conducted in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines. The protocol was registered with PROSPERO online platform (CRD42023442502).

Data sources and searches
A systematic search of the Cochrane Library, PubMed, and Web of Science databases was conducted to identify English-language trials published up to August 2025. The search was performed using keywords including PD-1, PD-L1, NSCLC, adverse event, safety, side effect, adverse effect, adverse reaction, untoward effects, untoward reaction, secondary reaction, secondary effect, side reaction, atezolizumab, pembrolizumab, durvalumab, nivolumab, tislelizumab, sintilimab, sugemalimab, camrelizumab, cemiplimab, penpulimab, serplulimab. The complete search strategy is provided in the supplementary materials.

Eligibility criteria
Eligible studies were selected based on the following criteria: (1) randomized controlled trials (RCTs) that evaluated any form of PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors’ irAEs, in comparison with blank, placebo or active control.(2) Participants included were patients with NSCLC, confirmed histologically or cytologically.(3)The intervention was PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors, with no restrictions imposed on dosage, treatment duration, age, gender, or geographic region. (4)The primary patient-relevant outcome was defined as the incidence of irAEs. Exclusion criteria comprised (1)duplicate publications, (2)studies with unclear methodology, (3)ongoing trials, (4)conference abstracts, (5)posters, (6)investigations with invalid interventions or outcomes, (7) incomplete data, (8)non-English articles. Detailed outcome parameters and interventions of interest were summarized in Supplementary Table S1.

Data extraction
Data extraction was independently conducted by two investigators. The extracted information included the first author’s name, publication year, trial design, PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors, statistical model, type of irAEs, sample size, patient age, sex, ethnicity, and histological type of NSCLC. Clinical outcomes involved the incidence of any irAEs. Adverse events were categorized according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events.(Common terminology criteria for adverse events ver.5.0.)

Safety
IrAEs were considered the primary outcome. An extensive and detailed data extraction was conducted by evaluating all available information from the main texts, supplementary materials, and ClinicalTrials.gov records. A total of 22 irAEs were assessed, including skin toxicity, severe skin toxicity, hypothyroidism, hyperthyroidism, hepatotoxicity, thyroiditis, colitis, diarrhoea, pancreatitis, pneumonitis, myocarditis, hyperglycemia, hypophysitis, adrenal insufficiency, nephritis, myositis, Guillain-barre syndrome, arthralgia toxicity, rhabdomyolysis, meningitis, autoimmune hemolytic anemia and ocular toxicity. Data were obtained exclusively from the enrolled RCTs, with exclusion criteria applied to irAEs that were reported solely in the experimental groups and observed in only one included study.

Risk of bias and certainty of evidence
The risk of bias was independently assessed by two investigators using the criteria recommended by the Cochrane Handbook. Five key domains were evaluated: random sequence generation, allocation concealment, blinding of participants and personnel, blinding of outcome assessment and completeness of outcome reporting. The primary endpoints were evaluated independently by two investigators, and the quality of evidence was classified as high, moderate, low, or very low certainty. All discrepancies regarding eligibility criteria, data extraction, and assessments of the certainty and risk of bias of evidence were resolved through discussion and consensus.

Data synthesis and statistical analysis
Summary statistics consisted of rate ratio (RR) for irAEs and corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs), which were converted to a common unit of measurement when necessary. A conventional pairwise random-effects model was employed to evaluate direct associations between outcomes and interventions. Heterogeneity in treatment safety among trials was assessed using the I² statistic and Cochran’s Q test. The t-statistic (Hartung-KnappSidik-Jonkman method) was applied in analyses involving small sample sizes (n < 10). Heterogeneity variance was estimated using the restricted maximum likelihood (REML) method for both indirect and direct comparisons. To assess the safety profile of each NSCLC treatment, absolute event rates were evaluated. A two-sided p-value of less than 0.05 was defined as statistically significant for all analyses, except for the Cochran’s Q test, for which a p-value of 0.10 was applied. Sensitivity analyses were planned to assess the impact of clinical trial phase and risk of bias.
A Bayesian NMA was performed to simultaneously evaluate the safety profiles of all PD-1/PD-L1 therapies, and results were synthesized using random-effects models. Safety ranking probabilities for PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors were evaluated within the NSCLC patients. Publication bias was assessed using Begg’s rank correlation test”和“Egger’s linear regression test.
The main characteristics were compared across safety evaluations to assess the distribution of toxicity modifiers and to identify potential sources of methodological and clinical heterogeneity. Data synthesis was conducted using STATA (version18.0, TX, USA), Review Manager (version 5.4, Copenhagen, Danmark), WinBUGS (version 1.4.3, Cambridge, UK), and GRADE profiler (version 3.6, Hamilton, Canada).

Pharmacoeconomic analysis
In this pharmacoeconomic evaluation, we utilized NMA data to assess all currently approved PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors for NSCLC treatment. Only recommended dosing regimens were considered. Treatment options lacking appropriate pricing data or with incomplete adverse reaction data were excluded, while the combination therapy of CTLA-4 and PD-1/PD-L1 was excluded from the analysis. Given the advantages of utilizing the US dollar for data standardization, the economic model was developed from the perspective of US payers. This study was conducted in accordance with the CHEERS (Consolidated Health Economic Evaluation Reporting Standards) reporting guideline.

Model structure
Markov models were used to evaluate cost-utility. The model was developed using TreeAge Pro Software (version 2020, Williamstown, Massachusetts, USA) with yearly cycles. In each cycle, participants with advanced and metastatic NSCLC could remain in one of four different health states: NSCLC, disease progression, stable disease or death. All participants in the model initially presented with NSCLC and could transition to adverse events, disease progression, death, or remain in the NSCLC state. In each cycle, participants with resectable NSCLC were categorized into one of four possible states: event-free survival (EFS), progressive disease (PD), irAEs, or death. All participants in the model initially presented with EFS. The simulations ran for 10,00 iterations, representing one patient at a time. The proportion of participants in specific Markov states at each time point was determined using our NMA estimates. The time frame was set at 15 years, with assessments ranging from the baseline age (63 years) derived from the NMA to the life expectancy (78 years) reported in US life-tables.

Sensitivity analysis
A sensitivity analysis was performed to address parameter uncertainty and to identify the most influential parameters. Probabilistic sensitivity analysis was performed, demonstrating the stability of the expected incremental cost-effectiveness ratios (ICERs) with respect to key parameters of the model, which are illustrated in tornado diagrams. Results

Literature search results and study characteristics
Figure 1 exhibits the study retrieval process. 60 eligible studies (60 studies, 23,992 participants involved, listed in Supplementary Table S1) were identified from 13,579 potentially relevant studies, which fulfilled the eligibility criteria, involving the following 13 treatments in Fig. 2: atezolizumab, cemiplimab, chemotherapy, durvalumab, durvalumab plus tremelimumab, nivolumab, nivolumab plus ipilimumab, pembrolizumab, penpulimab, serplulimab, sintilimab, sintilimab plus IBI305, sugemalimab, and tislelizumab. Characterizations of the retrieved studies were presented in Supplementary Text S1. Baseline characteristics included in this NMA were age, sex, sample size, duration, dosage, and comparator arms which were balanced generally across studies. For each study, the risk of bias was assessed in Supplementary Fig. S1, which identified the primary limitations as possible lack of performance bias (37/60, 61.67%) listed in Supplementary Table S2. In terms of safety comparison, the incidence of irAEs of drug therapies for lung cancer was evaluated for 13 interventions, and Supplementary Fig. S2 presented the network of eligible comparisons for all therapies. Safety

Thyroid Toxicity
The incidence of thyroid toxicity was reported for thirteen treatments. Compared with chemotherapy, PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitor therapy was significantly associated with an increased risk of both hypothyroidism (RR 6.36,95%CI 5.10–7.94) and hyperthyroidism (RR 4.48, 95% CI 3.63–5.54). Furthermore, significant differences in thyroid toxicity risk were observed among the different PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors. In terms of hypothyroidism, sugemalimab had the highest rate compared to chemotherapy(RR 15.90, 95% CI 2.19–115.30). In terms of hyperthyroidism, penpulimab had the highest rate compared to chemotherapy(RR 23.79, 95% CI 1.37–413.98).

Skin Toxicity
The incidence of skin toxicity was reported for twelve inhibitors. Compared with chemotherapy, treatment with PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitorswas associated with a significantly increased risk of skin toxicity (RR 2.76, 95% CI 2.11–3.61). For skin toxicity, nivolumab plus ipilimumab had the highest rate(RR 17.81, 95% CI 1.05–302.24).

Hepatotoxicity
The incidence of hepatotoxicity was reported for ten inhibitors.Compared with chemotherapy, treatment with PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors was significantly associated with an increased risk of hepatotoxicity (RR 1.87, 95% CI 1.48–2.35). In hepatotoxicity, durvalumab plus tremelimumab had the highest rate(RR 13.02, 95% CI 2.47–68.54) of hepatotoxicity compared with chemotherapy.

Pneumonia
The incidence of pneumonia toxicity was reported in twelve inhibitors. Compared with chemotherapy, treatment with PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors was associated with a significantly increased risk of pneumonia (RR 3.76, 95% CI 2.44–4.30).

Hyperglycemia
The incidence of Hyperglycemia toxicity was reported for eight inhibitors. Compared with chemotherapy, treatment with PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors was associated with a significantly increased risk of hyperglycemia (RR 2.75, 95% CI 1.65–4.60) (Fig. 3).

The results of the cost-utility analysis of effective regimens for advanced and metastatic NSCLC are shown in Fig. 4. Each regimen was ranked in accordance with both aspects of safety and cost summarised in Fig. 4. Agent chemotherapy was identified as the minimum-cost option. Based on disability adjusted life years (DALYs), regimen Pembrolizumab was recommended as the dominant treatment. The effects and costs of all treatment options, including those for PD-1/PD-L1 and chemotherapy, were provided in Table 1. With increasing effectiveness, regimen pembrolizumab was also found to exhibit a better safety profile, as illustrated in Fig. 4. Several sensitivity analyses were conducted to verify the stability of the model under various assumptions. As shown in the tornado diagrams in Fig. 4, parameters mortality of lung cancer and skin toxicity of atezolizumab were identified as the most influential factors for atezolizumab, suggesting that atezolizumab may represent the most cost-effective option. Similarly, the tornado diagrams for pembrolizumab indicated that variations in discountrate and mortality of lung cancer most substantially affected the outcome, supporting the cost-utility superiority of pembrolizumab.

Discussion
Different PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors exhibit distinct toxicity profiles, and significant interpatient heterogeneity may occur even with the same treatment regimen. In term to hypothyroidism and hyperthyroidism, pembrolizumab performed better safety compared to atezolizumab, supported by high-level evidence. Following a cost-effectiveness analysis in advanced and metastatic NSCLC, pembrolizumab was found to be the dominant strategy based on mortality, DALY, irAEs and progressive-free survival, while chemotherapy alone incurred the lowest cost at $1090.82. In neoadjuvant therapy for NSCLC, pembrolizumab was demonstrated to be more cost-effective compared with nivolumab, with a treatment cost of $519,425. A similar conclusion was reached in adjuvant therapy. With a treatment cost of $563,106, pembrolizumab was shown to be the more cost-effective option compared to atezolizumab.
Consistent with previous studies on the safety of ICIs, our analysis confirmed that PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors are associated with a higher risk of irAEs compared with conventional chemotherapy. However, we have obtained some different findings. Unlike Gu et al., who exclusively focused on high-grade irAEs, we analyzed all grades of irAEs and covered more organ systems. Although Xu et al. reported irAE incidences for ICIs monotherapy, combination therapy, and chemotherapy across all cancer types and concluded that atezolizumab had the most favorable safety profile, our NMA was specifically restricted to patients with NSCLC. Additionally, our study incorporated PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors developed in China, enabling a more comprehensive and updated safety assessment.
This bayesian NMA and cost-effectiveness analysis represents the first comprehensive assessment of the risk of any irAEs with the use of PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors, providing a ranking of treatment safety and identifying a dominant strategy based on both economic and safety outcomes. All trials included in this meta-analysis were rigorously designed. Due to the insufficient evidence regarding the safety and cost-effectiveness of PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors in NSCLC and the controversial role of PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors as a more expensive therapy compared with conventional therapy, this meta-analysis was conducted to generate more robust evidence to inform clinical decision-making.
Despite the encouraging results, this study has several limitations that should be acknowledged. First, the general weakness of meta-analysis, the heterogeneity between included studies, patients in each trial received PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors alone or different combination regimens and the anti-tumor mechanisms of ICIs were different, which increase confounders to our study. Secondly, the number of included trials was relatively small. Several inhibitors included in our analysis were represented by limited evidence; for instance, only one trial evaluated sintilimab in combination with IBI305 which may lead to a limitation in the evaluation of results in this study.
This analysis indicates that irAEs can affect multiple organ systems, including the circulatory, digestive, endocrine, musculoskeletal, nervous, respiratory, and urinary systems. Therefore, early prediction, detection, and treatment of irAEs are essential to improve clinical management and patient outcomes. Furthermore, our study demonstrated that combination therapy involving CTLA-4 and PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors may be associated with an increased incidence of irAEs, particularly affecting the endocrine system. However, as the number of trials evaluating combination regimens included in this NMA was limited, further large-scale studies are warranted to better compare the safety profiles of PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors and CTLA-4 inhibitors, and to provide more appropriate treatment strategies for patients.
