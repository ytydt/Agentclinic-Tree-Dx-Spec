Economic evaluation of proton pump inhibitors in patients with gastro-oesophageal reflux disease: a systematic review
Abstract
Objectives
Our aim was to systematically review the cost-effectiveness of proton pump inhibitor (PPI) therapies and surgical interventions for gastro-oesophageal reflux disease (GORD).
Design
The study design was a systematic review of economic evaluations.
Data sources
We searched PubMed, Embase, Scopus, and Web of Science for publications from January 1990 to March 2023. Only articles published in English were eligible for inclusion.
Eligibility criteria
Studies were included if they were full economic evaluations comparing PPIs with surgical or alternative therapies for GORD. Excluded were narrative reviews, non-peer-reviewed articles, and studies not reporting cost-effectiveness outcomes.
Data extraction and synthesis
Two reviewers independently extracted data on study design, comparators, time horizon, and cost-effectiveness outcomes. The quality of studies was assessed using the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) checklist for economic evaluations.
Results
A total of 25 studies met the inclusion criteria. Laparoscopic Nissen fundoplication (LNF) was found to be cost-effective in long-term horizons, while PPIs were preferred for short- to medium-term outcomes. Differences in healthcare settings and methodological approaches influenced the study findings.
Conclusions
Strategic purchasing decisions for GORD treatment should consider the time horizon, healthcare setting, and cost structures. LNF may provide better long-term value, but PPIs remain effective for managing symptoms in the short term.
Study registration
PROSPERO, CRD42023474181.

WHAT IS ALREADY KNOWN ON THIS TOPIC
Gastro-oesophageal reflux disease presents a growing public health challenge, causing clinical problems and significant burdens. Identifying affordable and effective treatment methods is highly rewarding.

WHAT THIS STUDY ADDS
According to studies, laparoscopic surgery and proton pump inhibitor drug therapy are the most cost-effective treatments for gastric reflux in many patients.

HOW THIS STUDY MIGHT AFFECT RESEARCH, PRACTICE OR POLICY
The study results can guide policymakers, researchers, and specialists in making informed decisions about clinical strategies, treatment approaches, health service packages, insurance coverage, and reimbursement.

Introduction
Gastro-oesophageal reflux disease (GORD) is a growing health challenge that not only leads to various clinical problems but also imposes a significant economic burden. The prevalence of this disease has been reported as 23% in South America, 18.1% to 27.8% in North America, 11.6% in Australia, 8.8% to 25.9% in Europe, 8.7% to 33.1% in the Middle East, and 7.8% in East Asia. In the United States, GORD has an annual economic burden of 40 billion dollars. This condition can manifest with primary symptoms such as heartburn, regurgitation, dysphagia, and chest pain, or secondary symptoms such as cough, loss of appetite, burping, voice changes, nausea, and foreign body sensation. Several medical treatments including antacids, histamine H2 antagonists, proton pump inhibitors, and surgical interventions are available for managing reflux. Proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) have been the most effective treatment for GORD due to their effective acid suppression. Currently, omeprazole, esomeprazole, rabeprazole, and pantoprazole are available over-the-counter (OTC). Compared with other medications, PPIs have significantly improved various symptoms and manifestations of GORD. In 2020, omeprazole was ranked as the second-highest dispensed item in England, with almost 35 million prescriptions filled and an annual cost of GBP 82 million, and in Switzerland, it amounts to 30 million francs per year. A study in 2020 showed that anti-reflux surgery is more cost-effective than medication in the long-term for patients with severe GORD. Given the increasing prevalence and economic burden of GORD, evaluating the cost-effectiveness of treatment strategies for this disease is crucial. Cost-effectiveness analysis of medication is a useful method for comparing the cost and outcomes of different treatment strategies. PPIs have been prescribed in patients with GORD for years, and studies have been conducted to examine their cost-effectiveness compared with other treatment methods. This study aimed to systematically review the studies on the cost-effectiveness of PPIs compared with other common treatments in managing GORD.

Methods
This systematic review was registered in PROSPERO, CRD42023474181. The review is reported in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines and the PRISMA checklist is available as an online supplemental file. A comprehensive literature search was performed on PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, and Embase to identify economic evaluation studies related to treatments of GORD from January 1990 to March 2023. We chose 1990 as the cut-off year for study inclusion because this period marked significant advancements in GORD diagnosis and treatment, particularly with the introduction and widespread adoption of proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) as the standard pharmacological treatment. Additionally, health economic evaluations gained prominence during this era, providing a robust foundation for cost-effectiveness analyses. The search strategy for covering the topics of economic evaluation studies on the treatment of GORD included a combination of terms such as cost-effectiveness, economic evaluation, proton pump inhibitors, and gastro-oesophageal reflux (see online supplemental appendix 1). Following the removal of duplicates, two of the researchers independently screened the studies based on titles and abstracts, and then full texts. Any disagreements were resolved through discussion with a third researcher. Data extraction and analysis were conducted by one researcher and reviewed by a second person. To facilitate comparison, incremental cost-effectiveness ratios (ICERs) and costs were converted to US dollars in 2022 using the Campbell and Cochrane Economic Methods Group (CCEMG).

Inclusion and exclusion criteria
Inclusion criteria were all full economic evaluation studies (cost-effectiveness analysis (CEA), cost-utility analysis (CUA), or cost-benefit analysis (CBA)) in which PPIs were investigated as the intervention or comparison arm. Exclusion criteria included editorial studies, review articles, or protocols. Only articles published in English were eligible for inclusion.

Quality appraisal
Economic evaluation studies were independently evaluated by two researchers based on the Consolidated Health Economic Evaluation Reporting Standards 2022 (CHEERS 2022) checklist. This checklist includes 24 questions that are categorised into six main groups: title and abstract, introduction, methods, results, discussion, and other relevant information. If the study fully met the requirements of each question, a score of 1 was given. If it partially met the requirements, a score of 0.5 was given. Otherwise, a score of zero was given. Studies with scores higher than 18 were considered good (above 75%), studies within the range of 12±17 were considered average (50±74%), and studies with scores lower than 12 (50%) were categorised as low.

Results
Out of 2,340 studies, 1,401 duplicates were removed. After screening titles and abstracts, 886 studies were excluded, followed by the exclusion of 28 studies based on full-text review. Ultimately, 25 studies were included in the data extraction stage (figure 1). The quality appraisal of 25 included studies showed that all studies fell into the category of good quality (above 75%) according to the CHEERS checklist. (The results of the quality appraisal are available as online supplemental appendix 2). Most studies (n=8) were conducted in England, five studies in the United States, two studies in Japan, two studies in China, two studies in Canada, as well as one study each in the Netherlands, South Korea, Italy, Poland, Norway, and Sweden. The majority of the studies (n=18) were conducted from a health system perspective. Four studies were conducted from a payer perspective, and two studies were conducted from a societal perspective. One study did not specify the perspective. Ten studies were conducted on a time horizon of 1 year or less, and six studies were conducted on a 5 year time horizon. In addition, five studies were conducted on a lifetime time horizon. Most studies (n=16) were based on the Markov model, and four studies were based on the decision tree. The majority of studies (n=21) were cost-effectiveness analyses, and three studies were cost-utility analyses. The willingness-to-pay threshold in England was set at £20 000 to £30 000 per quality-adjusted life year (QALY), and in the USA, it was set at $25 000 to $50 000 per QALY. This threshold was considered to be less than 5000 yen per month in Japan, 133 000 euros per treated patient in Italy, and 350 000 Canadian dollars in Canada. The discount rate for costs and benefits was reported to be between 3% and 6%. The discount rate was not reported in studies with a one year time horizon. Cost-effectiveness
Online supplemental appendix 3 illustrates the findings on the cost-effectiveness of PPIs compared with other treatments for GORD. Among the included studies, laparoscopic Nissen fundoplication (LNF) and PPIs were the most cost-effective treatments for GORD. In 2022, Wang et al demonstrated that treatment with vonoprazan (VPZ) resulted in 4.35 QALYs at a total cost of $1354 over 5 years, and the treatment of GORD with VPZ, compared with the PPI group, yielded 0.02 QALYs and savings of $943 in costs. Therefore, VPZ was considered the dominant treatment option. Petryszyn et al, examined the cost-effectiveness of esomeprazole compared with pantoprazole from the perspective of the payer (patient) in 2016. The results of this study indicated that substituting pantoprazole 20 mg with esomeprazole 20 mg was more effective in 6 month maintenance treatment with a significant ratio of incremental cost-effectiveness. Funk et al, examined the cost-effectiveness of PPI therapy, Stretta, EsophyX, and LNF in patients with GORD from a healthcare system perspective in 2015. The costs for these interventions were $11,587, $16,533, $14,511, and $24 143 respectively, and the quality of life for them was found to be 0.21, 0.22, 0.22, and 0.22 respectively. Stretta and LNF were the most cost-effective options over 30 years, with the values obtained being $2470.66 and $5579.28 per QALY respectively. Cookson et al, conducted a study in 2005 to evaluate the cost-effectiveness of PPI therapy compared with LNF in patients with GORD from the perspective of the National Health Service (NHS) in England. The results of this study indicated that the incremental cost of LNF compared with PPI therapy for each patient with a physiological acid score (less than 13.9) in the past 3 months was £5515. The incremental cost per unit of combined gastrointestinal and mental well-being score in 12 months was £293. From the perspective of the National Health Service, LNF, compared with maintenance therapy with PPI, might have cost-saving benefits after 8 years (in support of LNF).

Comparison between PPIs and LNF
Overall, in 10 studies, PPIs were compared with LNF, and in only three studies PPIs were found to be more cost-effective options. Most of these studies were conducted in England and the United States (n=7).

Comparison of PPI drug options
In eight studies, the cost-effectiveness of omeprazole, esomeprazole, rabeprazole, ranitidine, and pantoprazole were compared. Esomeprazole was found to be more cost-effective in four studies conducted in Norway, Poland, Italy, and England. Rabeprazole was evaluated in two studies conducted in England, and omeprazole was assessed in two studies conducted in Sweden and the United States. Compared with other drug options, these drugs were found to be more cost-effective.

Comparison of the countries under study
Eight studies were conducted in England between 1998 and 2023, with the results of these studies supporting the LNF option in four studies and supporting rabeprazole and esomeprazole in four other studies conducted with different PPI drugs. Five studies were conducted in the United States, with the results of one study supporting LNF and the other studies identifying the option of PPI therapy as cost-effective. Two studies were conducted in Japan in 2019 and 2021, examining the cost-effectiveness of vonoprazan (P-CAB) as a potassium blocker compared with lansoprazole (PPI), and in both studies, the potassium-competitive acid blockers (P-CAB) strategy was found to be more cost-effective. One study was conducted in Norway, and its results supported esomeprazole. Two studies were conducted in Poland and the Netherlands, with reports supporting esomeprazole and LNF, respectively.

Discussion
This systematic review evaluated the cost-effectiveness of PPIs and surgical interventions for managing GORD. By analysing 25 economic evaluations, the review highlights the economic and therapeutic implications of treatment strategies in diverse healthcare settings.

Key findings and interpretation
The findings reveal that PPIs remain the preferred cost-effectiveness strategy for short- to medium-term management due to their relatively low cost and effectiveness in symptom control. For example, studies such as those by Hansen et al, and Wahlqvist et al demonstrated that on-demand and continuous PPI regimens were cost-effective in maintaining symptom-free states, with ICERs well below acceptable thresholds. This conclusion is consistent with findings from Gawron et al, who emphasised that PPIs, owing to their low initial costs and high effectiveness in maintaining symptom-free days (SFMs), dominate short-term treatment. Similarly, Maresova et al, underscored the utility of PPIs in reducing direct medical costs, particularly in healthcare systems where pharmacological interventions are subsidised. However, the high dependence on lifelong therapy and potential for cumulative costs over time highlight the need for alternative strategies in long-term management. Conversely, LNF emerged as a cost-effective choice in the long-term horizon. Studies such as those by Epstein et al, and Comay et al, highlighted that while LNF incurs higher initial costs, its durability and reduced need for ongoing medication make it economically favourable over time. The durability of symptom control post-surgery reduces the financial burden on both patients and healthcare systems, a factor that becomes critical when considering resource allocation. Our findings align with earlier studies, such as Gawron et al, which emphasised PPIs' cost-effectiveness in symptom control and initial treatment strategies. However, notable differences arise due to variations in methodological approaches. Gawron et al, primarily focused on short-term cost-effectiveness metrics, such as cost per symptom-free day, while this review incorporates long-term measures like QALYs, favouring surgical interventions. Maresova et al, similarly noted that surgical strategies become more favourable in healthcare systems with high pharmaceutical costs, tipping the economic balance in favour of upfront investment. Additionally, the inclusion of newer treatments, such as RefluxStop in Harper et al, (2023), sets this review apart. RefluxStop was shown to outperform both LNF and magnetic sphincter augmentation (MSA) in cost-effectiveness, introducing a novel dimension to surgical strategies. This highlights the evolving landscape of GORD management, where technological advancements are reshaping cost-effectiveness considerations.

Therapeutic implications

Short-term management
PPIs remain indispensable for managing mild to moderate GORD symptoms due to their affordability and widespread availability. The choice of PPI, as seen in studies like those by Hansen et al,should consider both cost and therapeutic effectiveness, with esomeprazole and rabeprazole frequently emerging as cost-effective options. Wahlqvist et al, emphasised the affordability of PPIs, particularly in healthcare systems with limited resources. However, studies in our review demonstrated variability in cost-effectiveness across different PPIs, as noted in Park et al, which highlighted omeprazole as a cost-effective option for GORD maintenance therapy. This aligns with findings by Yokoya et al, which showed that newer agents like vonoprazan could surpass traditional PPIs in cost-effectiveness when considering long-term outcomes.

Long-term management
For patients requiring prolonged symptom control, surgical interventions like LNF offer significant benefits. Studies by Epstein et al, and Faria et al, demonstrated that the upfront costs of surgery are offset by reduced reliance on PPIs and improved quality of life. Also, a study by Harper et al, demonstrated that while LNF incurs higher upfront costs, its durability and ability to reduce dependency on medication result in significant savings and improved quality-adjusted life years (QALYs) over extended time horizons. These findings underscore the importance of patient selection, as surgical outcomes are particularly favourable for patients with severe GORD or those refractory to medical therapy.

Emerging therapies
The incorporation of advanced technologies like RefluxStop introduces new opportunities for cost-effective GORD management. Harper et al, demonstrated that RefluxStop achieved higher QALYs with lower ICERs compared with traditional surgical options, suggesting its potential as a game-changer in GORD treatment paradigms. Over 5 years, introduction of RefluxStop resulted in an almost neutral impact on the existing budget with a 0.316% increase in the annual Italian national health service (SSN) spending on GORD treatment.

Strengths and limitations
This systematic review offers a comprehensive evaluation of GORD management strategies by synthesising data from 25 studies across diverse healthcare settings, incorporating both short- and long-term outcomes through robust metrics such as ICERs and QALYs. A key strength lies in its inclusion of recent interventions, such as RefluxStop, alongside traditional approaches, providing insights into the evolving landscape of GORD treatments. Additionally, the focus on economic evaluations spanning different time horizons ensures the findings are relevant for strategic healthcare decision-making. However, limitations include significant heterogeneity in study designs, populations, and methodologies, which complicate direct comparisons. The potential for publication bias and the underrepresentation of paediatric and elderly populations also restricts the generalizability of the findings. Finally, variability in healthcare systems and cost structures across regions highlight the need for cautious interpretation of results when applied globally.

Future directions
Future research should focus on standardising methodologies for economic evaluations, particularly in terms of time horizons and outcome measures, to enhance comparability. Additionally, real-world studies that account for patient heterogeneity, including comorbidities and socioeconomic factors, are essential for validating cost-effectiveness outcomes. The integration of emerging therapies, such as RefluxStop, into routine clinical practice should also be investigated, particularly in settings with constrained surgical resources.

Conclusion
This review underscores the importance of aligning GORD management strategies with patient characteristics, healthcare system capabilities, and time horizons. While PPIs remain the most cost-effective option for short-term symptom control, surgical interventions like LNF and RefluxStop offer significant long-term benefits. Decision makers must consider these insights to optimise resource allocation and improve patient outcomes.
