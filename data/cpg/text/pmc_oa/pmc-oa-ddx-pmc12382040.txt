Imeglimin as an effective therapeutic approach in management of type 2 diabetes mellitus: an umbrella review and systematic review, meta-regression and meta-analysis
Background
Imeglimin as a novel antidiabetic agent has emerged promising effects compared to previously established treatments. This updated systematic review and meta-analysis and umbrella review evaluated the efficacy and safety of Imeglimin in managing Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (T2DM).
Methods
A systematic search of PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, Embase, and Cochrane Central was conducted up to June 2025. Randomized controlled trials (RCTs) with at least 12 weeks of follow-up, involving adult T2DM patients, were included. Data were independently extracted by two reviewers, and any discrepancies were resolved by a third investigator. Outcomes were pooled using random-effects or fixed-effects models based on heterogeneity. The quality of the included trials was assessed using the Cochrane Risk of Bias 2.0 tool. Also, the Grading of Recommendations, Assessment, Development, and Evaluations (GRADE) approach was used to evaluate the certainty of evidence.
Results
Twelve RCTs evaluating the effect of imeglimin on metabolic factors were included. Imeglimin significantly reduced fasting plasma glucose (FPG) (SMD: -0.51; 95% CI: -0.72, -0.29, P < 0.001; I2 = 75.27%, P-heterogeneity < 0.001) and HbA1c (SMD: -0.45; 95% CI: -0.86, -0.05, P = 0.031; I2 = 94.10%, P-heterogeneity < 0.001). Also, it improved homeostasis model assessment of β-cell function (HOMA-β) (SMD: 0.59; 95% CI: 0.18, 1.01, P = 0.020; I2 = 59.30%, P-heterogeneity = 0.06). Whereas, imeglimin failed to affect insulin, level, HOMA-IR, and c-peptide level, significantly (P > 0.05). However, its unfavorable effect was shown in term of LDL (SMD: 0.32; 95% CI: 0.11, 0.53, P = 0.017; I2 = 0.0%, P-heterogeneity = 0.68).
Conclusion
Imeglimin has demonstrated efficacy and a favorable safety profile in the management of T2DM, particularly regarding glycemic control and lipid profile. Further large-scale trials across diverse populations are warranted to confirm these outcomes.

Introduction
Type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) is a multifactorial metabolic condition that has emerged as a global health crisis. Its incidence is steadily increasing, particularly across South Asia, with India frequently reported as having one of the highest disease burdens. As of 2019, diabetes affected approximately 463 million people worldwide, representing a global prevalence of 9.3%. In 2021, the International Diabetes Federation anticipated that 537 million 20–79 years old people were diagnosed with diabetes; and this number is predicted to reach 643 million by 2030, and 743 million by 2045. With the growing prevalence of T2DM, significant innovative efforts have been carried out for its management in the recent years. Several pharmacological and non-pharmacological interventions such as metformin, sulfonylureas, insulin therapy have been developed for glycemic control in T2DM patients. Although they are generally effective, they have some limitations such as gastrointestinal complications, hypoglycemia, and weight gain. In addition, complementary therapy as an adjunctive strategy gained more attention. For example, monotherapy by Abelmoschus esculentus (okra) has shown promise in the control of fasting blood glucose (FBG); However, it fails to exerts sustained beneficial effects in long-term to affect HbA1c level. This issue underscores the gap to develop a novel therapeutic approach. In this context, “imeglimin” emerged more safe characteristics to control diabetic patients.
Imeglimin hydrochloride is the first agent in the novel “glimin” class of oral hypoglycemic drugs, developed by Poxel in collaboration with Sumitomo Dainippon Pharma. It was initially approved for the treatment of T2DM in Japan in 2021. Following its launch in Japan, imeglimin received regulatory approval from the Drug Controller General of India (DCGI) in October 2022 and has since been introduced into the Indian market and other Asian countries.
Imeglimin is synthesized in a single-step chemical process from metformin and tetrahydrotriazine precursors. It exerts multifaceted effects on various metabolic organs, including the pancreas, liver, and skeletal muscle, contributing to improved glycemic control. Beyond its glucose-lowering action, imeglimin has been shown to ameliorate endothelial dysfunction in patients with T2DM through mechanisms that are both dependent on and independent of glycemic regulation. Preclinical studies have also demonstrated its protective effects against ischemia-induced brain injury and its ability to enhance cardiac and renal function. These effects are thought to be mediated through improvements in mitochondrial bioenergetics, endothelial function, and endoplasmic reticulum homeostasis. Collectively, these findings suggest that imeglimin may offer therapeutic benefits in managing not only hyperglycemia but also diabetes-related vascular and organ complications. While imeglimin has shown the potential to be used as monotherapeutic, or as part of an anti-diabetic combinatorial therapy with currently available medications, its clinical potency remains to be conclusively addressed. Moreover, previous meta-analytic studies have typically evaluated a limited number of factors regarding this issue. Therefore, we conducted a comprehensive systematic review and meta-analysis to assess a broader range of parameters related to the antidiabetic efficacy and safety profile of imeglimin, administered either as monotherapy or in combination with other agents.

Materials and methods
This meta-analysis was conducted in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) criteria (Page et al. 2021), and the principles outlined in the Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions. The study protocol is registered with PROSPERO.

Search strategy, study selection and data extraction
A systematic electronic search was conducted in PubMed, Embase, and the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials using the search terms: (Imeglimin) AND (diabetes) (Supplementary Table 1). Studies published from database inception to July 1, 2025, were considered for inclusion. Seven reviewers participated in the screening and selection of eligible studies. Data extraction was independently performed by three authors using standardized data extraction forms. In addition to study outcomes, the following information was collected: first author, study design, year of publication, country of origin, intervention and comparator doses, sample size, and study duration. Any discrepancies were resolved through discussion with two additional authors.

Study selection criteria
The following PICOS criteria were applied to identify and select studies for inclusion in this meta-analysis: (1) Population (P): adults diagnosed with T2DM; (2) Intervention (I): administration of imeglimin, either as monotherapy or in combination with other antidiabetic agents for the management of T2DM; (3) Comparator (C): patients receiving either a placebo, another approved T2DM medication, or a combination thereof; (4) Outcomes (O): changes from baseline in glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c), fasting plasma glucose (FPG), homeostatic model assessment of β-cell function (HOMA-β), homeostatic model assessment of insulin resistance (HOMA-IR), lipid profile, and safety parameters, including serious adverse events (SAEs) and non-serious adverse events (NSAEs); and (5) Study design (S): randomized controlled trials (RCTs). Only studies with a minimum follow-up duration of 12 weeks and involving adult participants with T2DM were considered. Studies focusing on other types of diabetes were excluded. Eligible studies were required to include at least two arms: one treatment group receiving imeglimin (either alone or in combination), and one control group receiving either a placebo or another oral hypoglycemic agent (as monotherapy or in combination).

Quality assessment and evidence evaluation
Two authors independently assessed the risk of bias for each included study using the Revised Cochrane Risk of Bias Tool for Randomized Trials (RoB 2). Each randomized controlled trial (RCT) was rated as having either a low risk of bias or some concerns. Any discrepancies between reviewers were resolved through discussion with a third independent author.
The methodological quality of the included meta-analyses was evaluated using the A Measurement Tool to Assess Systematic Reviews (AMSTAR)2 questionnaire. The AMSTAR method comprises 11 questions to which reviewers must respond with yes, cannot answer, no, or not applicable. Two authors, independently evaluated the methodological quality of the included studies. Any disagreements were resolved via consulting with third investigator.
The Grading of Recommendations, Assessment, Development, and Evaluations (GRADE) tool was used to evaluate the overall certainty of the evidence for the included studies. The quality of evidence was categorized into four categories based on evaluation criteria, i.e., high, moderate, low, and very low.

Data synthesis
Effect sizes for continuous outcomes were assessed by calculating mean differences (MDs) and standard deviations (SDs) for both intervention and control groups. When these values were not directly reported, they were derived from pre- and post-intervention means and SDs following the methods outlined in the Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions. When pooling data from studies using different measurement scales, standardized mean differences (SMDs) with corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated. Adverse events (AEs), including both serious and non-serious events, were analyzed as risk ratios (RRs). All analyses were conducted using a random-effects model. Sensitivity analyses were performed by sequentially excluding individual studies to evaluate their influence on the overall pooled estimates. Moreover, using meta-regression analysis, we examined the presence of any linear relationship between observed effect size and sample size, treatment duration, age, and dosage of studies. Statistical heterogeneity across studies was assessed using both the Cochran’s Q test and the I² statistic. Begg’s adjusted rank correlation and Egger’s regression asymmetry tests were used to detect small study effects. Additionally, we visually inspected funnel plots to assess publication bias in biomarkers with at least ten studies. Statistical analysis was performed using STATA, version 16 (Stata Corp., College Station, TX).

Results

Study selection and characteristics
Following our initial search in the following databases: PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, Embase, and Cochrane Central (from inception till July 2025), we found 301 studies. Forty-three duplicates were identified and removed. Accordingly, 258 studies were excluded based on title and abstract screening. Of the remaining, 21 studies were assessed using full-text and nine publications were excluded. Finally, twelve RCTs fulfilled all the inclusion criteria and were incorporated into the qualitative analysis. The PRISMA study flow chart has been shown in Fig. 1. In addition, six meta-analyses were included in our umbrella review. In the present systematic review and meta-analysis twelve RCTs were included (Table 1). All these studies were published between 2012 up to 2025. Totally, 1,774 participants were included in the current meta-analysis of RCTs. The age range of included individuals was 54.3 to 66.9 years old. The administration dosage of imiglimin varied from 500 to 2000 mg BD. Also, intervention duration was between 8 and 24 weeks.

Umbrella review
Four meta-analyses reported changes in FPG after imeglimin supplementation. Of these, three meta-analyses found significant reductions with effect sizes of -0.94 to -1.18. Six meta-analyses reported changes in HbA1C, found significant reductions with effect sizes of -0.26 to -0.81. In addition, 3 meta-analyses found no significant reductions in HOMA-IR after imeglimin supplementation. No studies found a significant decrease in insulin.

Meta-analysis

Efficacy of imeglimin on HbA1c level
Data from eleven studies with nineteen effect size were included in the meta-analysis for HbA1c levels (Fig. 2A). Overall, imeglimin showed a significant reduction in HbA1c levels compared to controls, with a pooled SMD of -0.45 (CI: -0.86, -0.05; P = 0.031; I²=94.10%, P-heterogeneity < 0.001). In addition, visually assessment of Galbraith plot indicated that most of the studies are consistent and are within the 95% confidence limits. However, one study is outside of this range, suggesting it may be an outlier which contributes to heterogeneity. Subgroup analysis revealed imeglimin efficacy on HbA1c in younger adults, higher BMI, higher doses (> 1000 mg BD) (Bis in Die; twice a day), in long-term treatment duration, and specifically in studies with RCT double blind designs. Meta-regression analysis demonstrated no significant linear relationship between effect size and sample size, age, treatment dosage, or intervention duration.

Efficacy of imeglimin on FPG level
Eleven studies with seventeen effect sizes were included for the meta-analysis of FPG levels (Fig. 2B). Overall, imeglimin showed a significant reduction in FPG levels, compared to control group with a pooled SMD of -0.51 (CI: -0.72, -0.291; P < 0.001; I² = 75.27%, P < 0.001). In addition, visually inspection of Galbraith plot demonstrated that most of the studies are in confidence bounds. However, one study is outside of this range, which indicates an outlier and contributes to heterogeneity. Subgroup analyses indicated that imeglimin was more effective in younger adults, higher BMI, higher doses, long-term treatment, and RCT studies to reduce FPG level. Meta-regression analysis demonstrated no significant linear relationship between effect size and sample size, age, treatment dosage, or intervention duration.

Efficacy of imeglimin on HOMA-β level
Four effect size with two studies, with 309 cases (imeglimin-treated) and 298 controls, were used for meta-analytical assessment of HOMA-β (Fig. 2C). There was a statistical difference between the two groups (SMD = 0.59; CI: 0.18, 1.01; P = 0.020), with moderate and non-significant heterogeneity (I2 = 59.30%, P = 0.060) amongst studies. This finding is further supported by the Galbraith plot that all studies fell within the 95% CI, indicating a high degree of consistency among studies.

Efficacy of Imeglimin on HOMA-IR level
Five effect size (390 cases and 386 controls), were included for the meta-analysis of HOMA-IR (Fig. 2D). There was a non-significant difference between the two groups (SMD = 0.06; CI: -0.04, 0.16; P = 0.192), with a low and nonsignificant heterogeneity (I2 = 0.0%, P = 0.910) amongst studies. Moreover, Galbraith plot visualization suggested low heterogeneity which is indicative of consistent studies. However, subgroup analyses based on imeglimin dosage did not exert beneficial effects on HOMA-IR level (P > 0.05).

Efficacy of Imeglimin on C-peptide level
The combined effect of five effect size encompassing 620 participants revealed that imeglimin has a non-significant effect on C-peptide level (SMD: 0.16; 95% CI: -0.04, 0.36: P = 0.090; I2 = 0.67%, P = 0.510) (Fig. 2E). In addition, heterogeneity plot demonstrated low between study variability with all five studies falling within the confidence limits. In addition, subgroup analyses showed that neither lower nor higher doses of imeglimin had a significant effect on C-peptide levels (P > 0.05).

Efficacy of imeglimin on insulin level
The pooled effect of five effect size involving 654 participants revealed that imeglimin has a non-significant effect on insulin level (SMD: 0.23; 95% CI: -0.02, 0.49: P = 0.065; I2 = 29.99%, P = 0.230) (Fig. 2F). In addition, a minimal between-study variability was shown in heterogeneity plot, as all five studies were contained within the confidence boundaries. It is worth noting that variability doses of imeglimin was not effective on insulin level based on subgroup analyses (P > 0.05).

Efficacy of imeglimin on lipid profile
Pooled effect of four effect size evaluating lipid profile revealed an increased level of low-density lipid profile (LDL-c) (SMD: 0.32, 95% CI: 0.11, 0.53: P = 0.017: I2 = 0.0%, P = 0.680) (Fig. 3A), while no significant effect was seen on high density lipid profile (HDL-c) cholesterol (SMD: -0.13, 95% CI: -0.28, 0.02: P = 0.069: I2 = 0.0%, P = 0.085) (Fig. 3B). The observed consistency in Galbraith plot was align with lower heterogeneity indicated by the I2 = 0.0%. Accordingly, all included studies for LDL-c and HDL-c were in confidence bound. Furthermore, the pooled data analysis of five studies in imeglimin treatment groups vs. control group was non-significant on triglyceride (TG) (SMD: -0.10, 95% CI: -0.32, 0.13: P = 0.304: I2 = 0.0%, P = 0.390) (Fig. 3C). Similarly, the lower heterogeneity of included studies for TG value was proofed with consistent studies in confidence limit in Galbraith plot. Subgroup analyses indicated that various doses of imeglimin is ineffective on TG level (P > 0.05).

Safety

SAEs
The overall RR for SAEs in imeglimin-treated versus control participants was not statistically significant (RR = 1.06). Subgroup analyses by dosage showed RRs of 0.35, 3.38, 1.43, and 0.36 for the 500 mg, 1000 mg, 1500 mg, and 2000 mg twice-daily (BID) groups, respectively.

NSAEs
The overall RR for NSAEs in imeglimin-treated versus control participants was 1.00, indicating no significant difference. Subgroup analyses by dosage yielded RRs of 0.98, 0.92, 0.83, and 1.36 for the 500 mg, 1000 mg, 1500 mg, and 2000 mg twice-daily (BID) groups, respectively.

Sensitivity analysis and publication bias
Sensitivity analyses based on leaving-out-one method indicated that the overall pooled effect on HbA1c was influenced by four studies. Similarly, exclusion of Dubourg et al. 2020 affected both HOMA-β, LDL-c levels. Also, Fouqueray et al.’s study influenced overall effect of imeglimin on both C-peptide and insulin level. In contrast, no single study significantly impacted the pooled estimates for FPG, HOMA-IR levels, HDL-c. Additionally, one study by Takahashi et al. was found to influence the overall effect of imeglimin on TG levels significantly.
Egger’s regression-based test indicated potential small-study effects for HbA1c (Egger’s and Begg’s tests = 0.0004 and 0.093, respectively), suggesting possible publication bias. This finding was supported with visual asymmetry in the funnel plot. Accordingly, trim and fill analyses were conducted with imputing six studies. Similarly, visual inspection of funnel plot showed an asymmetry distribution for FPG (Egger’s test = 0.015). While no small study effect was seen for HOMA-β (Egger’s = 0.240), HOMA-IR (Egger’s = 0.285), C-peptide (Egger’s = 0.472), insulin (Begg’s = 0.08), LDL-c (Egger’s = 0.426), and HDL-c (Egger’s = 0.364). Detailed results of the sensitivity analyses and publication biases (funnel plots) for each individual meta-analyses are presented in Supplementary Fig. 1–2.

Discussion
Imeglimin, a novel oral antidiabetic agent, was first approved for clinical use in Japan and China in 2021, followed by its approval in India by the Drug Controller General of India (DCGI) in October 2022. It represents the first compound in a new class of glucose-lowering agents known as “glimins,” distinguished by the presence of a tetrahydrotriazine ring in their molecular structure. Imeglimin exhibits a unique dual mechanism of action, targeting key pathophysiological defects in T2DM, including impaired insulin secretion and mitochondrial dysfunction.
The results indicate that there are significant differences between imeglimin and the control group (placebo or placebo with an add-on drug) in the overall effect of reducing HbA1c levels in higher doses of imeglimin. Also, imeglimin exert more pronounced reducing effects in younger adults which suggests that younger adults, which suggests increased β-cell sensitivity in response to treatment and a subsequent enhancement in GSIS level. In addition, long-term intervention was associated with more favorable effects which indicates that β-cells preservation may require extended duration to achieve optimal therapeutic effects. Additionally, patients with higher BMI, showed significant improvement in HbA1c level. This may be attributed to the greater degree of insulin resistance in overweight or obese patients which is targeted by imeglimin’s mechanism of action. Similarly, FPG, overall analysis indicted significant beneficial effects of imeglimin, when compared to controls. It is worth noting that therapeutic effects of imeglimin was shown in any doses to reduce FPG level. This issue highlights that imeglimin’s glucose-lowering effect on FPG is not dose-dependent and can exert its positive properties across any doses. Also, younger adults with higher BMI experienced greater improvement in FPG level which indicates better preserved β-cell function in younger adults and insulin resistance as a hallmark of obesity. Our analysis yielded maximum reduction by imeglimin in higher doses in HbA1c and FPG. While definitive conclusions regarding the superiority of imeglimin over metformin cannot be drawn, the findings reported in the aforementioned meta-analysis were limited by substantial heterogeneity, as indicated by elevated I² values. In contrast, our analysis demonstrated lower heterogeneity, suggesting more consistent effects. Nonetheless, further investigations involving larger sample sizes and conducted under rigorous clinical conditions are warranted to validate these findings. Notably, similar favorable outcomes were observed for the HOMA-β index, likely reflecting imeglimin’s protective effects on pancreatic β-cell function and its capacity to enhance insulin secretion.
The mechanism of action of imeglimin consists of two main effects: (a) improved insulin action with the potential to inhibit hepatic glucose output and improve insulin signaling in both skeletal muscle and liver. Imeglimin also reduces insulin resistance, and increases the effectiveness of insulin by preventing the liver’s production of glucose; and (b) enhanced insulin secretion with the preservation of β-cell mass. Glimins enhance the glucose-stimulated insulin secretion (GSIS) and maintain the insulin-producing β-cells. Moreover, meglimin exerts mitochondrial protective effects through partial inhibition of mitochondrial complex I, correction of deficient Complex III activity, reduction of reactive oxygen species (ROS) production, and prevention of mitochondrial permeability transition pore opening, imeglimin rebalances respiratory chain activity. In addition, it promotes NAD+ synthesis, increases calcium ion mobilization, and enhances glucose-stimulated ATP production through the salvage route in rodent models of T2DM. However, this meta-analysis demonstrated that imeglimin treatment was not successful to affect HOMA-IR, insulin, and C-peptide level. This finding underscores that imeglimin-mediated mechanisms through enhancement of β-cell function and GSIS are bolder than insulin sensitivity or insulin secretion.
Furthermore, pooled effect of lipid profile assessment following imeglimin administration revealed increment in the LDL-c level which can be considered as an adverse effect of imeglimin and raises concerns regarding its potential effects on lipid metabolism. This finding requires further investigation to be further confirmed.
In addition, our umbrella review revealed that most of meta-analyses demonstrated a decrease in FPG and HbA1c level. While, most of these meta-aalyses failed to reach statistical clinical significancy about HOMA-β, C-peptide and insulin.
This comprehensive meta-analysis offers several strengths compared to previous analyses of imeglimin. Firstly, it represents the most up-to-date synthesis of evidence on imeglimin, a drug garnering significant clinical interest, particularly in Asian countries such as India. Secondly, all included studies were of high methodological quality, conducted with standardized protocols that ensured rigorous quality control and relatively low risk of bias. Additionally, these studies were multicentric and spanned multiple countries, thereby enhancing the external validity and generalizability of our findings. Thirdly, the control groups across studies were consistent, typically comprising placebo or placebo combined with an add-on therapy, reducing variability. Finally, unlike prior meta-analyses, our review examines a broad dosage range of imeglimin, from 500 mg to 2000 mg twice daily, providing valuable insights into its dose-dependent efficacy and safety.
Lastly, this review has certain limitations. Primarily, the strict inclusion criteria resulted in the selection of only twelve RCTs, which may limit the overall strength of the meta-analytical evidence. The availability of additional high-quality RCTs would have enhanced the robustness of our conclusions. Furthermore, the inclusion of data from RCTs conducted in ethnically diverse populations would have improved the generalizability of our findings across different demographic groups.

Conclusion
Our meta-analysis with twelve included RCTs demonstrated the efficacy of imeglimin, an emerging oral anti-diabetic agent. At the higher doses, imeglimin appears to result in negligible changes in HbA1c and FPG levels, as compared to the control group. Further, it could exert its favorable effects on HOMA-β level significantly. Conclusively, imeglimin can be considered an efficacious and safe drug for the treatment of T2DM.
