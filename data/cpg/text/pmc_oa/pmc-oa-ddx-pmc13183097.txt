Clinical evaluation of airway stent implantation in the treatment of airway stenosis: A systematic review and meta-analysis
Background:
Airway stents, primarily metal and silicone types, are widely used to manage airway stenosis by providing a barrier against tumor or granulation tissue invasion and by stabilizing the airway lumen against external compression. However, different stent materials and designs are associated with varying complications, including granulation hyperplasia, mucus retention, and stent displacement. Understanding these outcomes is essential for optimizing stent selection and clinical management.
Methods:
A systematic analysis of the included studies was conducted. Risk of bias was assessed using RevMan 5.3 and Stata software. Heterogeneity among studies was evaluated using the Q-test and I2 statistics. Complications and therapeutic outcomes were compared between benign and malignant airway stenosis cases treated with airway stent implantation.
Results:
The analysis showed that patients with benign airway stenosis experienced a lower probability of complications following stent implantation compared with those with malignant airway stenosis. Moreover, the therapeutic effect of stent implantation was superior in benign cases. The meta-analysis further confirmed that airway stents are more effective for benign airway stenosis, with fewer adverse events and better overall outcomes.
Conclusion:
Airway stent implantation demonstrates greater efficacy and fewer complications in the treatment of benign airway stenosis compared with malignant airway stenosis. Awareness of stent-specific complications can guide clinicians in personalized management strategies to improve disease recovery and enhance the clinical utility of airway stents.

1. Introduction
Airway stenosis is the reduction of the airway caliber of the trachea, the left and right main bronchi, and the middle bronchus due to various reasons. The criteria that are generally considered to require intervention are: ≥50% obstruction of the cross-sectional area of the trachea, main bronchi, intermediate bronchi, and lobar bronchi, severely impairing the ventilation function of the lung, resulting in complications such as dyspnea, hemoptysis, atelectasis, and obstructive pneumonia. Airway stenosis is generally divided into malignant and nonmalignant diseases, with malignant disease factors leading to central airway stenosis far exceeding nonmalignant disease factors. The diagnosis and management of airway stenosis is an important clinical issue, and airway resection and surgical reconstruction remain the preferred approach, but not all patients are candidates for surgery. With the continuous progress and development of biological technologies such as biomedical material science, endoscopic interventional and imaging, endoscopic therapy is important for palliative care and patients who are considered inoperable. Airway stents are endobronchial cylindrical prostheses of various materials. At present, airway stents include metal stents, silicone stents, polyflex plastic stents, bioengineered stents, and biodegradable stents. Commonly used are metal stents and silicone stents, which provide a barrier effect by protecting the airway lumen from tumor or granulation tissue growth and create a splinting effect by balancing external pressure. The 2 main types of stents commonly used in clinical practice are still “metal stents” and “silicone stents,” both of which can be used to treat benign and malignant airway diseases. Metal stents can be placed through flexible bronchoscopes, and metal stents are the most widely used in our country. These are the most common clinically in our country.
Metal stents are divided into nickel-titanium alloy stents and stainless-steel stents according to different materials, and can be divided into bare metal stents and covered metal stents according to whether they are covered or not. Bare metal stents are not easily displaced after implantation, and the discharge of secretions is relatively easy. However, after prolonged placement, new granulation tissue hyperplasia occurs due to the stimulation of the mucosal tissue by the metal, resulting in restenosis (RS) of the lumen, which is more difficult to treat than the primary disease, especially when the granulation tissue invades the inside of the stent. Metal-covered stents are covered with a layer of thin film based on bare metal stents, so as to avoid direct contact between the metal and the tracheal mucosa. In this case, the irritation to the tracheal mucosa is relatively reduced, and the granulation hyperplasia is also reduced accordingly. However, due to the presence of the covering, it is more likely to be displaced, and there are still bare metals at both ends of the stent, resulting in more granulation proliferation at both ends of the covered stent. Silicone stents are less irritating to the airway mucosa than metal stents and lead to less granulation hyperplasia. However, the wall thickness of silicone stents is significantly thicker than that of metal-covered stents, resulting in a smaller diameter than metal-covered stents, which are prone to secretion retention. The mechanism of granulation hyperplasia is as follows. After the stent is placed in the trachea, it acts as a foreign body to continuously press and stimulate the tracheal wall, and the airway wall is in the stage of inflammation, which leads to damage to the tracheal mucosa, and the damage to the tracheal mucosa destroys the local blood circulation of the mucosa. Mucosal ischemia, hypoxia, ischemia, and hypoxia environment further lead to increased production of various inflammatory factors and hypoxia-inducible factors, resulting in pathological changes such as erosion and necrosis of tracheal mucosa, and even damage to tracheal cartilage. At the same time, various inflammatory factors also lead to the excessive proliferation of fibroblasts and the proliferation of capillaries, and then the proliferation of granulation tissue in the trachea. Excessive proliferation of granulation tissue blocks the trachea, causing the patient to present with symptoms of airway obstruction. Stent displacement is related to the material characteristics of the stent, the type of stent, the shape of the stent, and whether the selected stent size is appropriate.
Bronchoscopy is an indispensable tool in the management of central airway diseases because it is an endoscopic technique that visualizes the inside of the airways for diagnostic and combined airway stenting, which provides a new treatment for central airway stenosis and various airway diseases. It is a fast, safe, direct, and effective method for the treatment of airway stenosis, which can prolong the survival time of patients and improve the quality of life. To create conditions for the next treatment, appropriate stent selection is vital, and many factors need to be considered, including equipment and technology, stent characteristics, indications for treatment, duration of stent placement, lesion location, quality of life of the patient, and survival time of the patient. Therefore, this work innovatively included the current domestic and foreign literature research on airway stent implantation in the treatment of airway stenosis, and systematically evaluated the therapeutic effect of airway stent implantation on benign airway stenosis and malignant airway stenosis through meta-analysis, aiming to evaluate the efficacy and safety of airway stent implantation treatment and provide evidence for clinical treatment. The novelty of the paper is to examine the homogeneity and specificity of benign airway stenosis and malignant airway stenosis, and suggest some beneficial treatments.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Literature retrieval
The Cochrane Library, PubMed, MEDLINE, EBSCO, Science Direct, and China National Knowledge Infrastructure databases were searched by computer to collect domestic and foreign literature on airway stent implantation in the treatment of airway stenosis. Relevant literature published from January 2000 to April 2025 was searched with “Airway stents, Airway metal stents, Dumon stents, Airway stent implantation, Airway stenosis, and Tuberculous airway stenosis” as the search words. All database searches used a combination of subject headings and free words, and were appropriately adjusted according to specific databases. The retrieval strategies were determined after multiple pre-retrievals. It should manually search professional journals to avoid oversights, and the research objects of the retrieved literature were all human.
A combination of subject headings and free words was adopted to conduct multiple searches to obtain references that can be included, and then a search engine was adopted to track down each literature. The quality of included literature was assessed using RevMan 5.3 software provided by the Cochrane Collaboration.

2.2. How to include and exclude the articles?

2.2.1. Inclusion criteria
Articles were included if they met all of the following criteria:
Studies involving patients with benign or malignant airway stenosis, including untreated and relapsed/refractory cases.
Studies reporting outcomes of airway stent implantation using metal or silicone stents.
Studies providing data on stent removal, stent patency/stability, RS, or stent-related complications.
Studies reporting at least one efficacy or safety endpoint, such as overall response (OR), complete response, partial response, or adverse events (AE).
Both prospective and retrospective clinical studies, as well as randomized controlled trials, were eligible for inclusion if they contained relevant clinical outcome data.

2.2.2. Exclusion criteria
Articles were excluded if they met any of the following conditions:
Sample size fewer than 5 patients, as very small samples could introduce bias and reduce statistical power.
Studies lacking sufficient or clear outcome data, or those with irretrievable or ambiguous information.
Conference abstracts, case reports, review articles, letters, editorials, or clinical experience reports without complete data.
Nonhuman studies (animal or cell experiments).

2.3. Data extraction
Literature screening and data extraction were independently performed by 2 professionals using unified Microsoft Excel (Microsoft, the United States) and cross-checked for inclusion in the final results. If there was any disagreement, it could be resolved through discussion. The main extracted data included: general information about the included articles: title, first author, publication year, etc; basic characteristics of the research subjects: number of cases, patient age, gender, etc; treatment process: selection of stent, severity of disease, etc; key elements of bias risk assessment: randomization method, whether to implement blinding, allocation concealment, etc; and concerned outcome indicators and outcome measurement data such as OR, complete response, PR, safety results AEs, etc

2.4. Assessment criteria of literature
The quality of involved articles was measured using the quality assessment of diagnostic accuracy studies (QUADAS) criteria suggested by Cochrane (the United States). The quality of the included original literature was estimated according to each evaluation index, and each study was evaluated according to “consistent,” “inconsistent,” and “uncertain.”

2.5. Statistical methods
Rev Man 5.3 software (Cochrane, the United States) and Stata software (Stata Corp, the United States) were used. The OR was used as the effect index for dichotomous variables, and the mean difference was used as the effect index for continuous variables. Each effect index gave a point estimate and its 95% confidence interval (CI). The heterogeneity among the included articles was analyzed by the X2 test (test level α = 0.1), and the size of heterogeneity was quantitatively judged with the inconsistency index (I2). If there was no statistical difference in heterogeneity among the results of the articles, a fixed effects model was used for meta-analysis; if there was a statistically significant difference in heterogeneity, a random effects model (REM) was used for meta-analysis, and subgroup analysis was used to explore possible sources of heterogeneity. The test level of meta-analysis was set to α = 0.05. Plot forest plots and summary receiver operating characteristic (SROC) curves, and plot funnel plots for asymmetric linear regression. Funnel plots for different treatment measures were used to test for potential publication bias, and sensitivity analyses were performed.

3.7. Reliability analysis
Sensitivity analysis was performed by changing the analysis model. The results of the meta-analysis showed that the summary results of different analysis models did not change significantly, indicating that the results of the included literature had good stability. Model analysis, such as funnel asymmetric linear regression analysis, can also show that the specificity and sensitivity were in good agreement.

4. Discussion
In recent years, with the rapid development of critical care medicine in my country, the application of mechanical ventilation has become more and more extensive. Mechanical ventilation often requires tracheal intubation or tracheotomy. Some patients develop airway stenosis after tracheal intubation and tracheotomy, and more and more airway stenosis is caused by such causes. Other causes include benign tracheo-bronchial tumors, infectious inflammation, relapsing polychondritis, Wegener’s granulomatosis, amyloidosis, trauma, and congenital causes. Airway stenosis is mainly treated by surgery, and the combination of tracheal resection and stump anastomosis is a commonly used surgical method, especially in young patients, which has great advantages. Surgical airway reconstruction has great advantages in the treatment of tuberculous airway stenosis, but when the airway is softened, the lesions are large, and it is difficult to accurately determine the stenosis site, so surgical airway reconstruction is not suitable. The efficacy of surgical treatment of post-tracheal intubation and post-tracheotomy-induced airway stenosis is currently unclear. Surgical operations are very complex, high-risk, and painful for patients. Surgical complications such as anastomotic stenosis and anastomotic leakage may occur, which seriously affect the quality of life of patients and even endanger their lives.
With the development of respiratory interventional techniques in recent years, bronchoscopy has become the main treatment for benign airway stenosis. Bronchial interventional techniques include airway ablation techniques and airway dilation techniques. Airway ablation is mainly used to remove proliferative granulation tissue and benign and malignant tumors in the airway. Airway dilation techniques are mainly used to dilate narrow airways, including balloon dilation and stenting. Balloon dilatation achieves the effect of dilating the airway by pressurizing the balloon, which can rapidly dilate the airway, but the effect is not durable enough, and it is not suitable for airway softening. Stent placement can continuously expand the airway, which can quickly relieve patients with respiratory obstruction symptoms such as chest tightness, shortness of breath, and cough. It can effectively increase the diameter of the airway in the stenotic segment and significantly reduce the dyspnea index of patients. This work found that the diameter of the trachea in both the silicone stent group and the metal-covered stent group was significantly increased after stent removal compared with that before implantation, and the dyspnea index was significantly lower than that before implantation. Both groups had patients with RS after stent removal, which was related to the timing of treatment, the presence of infection, the etiology of airway stenosis, underlying diseases, and genetic factors. There was no significant difference in RS rates between the 2 groups. There is a lack of research on the mechanism of RS after treatment of benign airway stenosis. It is believed that the stress response caused by mechanical compression of the airway and the chronic inflammatory response of the trachea can lead to the excessive proliferation of fibroblasts and the increase of collagen fiber synthesis. This results in tracheal stenosis. In addition, it is also related to the patient own genetic susceptibility. Further research on the pathogenesis of tracheal stenosis, reducing fibroblast proliferation and collagen fiber synthesis, is an important measure to prevent and treat airway stenosis.
To reduce the occurrence of complications and RS caused by stent placement, the following aspects should be paid attention to. First, an appropriate time should be selected for stent placement, and necessary review and treatment should be performed after stent placement. The study found that if the appropriate timing of treatment was selected, the patient recent occurrence of RS would be significantly reduced. If there is an infection in the airway, active anti-infective treatment should be given first. Stent placement should be considered after the infection is controlled. For severe airway stenosis, electric resection can be given to excise the hyperplastic granulation tissue, and treatment methods such as freezing, laser ablation, and balloon dilation can be used. Some studies have also found that proper timing of stent placement can significantly reduce the incidence of airway RS. In benign airway stenosis caused by bronchial tuberculosis, stent placement in the airway is in the stage of scar repair, and the incidence of RS is lower. Implantation of the stent at the right time will significantly reduce the incidence of RS. Bronchoscopy should be reviewed promptly after stent placement, initially weekly. After the condition is stable, the review time can be appropriately extended, and the review can be timely when clinical symptoms appear. Cryotherapy can be performed when granulation hyperplasia is found, and cryotherapy has a lower chance of granulation hyperplasia than other treatments. For those with mild hyperplasia and stable granulation, it can be temporarily not treated, so as to avoid excessive stimulation and aggravate granulation hyperplasia. To reduce the scarring after stent placement, radiotherapy can be combined, and the timing of radiotherapy should also be grasped. Studies have shown that the timing of local radiotherapy is a factor affecting the recurrence of keloids, and it should be performed when the airway inflammation is mild and there is no obvious infection, otherwise it may lead to poor wound healing and aggravation of infection.
In this work, the published clinical data were collected, sorted, screened, and meta-analyzed; the specificity and sensitivity of the treatment were analyzed, and the ORR was examined to evaluate the therapeutic effect. Overall, the sensitivity and specificity of the treatment of benign airway stenosis were similar to those of the treatment of malignant airway stenosis in terms of efficacy evaluation. On the other hand, the data from a single study using airway stents for malignant airway stenosis were better than those for benign airway stenosis, demonstrating that airway stent implantation performed well in both groups of patients. In terms of curative evaluation, the sensitivity and specificity of treating benign airway stenosis were better than those of malignant airway stenosis, which proved that airway stent implantation has more curative value for benign airway stenosis patients. In terms of stability evaluation, the sensitivity and specificity of the treatment of benign airway stenosis were better than those of the treatment of malignant airway stenosis, which proved that the use of airway stent implantation has better stability in patients with benign airway stenosis. In terms of complications, the complication probability of benign airway stenosis treated by airway stent implantation was lower, and the probability of remigrations after treatment was higher. Malignant airway stenosis had a higher chance of complications than benign airway stenosis with airway stent implantation, with a higher chance of posttreatment mucus retention.
In conclusion, this work summarizes and evaluates the efficacy of airway stent implantation in the treatment of airway stenosis and provides evidence-based recommendations for clinical practice guidelines. In clinical research, patients with different conditions should be reviewed to give full play to the therapeutic value of stent implantation. Subsequent research would collect more indicators and compare the differences of different stents in detail, so as to provide more accurate reference opinions for clinical treatment.

5. Conclusion
In this work, literature related to airway stent implantation in the treatment of airway stenosis was screened and included in a meta-analysis to explore the efficacy of airway stent implantation in benign and malignant airway stenosis. Meta-analysis results confirmed that the effect of airway stent implantation was better in benign airway stenosis. Of course, it inevitably had certain defects. The categories of stents in the included clinical trials were not uniform, and there was no completely uniform standard for AE rating indicators. Because there were few randomized controlled trials and different outcome indicators, they were not involved in this Meta-analysis, and the level of evidence had been dropped. Succeeding research would collect more indicators and compare the differences of different stents in detail, so as to provide more precise reference opinions for clinical treatment.
