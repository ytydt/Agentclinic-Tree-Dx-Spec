Safety and Oncologic Outcomes of Endoscopic Endonasal Transcribriform Approach for Locally Advanced Sinonasal Malignancies
ABSTRACT
Introduction
The past two decades have seen a paradigm shift in the surgical treatment of sinonasal malignancies, moving from traditional open approaches to minimally invasive endoscopic techniques. Technological advancements have enabled the use of the endoscopic endonasal transcribriform approach (EETA) for tumors involving the anterior skull base (ASB). This study evaluates the oncologic outcomes of EETA for locally advanced sinonasal malignancies with ASB involvement.
Methods
We conducted a retrospective cohort study at a tertiary cancer center, including patients treated with EETA for sinonasal malignancies from January 2012 to January 2024. Data on demographic characteristics, clinical presentations, treatment protocols, and survival outcomes were analyzed. Survival probabilities were assessed using Kaplan–Meier curves, and subgroups comparisons were made using the log rank test.
Results
Forty‐nine patients were included, predominantly with primary tumors (91.8%) and advanced stages (cT3–T4: 89.8%). EETA achieved R0 resection in 82.1% of cases. All eligible patients (88.9%) underwent postoperative radiotherapy, with 97.5% beginning treatment 6–8 weeks after surgery. The mean follow‐up duration was 43.2 months. The 5‐year overall survival (OS) was 66.5%, disease‐free survival (DFS) was 58.7%, and local relapse‐free survival (LRFS) was 75%. Patients with olfactory neuroblastoma had significantly higher OS and DFS compared to intestinal‐type adenocarcinoma. Margin status significantly impacted survival, with better outcomes observed in patients with clear margins.
Conclusion
EETA offers a viable and effective alternative for treating locally advanced sinonasal malignancies with ASB involvement, achieving acceptable oncologic outcomes with reduced morbidity.

Introduction
Over the last two decades, there has been a notable evolution in the surgical management of sinonasal malignancies, with a clear shift toward exclusive endoscopic approaches instead of open or combined techniques. This transition is supported by robust evidence from retrospective analyses, demonstrating equivalent oncologic outcomes and reduced morbidity.
Advancements in video‐endoscopy and endonasal endoscopic instrumentation have significantly expanded the indications for these techniques, now encompassing tumors involving or adjacent to the anterior skull base (ASB).
While endoscopic endonasal approaches are well established for treating benign ASB tumors and early‐stage sinonasal malignancies, their application in locally advanced cancers has historically been met with some hesitancy. However, emerging data, including multicenter studies like MUSES, have addressed earlier concerns regarding piecemeal resection and its potential impact on survival, showcasing promising oncologic outcomes.
Few retrospective case series have been published, although with encouraging oncological results.
We herein present the oncologic results of what we believe to be one of the largest series of endoscopic endonasal transcribriform approaches (EETAs) in the treatment of locally advanced malignant tumors arising from the sinonasal tract with ASB involvement.

Methods

Definition
In our center, we defined EETA as a minimally invasive endoscopic procedure that involves a bilateral radical ethmoidectomy with transnasal craniotomy extending laterally from one lamina papyracea to the other, and anteroposteriorly from the posterior wall of the frontal sinus to the planum sphenoidale. This procedure is associated with a bilateral frontal sinusotomy (Draf Type 3) and a large bilateral sphenoidotomy. The resection technique was a “multi‐bloc” technique with centripetal resection consisting of removing the deepest infiltrated layer with a clear peripheral layer underneath. Multiple peripheral oriented margins (±frozen sections) are performed after completing the resection of the main surgical specimen.
No intraoperative conversion to open or combined approach was performed in our study.

In all patients, we adopted a multilayered fascia lata reconstruction technique. When feasible, we harvested a vascularized local flap as the final layer, mainly nasoseptal (NS), and a Silastic sheet was used to support the reconstruction during the first month postoperatively.
All EETA procedures with dural resection were performed by a multidisciplinary surgical team combining a head and neck surgeon with a neurosurgeon. All cases are previously discussed in a multidisciplinary tumor board dedicated to skull base tumors with head and neck surgeons, radiation oncologists, medical oncologists, neuroradiologists, and neurosurgeons.

Study Design
This is a retrospective cohort study based on data collection and analysis from patient chart review in a single tertiary cancer center in France.
The study protocol was reviewed and approved by the institutional review board (IRB). The IRB registration number is 2024‐354.

Eligibility and Data Collection
Patients who were treated surgically by an EETA for a malignant sinonasal tumor involving the ASB from the 1 January 2012 to 1 January 2024 were eligible for inclusion.
Exclusion criteria encompassed patients with no resection of ASB, patients operated on by an external or combined approach, and patients with benign tumors.
Anonymized data collection was done on an institutional sinonasal malignancies protected Redcap database.

Descriptive Analyses
All eligible and included patients were reviewed for demographic characteristics (age and sex), comorbidities based on the Charlson Comorbidity Index, past oncologic, and social history.
Clinical and radiological variables were also collected, in particular tumor's presentation (primary or recurrence), tumor's epicenter, and tumor's local extensions.
Therapeutic protocols with perioperative, neoadjuvant, and adjuvant treatments were also described along with the histopathologic diagnosis and, when available, the margins status, the use of intraoperative frozen section analysis, and the histopathologic dural involvement status.
Postoperative complications were categorized as major or minor. Major complications included cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) leak, intracranial infections (cerebral abscess, subdural empyema, and meningitis), and major vascular events (stroke and pulmonary embolism). All other complications were considered minor. All complications were monitored and recorded postoperatively. Major complications were evaluated for their impact on patient outcomes, while minor complications were documented for their prevalence.
Clinical and pathologic staging were also reported according to the 8th edition of the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) cancer staging manual.

Survival Analyses

Primary Objective
The main objective was to evaluate the oncologic results of our series of patients presenting with a primary tumor in terms of overall survival (OS), local relapse‐free survival (LRFS), distant metastases‐free survival (DMFS), and disease‐free survival (DFS).
Patients treated in the second semester of the year 2023 were excluded from survival analyses due to a lack of follow‐up or ongoing adjuvant treatment.
Causes of death and topography of locoregional and metastatic relapses were described.

Secondary Objectives
Comparative survival analyses were specifically conducted on patients with primary tumors. These comparisons were conducted based on histopathologic diagnosis, margin status, cT stage, pT stage, and meningeal involvement status.
The effectiveness of salvage surgery in terms of survival probability was also studied in patients who were treated for a recurrent tumor.

Statistical Analyses
All analyses were done using R Studio software version 2023.09.0.
In the descriptive analyses, categorical variables were reported using counts and/or percentages. Continuous quantitative variables were reported by their mean, median, maximal, and minimal values.
As for the survival analyses “Survminer R package” was used for calculating survival functions and for plotting Kaplan–Meier curves. The comparison of the subgroup's survival distributions was done using a pairwise log rank test. We have tried to limit the comparisons to subgroups of minimum 10 patients to comply with the validity conditions of the log rank test. Survival probabilities at 60 months were reported. Adjusted p values using the Benjamini–Hochberg method were reported to account for multiple comparisons. The significance level was fixed at 5%.
Due to the small sample size and limited number of events, conducting a multivariate analysis was deemed impractical. Therefore, we focused on descriptive and univariate analyses to provide meaningful insights without overextending the limitations of our data.
We also chose not to impute missing data in some explicative variables to pertain to transparency while addressing a very heterogeneous topic in terms of histopathologic diagnoses.

As expected, the pathological spectrum was diverse, with ITAC (47%) and olfactory neuroblastoma (ON—28.6%) being the most frequent diagnoses. A substantial proportion of the tumors were classified as advanced stages, with 89.8% of cT3–T4 stages, of which 40.8% were cT4b. At diagnosis, nodal involvement was only found in four patients (three ON and one ITAC). Distant metastases at presentation were almost nonexistent (cM0, 98%). The only patient included with a metastatic disease had an oligometastatic adenoid cystic carcinoma with a unique resectable lung metastasis. Considering the particularly slow progression of this specific histotype, primary tumor resection was performed, followed, 1 month later, by the metastasis resection.
Preoperative contact between tumor and dura or radiologic dura involvement (enhancement ± thickening) was noted on MRI in 46.9% of cases, although pathological confirmation was less frequent (28.6%). Eight patients (16.3%) presented with olfactory bulb invasion. No patient with brain parenchyma invasion was included, as we considered it a contraindication for an oncologic resection.
As shown in Table 2, 40 patients (88.9%) with primary tumors received the complete multimodal therapeutic protocol consisting of oncologic endoscopic resection followed by adjuvant radiotherapy ± concomitant chemotherapy. Among them, 97.5% (n = 39) started the adjuvant treatment within 6–8 weeks after surgery. For 12 patients with high‐grade tumors (5 ON, 3 ADK, 2 NEC, 1 SCC, and 1 Ewing sarcoma) an induction chemotherapy was performed. Three of the cN+ patients (two ON and one mucinous ITAC) with level IIA–III nodal metastases underwent neck dissection. The dura was resected in 47 cases (95.9%) and ASB defects were systematically reconstructed according to our previously described multilayered technique. Specifically, a NS flap was performed as a third layer supporting two previous layers of fascia lata: one intracranial intradural and one intracranial extradural (n = 18). When this flap was not feasible (n = 27) due to tumor extensions or previous surgical procedures, a third layer of fascia lata was used. However, for salvage surgery after previous radiotherapy, a vascularized flap was mandatory as a third layer. Thus, when the NS flap was not available and needed, a regional flap (temporoparietal fascia or pericranial) was harvested (n = 2). The remaining two patients, with low‐grade tumors and no dural involvement, had a reconstruction based solely on the NS flap and had finally a negative margin resection.

Intraoperative frozen section analysis for margin assessment was done in 13 out of 49 cases (2 SCC, 4 ON, 5 ITAC, 2 NEC). The most frequently evaluated anatomical sites included the superior dura (falx) and lateral dura, presphenoid mucosa, mucosa of the frontal recess, and the nasopharynx. Intraoperative findings were concordant with final pathology in 12 of 13 cases (negative–negative correlation); one case of sinonasal squamous cell carcinoma showed a negative frozen section but a positive final pathology result.

Primary Analyses
Primary survival analyses on the 43 patients presenting with primary tumors and treated before mid‐2023, with a mean follow‐up of 43.2 months (min: 3.8–max: 133.4), have found a 5‐year OS, LRFS, DFS, and DMFS probabilities of 66.5%, 75%, 58.7%, and 71.2%, respectively. The corresponding Kaplan–Meier curves are shown in Figure 2. Twelve deaths (27.9%) were registered, eight of which were due to metastatic tumor progression, two to local recurrences, one to ischemic stroke, and one of unknown etiology at 124 months postoperatively.

A local relapse was diagnosed in six patients (one mucinous ITAC, one non‐mucinous ITAC, one non‐ITAC ADK, one SCC, one chondrosarcoma, and one malignant phosphaturic tumor). Recurrences were multifocal with an invasion of the remaining ASB bone (n = 4), frontal sinus (n = 2), sphenoid sinus (n = 1), dura (n = 2), frontal lobe (n = 1), and sella turcica (n = 2).
Particularly, recurrences on the ASB bone (n = 4) consisted of a focal regrowth at the primary resection margin. Among those patients, one had contiguous dura invasion (malignant phosphaturic tumor) and two others had sella turcica involvement (one SCC and one non‐mucinous ITAC).
Only one patient (2.3%) with a cT4bN0M0 high‐grade non‐ITAC ADK experienced a level II–III nodal relapse at 6.2 months postoperatively and underwent a salvage neck dissection. Nine patients (four mucinous ITAC, three non‐mucinous ITAC, and two NEC) encountered distant metastases with various locations: bone (n = 6), lung (n = 4), carcinomatous meningitis (n = 3), brain (n = 2), liver (n = 2).

Secondary Analyses
On secondary survival analyses, patients diagnosed with olfactory neuroblastoma in our cohort had a significantly higher survival probability in OS (p = 0.038), DMFS (p = 0.039), and DFS (p = 0.028) but not in LRFS (Figure 3). Moreover, we noticed a tendency toward better OS, DMFS, and DFS in the non‐mucinous ITAC subgroup compared to the mucinous ITAC subgroup, although these differences were not statistically significant.

Margin status was reported in 39 cases (83%) with clear margins (R0) in 82.1% (n = 32). Among the three patients who came to our center for salvage surgery due to recurrent tumors, one experienced a local relapse 88 months after the surgery. This patient had a low‐grade olfactory neuroblastoma. No other oncologic events have been reported to date.
Three patients (6.1%) had major postoperative complications: cerebral abscess (n = 2); subdural empyema (n = 1); pulmonary embolism 2 months postoperatively (n = 1). None of those complications was fatal, with no residual neurological deficit. Interestingly, no postoperative CSF leak was ever observed. Minor complications recorded were mainly atrophic rhinitis (n = 22), mucocele (n = 2), transient central pituitary disorders (n = 2), epistaxis (n = 2), endonasal synechia (n = 3), and transient postoperative delirium (n = 2).

Discussion
In this retrospective cohort study, we evaluated the oncologic outcomes of the EETA for treating locally advanced sinonasal malignancies involving the ASB. Our results indicate that EETA offers very promising oncologic outcomes while achieving an R0 resection in 82.1% of cases and a 5‐year LRFS of 75% with a 5‐year OS of 66.5%.
Efforts have been made globally to fill the gap in research on sinonasal malignancies, particularly regarding their management through endoscopic techniques. However, publications specifically addressing the EETA remain notably scarce. To the best of our knowledge, this study represents one of the largest single‐center cohorts examining this subject, focusing on locally advanced sinonasal cancers when expanded EETA with complete removal of ASB (from posterior wall of the frontal sinus to planum) is required.
Our study demonstrates that EETA yields outcomes comparable to, or even more favorable than, those achieved with traditional external approaches. Specifically, in Ganly et al.'s 2005 multi‐institutional study on the craniofacial approach, they reported a 5‐year OS rate of 48.3% and a 5‐year DFS rate of 45.8%. In contrast, our series achieved a 5‐year OS rate of 66.5% and a 5‐year DFS rate of 58.7%. However, it is essential to interpret our better oncologic outcomes cautiously. Ganly et al.'s study included patients with extensive tumors, which would be currently contraindicated for purely endoscopic approaches. In addition, the high rate of complete therapeutic protocol adherence (88.9%) in our series, combining surgery with adjuvant radiotherapy, may contribute to the explanation of our improved oncologic outcomes. Also, the few severe postoperative complications and the absence of operative related mortality in our cohort, compared to a 4% mortality rate previously reported, compellingly supports the transition toward minimal invasive surgical techniques like EETA for locally advanced sinonasal malignancies. The notably low local recurrence rate in our cohort is largely attributed to the high rate of patients who received the comprehensive treatment regimen, which encompassed both surgery and radiotherapy. This level of adherence was less achievable with previous conventional surgical techniques.
Like other European series, the most common tumors in our cohort in this specific anatomic localization were ITAC and olfactory neuroblastoma. While the majority of our patients had a locally advanced tumor (cT3–T4: 89.9%), yet we were able to achieve clear margin resection in 82.1% of cases. Like the recent results published in the “MUSES” study, we demonstrated that margin status is a potent predictive factor of survival in sinonasal malignancies involving the ASB, showing, once again, the feasibility and accuracy of margin status evaluation in endoscopic resection of sinonasal cancers. Therefore, given the potential discrepancies between imaging predictions and pathology results, practitioners should focus on conducting a detailed margin analysis. This can be achieved by an extensive intraoperative sampling for a precise anatomical mapping and by a comprehensive standardized reporting of all samples and margins by the pathologist.
When used, intraoperative frozen section results were concordant with final pathology findings in 12 of 13 cases (92%), demonstrating the reliability of frozen section analysis at sampled sites.
Our ASB reconstruction strategy, using multilayered fascia lata ± local flaps, has also shown very good results specifically with no reported CSF leaks, few local complications, and very few local relapses. Interestingly, no local relapse was ever observed on the local flaps, showing the safety of this procedure as long as the flap is harvested away from the tumor.
While this study contributes valuable insights into the efficacy of EETA for sinonasal malignancies, it is important to acknowledge its limitations. The retrospective design carries risks of selection and information biases, potentially impacting the generalizability of the findings. The relatively small sample size, although substantial for this rare condition, limits the power to detect smaller effect sizes. For example, this aspect has surely affected the comparison by meningeal involvement status, yet it should be noted that this might also be caused by diversity of pathological subtypes. Histology is considered one of the most critical prognostic factors in sinonasal malignancies, as each histological type exhibits distinct tendencies for dural involvement. Furthermore, the lack of a statistically significant difference in survival analyses between cT4b tumors and other stages might be attributed to low statistical power. However, given that the patient numbers in both groups were relatively balanced, this result could be due to the relatively low incidence of events. To delve deeper into this aspect, multicentric studies are required. If these studies confirm the favorable current findings, the cancerology communities may need to reconsider the classification of T4b tumors, which are currently considered unresectable.

Conclusion
The EETA has emerged as a promising minimally invasive technique for managing locally advanced sinonasal malignancies with ASB involvement. Our study demonstrates that EETA can achieve oncologic outcomes at least equivalent to traditional open approaches while significantly reducing postoperative morbidity. With an 82.1% rate of R0 resection, a 5‐year OS of 66.5%, and a 5‐year LRFS of 75%, EETA offers a viable alternative for these complex cases. Despite these encouraging results, it is crucial to recognize the inherent limitations of our study, including its retrospective nature and relatively small cohort size. These factors necessitate cautious interpretation and highlight the need for further research, ideally through large‐scale, multicentric studies, to validate our findings and refine surgical protocols. Our study underscores the potential of EETA to redefine the surgical management paradigm for sinonasal malignancies involving the ASB and confirms the endoscopic endonasal approach as the first‐line surgical option in appropriate clinical settings.
