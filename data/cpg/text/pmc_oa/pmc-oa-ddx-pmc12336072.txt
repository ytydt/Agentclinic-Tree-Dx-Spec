Phytotherapy with Fruit Seed Extracts as a Promising Approach for the Treatment of Inflammation
Purpose of Review
This narrative review aims to explore the anti-inflammatory potential of fruit seed extracts, emphasizing their bioactive components such as antioxidants, polyphenols, fatty acids, vitamins, and minerals. By examining studies published from 1993 to 2024, it evaluates how these natural compounds may contribute to reducing inflammation and oxidative stress.
Recent Findings
Recent literature indicates a strong correlation between the intake of fruit and vegetable-based compounds and reduced inflammatory markers. In contrast, diets high in sugars, saturated fats, and additives are linked with heightened inflammatory responses. Several fruit seed extracts demonstrate notable anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and anticancer properties, largely attributed to their high concentrations of polyphenols and other phytochemicals.
Summary
Fruit seed extracts contain bioactive compounds that modulate key inflammatory signaling pathways, such as NF-κB and MAPK, and exhibit strong antioxidant properties. These mechanisms contribute to their potential use as natural therapeutic agents for managing chronic inflammatory conditions, including cardiovascular disease, arthritis, and metabolic disorders. Future research should focus on clinical validation, standardization of extract formulations, and understanding long-term safety and efficacy in human populations.

Introduction
Inflammation is a complex immune response to harmful stimuli such as infections, toxins, or injury. It involves the activation of innate immune pathways and may, in some cases, lead to programmed cell death mechanisms such as apoptosis or pyroptosis. Its core function is to eliminate the source of cellular damage, clear dead or injured cells, and initiate tissue repair. Immune cells release signaling molecules like cytokines and chemokines, which recruit leukocytes to the site of injury or infection to contain and resolve the damage.
Fruit seed extracts, such as those from avocado, camu-camu, chia, and grape, are rich in bioactive compounds, including polyphenols, flavonoids, antioxidants, vitamins, minerals, and fatty acids (FAs), which collectively contribute to their anti-inflammatory properties. Flavonoids, a prominent group of polyphenols, have demonstrated the ability to scavenge free radicals, mitigate oxidative stress (OS), and protect cellular integrity.
These compounds exert anti-inflammatory effects through multiple mechanisms, notably by suppressing pro-inflammatory cytokines such as tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α) and interleukin-6 (IL-6), and by inhibiting the activation of nuclear factor-kappa B (NF-κB), a key transcription factor in inflammatory signaling. For instance, flavonoids like quercetin and catechins interfere with NF-κB’s nuclear translocation, thus reducing cytokine production and inflammatory gene expression.
Additionally, vitamins (A, C, E, and K), minerals (potassium, calcium, magnesium), dietary fiber, and FAs like eicosanoids contribute to immune modulation and inflammation resolution. These actions may help prevent or manage chronic inflammation-related conditions, such as cardiovascular disease, type 2 diabetes, neurodegenerative disorders (e.g., Alzheimer’s), rheumatoid arthritis, and certain cancers.
Epidemiological studies have consistently shown an inverse relationship between fruit and vegetable intake and systemic inflammation, with lower levels of inflammatory markers like CRP and TNF-α observed in both adults and adolescents. In contrast, the modern Western diet (high in refined sugars, saturated fats, and additives) has been linked to elevated inflammation levels.
This narrative review aims to examine the inflammation-modulating potential of selected fruit seed extracts, synthesizing findings from in vitro experiments, animal models, and clinical studies, and to highlight their potential roles in prevention and therapy.

Mechanism of Inflammatory Response
The mechanism underlying inflammation is highly complex and not yet fully understood. Restoring cellular homeostasis in response to any harmful condition is one of inflammation’s primary goal as a defense mechanism for the host. Therefore, the physiological state of homeostasis is intricately linked to the mechanism that initiates inflammation. It follows a complex process involving vasodilation, increased vascular permeability, immune cell recruitment and signaling, the release of chemical mediators, phagocytosis, and ultimately, tissue repair and resolution. Vasodilation increases blood flow to the affected area, allowing more immune cells and nutrients to reach the site. White blood cells, like neutrophils and macrophages, repel pathogens and remove debris. Chemical mediators such as histamine, prostaglandins (PGs), and cytokines play central roles in initiating and regulating the inflammatory response. Histamine is released by mast cells during the acute phase of inflammation, leading to increased vascular permeability and contributing to the characteristic redness and swelling. Prostaglandins also act early by sensitizing nerve endings and promoting vasodilation. In contrast, cytokines such as TNF-α and IL-6 are more involved in the chronic phase, sustaining inflammation and mediating systemic effects such as fever, leukocyte recruitment, and tissue remodeling. Phagocytosis, a key innate immune process, involves immune cells engulfing and degrading pathogens and damaged tissue at the site of inflammation. While these responses are essential for host defense and tissue repair, persistent or dysregulated inflammation can result in tissue damage and contribute to the development of chronic diseases. Selected Fruit Seed Extracts as Source of Anti-Inflammatory Compounds
Bioactive compounds found in fruits – such as dietary fibers, polyphenols, flavonoids, antioxidants, complex carbohydrates, and other plant-derived nutrients – exhibit antioxidant, antimicrobial, anticancer, and anti-inflammatory properties. However, the human body often misses out on these beneficial elements because many fruit parts, including seeds and peels, are typically discarded. These fruit and vegetable by-products – such as peels, seeds, pomace, and rinds – are generated in large quantities and often dumped into the environment, contributing to pollution. Thus, plant debris, such as seeds, peels, and petals, can be profitably employed as a source of natural medicine or chemicals in a variety of enterprises. Hence, extraction of bioactive components from e.g. seeds and creation of enriched food products. Through such enrichment, it might be possible to provide patients with more bioactive compounds that might positively affect their health.

Avocado Seed Extract
The avocado (Persea americana P. Mill) is a dicotyledonous plant from the Lauraceae family native to Central America and Mexico but now is also grown all over the world in high demand in many nations. Despite being frequently discarded, avocado seeds make up a large amount (13%-17%) of the fruit and are rich in a variety of bioactive compounds, including polysaccharides, proteins, lipids, minerals, vitamins, phenolics, flavonoids, and condensed tannins. Efficient management of by-products, seeds in particular, may provide economic and environmental benefits. Avocado seeds are a natural source of bioactive compounds that are useful in culinary, pharmaceutical, and cosmetic sectors. They have a strong antioxidant potential and prevent food oxidation, preserving food’s nutritional and sensory characteristics. Avocado seed extract contains anti-inflammatory constituents such as catechins, flavonoids, and acetogenins like persenone A and B. In RAW264.7 murine macrophages, treatment with 20 µM of persenone A significantly reduced the expression of nitric oxide synthase (iNOS) and COX-2 proteins following stimulation with LPS and IFN-γ.
Similarly, acetogenins derived from Laurencia obtusa (red algae) were tested in human peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) treated with carrageenan, an inflammatory agent. The treatment significantly decreased the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6, indicating potential anti-inflammatory activity. Alkhalaf et al. examined the lipid extracts from avocado fruit peel and seed using chloroform/methanol extraction and gas chromatography-mass spectrometry. The antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and anticancer activities of these extracts were assessed in HepG2 (hepatocellular carcinoma) and HCT116 (colorectal carcinoma) cell lines, where avocado seed extract showed stronger inhibitory effects on cancer cell growth than fruit peel extract. In a mouse skin inflammation model, the tumor promoter TPA (12-O-tetradecanoylphorbol-13-acetate) was used to induce ROS accumulation. Mice treated with 810 nmol of persenone A showed a significant reduction in TPA-induced hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) production. Notably, administering persenone A prior to a second TPA application effectively inhibited H2O2 production, whereas a single treatment did not show the same effect. These findings suggest that persenone A has potential as a preventive agent for inflammation-related diseases, including cancer.

Camu Camu Seed Extract
Myrciaria dubia, often referred to as camu-camu (CC), is a small, sour, and tangy fruit that grows particularly in Peru and Brazil. It is well-known for its exceptionally high vitamin C levels, containing more of it than any other known fruit. Hence, CC seed extract is characterized as a powerful antioxidant. Another main biologically active ingredient in CC seed extract is betulinic acid (BA) – a pentacyclic triterpenoid with antiretroviral, antimalarial, and anti-inflammatory properties. Betulin and BA are compounds with minimal cytotoxicity that show considerable potential in mitigating inflammation through the suppression of IL-6 secretion. Research by Yazawa et al. demonstrated that BA could reduce the inflammatory response in RAW 264.7 mouse macrophage cells by inhibiting the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines and enzymes, and suppressing NF-κB upregulation. This effect is attributed to BA’s inhibitory action on IkappaB kinase (IκB) phosphorylation. In the same study, BA blocked the release of NO, a key inflammatory mediator, from macrophage-derived cells, confirming its anti-inflammatory effects at the cellular level.
A study by Zhu et al. demonstrated that BA, administered orally at doses up to 50 mg/kg daily for 14 days, alleviates oxidative stress induced by a single high dose (25 mg/kg) of dexamethasone in mice. This suggests that BA may serve as a safer and potentially complementary alternative to corticosteroids. While dexamethasone is a highly potent anti-inflammatory agent, it carries well-documented adverse effects, including immunosuppression and metabolic disturbances with long-term use. In contrast, BA showed protective effects at higher doses but with a lower toxicity profile. Although higher concentrations of BA may be required to match the anti-inflammatory potency of dexamethasone, its natural origin and favorable safety margin underscore its potential value for longer-term or adjunctive therapy. Further supporting this, a methanol-based extract of CC seeds significantly reduced edema in male ddY mice, a model of spontaneous murine IgA nephropathy, underscoring its systemic anti-inflammatory capacity. In the same study, oral pretreatment with CC seed extract was shown to decrease paw swelling in mice in a dose-responsive manner, by inhibiting localized inflammation.
Taken together, these findings suggest that CC seed extract, through its active compound betulinic acid, holds strong potential as a natural, low-toxicity anti-inflammatory agent. While not as immediately potent as synthetic steroids like dexamethasone, BA may offer comparable functional outcomes with significantly reduced risk of side effects, making it a promising candidate for immune-related disease management and chronic inflammation prevention.

Chia Seed Extract
Salvia hispanica L. known as chia, contains various bioactive compounds such as omega-3 fatty acids (e.g. alpha-linolenic acid, ALA), polyphenols (e.g. rosmarinic acid, daidzein, myricetin, caffeic acid, quercetin), vitamins (A, B complex, D, E, K), minerals (magnesium, phosphorus, calcium, potassium), fiber, proteins and antioxidants. Where polyphenolic compounds play a significant role in reducing inflammation and oxidative stress. Rosmarinic acid (RA), as the main polyphenol in chia seeds, reduces inflammation by inhibiting inflammatory mediators, reducing oxidative stress and modulating inflammatory pathways by suppressing NF-κB activation and decreasing the expression of HMGB1, a nuclear protein involved in chromatin architecture and immune responses. RA helps regulate the immune response as well as inhibits inflammatory enzymes promoting cellular protection and overall health. Second major component of chia seed extract, daidzein, has potential anti-inflammatory effect as it inhibits mediators in MAPK and NF-κB signaling pathways, which are crucial in immune response.

Gabiroba Seed Extract
Gabiroba (Campomanesia xanthocarpa Berg.) seed extract is rich in caryophyllenes, mainly β-caryophyllene which by suppressing the release of pro-inflammatory substances and hence controlling cell growth. Petry et al.. carried out a comprehensive range of studies to investigate the anti-inflammatory capabilities of gabiroba seed extract. Firstly, formalin test which consists of two phases: phase 1 and phase 2, during which the animal exhibits painful responses was performed as these periods are separated by a quiet interval known as interphase, during which nociceptive responses are reduced or eliminated entirely. The extract was administered orally at doses of 30, 60, and 120 mg/kg BW. In phase 1 the C. xanthocarpa extract test doses did not show any discernible antinociceptive effects. In phase 2, both the extract-treated and diclofenac potassium-treated groups showed a substantial decrease in nociceptive response duration compared to vehicle-treated animals. Gabiroba seed extract at 60 mg/kg body weight significantly reduced paw edema in a carrageenan-induced inflammation model and inhibited neutrophil migration in vitro at low concentrations, further demonstrating its anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory effects. Acute toxicity tests at 2000 mg/kg showed no adverse effects on body weight, food intake or organ weights, confirming its safety. In conclusion, the extract effectively reduced pain, swelling and regulated immune cell function, proving its potential as a future anti-inflammatory agent.

Grape Seed Extract
Grape seed extract (GSE) is most commonly derived from the seeds of red wine grapes (Vitis vinifera L.), though it can also be obtained from other varieties such as table grapes. It is rich in antioxidants, including phenolic compounds like proanthocyanidins, flavonoids, and others. Due to antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and potentially anti-microbial properties, GSE is marketed as a dietary supplement for multiple conditions, including venous insufficiency, wound healing enhancement, and inflammation reduction. Moreover, it has been already studied for its role in cardiovascular disease, as well as reducing OS. Of note, the main component of grape seed extracts, proanthocyanidins, have been shown to have anti-inflammatory properties via modulation of pathways associated with inflammatory response and hence lowering the buildup of cytokines involved in inflammation or nitric oxide (NO). A systematic review and analysis by Foshati et al. demonstrated that nutritional support with GSE lowers lipid peroxidation biomarker levels, including plasma malondialdehyde and oxidized low-density lipoprotein (oxLDL), which is essential in the production of foam cells (lipid-laden macrophages). This study indicated that supplementation with GSE can significantly affect the body’s redox system, primarily by inhibiting lipid peroxidation, while having neutral effects on inflammatory responses. A study by Nallathambi R. et al. to investigate the impact of GSE on LPS-induced inflammation in Caco-2 cells, treatment with GSE resulted in increased expression of anti-inflammatory cytokines, including IL-10 and transforming growth factor β (TGF-β). An experiment conducted by Zhou DY. et al.. to evaluate the impact of proanthocyanidins from GSE on inflammation in a mouse model of chronic asthma showed that the compound has significant effect on IL-4 cytokine expression. Proanthocyanidins from GSE inhibited inflammation in BALB/c mice and reduced levels of IL-4 cytokine associated with Th2 immune response that serves a crucial function allergic inflammation as well as asthma pathogenesis. Hence, improving the course of asthma.
Based on research conducted by Wang et al.., supplementation with 1% GSE for 16 weeks presents a protective effect on inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) indicators in IL10-deficient mice. This was achieved by reducing the expression of the pore-forming tight junction protein, claudin 2, and as a result inhibiting the inflammatory response in the gut, as well as modifying the gut microbiota profile. On the other hand, the immune-inflammatory reactions in the large intestine of IL10-deficient mice were significantly reduced after GSE supplementation, evidenced by decreased mRNA levels of key inflammatory mediators such as TNF-α, IL1, IL6, and IFN-γ. GSE supplementation also led to a decrease in neutrophil infiltration in the large intestine of IL-10-deficient mice. In summary, the study demonstrated that dietary intake of GSE provides a protective effect against IBD in mice, possibly by modulating gut microbiota, suppressing inflammatory responses, and enhancing gut barrier integrity. These findings suggest that GSE supplementation could serve as a complementary strategy for the prevention or treatment of IBD and other gut-related disorders.

Kiwi Seed Extract
Kiwifruit (Actinidia deliciosa (A. Chev.) C. F. Liang & A. R. Ferguson) seeds serve as a valuable source for extracting health-promoting compounds such as omega-3 FAs (anti-inflammatory alpha-LA in particular) and procyanidin. Additionally, kiwi seed extract is rich in essential minerals e.g., vitamin E and polyphenols, which contribute significantly to lower OS. Research carried out by Tanaka et al.. focused on assessment of the anti-inflammatory effects of kiwi seed extract and its potential analgesic properties. The investigation involved assessing the impact of the kiwifruit seed extract on PGs production in LPS-stimulated RAW264.7 murine macrophages. Additionally, the analgesic and anti-inflammatory effects of the extract were evaluated in a mouse model of acute inflammation induced by acetic acid. The Writhing test, a chemical method used to induce peripheral pain through the injection of irritants such as phenylquinone and acetic acid in mice, was also performed. Kiwi seed extract (100–400 mg/kg BW) barely inhibited the writhing behavior. Though, the effects were not statistically relevant. However, after supplementation with kiwi seed extract (100–400 mg/kg BW), dye leakage was reduced as the abdominal capillaries’ permeability increased. The kiwi seed extract significantly suppressed prostaglandin E2 (PGE2) production in RAW 264.7 macrophage cells in a concentration-dependent manner (1–100 µg/mL), which is a clear indicative of inflammation suppression. Overall, the study suggested that kiwi seed extract holds potential for anti-inflammatory and analgesic applications, supported by its ability to suppress PGE2 production and demonstrate inflammation-modulating properties in an acute inflammation model in mice.

Moroccan Date Seed Extract
Moroccan date seed extract derived from the date palm tree (Phoenix dactylifera L.) is rich in antioxidants, essential FAs, and phenolic compounds. Its antioxidant properties effectively combat OS, while the essential FAs contribute to skin health, potentially offering anti-inflammatory effects. Moroccan date seed extract is widely employed in dietary supplements, as it is stated to support overall wellness. Notably, research by Bouhlali et al. suggested that Moroccan date seed extract may exert anti-inflammatory effects by inhibiting protein denaturation, stabilizing lysosomal membranes, scavenging free radicals (including NO), and reducing the production of fibrinogen and C-reactive protein (CRP).

Passion Fruit Seed Extract
Passion fruit (Passiflora edulis S.) is a fruit rich in bioactive compounds that exhibit strong antioxidant, antiviral, antifungal as well as antimicrobial properties. The liquid chromatography analysis of polyphenolic compounds presents in the purified polyphenolic fraction of the passion fruit seed extract by Siniawska & Wojdyło revealed the presence of flavones (luteolin, apigenin, acacetin and chrysoeriol derivatives), flavonols, flavan-3-ols, phenolic acids and anthocyanins. Flavones were the most abundant, followed by anthocyanins, flavonols, flavan-3-ols and phenolic acids, recognized for their antioxidant and inflammation-modulating properties. Moreover, passion fruit seed extract has shown a significant anti-diabetic activity through the inhibition of crucial enzymes associated with carbohydrate metabolism, such as α-amylase, α-glucosidase, and pancreatic lipase. Studies have also emphasized that flavonoids can modulate sugar metabolism, improve the action of insulin, reduce inflammation and OS. In particular, peels have shown to inhibit α-amylase and exhibit antidiabetic effects in animal models of diabetes, and also improve pancreatic β-cell function. In addition, polyphenolic fractions from the extract have been linked to the inhibition of α-glucosidase and α-amylase, essential enzymes associated with carbohydrate metabolism. Phenolic acids have been found to be specifically effective in inhibiting these enzymes, thus helping to regulate blood glucose levels. According to Suleria et al.., passion fruit seed extract showed strong antioxidant properties, as measured by antioxidant tests such as ABTS and ORAC. Moreover, it has been emphasized that the flavonoids, flavan-3-ols and phenolic acids present in the extract contribute to its free radical scavenging capacity, which is crucial in combating OS-related disorders, e.g. cancer, cardiovascular disease as well as neurological disorders.

Pomegranate Seed Extract
Pomegranate (Punica granatum L.) seed extract is renowned for its potent antioxidant properties. Enriched with beneficial compounds such as ellagic acid (EA) and punicalagins, it possesses antioxidant and inflammation-reducing effects and is being researched for its potential advantages in areas like cardiovascular health and mitigating oxidative stress. Additionally, pomegranate seed extract finds applications in skincare and cosmetic products as it contains flavonoids, hydrolysable tannins and FAs (mainly punicic acid, linoleic acid (LA), stearic acid and palmitic acid). Flavonoids in pomegranate seed extract exhibit anti-inflammatory effects by reducing the generation of reactive oxygen species (ROS) and downregulating various inflammatory mediators, including NF-κB, mitogen-activated protein kinases (MAPK), and signal transducers and activators of transcription (STAT).
Pomegranate seed extract was also examined due to its anti-tumor properties. This in vitro study included tests on: ovarian cancer (SKOV3 cells), breast adenocarcinoma (MCF-7 cell line), prostate adenocarcinoma (PC-3 cells), as well as non-small cell lung cancer (A549 cells). The extract showed significant growth inhibition in all tumor cells tested, even at low doses, where SKOV3 cells showed the highest response and A549 cells were the least sensitive. Furthermore, pomegranate extract presented antioxidant activity and the ability to interfere with tumor cell proliferation, cell cycle, angiogenesis as well as invasion, highlighting its potential as a natural chemopreventive as well as anticancer agent.

Strawberry Seed Extract
Strawberry (Fragaria × ananassa Duchesne) seed extract (SSE) is abundant in essential FAs, antioxidants and polyphenols. This extract is also rich in flavonoids, and EA, providing robust antioxidant properties that effectively counteract OS and contribute to overall good health. The primary constituent of SSE is tiliroside, which is a natural glycosidic flavonoid. It demonstrates preventive effects against various inflammatory conditions, such as ulcerative colitis and neuroinflammation. This is attributed to its wide-ranging biological activity, which includes anti-inflammatory and antioxidant effects. Zhong et al.. conducted experiments to investigate tiliroside’s anti-inflammatory properties, focusing on its potential to block the activation of NLR family pyrin domain containing 3 (NLRP3) inflammasome activation, which is crucial in various inflammatory diseases (acute lung injury, atherosclerosis, type 2 diabetes, gout, lupus, rheumatoid arthritis, non-alcoholic steatohepatitis, and neurodegenerative disorders). Using human monocytic cell line (THP-1-derived macrophages) treated with lipopolysaccharide (LPS) and adenosine triphosphate (ATP), authors explored tiliroside’s effects on inflammasome activation and its underlying mechanisms, particularly 5’ adenosine monophosphate-activated protein kinase (AMPK) activation and mitochondrial protection. Tiliroside treatment reduced caspase-1 and IL-1β maturation, protein containing perinuclear structure that functions as a signaling platform to facilitate the enhanced maturation of cytokines through caspase-1 activation, and pro-inflammatory cytokines expression. It also promoted AMPK activation, mitigated mitochondrial ROS production and improved mitochondrial membrane potential, suggesting its potential in managing NLRP3-related inflammatory diseases. Gasparrini et al.. investigated the potential of SSE as a counter-inflammatory measure, taking into account the significant role the chronic inflammatory states play in several chronic conditions. Namely, the researchers investigated the anti-inflammatory effects of SSE on human dermal fibroblast (HDF) cells exposed to inflammatory stress caused by LPS. The study focused on analyzing the effect of methanol-based extracts from Alba strawberry seeds on LPS-induced OS, using viability assays, measuring ROS and nitrite generation, as well as assessing oxidative damage to biological macromolecules. The findings indicated a distinct and dose-dependent protective impact of SSE against LPS-induced damage in HDF cells. It was summarized that SSE not only enhanced cell viability but also reduced ROS and nitrite levels and mitigated damage to cellular lipids, proteins, and nucleic acids. Importantly, these findings underscored the potential health benefits of incorporating SSE into the diet as preventive measures against chronic pathologies associated with inflammatory states. Furthermore, the study suggested that SSE possesses the ability to counteract OS induced by inflammatory stimuli, modulate the inflammatory cascade, and protect against oxidative damage at the cellular level.

Sunflower Seed Extract
The sunflower (Helianthus annuus L.), a yearly plant belonging to the Asteraceae family, has historically been utilized as a medicinal herb because of its numerous health advantages. These seeds are particularly abundant in vitamin E, phenolic acids, and flavonoids, which contribute to their antioxidant and anti-inflammatory effects. Sunflower seed leftovers following oil extraction contain phenolic chemicals, which constitute 1–4% of the total mass, with chlorogenic acid (CGA) playing a substantial role in obesity. As obesity is characterized by low-grade chronic inflammation, which contributes to a variety of metabolic disorders, sunflower seed extract due to CGA content might be beneficial during this condition. CGA exerts anti-inflammatory effects primarily via modulation of key signaling pathways, including AMPK and PI3K/AKT. These pathways are known to inhibit the nuclear translocation of NF-κB, thereby reducing the expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6. Therefore, they play a key role in reducing inflammation. Sunflower seed extract has been shown to have benefits on body fat in earlier research, but only one clinical investigation has been conducted. It has been emphasized that sunflower seed extract treatment not only markedly decreased body weight gain and adipose tissue mass in a prior experimental work using obese mice on a high-fat diet, but it also repressed adipogenesis and increased lipolysis and thermogenesis.

Watermelon Seed Extract
Watermelon (Citrullus lanatus) seed (CLS) extract contains a diverse range of nutrients, including proteins, essential FAs, and other bioactive components. The main active constituents are LA and total unsaturated FAs. Yu et al. investigated the anti-inflammatory effects of conjugated isomers of LA in RAW 264.7 mouse macrophages. The study demonstrated that conjugated LA activated peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma (PPARγ), leading to the downregulation of IFNγ-induced inflammatory responses. Specifically, LA treatment reduced the production of inflammatory mediators such as NO, prostaglandin E2 (PGE2), and TNF-α, and lowered the levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines IL-1β and IL-6. Additionally, LA inhibited antigen-induced histamine release, suggesting a broader role in inflammation modulation.
Bazabang et al.. examined the protective effects of flavonoids extracted from Citrullus lanatus seeds (FCL) in a rat model of ethanol-induced chronic kidney injury. Rats were assigned to control, ethanol-only, FCL-only, and co-treatment groups including FCL or silymarin with ethanol. FCL treatment significantly attenuated ethanol-induced elevations in serum urea, creatinine, uric acid, and NO levels. It also improved oxidative stress markers – increasing SOD, CAT, and GPx, and reducing MDA – while suppressing inflammatory cytokines TNF-α and IL-1β. Histological analysis supported these biochemical findings, showing reduced renal tissue damage. These results suggest that FCL offers potential for renal protection and inflammation reduction, and may be considered for future oral therapeutic development.

Future Perspectives
Various fruit seed extracts demonstrate anti-inflammatory properties due to their rich content of diverse bioactive compounds, e.g. proanthocyanidins, flavonoids, hydrolysable tannins, FAs, and antioxidants, presenting potential therapeutic effects. The Multi-Ethnic Study of Atherosclerosis (MESA) examined the relationship between dietary patterns and inflammation markers by linking questionnaire responses to concentrations of high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (HS-CRP) and IL-6. Four dietary patterns were obtained: Pattern 1 (Whole Grains and Fruit) and Pattern 2 (Vegetables and Fish) were associated with reduced levels of HS-CRP and IL-6, while Pattern 3 (Fats and Processed Meat) and Pattern 4 (Beans, Tomatoes, and Refined Grains) were associated with increased levels of CRP and IL-6. Additionally, Pattern 1 was linked to reductions in CRP, IL-6, homocysteine, and soluble intercellular adhesion molecule-1 (sICAM-1), whereas Pattern 2 was also associated with a decrease in IL-6 levels. This suggests that diet rich in whole grains and fruit as well as diet rich in vegetables and fish may have anti-inflammatory and protective effects on the endothelium. According to presented studies, a dose of 100 mg of fruit seed extract should provide health benefits while avoiding the side effects and toxic values of compounds that negatively affect health, such as lectins. Also, this amount may protect against an overdose of phytochemicals contained in the extract, as anti-inflammatory and anti-oxidative properties in excess levels changes to pro-inflammatory and pro-oxidative. The vast majority of studies conducted to test the properties of extracts are performed on animals or cells, creating limitations that do not allow the determination of an exact safe dose for humans. Therefore, choosing lower values or even below may prevent patients from occurrence of adverse effects.
Future studies should aim to clarify and explore the precise mechanisms of action of these extracts so that they can be used to treat inflammation. Additionally, investigating the possible synergistic effects of combining these extracts with conventional therapies could further improve their therapeutic potential as well as help relieve the discomfort of taking anti-inflammatory drugs.

Discussion and Conclusions
The extraction process yields various plant extracts from different plant parts like leaves, roots, stems, petals, juice, or seeds, which find broad applications in pharmacy and cosmetology due to their diverse therapeutic actions. Fruit seed extracts, rich in phytochemicals, have long been utilized for preventing and treating diseases, particularly during periods of heightened physical and mental stress. These extracts exhibit significant anti-inflammatory properties, albeit their efficacy may vary based on the specific combination of bioactive compounds present. Natural variations in plant composition, influenced by factors like growth conditions and harvesting methods, necessitate standardization of extracts to ensure consistent levels of key bioactive compounds, ensuring therapeutic efficacy and minimizing variability in results or side effects. Future research should prioritize optimizing extraction methods and standardizing extract compositions, focusing on those with the highest therapeutic potential for developing natural anti-inflammatory agents across healthcare applications. Standardized extracts offer reassurance to patients/consumers regarding the presence of active compounds in appropriate concentrations without adverse effects.
Comparing all the extracts presented, the most promising for further research and development of companion therapies are extracts from passion fruit seeds, Camu Camu, grapes and avocado. These extracts exhibit antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties, attributed to phytochemicals like flavonoids and polyphenols. These extracts are intensively researched, and the amount of research is significant and increasing every year, plus the components present in these extracts are well identified and studied. In a laboratory conditions, research is slowly beginning to move from treating animals to humans, such as in the case of passion flower seed extract, so that the effects of this extract on humans can be determined. In addition, the availability of these extracts is very affordable and the price of individual extracts is low. Potential treatment options created from these seeds’ extracts may reduce the cost of drug therapy, improving the quality of life for lower-income patients.
