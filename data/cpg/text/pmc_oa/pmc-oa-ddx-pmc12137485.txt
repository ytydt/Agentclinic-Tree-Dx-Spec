An evaluation of the effectiveness and safety of endoscopic colon stenting in the treatment of obstructive left colon cancer: a systematic review and meta-analysis
Objective
The objective of this study was to compare the long-term outcomes of colonic stenting in combination with elective surgery versus emergency surgery for the treatment of obstructive left colon cancer.
Background
Obstructive left colon cancer often presents a clinical challenge due to the acute nature of the obstruction, which requires prompt intervention. Traditionally, emergency surgery has been the standard approach to manage such cases, but it carries a higher risk of complications and morbidity. Colonic stenting has emerged as an alternative to provide a bridge to elective surgery, potentially reducing the need for urgent surgical intervention and improving patient outcomes. However, the long-term efficacy and safety of stenting in conjunction with elective surgery compared to emergency surgery remain topics of ongoing research and debate.
Methods
A comprehensive search of domestic and international databases was conducted up to June 2022 to identify clinical research publications on the use of colonic stenting in conjunction with elective and emergency surgeries for obstructive left colon cancer. After assessing the quality of the included literature and extracting the relevant outcome indicators, data analysis was performed using RevMan 5.3 software.
Results
The analysis revealed statistically significant differences in the total and local recurrence rates between the experimental group (stenting combined with elective surgery) and the control group (emergency surgery) [OR = 1.83, 95%CI (1.35,2.48), P < 0.0001, I2 = 76%, Z = 3.93]. Total survival duration and disease-free survival tenure were also found to be statistically significant [OR = 2.40, 95%CI (1.63,3.55), P < 0.0001, I2 = 49%, Z = 4.41]. However, the complication rate did not show a statistically significant difference [OR = 1.12, 95%CI (0.81,1.55), P = 0.05, I2 = 90%, Z = 0.67].
Conclusion
Although our statistical analysis showed significant differences in recurrence rates and survival times between stenting and emergency surgery groups, the clinical significance of these differences must be weighed against the short-term benefits of the stenting approach. When considering the total clinical picture, including complication rates, quality of life factors, and the ability to perform more targeted elective procedures, colonic stenting combined with elective surgery remains a valuable option for obstructive left colon cancer. Further research is warranted to confirm these findings and to refine the selection criteria for patients who would benefit most from colonic stenting.

Introduction
Colon cancer is one of the most prevalent gastrointestinal tumors globally, ranking fourth in incidence among all types of cancer. Each year, approximately 400,000 men and 300,000 women are diagnosed with the disease, with about 30% presenting with acute obstruction. Of these obstructive cases, 70% occur in the left colon, posing risks of colonic necrosis, perforation, or sepsis. Left-sided colon cancer encompasses tumors in the transverse colon’s left half, the splenic flexure, the descending colon, and the sigmoid colon, and in a broader sense, it also includes cancers of the upper rectum. Emergency surgery, while the preferred approach for managing colon cancer obstruction, carries a significant mortality risk, ranging from 15 to 20%, often due to suboptimal preoperative bowel preparation and the management of complications. In contrast, elective surgery boasts a much lower mortality rate, between 0.9% and 6%. Given these statistics, there is a pressing need to convert emergency procedures into elective ones. Colonic stenting emerges as a potential solution to this challenge. However, the approach is not without controversy, as there remain unresolved issues and ongoing debates regarding its efficacy and safety.
Dohmoto originally suggested colonic stenting as a palliative technique to treat intestinal blockage and lessen fistulas in 1991. Self-expandable metal stents were originally used as a surgical bridge by Tejero et al. to treat obstructive colorectal cancer that may be resectable, allowing for proper intestinal preparation and surgical choices. According to several meta-reports relying on randomised controlled trials, elective surgery in combination with colonic stents reduces mortality and improves short-term outcomes. Combination elective colon stenting (SBTS) for left colon cancer is advised to be carried out in facilities with experience in colon stenting, according to the World Association of Emergency Surgery recommendations. Stenting may jeopardise long-term oncology outcomes in patients who may be treatable for cancer, despite colonic stents having technical and clinical success rates of 90% as described in the literature. Early studies have shown that tumor perforation or shedding of tumor cells during stent implantation can lead to the spread of malignant cells. Tumor manipulation by endoscopy and shear force of colonic perfusion is thought to allow local diffusion of tumor cells into the circulatory system. The long-term prognostic investigation was conducted in all 3 randomized controlled trials, and tumor recurrence was increased in the stent group, but the sample size was small (15 ~ 26 patients in the stent group), and the evidence of evidence-based medicine was not sufficient.
To gain a comprehensive understanding of the enduring impacts of stenting and elective surgery in the treatment of obstructed left colon cancer, a systematic study was meticulously conducted. The primary objective of this systematic review was to contrast the long-term outcomes of elective surgical interventions and colonic stenting with those of emergency operations for patients suffering from obstructive left colon cancer. Our investigation yielded significant insights into the comparative efficacy and safety profiles of these treatment modalities over an extended period.

Materials and methods

Subjects and outcome indicators
Individuals with resectable obstructive left carcinomas made up the study’s subjects. A colonic stent was used in conjunction with elective surgery for the experimental group, while emergency surgery was used for the control group. Total recurrence rate, local recurrence rate, overall survival time, and disease-free survival time are the outcome metrics. The study included both randomized controlled trials and clinical cohort studies (both prospective and retrospective).

Inclusion criteria
(1) Studies that have been published in full; (2) The subjects were left colon cancer with complete ileus confirmed by clinical manifestations and imaging examination; (3) Preoperative stent implantation along with elective surgery was the course of treatment for the experimental group, whereas direct emergency surgery was the course of treatment for the control group; (4) The study type was a randomised controlled trial or clinical cohort study (including retrospective cohort studies); (5) Oncology follow-up results (Overall survival, disease-free survival, and recurrence rate) had to be included for longer than three years.

Exclusion criteria
(1) Colon obstruction caused by non-left colon cancer was studied; (2) Colonic stents are used for palliative relief of colonic obstruction; (3) There was no long-term follow-up and incomplete data; (4) Literature with low-quality score.

Search strategy
Flow chart of the literature screening
The search covered PubMed, Cochrane, Web of Science, Embase, CBM, CNKI, CECDB and CQVIP databases, with the most recent search completed in December 2024. The following MeSH terms and free words were used during the study: “endoscopic colon stenting,” “obstructive left colon cancer,” and their combinations. In order to find more interesting research papers, the reference list of relevant earlier reviews was systematically reviewed. Our search initially identified numerous potential studies, including both RCTs and non-RCTs. After screening, 19 additional studies were found that did not meet our inclusion criteria due to insufficient follow-up or incomplete data reporting, and these were listed in the supplementary Table S2 No additional studies meeting the inclusion criteria were identified for the years 2023–2024 Fig 1.

Data extraction
Independently reviewing the literature, extracting data, and cross-checking the findings of the included studies were all done by two researchers. Studies in which there is disagreement and it is difficult to determine whether to include them will be discussed or decided by a third investigator. Studies that do not meet the inclusion criteria explain reasons for exclusion. The extracted data mainly included: the title of the literature, author, publication time, country, study method, sample size, stent type, surgical method, follow-up time and median follow-up period, total recurrence rate (The number of cases with all types of recurrence divided by the total number of cases undergoing radical hysterectomy), local recurrence rate (The recurrence rate of tumors at or near the primary site), etc. In terms of overall survival(The proportion of all participants who survived during the study period) and disease-free survival (The proportion of patients with early-stage malignant tumors who have survived for five years without recurrence or metastasis after treatment), try to extract or calculate from the original text. Try to get in touch with the author to get information if it isn’t included in the original text. If the data were not accessible, the most precise data extraction from the Kaplan-Meier survival curve was performed using the Engage Digitizer4.0 software & hazard ratios were calculated using the method described by Parmar and colleagues. HR), Risk ratio (lnHR) and risk ratio standard error (SeHR).

Exclusion reasons
A total of 19 full-text articles were assessed but subsequently excluded from the analysis. The specific reasons for exclusion were as follows: (1) Studies involving right-sided or transverse colon obstruction (n = 6); (2) Studies using stents only for palliative purposes (n = 4); (3) Studies with follow-up periods of less than 3 years (n = 7); and (4) Studies with duplicate patient cohorts already included in multicenter trials (n = 2). Details of these excluded studies can be found in Supplementary Table S2.

Quality assessment and bias risk analysis

A-B: Funnel plot of literature publication bias
For randomized controlled trials, the quality assessment criteria recommended in the Cochrane Manual were used to assess quality in six areas: randomised sequential generation, allocation hiding, participant blindness, outcome assessment blindness, biased reporting, inadequate data analysis, and other forms of bias. The quality & risk of inclusion bias were evaluated for prospective cohort studies using the library score (Figs. 2 and 3).

Statistical analysis
The Cochrane Collaboration’s RevMan 5.3 programme was used for the meta-analysis. First, the 2 test and I2 test were used to assess the heterogeneity of related studies. If P ≥ 0.1 and I2 ≤ 50%, Fixed effect models were selected since inter-study heterogeneity seemed unlikely. If P < 0.1 and I2 > 50% For meta-analysis, the random-effect model was employed to show study heterogeneity. For counting data, relative risk was employed as the measure of effect size, and for time survival data, risk ratio. For all analyses, the 95% confidence interval (CI) was determined and the analytical test level was α = 0.05.

Total and local recurrence rates
Meta-analysis of total and local recurrence rates between two groups
The heterogeneity assessment of the 15 randomized controlled trials (RCTs) and cohort studies included in the analysis of global and local recurrence rates indicated a low level of inconsistency among the studies, justifying the use of a fixed-effects model for meta-analysis. The meta-analytic results, as depicted in the forest plot for total and local recurrence rates from the included studies, revealed a statistically significant difference in both total and local recurrence rates between the experimental and control groups. The odds ratio (OR) was 1.83, with a 95% confidence interval (CI) ranging from 1.35 to 2.48, indicating a higher likelihood of recurrence in the experimental group compared to the control group (P < 0.0001). The heterogeneity among the studies was moderate, as indicated by an I2 statistic of 76%, and the overall effect was significant, as evidenced by a Z-score of 3.93 (Fig. 4).

Total survival time and disease-free survival time
Comparison of two groups’ meta-analyses of overall survival and disease-free survival times
The meta-analysis results, as depicted in the forest plot of total recurrence rate from the included studies, revealed statistically significant differences in both overall survival time and disease-free survival time between the experimental group and the control group. The odds ratio (OR) was found to be 2.40, with a 95% confidence interval (CI) ranging from 1.63 to 3.55, indicating a significant effect in favor of the experimental group. This finding was further supported by a P-value of less than 0.0001, signifying a very low probability that the observed differences occurred by chance. The heterogeneity across studies was moderate, as indicated by an I2 statistic of 49%, and the overall effect was statistically significant, as evidenced by a Z-score of 4.41 (refer to Fig. 5).

Complication rate
Comparison of two groups’ complication rates in a meta-analysis
The meta-analysis results presented in the forest plot of total recurrence rates from the included studies indicate that there was no statistically significant difference in the incidence of complications between the experimental and control groups, as evidenced by the non-overlapping diamond and vertical lines in the forest plot for complication incidence (OR = 1.12, 95% CI [0.81, 1.55], P = 0.05, I² = 90%, Z = 0.67) (Fig. 6).

Discussion
This comprehensive research compared the long-term outcomes of stenting as a bridge to surgery for obstructive left colon cancer to emergency surgery (23–25). **While our statistical analysis showed higher recurrence rates in the stent group, this must be interpreted within the broader clinical context.
In acute left colon obstruction, several surgical approaches are available. The traditional three-stage procedure includes proximal enterostomy in stage I, tumor resection in stage II, and stoma closure in stage III. However, this approach has significant drawbacks including long hospital stays, poor patient compliance, increased risk of tumor implantation and metastasis, and sometimes losing the opportunity for tumor resection altogether.

With improvements in perioperative management, surgical techniques, appropriate antibiotic use, and enhanced nutritional support, single-stage surgery has become increasingly accepted. However, postoperative complications remain high at 40–60%, with anastomotic leakage rates up to 14% and mortality around 10%.
Research suggests that colon lavage or decompression followed by single-stage anastomosis can effectively reduce anastomotic leakage and other complications, though concerns exist regarding potential contamination during lavage.
Compared with colonic lavage, stenting combined with elective surgery had advantages in postoperative mortality, anastomotic fistula incidence and wound infection rate. Colonic stents are used to decompress acute cancerous ileus and thus transform emergency surgery into an elective procedure. Successful stent implantation combined with elective surgery can manage complications under optimal conditions, improve patient nutrition, accurately stage the tumor, and develop individualized treatment plans. In addition, restoring intestinal function can reduce colostomy and significantly improve patient’s quality of life. Patient with left-sided malignant colonic obstruction have a worse prognosis than those who are not. The increase of intercellular pressure caused by mass compression plays an important role in tumor cell metastasis.
Nearly three decades after stenting was first described, the debate over the use of self-dilating stents in combination with elective surgery for obstructive left colon cancer remains unresolved. For short-term outcomes of stent implantation, Arezzo et al. ‘s systematic review based on eight randomized controlled trials, including 497 patients. Compared with emergency surgery, stenting combined with elective surgery significantly reduced the overall incidence (34% vs. 51%), the rate of immediate stoma (34% vs. 51%), & permanent stoma rate of (22% vs. 35%), and significantly increased the rate of stage I anastomosis (70% vs 54%). The uncertain long-term oncology outcome of stenting combined with elective surgery, compared with the apparent short-term benefit, has been a subject of debate among surgeons. In the first retrospective study, According to Saida et al., stenting in conjunction with elective surgery had long-term cancer outcomes that were comparable to emergency surgery (44% vs. 40%) in terms of 5-year overall survival.

Despite these concerns, the potential tumor risk associated with self-expanding stents can be balanced by several positive oncological outcomes. Colonic stent implantation provides conditions for laparoscopic surgery. Compared with open surgery, laparoscopic surgery has the advantages of a clear surgical field, fine operation, less trauma to patients and faster postoperative recovery. Combined with laparoscopic surgery, stent implantation has advantages in postoperative intestinal function recovery and complications. Despite being high-risk patients, laparoscopic surgery had a lower risk of death based on population baseline. Adjuvant chemotherapy can begin earlier when stents are used in conjunction with elective surgery, with fewer postoperative problems and a greater survival rate. Additionally, the low fistula incidence of stent implantation may greatly enhance the patient’s quality of life.
The following restrictions apply to the findings of this study: Despite our efforts to include all relevant studies, some potential literature may have been missed; The methods of operation were different across the included literature; Data on overall survival and disease-free survival were taken from the Kaplan-Meier curve and approximated in some cases. The heterogeneity in study design and patient populations may influence the interpretation of results.
The optimal management strategy for patients with obstructive left colon cancer appears to be treatment in facilities with expertise in stenting techniques, where complications can be minimized and appropriate patient selection can be employed. Our findings support stenting as a bridge to surgery as a clinically valuable approach despite the statistical differences in recurrence rates. Given the moderate quality of available evidence, these recommendations should be implemented with caution, particularly in centers with high experience in stenting techniques.
Clinical implications of our research suggest that:
Patient selection is critical - those with higher perforation risks during stenting should be considered for direct emergency surgery.
Centers with expertise in stenting techniques are likely to achieve better outcomes.
The timing of surgery after stent placement may influence oncological outcomes.
A multidisciplinary approach involving endoscopists, surgeons and oncologists is essential for optimal management.
These findings provide guidance for clinicians in daily decision-making regarding the management of obstructive left colon cancer. Future research should focus on identifying optimal patient selection criteria and determining the ideal timing for surgery following stent placement.

Conclusion
In conclusion, our meta-analysis revealed statistically significant differences in recurrence rates and survival times between stenting and emergency surgery approaches. However, these statistical findings must be interpreted within the broader clinical context. When considering quality of life factors, the avoidance of stomas, and the ability to perform more targeted surgical approaches after decompression, stenting as a bridge to surgery remains a valuable option for obstructive left colon cancer. Further research is warranted to confirm these findings and to refine the selection criteria for patients who would benefit most from colonic stenting.
