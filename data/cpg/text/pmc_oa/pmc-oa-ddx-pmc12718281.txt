Transorbital approach: a bibliometric analysis from 1948 to 2024
Background
Transorbital surgery has substantially evolved over the last century, with some notable and at times controversial developments, such as its use for transorbital leucotomies, the induction of cerebral ischemia in animal stroke models, and, more recently, the development of neuroendoscopic transorbital procedures. This review aims to analyze the evolution of transorbital surgery from 1948 to 2024 using scientometric techniques, assessing its impact and emerging trends over time, particularly during the past two decades marked by the surge of the endoscopic transorbital approach (eTOA).
Methods
An advanced search of the Web of Science Core Collection was performed using the topic “Transorbital.” Publications from 1948 to 2024 were screened for thematic relevance and analyzed with R (bibliometrix), Python, VOSviewer, Genderize.io, and Microsoft Excel. Scientometric indicators included publication and citation trends, keyword evolution, institutional and geographic output, collaboration networks, and gender distribution. Full and fractional counting were applied for country- and institution-level analyses.
Results
A total of 538 publications were included. Scientific activity increased markedly after 2010, accounting for 64% of all articles and demonstrating higher international collaboration. A thematic shift was observed from early ischemia models toward anatomical and clinical applications of the endoscopic transorbital approach. The most prolific institutions were the University of Naples Federico II, the University of Barcelona, Sungkyunkwan University, and the University of Washington, while the United States, Italy, South Korea, and Spain contributed the largest national outputs. Gender analysis showed persistent underrepresentation of female authors.
Conclusion
The transorbital approach has evolved into a versatile minimally invasive route supported by a rapidly expanding research community. Despite the growth in publication volume and collaborative activity, prospective clinical evidence remains limited. This bibliometric review outlines historical trends, current research structures, and opportunities for future investigation in transorbital surgery.

Introduction
The transorbital approach is a minimally invasive surgical technique that involves using the orbit as an anatomical corridor to access intraorbital structures, the skull base and other adjacent regions. The first transorbital surgeries were documented in the nineteenth century, in the field of ophthalmology, presented as an alternative to treat orbital lesions and tumors while avoiding eye enucleation. The first transorbital procedure in neurosurgery is attributed to C.H. Frazier in 1912. In the early twentieth century, ophthalmologists and neurosurgeons held differing views on the management of orbital tumors, with neurosurgeons more inclined toward transcranial techniques. However, in the field of psychosurgery, A. Fiamberti performed the first transorbital lobotomy in 1937, a technique later popularized in the United States by W. Freeman, which was eventually abandoned due to a lack of supporting evidence and widespread public opposition. Since the 1970 s, numerous animal studies on cerebral ischemia using transorbital occlusion of the middle cerebral artery have been conducted, reporting a low risk of complications and straightforward vascular access.
Endoscopic surgery represented a paradigm shift in minimally invasive approaches. In 1981, Norris and Cleasby first described the use of endoscope-assisted orbital surgery in a case series of 15 patients with various orbital and periorbital lesions, reporting good outcomes. Nevertheless, craniofacial open resection and transnasal endoscopic approaches remained the preferred routes to the anterior skull base until 2010, when Moe et al. presented transorbital neuroendoscopic surgery as a safe and valuable technique to access the ventral component of the anterior skull base. This publication consisted of a case series involving 20 transorbital neuroendoscopic procedures in sixteen patients, which reported no significant complications or treatment failures. Subsequent anatomical studies demonstrated the feasibility of the endoscopic transorbital approach (eTOA) to access structures such as the cavernous sinus, Meckel’s cave, middle cerebral artery, petrous apex, and skull base regions. Other anatomical works analyzed combined transorbital approaches to reach complex areas like the tentorial and petroclival regions, integrated anatomical and clinical perspectives, or assessed extended variants such as the lateral orbital rim osteotomy.
In addition to anatomical studies, several retrospective clinical series and cohort studies have reported outcomes of various transorbital techniques and surgical targets. These include the superior eyelid approach to the middle cranial fossa and Meckel’s cave, the lateral eTOA, and analyses of complications associated with the superior eyelid eTOA. Further applications include the use of eTOA for spheno-orbital and sphenoid wing meningiomas. Comparative studies have evaluated eTOA against alternative approaches such as the endoscopic endonasal approach and the extended mini-pterional approach.
Prospective clinical data remain limited. Notable contributions include a 107-patient cohort reported by Balakrishnan and Moe, and a case series on cranio-orbital tumors by Kong et al.. Several systematic reviews have also been published, some offering general overviews of eTOA, while others focus on specific indications such as Meckel’s cave lesions, temporal lobe epilepsy, trigeminal schwannomas, spheno-orbital meningiomas, or combined approaches.
Despite the increasing number of recent publications on this approach and its historical significance, no comprehensive bibliometric analysis has been conducted to date. The aim of this study is to employ scientometric tools to perform a quantitative analysis of the scientific literature, focusing on publication metrics, citation patterns, geographic and institutional contributions, co-authorship networks, and temporal trends, thereby providing a visualized landscape of the evolution, current relevance, and short-term future perspectives of this promising technique.

Materials & methods

Data collection and selection criteria
PRISMA 2020 flow diagram illustrating the literature selection process (adapted from Page MJ et al. BMJ 2021;372:n71). The database summary has been generated with bibliometrix
The Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) database was selected for the data acquisition after considering other databases such as Scopus and PubMed due to the quality and completeness of metadata it provided. Cross-checking with Scopus and PubMed revealed no major discrepancies. An advanced search was performed with the following query: ((TS = (TRANSORBITAL)) AND LA = (English)) AND DT = (Article OR Review). The document type “Article” in the WoSCC includes original research papers, brief communications, technical notes, chronologies, full papers, and case reports that are considered citable. Standalone video articles, as well as operative videos published as citable articles or as supplementary material, were also included whenever indexed. The publication date range was set from January 1, 1948, to December 31, 2024. Data collection took place on March 12, 2025, but citation patterns were frequently updated during the development of the study. Publications related to transorbital ultrasound and transorbital penetrating injuries were excluded by consensus, as they were not directly related to surgical transorbital approaches. A screening of titles and abstracts was performed independently by two authors. Publications from 2025 listed as “early access” in 2024 were also excluded. Duplicate records were assessed, and none were found. Of the 976 results retrieved in the initial search, 538 publications were included and 438 were excluded. Only publications in English were analyzed, while the database search retrieved 15 results in German, 6 in French, 4 in Spanish, 1 in Japanese, and 1 in Russian when not refined by language (2.7% of the total). The search and selection algorithm has been detailed in Fig. 1. For some figures, the time frame 2010–2024 was selected, based on the 2010 publication by Moe et al., which is widely regarded as the seminal work that introduced transorbital neuroendoscopic surgery into clinical neurosurgical practice.

Data analysis and visualization
The full records and cited references were exported from the WoSCC database. Data analysis and graphical illustration were performed using RStudio (version 2024.12.1 + 563) with the bibliometrix package, as well as Python (version 3.11.10) with the libraries pandas, matplotlib, numpy, wordcloud, folium, and pycountry. Co-authorship networks were generated using VOSviewer (version 1.6.20), with minor modifications applied to distinguish between research groups. Gender inference was performed with Genderize.io (accessed in June 2025), a tool that has demonstrated an overall accuracy > 95% in its predictions. Microsoft Excel (version 16.96.1) was employed to analyze the data and generate the article table.
Publication and citation trends over time were analyzed with the “Average Citations per Year” metric generated by bibliometrix, which accounts for both the average number of citations per publication and the time elapsed since publication. It is calculated by dividing the total number of citations of each article by the number of years since its publication, with the formula: Total Citations/(2025 − Year of Publication + 1). The resulting values are then averaged across all articles published in a certain year. Regarding clinical case series, all publications from 2010 to 2024 were screened to identify studies reporting patient cohorts. While the number of case series increased markedly after 2010, the cohort size varied widely and did not follow a consistent temporal trend. Therefore, cohort size was not used as a quantitative variable in the bibliometric analysis to preserve clarity and focus on the major trends.
Regarding the analysis by country, author affiliations were processed with Python to extract and normalize country names, followed by manual review to ensure accuracy. Two complementary counting methods were applied to quantify national scientific output: full and fractional counting. In the full counting approach, each country listed in the author affiliations of a publication was credited with one full publication and the total citation count of that article, irrespective of the number of co-authoring countries. This method tends to overrepresent international collaborative publications, as each country receives full credit. In contrast, fractional counting distributes credit proportionally among all contributing countries, assigning each one a fraction of the total publication and citation counts based on the number of countries involved.
Authors’ institutional affiliations were extracted from the database using Python. A manual normalization process was performed to group different variants of the same institution (e.g., university hospitals or research centers always associated with a university in the database) under a unified name. Additionally, generic institutional aggregators, such as university or health systems (e.g., “University of California System”, “Ochsner Health System”), were excluded when the specific institution was already listed by the author, in order to avoid double-counting and overrepresentation. The number of publications and citations by institution was analyzed with the full counting and fractional counting methods.
In the analysis of sources and authorship impact, different indices were calculated with bibliometrix. The h-index reflects both the productivity and citation impact of an author or journal, representing the number of publications (h) that have received at least h citations. The g-index is an alternative that gives more weight to highly cited papers, being defined as the highest number (g) such that the top g articles received together at least g2 citations. The m-index adjusts the h-index by the number of years since the first publication, offering a normalized measure of research impact over time.

Results
A total of 538 publications from 1948 to 2024 met the inclusion criteria and were analyzed. These documents were published in 170 different sources and authored by 2,383 different contributors, with only 20 single-author papers. The average number of co-authors per document was 6.28, and the international co-authorship rate reached 20.63%. An average of 20.58 citations per document was reached. When the dataset was restricted to the period 2010 to 2024, considering the 2010 publication by Moe et al. as a landmark for eTOA, an increase in scientific activity was observed. The number of publications reached 339, accounting for about 64% of the total output, with a markedly higher annual growth rate of 21.11%. The subset involved 1,747 authors across 98 sources, with only 4 single-author papers, and an even greater degree of collaboration: 7.65 co-authors per document and 28.91% international co-authorship. However, the average number of citations per document was lower, with 14.2 citations per publication.
Metadata quality assessment revealed completeness among the whole database in the following categories: authors, document type, journal, language, publication year, title, and total citations. Minor deficiencies were found in these fields: cited references (1.86% missing), affiliations (7.43%), corresponding author (7.43%), DOI (7.81%), and abstract (8.92%). Author keywords and Keywords Plus (generated from the cited titles by Web of Science) were missing in 28.25% and 29.18% of the documents, respectively. However, when selecting the timeframe 2000–2024, the quality of metadata improved: missing cited references dropped to 0.79%, missing DOIs to 3.43%, abstracts to 2.64%, author keywords to 8.71%, and Keywords Plus to 10.55%.

Publication and citation trends over time
Annual scientific output and citation impact of transorbital surgical literature from 1948 to 2024. The blue bars represent the number of publications per year (left axis), while the orange line shows the average number of citations per year for articles published in each year (right axis). A sustained increase in publication activity is observed after 2010, coinciding with the introduction of endoscopic transorbital approaches. Peaks in average citation impact are visible around 1970, 1980–1987, 1998–2001, 2010, and 2018
To better understand the peaks in citation impact shown in Fig. 2, a closer examination of the articles contributing to those years was performed. The citation peak in 1970 corresponds to a single but highly influential experimental study by Hudgins and Garcia, which introduced a transorbital technique for surgical access to the middle cerebral artery (MCA) in primate models. Their method involved orbital exenteration followed by dissection through the orbital roof, allowing direct visualization and occlusion of the MCA origin without retraction of cerebral tissue. This technique was a modification of the retro-orbital, extradural approach previously described by Sundt and Waltz, in which the MCA was accessed via a frontotemporal scalp incision and craniectomy extending down to the sphenoid wing, without traversing the orbital cavity.
The citation peaks in 1980, 1983, and 1987 correspond to a sustained period of innovation in experimental stroke models using transorbital surgical access to the middle cerebral artery, primarily in cats and non-human primates. In 1980, the studies by Hossmann and Schuier established a reproducible feline model of MCA occlusion through a transorbital route, enabling controlled induction of focal ischemia and systematic analysis of ischemic thresholds and edema formation. By 1983, authors such as Mies and Garcia had employed this technique to achieve precise occlusion of the MCA in animal models and to investigate the temporal evolution of infarction and define perilesional perfusion zones, which would later be known as the ischemic penumbra. In 1987, the transorbital route was again employed in primate and rodent models to study the effects of various anesthetic agents and techniques on infarct size and neuroprotection.
A citation peak is also observed around the year 2000, which corresponds to a transitional phase in the evolution of the transorbital corridor from experimental models to renewed clinical applications. During this period, several highly cited studies explored orbitocranial and paraclinoid surgical approaches, often integrating orbital structures into the access route to reach vascular or anterior skull base targets. De Jesús et al. reported on the management of paraclinoid and ophthalmic aneurysms using various surgical strategies, including a frontotemporal transorbital approach, thereby reinforcing the anatomical feasibility of orbital extensions. Steiger et al. described a transorbital keyhole approach to the anterior communicating artery, emphasizing reduced brain retraction and improved aesthetic outcomes. Although these procedures were not endoscopic, they demonstrated a renewed interest in the orbit as a viable surgical corridor and laid the conceptual groundwork for the development of the eTOA in the following decade.

The citation peak in 2010 aligns with the publication by Moe et al., which reported the first clinical application of endoscopic transorbital surgery in neurosurgery. This prospective series included 20 procedures addressing anterior skull base pathologies, such as fractures, tumors, cerebrospinal fluid leaks, and optic nerve compression. In 2011, this approach was further validated by a 107 patient case series of endoscopic transorbital procedures published by Balakrishnan et al., which reported achieving surgical goals in 106 out of 107 patients, with minor complications in 17 cases, primarily involving persistent diplopia and vision changes. A complete list of the 30 most cited publications between 2010 and 2024 is displayed in Table 1.
A last peak in average citations per year emerged in 2018, reflecting a concentrated surge in both anatomical and clinical literature exploring variations and applications of the eTOA. Di Somma et al. published a detailed anatomical study describing modular transorbital corridors and their potential to access both lateral and medial regions of the anterior and middle cranial fossae. Multiple studies in the same year reported on the feasibility, safety, and clinical outcomes of transorbital surgery for spheno-orbital meningiomas, including techniques for hyperostotic bone removal and combined approaches for decompression and tumor resection. Jeon et al. published the first clinical series employing the eTOA to access Meckel’s cave and middle cranial fossa tumors. Interestingly, a decline in average citations per article was observed in 2023 and 2024, with 5.61 and 2.71 citations per publication, respectively, which contrasts with the growth in publications also observed in these years (70 articles in 2024 and 49 in the year 2023).

Keywords analysis
Author keyword frequency representation for two distinct time periods, illustrating the evolution of thematic focus in the transorbital literature. The size of each keyword is proportional to its frequency during the respective period. The left panel (1948–2009) is dominated by experimental and vascular terms such as “cerebral ischemia,” “focal cerebral ischemia,” “middle cerebral artery occlusion,” and animal models like “cats” and “primates.” The right panel (2010–2024) shows a predominance of surgical and anatomical terms, including “transorbital,” “skull base,” “endoscopic,” “cavernous sinus,” and “meningioma,” as well as specific surgical targets such as “Meckel’s cave” and the “middle cranial fossa”
Analysis of keyword frequency revealed a significant shift in thematic emphasis between the two studied time periods, prior to and following 2010 (Fig. 3). In the 1948–2009 subset, the most frequent terms were mainly associated with experimental models of cerebral ischemia, with “cerebral ischemia” (n = 17), “focal cerebral ischemia” (n = 7), and “middle cerebral artery occlusion” (n = 6) leading the list. Terms like “cats” and “primates” also appeared frequently, indicating the use of animal models in preclinical research. In contrast, the 2010–2024 period was characterized by surgical and anatomical terms linked to clinical applications of transorbital approaches: “Transorbital” (n = 49), “skull base” (n = 35), and “endoscopic” (n = 33) were the most common, followed by terms such as “orbit,” “cavernous sinus,” and “meningioma”.

Analysis of authors’ gender
Gender distribution of authors in the database from 2000 to 2024, inferred using Genderize.io with a selected prediction certainty of ≥ 0.9. The Y-axis represents the number of authors. Female authors are shown in the orange portion of the bars, and male authors in the blue portion. Out of the 379 articles identified in the period 2000–2024, 90% (341 publications) included the full names of the first author, while 10% (38 publications) lacked first names and were therefore manually excluded from the gender analysis. The Genderize.io API (accessed in June 2025) was used to infer gender from the remaining subset. Only names with a prediction certainty of ≥ 0.9 were retained for further analysis. This led to the exclusion of 79 names, resulting in a final set of 262 first names (69% of the initial 379 articles) whose gender could be confidently determined. Among these, 216 (82.4%) were identified as male and 46 (17.6%) as female. The annual gender distribution of these first authors is shown in Fig. 9a.
Among the 341 articles with full author names, 2,592 author entries were identified, of which 1,744 were unique. During manual standardization, a total of 363 names were excluded due to inconsistencies, leaving 2,229 complete entries. Of these, 1,820 had a prediction certainty of ≥ 0.9, corresponding to 81.7% of the total, and were included in the final analysis. 1,525 authors (82%) were inferred to be male and 295 (18.3%) to be female. The year-by-year gender distribution of all contributing authors is presented in Fig. 9b. A linear regression analysis was performed to evaluate whether there has been a statistically significant increase in female authorship (gender probability ≥ 0.9) from 2000 to 2024. The results revealed a positive regression coefficient (0.0024), but the trend was not statistically significant (p = 0.3571), and the model accounted for only a small fraction of the variation (R2 = 0.0532).

Discussion
This review provides a comprehensive scientometric overview of the transorbital approach by offering a framework that captures long-term publication dynamics, thematic evolution, and collaborative structures. While Houlihan et al. previously examined the historical milestones of this surgical approach, the present bibliometric analysis offers a complementary perspective, enabling a large-scale quantitative assessment that would be impractical through a conventional systematic review. Due to a lack of standardized bibliometric information in the early developmental period of this approach, from H. Knapp’s description in 1875 to the first publications by W. Freeman in 1948, this timeframe could not be included in the database.
The temporal and keyword analyses together delineated a conceptual transition in the field. Early literature was dominated by preclinical models of cerebral ischemia, whereas after 2010 the focus shifted toward detailed anatomical characterization and clinical implementation of endoscopic transorbital techniques, which fundamentally expanded the surgical possibilities of the orbital corridor. The decrease in average citations observed in 2023–2024 likely reflects the short citation window rather than a reduction in scientific interest.
A small group of neurosurgical journals accounted for over one-third of all publications, reflecting their pivotal role in consolidating emerging trends within the field. The geographic and institutional patterns similarly point to a consolidated research ecosystem, with well-defined clusters of expertise and frequent cross-institutional collaborations, particularly among leading centers in Italy, South Korea, Spain, and the United States. Fractional counting also highlights institutions whose relatively modest publication volume is offset by high citation impact, suggesting focused and influential research activity. It is important to note, however, that bibliometric indicators such as publication and citation counts primarily reflect scholarly activity and institutional visibility, and are not direct measures of research quality.
Patterns of authorship illustrate the multidisciplinary and international character of endoscopic transorbital surgery, reflecting collaborations across neurosurgery, otolaryngology, and ophthalmology in multiple regions. At the same time, the persistent underrepresentation of female authors mirrors the broader gender imbalance observed in neurosurgical academia. Although this study applied gender-inference tools with higher reported accuracy than those used in earlier bibliometric analyses, some degree of uncertainty remains inherent to automated name-classification methods. Nevertheless, the overall trend is consistent and highlights an opportunity to strengthen gender diversity within the research community developing this surgical corridor.
Overall, the findings illustrate how the eTOA has matured from an experimental route to a versatile minimally invasive approach supported by a rapidly growing and globally distributed research community. The current evidence base continues to rely predominantly on anatomical studies and retrospective clinical series, with limited prospective data. The wide variability observed in the size of published clinical cohorts, without a clear temporal trend, likely reflects the uneven diffusion of the eTOA across institutions. These factors suggest that many clinical applications are still concentrated in highly specialized centers, which naturally limits the generation of large, homogeneous patient series. Future work integrating standardized outcome reporting and multicenter prospective designs will be essential to further refine indications, assess long-term results, and consolidate the role of endoscopic transorbital approaches within skull base surgery.

Study limitations
The data analyzed in this study were extracted exclusively from the WoSCC. Although cross-checked with PubMed and Scopus, differences in database coverage may have resulted in minor omissions. Metadata completeness was lower for the period between 1948–2000, especially regarding keywords. Recent publications (2023–2024) had limited time to accumulate citations, potentially underestimating their impact. Only English-language documents classified as “Article” or “Review” under the WoSCC document-type categories were included. Manual standardization using Python improved the consistency of affiliations, although some ambiguities may have persisted. Gender analysis was constrained by missing first names in early records and by a reduced accuracy in classifying names from East Asian countries.

Conclusion
From Krönlein’s approach to the development of modern neuroendoscopic techniques, the transorbital approach has been a valuable surgical route for more than a century. This bibliometric review serves as a reference for researchers by mapping publication trends, key contributors, and thematic developments in the field, thus facilitating both the contextualization of past advances and the identification of future directions in the study and clinical application of the transorbital corridor. Since the clinical implementation of eTOA in neurosurgery in 2010, a significant growth in publications from different institutions and countries, with a high degree of collaboration, has been noted. Although more clinical works are being performed, prospective studies are still limited. Nevertheless, the growing interest within the scientific community, the rapid development of novel applications, and the positive clinical outcomes documented in multiple case series indicate that this minimally invasive approach is steadily consolidating its role within the neurosurgical armamentarium.
