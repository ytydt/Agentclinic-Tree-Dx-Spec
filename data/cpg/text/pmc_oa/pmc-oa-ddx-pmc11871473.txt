Evaluation of the effect of metformin as a radiosensitiser in solid tumours: A systematic review
Highlights
Metformin has demonstrated in preclinical studies its ability to enhance the effect of radiotherapy.
Metformin combined with radiotherapy, showed a significant benefit in survival and response to treatment in prostate, liver and gynecological tumors.
The benefict results in lung, rectal and head and neck cancer were inconsistent.
The mechanisms of action are improving the efficacy of radiotherapy by activating the AMPK pathway, inhibiting mTOR and reducing glutathione levels.
Despite promising results, the review highlights the lack of randomized clinical trials confirming the efficacy of metformin as a radiosensitizer in clinical practice.
Background
Metformin is an antidiabetic drug that has shown its benefit in increasing the effect of radiotherapy in the treatment of solid tumors in preclinical studies. The objective of this systematic review is to study the effect of metformin as a radiosensitizer in studies carried out in clinical practice.
Methods
Systematic review carried out according to PRISMA criteria of clinical trials, systematic reviews and observational studies focused on the influence of metformin as a radiosensitizer in solid tumors. The studies were published between the years 2010 and 2022. The results of the studies have been analyzed in terms of survival (OS, PFS, DFS, DMFS) and response (ORR) between patients treated with metformin and without it.
Results
A total of 16 studies have been found in the literature (the most frequent tumor was prostate cancer, 5 studies). External radiotherapy was administered in all the studies and in two of them to greater brachytherapy. The use of metformin with radiotherapy showed a consistent benefit in terms of survival and response in tumors of prostate, hepatic and gynecological origin. The benefit in the rest of the tumors analyzed (lung, rectal, and head and neck cancer) is doubtful, and the results are contradictory. The greatest benefits were observed in prostate tumors both in OS and SLE.
Conclusions
The use of metformin in combination with radiotherapy in solid tumors is one of the most promising treatments under development in oncology. The benefit observed in real-life studies makes it necessary to develop clinical trials that specifically evaluate its use in clinical practice in the future.

Introduction
Type 2 diabetes mellitus (DM2) is a disease whose incidence and prevalence has increased exponentially in recent years, in our environment prevalence of diabetes of 13.8 %, which means that more than 4.5 million Spaniards suffered from type 2 diabetes. Characteristically, it presents with an increase in insulin resistance, and compensatory hyperinsulinemia, chronic inflammation, obesity, and hyperglycemia itself, which determines the complications in the medium and long term. In addition, a relationship has been seen with other diseases such as cancer, with an incidence of tumors up to four times higher in diabetic patients.
Metformin is an antidiabetic known for decades belonging to the group of biguanides, who has reached our days as the most widely used antidiabetic in the world due to its efficacy, safety and ease of use, which makes it considered as the first therapeutic step in different clinical guidelines. One of the potential fields for metformin outside of the TDM2 context is its impact on cancer. Since the last century, different authors have proposed that biguanides have a role as protectors against aging and a role as antitumor agents. It has been described that metformin has activity in a) cancer prevention, reducing the incidence of cancer in patients with DM2 under treatment with this drug, b) and as an antitumor agent, raising awareness against different therapeutic weapons, either in studies in in vitro as well as in vivo studies.

In different studies it has been observed how metformin decreases the risk of developing prostate cancer or breast, as well as that the use of treatment with metformin in patients treated with radiotherapy (RT) and/or chemotherapy (CT) improves the data of local control of the disease in different tumors such as prostate, breast, brain or colorectal, although with data not yet well established in terms of survival in all of them. This is due to the anti-proliferative activity of metformin through inactivation of mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR)-dependent pathways, activation of AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK) pathways, and reduction of the activity of epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) and mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK). In turn, AMPK regulates the expression and phosphorylation of p53, essential in apoptosis, autophagy, and other antiproliferative effects. On the other hand, the anti-inflammatory effects exerted through metformin allow a decrease in dysplasia and carcinogenesis in multiple tumors. Another point to take into account is the effect of metformin as an immunostimulator, enhancing the antitumor actions of the immune system itself. Added to the above, one of the current points under study of the action of metformin is its effect on sensitization to RT by tumor cells. The increase in radiosensitivity is mediated by the combinatorial action of RT with metformin on complex I of the electron transfer chain, thus reducing oxygen consumption and increasing reactive oxygen species (ROS). In the cell. This leads to a reduction in hypoxia, a state that is related to radioresistance. As a result of the above, damage to deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA) occurs with the consequent cell death. In addition, the combination of these two treatments produces a reduction in glutathione, a key antioxidant for the prevention of DNA damage. Therefore, metformin could be a particularly important weapon in the future in the treatment of different neoplasms that require RT treatment (Fig. 1). For this reason, the main objective of this systematic review is the evaluation of the sensitizing action of metformin in the radiotherapy treatment of solid tumors by evaluating the studies (clinical trials and observational studies) existing to date (Fig. 2).

Material and methods

Search strategy
Following the quality criteria included in the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA), a search was carried out in PubMed, COCHRANE, Science Direct, EMBASE and the clinical trial registry (https://www.clinicaltrials.gov) for clinical trials, systematic reviews and observational studies focused on the influence of metformin in patients diagnosed with cancer who have received RT as part of the therapeutic strategy. Publications in English, French and Spanish from 2010 to 2022 were included.
The search terms and their combinations were (metformin OR diabetes) AND (cancer OR tumor OR neoplasm OR malignant) AND (radiotherapy OR radiation OR ionizing radiation) AND (review OR systematic OR meta-analysis OR study OR analysis). In addition, the following filters were applied: “clinical trial”, “meta-analysis”, “review”, “systematic review”, “species: human”, “english,”french”, and”spanish”.

Selection of studies
Two authors reviewed the titles and abstracts independently (Dr. González del Portillo and Dr. Olivares). Those publications that met the inclusion criteria were analyzed in depth and selected for the present study with confirmation by a third independent reviewer (Dr. Miramontes-González).

Inclusion criteria
Clinical trials, qualitative reviews, quantitative reviews that analyze the association between the sensitizing effect of metformin with increased survival and response to radiotherapy in solid tumors.
Solid tumors that have received synchronous treatment with metformin and radiotherapy.
The included studies must indicate the survival or relapse times that are necessary in each case to analyze the data, depending on the tumor staging.

Exclusion criteria
Studies published as abstracts or communications to conferences.
Studies with non-assessable measures of response to cancer treatment.
Trials or studies conducted on patients with non-solid tumors.
The search carried out with the previously described characteristics included a total of 221 articles, of which 15 articles (with 16 studies) were finally included. The included publications corresponded to clinical trials and observational studies that collected the influence of metformin treatment in cancer patients undergoing radiotherapy treatment. The studies discarded in the first place (201 articles) were because they were studies based on narrative reviews or conference papers. Subsequently, 5 more studies were discarded, of which 4 were abstracts and 1 was in a language other than English.

Data extraction and statistical analysis
The data extracted by the two researchers independently were: title, first author, year of publication, number of patients included, type of population selected and primary cancer, clinical trial phase, clinical trial arms, previous metformin treatment/ concomitant, radiotherapy treatment (dose and fractionation), concomitant systemic treatment, overall survival, free time until recurrence, cancer-specific survival and progression-free survival, relative risk (RR), odds ratio (OR), hazard ratio (HR) and interval of confidence (CI) corresponding to systematic reviews and meta-analyses.
The software used was SPSS, version 25 (IBM®). The data from the analyzed studies have been expressed, joining them quantitatively and qualitatively, trying to express the data in the most homogeneous way possible. Since the studies carried out express the data in different terms, due to the different tumor locations, histologies and stages, the data analyzed have been summarized in survival and response rates (pathological and by RECIST1.1 criteria).

Results

General characteristics of the sample
Of the 16 included studies (15 articles), one included patients diagnosed with head and neck cancer, one with endometrial cancer, two with lung cancer, three with rectal cancer, three with esophageal cancer, one with hepatocarcinoma, and five with cancer of prostate. The histology collected in the studies included adenocarcinoma subtype tumors in 14 of them (87.5 %) and squamous in 7 (43.75 %). All studies included patients with a diagnosis of local or locally advanced disease, only one study included patients at all stages, including advanced stages. Treatment options included were: three studies included patients who had received radical CT-RT treatment, one adjuvant RT, six neoadjuvant CT-RT, and six radical RT monotherapy, including external RT and brachytherapy (BT). In all the studies, the patients were diagnosed with type 2 diabetes mellitus and received a daily dose of metformin, variable between 250 mg and 2550 mg, which could be divided into a single daily dose or several.

Characteristics of cancer treatment
Regarding RT, the 16 studies included treatment with external RT and two of them also included BT, both in prostate cancer. The type of technique used for external RT included advanced techniques with intensity modulated RT (IMRT) and volumetric modulated arc RT (VMAT) in eight papers (50 %), as well as conventional 3D RT (3D-CRT) in 5 five papers. (31.25 %). The two prostate cancer studies in which BT was treated used low rate BT (LDR) with Palladium-103 and Iodine-125 seeds (6.25 %) and another high rate (HDR). The total dose and fractionation used is highly variable depending on the location and histology, highlighting hypofractionation and fractionated stereotactic RT (Stereotactic Body Radiotherapy, SBRT) in three studies of prostate cancer (total dose of 25–60 Gy in 3–6 fractions) and hepatocellular carcinoma (18.75 %), as well as hyperfractionation in one study on lung cancer (6.25 %). The rest of the works include conventional RT fractionations around 1.8–2 Gy per fraction, reaching doses: a) for radical treatments of 70 Gy in head and neck tumors, up to 66 Gy in lung tumors or up to 80 Gy. Gy in prostate tumors; b) for neoadjuvant treatments up to 54 Gy in rectal cancer and 50.4 Gy in esophageal cancer.

The included studies have analyzed the influence of being treated with oral metformin on the oncological outcome. Depending on the location of the primary tumor and the endpoint analyzed, statistical significance has been observed in different scenarios (Table 2, Table 3).

Head and neck tumors
In the case of the only study focused on head and neck tumors, Spratt et al. only found statistical significance for distant metastases-free survival (DMFS, p = 0.011) for those patients who were on systemic treatment with metformin, but there was no significance in terms of local or regional progression-free survival (LFFS; regional failure-free survival, RFFS), or overall survival (OS).

Tumors of gynecological origin
In the case of gynecological tumors, the group of Ko et al. found that there were significant differences in recurrence free survival (RFS, p = 0.02), presenting almost twice the risk of having a recurrence in those patients Non-metformin users had 1.7 times worse RFS (95 % CI: 1.3–2.6, p = 0.01). Regarding OS, they also found statistical significance in favor of patients on metformin treatment (p = 0.003), the risk of death being twice as high in non-metformin users were (95 % CI: 1.3–3.2, p = 0.003).
Han et al. conducted a phase II study to assess the possibility of using metformin to reduce the level of tumor hypoxia and thus improve the response to radiotherapy in cases of cervical cancer. They analyzed the presence of cellular hypoxia by positron emission tomography (PET) imaging with hypoxia tracer fluoroazomycin arabinoside (FAZA) and how it evolved after one week of treatment with metformin. The primary endpoint was a change in fractional hypoxic volume (FHV) between FAZA-PET scans. They concluded that the use of metformin decreases tumor hypoxia, but did not detect a significant impact on survival.

Non-small cell lung cancer
Regarding lung cancer, the studies focused on non-small cell lung cancer show variability among the published studies. Ahmed et al. did not find statistically significant differences for any of the parameters analyzed. However, Wink et al. did find statistically significant differences for progression-free survival (PFS, p = 0.01), DMFS (p = 0.01), although not for locoregional failure. −free survival, LRRFS) or OS.
On the other hand, Tsakiridis et al. conducted a randomized clinical trial. They analyzed in 54 patients with locally advanced non-small cell lung cancer the use of metformin together with chemoradiotherapy. Their results were worse for patients receiving metformin in terms of oncologic outcome (locoregional disease progression, distant metastases, death, or withdrawal) and toxicity compared to the control arm without metformin. Thus, they concluded that they did not recommend the use of metformin in conjunction with chemoradiotherapy in patients with locally advanced non-small cell lung cancer in patients who do not have diabetes.
That is, neither of these results found an impact of the use of metformin on clinical outcomes in patients with non-small cell lung cancer.

Gastrointestinal cancers
In rectal cancer, there are several groups that analyze the impact of metformin on the oncological outcome. The group led by Skinner et al. obtained significant differences for OS (p = 0.008), as well as for pathologic complete response (pCR, p = 0.03) in the surgical specimen after neoadjuvant treatment with chemotherapy and radiotherapy, and in disease-free survival (DFS, p = 0.013), in favor of those patients receiving treatment with metformin. In contrast, Oh et al. found no statistically significant differences in survival or locoregional control. Regarding the pathological response after neoadjuvant treatment, this group did not find significant differences, but it did for the improvement of the tumor grade (tumor regression grades, TRG, p = 0.029).
Another tumor of the digestive area included is esophageal cancer. Of the three included studies focused on this type of tumor, only the group of Van de Voorde L et al. found statistically significant differences for OS (p = 0.012). On the other hand, for pCR it was statistically significant only in the study by Skinner et al., as this same author had defined for esophageal cancer, in favor of patients receiving treatment with metformin versus diabetic patients without metformin (p = 0.01) and compared to non-diabetic patients (p = 0.05). Regarding the DFS, this group did not find statistical power overall, but in a sub-analysis they observed that those patients who achieved pCR did achieve statistical significance for DFS (p = 0.01) compared to those who did not achieve pCR. On the other hand, Skinner et al. also found statistical significance in relation to locoregional control (loco-regional control, LRC) in favor of diabetic patients treated with metformin compared to non-diabetic patients (p = 0.05) and compared to to diabetics who are not being treated with metformin (p = 0.03).
In the case of hepatocellular carcinoma, Jang et al. found that progression-free survival (PFS) and OS were statistically significantly better in those patients receiving treatment with metformin.

Prostate cancer
Regarding the prostate, all the studies found some statistically significant finding for one of the endpoints. It should be noted that all patients were receiving concomitant treatment with androgen deprivation therapy. Zannella et al. found that patients on metformin treatment had a lower biochemical relapse-free rate than those who were not on metformin treatment (biochemical relapse-free rate, bRFR, p = 0.034). Spratt et al. observed statistically significant differences compared to diabetic patients without treatment with metformin in terms of cancer-specific survival (prostate cancer–specific mortality, PCSM) (p = 0.008), specific PSA (prostate specific antigen) recurrence-free survival (prostate-specific antigen-recurrence-free survival, PSA-RFS) (p = 0.004) and distant metastases-free survival (DMFS) (p < 0.001), but this was not the case against non-patients. diabetics for these two endpoints (p = 0.09, p = 0.07 and p = 0.10, respectively). Overall, OS at 10 years was statistically significant in favor of diabetic patients treated with metformin, but by groups, the statistical difference was only demonstrated compared to diabetic patients without metformin (p = 0.001), but not compared to non-diabetics (p = 0.14).
Another work on prostate cancer is that of Taira et al.. He observed that cancer-specific survival was significantly higher for diabetic patients treated with metformin compared to non-diabetics (p < 0.001), but not compared to non-diabetics. There were no significant differences regarding biochemical progression-free survival (bPFS). Regarding OS, globally significant differences were found both in the univariate and multivariate analysis (p < 0.001, p = 0.002), although by groups there was statistical significance in favor of treatment with metformin compared to non-diabetics.
Zaorsky et al. also published a study on prostate cancer. They observed that OS at 5 years was significantly worse for those diabetic patients without specific treatment or with an antidiabetic drug other than metformin (p = 0.002 and p = 0.03, respectively). Freedom from biochemical failure (FFBF) and survival or cause specific mortality (CSS) were significantly worse in the group of diabetic patients without specific treatment (p < 0.0001, p = 0.01, respectively). The results of Ranasinghe et al. in prostate cancer found no statistical significance or biochemical recurrence (biochemical failure, BF), metastasis or OS. It only found significant differences in death due to specific causes due to prostate cancer in patients who, in addition to RT, received antiandrogenic treatment (p = 0.045).

Discussion
Metformin is the most widely used antidiabetic in the world, considered the first therapeutic step drug for the treatment of DM2 due to its efficacy and safety profile. Beyond its value as a hypoglycemic agent, the use of metformin has been extended to the treatment of other diseases, including cancer, both in its prevention as well as its sensitizing effect to the different oncological treatments, both systemic and radiotherapy, and whose effect is currently under study.
Regarding RT, it is well known that tumor cells are less sensitive to ionizing radiation in hypoxic states. In this context, it has been shown at a preclinical level that the combined treatment of metformin with RT reduces oxygen consumption and increases ROS in the cell, thus reducing hypoxia, metformin acting as a sensitizer against ionizing radiation. Therefore, metformin, which is a drug with a high safety profile, could be essential in the future in the treatment of different types of solid tumors.
When carrying out an approach on this subject and the different studies carried out in this field, the main problem that can be found is the great heterogeneity of the studies. As there are no clinical trials that evaluate the efficacy of metformin and the different tumors are very different from each other, the validation of the use of metformin becomes more complex. The first thing that is observed is the great need for future clinical trials in the evaluation of the effect of metformin as a radiosensitizer in solid tumors. In this systematic review, the different solid tumors are of very different origins, which makes the results more interesting from the global point of view. Therefore, this study aims to bring metformin closer to its use in cancer patients.
Among the different tumors, the one that stands out the most in the analysis of the results are prostate cancers. In all the studies some of the objectives studied have been favorable for the use of metformin. It is likely that the great importance that radiotherapy plays in the treatment of prostate cancer is the reason why metformin has better results in this tumor. Other tumors such as rectal cancer or gynecological tumors combine systemic treatments (platinum or fluoropyrimidines) with radiotherapy, so the effect of metformin may be diminished. In several of the studies in the prostate, it is observed how the results with metformin not only improve in terms of response or appearance of recurrences (locoregional or metastatic), but also in OS. This objective, not always analyzed in most studies, makes it possible to show how metformin affects the results in prostate cancer and possibly in other tumors if clinical trials are carried out to analyze this data. In addition, the fact that OS is better with metformin in a type of tumor with such a high survival rate such as the prostate that requires longer follow-ups makes the existence of other studies in solid tumors more imperative.
Contrary to prostate cancer, there is another tumor where survival is significantly shorter such as hepatocellular carcinoma, where the results are also very favorable for the use of metformin as a radiosensitizer. Although only one study has been performed in this field (Jang et al), the results are very promising both in terms of PFS and OS. In older patients, capecitabine was used together with metformin, so the radiosensitizing effect of this drug is even more evident. The other tumors where the use of metformin is also consistently favorable is in cancers of gynecological origin. The only study (Ko et al) in this field shows very favorable results both in terms of OS and RFS. In addition, in these tumors the use of platinum is consistent in routine clinical practice, so that tumors of prostate, hepatic and gynecological origin show a clear benefit with the use of radiotherapy combined with metformin in the different studies in the literature.
The rest of the studies show contradictory results regarding the benefit of metformin together with radiotherapy. Where there seems to be a greater benefit is in cancers of esophageal origin, where the results seem generally favorable, although the two studies carried out have very different objectives. In the study by Van der Voorde et al., there is an improvement in OS and in the study by Skinner et al., there is an improvement in terms of pCR. Although no clear conclusions can be drawn from these studies due to the heterogeneity in the design and results of the studies, there is a very promising data such as the improvement in the pCR results. Currently, the different studies on localized and locally advanced esophageal cancer, especially in the field of immunotherapy, look for favorable results in terms of pCR because this point is known to increase PFS and OS in these tumors. Therefore, the fact that metformin can increase pCR in esophageal tumors or in other tumors such as those of rectal origin, makes it essential to study this phenomenon in order to characterize it in the future.
In the rest of the studies carried out and analyzed in this systematic review (rectal, lung and head and neck cancer) the results are too contradictory to be able to draw conclusions. In these tumors, although there are promising data such as the study by Skinner et al., where OS and pCR are favorable for the use of metformin, there are others such as the one carried out by Oh et al. where no improvement was observed with the use of metformin. Therefore, no exact conclusions can be drawn, so the use of metformin in these tumors will require further evaluation in preclinical and clinical studies to be able to make inferences about its use.
A limitation of this review is the retrospective nature in most studies, which may introduce selection bias regarding selection criteria, the patients considered to be fit for oncology treatment, or type of oncology treatment.

Conclusions
Metformin is a drug with a known and favorable safety profile that in preclinical studies has shown a benefit in its use as a radiosensitizer in the treatment of solid tumors. In studies in real-life patients, metformin has shown benefit in tumors of prostate and gynecological origin, however, there are currently no clinical trials that have evaluated the benefit of metformin in combination with radiotherapy in solid tumors. The promising results of the use of this drug in different cancer treatment scenarios make it necessary to carry out future clinical trials that evaluate metformin in combination with radiotherapy for the improvement of the results of cancer treatments in terms of OS, PFS, and pCR.
