Liposomes from red betel (Piper crocatum) extract: A new approach in the delivery of anti-inflammatory drugs
Background:
Piper crocatum extract contains bioactive compounds, such as flavonoids and phenolics, that exhibit anti-inflammatory activity by inhibiting cyclooxygenase-2, suppressing nuclear factor kappa B, and modulating pro-inflammatory cytokines such as tumor necrosis factor-alpha and interleukin-6. Liposomal encapsulation enhances these compounds’ stability, bioavailability, and targeted delivery, making it a promising strategy for anti-inflammatory drug development.
Aim:
This review evaluates the potential of P. crocatum liposomes as drug carriers for inflammatory disorders and identifies key research gaps.
Methods:
A systematic literature review was conducted using the Scopus database, following the PRISMA 2020 protocol and assisted by Parsif.ai for screening and data extraction.
Results:
Piper crocatum liposomes improved the physicochemical stability and therapeutic activity of encapsulated compounds, exhibiting enhanced antibacterial and anti-inflammatory effects. Their ability to modulate inflammation at the molecular level and deliver agents to specific sites demonstrates strong potential for therapeutic and preservative applications.
Conclusion:
Piper crocatum liposomal formulations offer a stable and effective delivery system for inflammation-related therapies, supporting further investigation into their clinical and pharmaceutical applications.

Introduction
Liposomes are widely recognized as drug delivery systems that can encapsulate both hydrophilic and lipophilic compounds. Their ability to protect bioactive constituents, enhance bioavailability, prolong systemic circulation, and facilitate targeted drug delivery has made them valuable tools for improving therapeutic outcomes. These vesicular systems are particularly beneficial when applied to plant-derived bioactive compounds, which often suffer from poor solubility and stability despite having significant pharmacological potential. Numerous medicinal plant extracts, such as Curcuma longa, Camellia sinensis, and Nigella sativa, have been successfully encapsulated in liposomes to enhance their anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, and antioxidant activities.
Among these, species from the Piperaceae family have gained attention for liposomal applications, primarily due to their rich phytochemical content. Several Piper species, including Piper nigrum L., Piper betle L., and Piper albi Linn, have shown promise for nanocarrier-based drug delivery. These species are commonly recognized for their piperine content, which has been encapsulated in liposomes to improve its solubility and antibacterial and anticancer potency. Studies have demonstrated enhanced cytotoxicity of piperine-loaded liposomes against colorectal cancer cells and improved stability and bioavailability of antibacterial and antioxidant compounds from P. betle. Piper albi extract was successfully encapsulated in liposomes to enhance its availability. reported improved encapsulation efficiency and slow release using thin-film hydration. These findings confirm the utility of liposomal encapsulation for plant-based therapeutics in enhancing pharmacological performance.
In contrast, Piper crocatum, commonly known as red betel), a species traditionally used in Southeast Asia medicine, remains underexplored in nano-based drug delivery systems. This species contains diverse bioactive compounds, such as flavonoids, tannins, polyphenols, and essential oils, which exhibit significant antibacterial, anti-inflammatory, and antioxidant activities. However, to date, no systematic effort has been made to assess its potential for liposomal encapsulation.
This review addresses this critical knowledge gap by exploring the potential of P. crocatum in liposomal drug delivery. Unlike previously studied Piper species that typically focus on single therapeutic effects or well-known compounds like piperine, P. crocatum offers a more complex phytochemical profile with synergistic bioactivities. Encapsulation of its extract in liposomes could improve compound stability, enhance targeted delivery, and minimize systemic side effects, particularly for managing inflammation associated with infectious diseases such as Salmonella typhi. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first review to systematically investigate the suitability of P. crocatum for liposomal encapsulation. This study lays the groundwork for future research into novel plant-based nanotherapeutics aimed at infectious and inflammatory conditions by synthesizing available pharmacological evidence and evaluating formulation strategies.
Liposomes are nanoscale spherical vesicles composed of one or more phospholipid bilayers surrounding an aqueous core. Their amphiphilic structure allows them to encapsulate both hydrophilic and hydrophobic molecules, such as plant-derived flavonoids and polyphenols, improving their solubility, bioavailability, and pharmacokinetics. These systems offer an effective vehicle to stabilize the bioactive compounds of P. crocatum, shield them from premature degradation, and facilitate controlled release at inflammation sites.
Liposomal encapsulation also plays a critical biochemical role in enhancing the stability of flavonoids and polyphenols by protecting them from enzymatic degradation, oxidative stress, and pH-dependent hydrolysis that commonly occurs in the gastrointestinal tract or bloodstream. Furthermore, liposomes improve cellular uptake by fusing with the phospholipid membranes of host cells, facilitating endocytosis or passive diffusion of the encapsulated compounds.
Flavonoids and phenolics from P. crocatum have been shown to modulate inflammatory responses at the molecular level. Specifically, they downregulate proinflammatory cytokines such as tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α) and interleukin-6 (IL-6), both of which are key mediators in acute and chronic inflammation. These bioactives also inhibit the activation of nuclear factor-kappa B (NF-κB), a central transcription factor involved in cytokine expression, leukocyte recruitment, and inflammatory enzyme production. The risk of side effects with anti-inflammatory drugs, such as gastrointestinal or cardiovascular disorders, will be reduced using P. crocatum extracts in medicine. Therefore, P. crocatum represents a better approach to treating inflammation and could improve patients’ quality of life by reducing side effects while conserving therapeutic benefits.
In addition, P. crocatum compounds upregulate endogenous antioxidant enzymes, such as superoxide dismutase (SOD) and catalase (CAT), helping to mitigate oxidative stress, which is closely linked to inflammatory pathway amplification. By suppressing reactive oxygen species (ROS) and inflammatory signaling cascades, P. crocatum supports tissue protection and inflammation resolution. The combination of these molecular actions, which are enhanced by liposomal encapsulation, provides a synergistic anti-inflammatory strategy, including modulation of inflammatory signaling, suppression of cytokine production (TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6), activation of antioxidant defenses (SOD, CAT), and enhanced bioavailability and cellular uptake of active phytochemicals. Moreover, liposomal delivery helps ensure site-specific release of the therapeutic payload, especially in conditions such as Salmonella typhi-induced intestinal inflammation, where targeted therapy reduces systemic side effects and microbial resistance. Despite these advantages, optimization of liposome composition (lipid type and cholesterol content), drug-loading efficiency, and long-term stability remain challenges. Further research is warranted to refine delivery parameters and validate efficacy in preclinical models and clinical settings (; Fulton and Najahi-Missaoui, 2023).
Liposomes show some promise in treating S. typhi infections and reducing inflammation, or even in developing medications. Using liposomes as a delivery vehicle also provides an opportunity for increased accuracy and more control over the delivery of medicine to the site of infection. This would minimize the risk of side effects and antibiotic resistance and increase its efficacy against S. typhi. Liposomes deliver the inflammatory medications to inflammatory regions during anti-inflammatory therapy, thereby enhancing treatment success with reduced systemic side effects. This skill is important in managing chronic inflammation and offers a more effective alternative to traditional treatments. Furthermore, liposomes make drug development easier by finding solutions to some challenges, including low drug bioavailability, stability, and solubility. Liposomal entrapment of therapeutic molecules with targeted site selection is crucial in enhancing drug development for effective and personalized treatment options. This could yield a new generation of treatments against various diseases, ranging from bacterial infections to inflammatory disorders.
Although using liposomes has advantages, formulating optimum liposomes, undergoing clinical trials, and translating them to the real world of medicine delivery is difficult. Understanding how drugs act in physiological settings and how liposomes interact with the medications encapsulated in them would improve the formulation process. Lipid degradation and premature drug release require research to develop stable and efficient liposomes. Clinical studies require liposomes to demonstrate efficacy and safety across patient populations, leading to complex and time-consuming trials that require substantial resources (; Fulton and Najahi-Missaoui, 2023). Despite the benefits of using liposomes in medicine, obstacles such as production scalability, costs, and acceptance by patients and the medical community hinder their adoption. Technological progress and continuous research efforts could enhance liposome design. As noted by, and, this could result in integration into practice, ultimately improving treatment results and enhancing the quality of life for patients.

Materials and Methods

Protocol and reporting standards
This study employed a systematic literature review approach in accordance with the PRISMA 2020 guidelines. Although the protocol was not registered with PROSPERO due to the non-clinical scope and exploratory nature of the study, all methodological steps were carefully structured to ensure transparency, reproducibility, and rigor. The SLR process consisted of three essential phases: (1) identification of literature sources, (2) literature quality assessment, and (3) data extraction and synthesis. The Parsif.ai platform was used to ensure the efficacy and consistency of the PRISMA protocol.

Selection of the database
Scopus was selected as the sole literature source due to its extensive coverage of peer-reviewed literature, especially in pharmaceutical sciences, drug delivery, and medicinal plant research. Its comprehensive indexing and advanced search features ensured the capture of all relevant publications related to P. crocatum liposomes, anti-inflammatory activity, and drug delivery systems. Its wide coverage and advanced filtering capabilities are sufficient to meet the scope of this review.

Search strategy
A literature search was conducted using keyword combinations relevant to the topic of interest. The search terms were “liposome “, “Piper crocatum “, and “red betel.” The search was limited to articles published between 2014 and 2024. All search results were managed and filtered using the Parsif.ai platform, which supports the structured implementation of the PRISMA protocol (Fig. 1).
Systematic schematic literature review.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria
The study used peer-reviewed articles published in English that focused on liposomal formulations of botanical extracts and reported antibacterial, anti-inflammatory, or antioxidant properties. The exclusion criteria include non-original articles (reviews, abstracts, and editorials), studies using only synthetic compounds, and insufficient pharmacological or methodological detail.

Screening
The search initially yielded 4,699 records from the Scopus database. After removing 3,056 irrelevant records based on title and abstract screening, eligibility was assessed for 1,643 full-text articles. An additional 1,543 articles were excluded for not meeting the inclusion criteria, leaving 110 articles for quality assessment. After further exclusion based on methodological rigor and relevance, 60 articles were included in the final analysis. The PRISMA flow diagram illustrates the study selection process (Fig. 2).
Scopus article identification and selection scheme 2013–2015.

Quality assessment
Two independent reviewers screened all articles at both the title/abstract and full-text stages to ensure consistency and minimize bias in the selection process. Discrepancies in study inclusion were resolved through discussion and consensus. Although Cohen’s kappa coefficient was not calculated, the dual-review process was designed to align with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses recommendations for methodological transparency and to minimize selection bias.

Data extraction and synthesis
Data from each included article were extracted into a standardized form, including the source and type of plant extract, liposome formulation technique (thin-film hydration, ethanol injection), biochemical characterization (encapsulation efficiency, stability, and bioactivity), and pharmacological outcomes (anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial). Findings were synthesized to identify trends, knowledge gaps, and future research directions on liposomal P. crocatum applications.
Although this study did not generate new experimental data, we synthesized findings from prior in vitro and in vivo studies that demonstrated the ability of P. crocatum to suppress pro-inflammatory cytokinesTNF-α, IL-6, inhibit cyclooxygenase-2 and NF-κB activation, increase antioxidant enzyme activity (SOD, CAT), and improve therapeutic outcomes in infected or inflamed tissues when delivered via liposomes. This approach ensures a comprehensive and evidence-based analysis of the potential of P. crocatum in anti-inflammatory therapy through liposomal delivery.

Ethical approval
Not needed for this study.

Discussion
This study provides a comprehensive synthesis of current research on the use of P. crocatum (red betel) in liposomal drug delivery systems, with a focus on antibacterial and anti-inflammatory applications. The results indicate a growing body of literature exploring plant-based liposomes, though P. crocatum remains significantly underexplored compared to other Piperaceae species such as P. nigrum and P. betle.
Our bibliometric analysis shows a marked increase in liposome-related publications over the past decade, with recent research shifting toward applied biomedical uses such as wound healing, biofilm inhibition, and targeted drug delivery. However, liposomal formulations involving P. crocatum are sparse, highlighting a major research gap in the development of multifunctional plant-based therapeutics.
Encapsulation of P. crocatum extract in liposomes is a promising strategy for enhancing the delivery and efficacy of its bioactive compounds. The extract is rich in flavonoids, tannins, and phenolic compounds that are known to exhibit antibacterial and anti-inflammatory effects. The bioactive compounds in P. crocatum exert antibacterial effects through multiple biochemical mechanisms. These mechanisms include disruption of bacterial membrane integrity, leading to leakage of cytoplasmic contents, generation of ROS that induce oxidative stress and cellular apoptosis, and inhibition of quorum-sensing pathways, thereby reducing virulence factor expression and biofilm formation. Encapsulation of these compounds in liposomes enhances their permeability across bacterial membranes and may increase intracellular accumulation.
Liposomes provide a suitable vehicle for these phytochemicals because of their biocompatibility and ability to enhance the solubility and stability of hydrophobic compounds. Liposome lipid composition plays an important role in determining their physicochemical properties and efficacy as a delivery system. Lipid composition critically influences liposomal behavior. The cholesterol/phospholipid ratio affects membrane fluidity. Higher cholesterol reduces fluidity, resulting in more stable vesicles. It also affects drug release kinetics. Tighter packing can delay release, promoting sustained delivery. Furthermore, it affects encapsulation efficiency because the presence of cholesterol stabilizes the bilayer and prevents premature leakage. Simultaneously, the encapsulation efficiency of bioactive compounds may vary based on the manufacturing method. Improved encapsulation efficiency and sustained release profiles can lead to better therapeutic outcomes, particularly for localized infections and inflammatory conditions.
The potential applications extend to the treatment of diseases involving bacterial infections and chronic inflammation, such as S. typhi infection, where inflammation and microbial proliferation co-exist. Liposomal delivery could help reduce systemic side effects while ensuring targeted therapeutic action. The role of liposomes in penetrating bacterial biofilms adds another layer of relevance, as biofilm-associated infections are notoriously resistant to conventional therapies. Despite their promise, the current literature provides limited data on standardized liposome preparation methods, dose-response relationships, pharmacokinetics, and in vivo efficacy of P. crocatum-based formulations. These gaps must be addressed to translate the experimental findings into clinical or industrial applications.
Although this review highlights several comparative aspects across different liposomal systems, quantitative comparisons of parameters such as encapsulation efficiency, stability, and bioavailability between P. crocatum and other liposomal plant extracts remain limited. This gap is due to the scarcity of available standardized data and direct comparative studies. Future research should focus on standardizing liposome formulation methods for P. crocatum extract, conducting in vitro and in vivo studies to evaluate anti-inflammatory and antimicrobial efficacy, assessing toxicity, stability, and controlled-release behavior, and exploring synergistic effects with conventional antibiotics or anti-inflammatory agents. This review underscores the potential of P. crocatum as a novel component in nanopharmaceutical research by identifying these opportunities. It also provides a foundation for the rational design of plant-based liposomal therapies that align with the ongoing shift toward natural, targeted, and multifunctional treatment strategies.

Conclusion
Encapsulation of P. crocatum (red betel) extract in liposomal formulations is a promising strategy for enhancing its antibacterial and anti-inflammatory potential. Liposomes improve the stability, bioavailability, and targeted delivery of bioactive compounds, making them suitable for the management of infectious and inflammatory diseases. This approach also holds potential in food preservation systems because of its natural antimicrobial activity. Future research should focus on evaluating the safety profiles, ability to penetrate bacterial biofilms, effects on normal microbiota, and long-term anti-inflammatory efficacy of P. crocatum liposomes as functional therapeutics.
