Comparative evaluation of calcium-enriched mixture and mineral trioxide aggregate in vital pulp therapy of molars: A systematic review
Vital pulp therapy (VPT) promotes pulp healing and prevents bacterial contamination. Mineral trioxide aggregate (MTA) improves outcomes but has drawbacks such as difficult handling, long setting time, discoloration, and cost. Calcium enriched mixture (CEM) cement, introduced as an alternative, offers easier handling, faster setting, and comparable biological properties. This systematic review evaluated the effectiveness of CEM versus MTA in VPT of primary and permanent molars. The review protocol was registered in the PROSPERO database (Registration Number: CRD420251030455). A systematic search of PubMed/MEDLINE, Scopus, Web of Science, and Cochrane Library was performed for randomized controlled trials (RCTs) published between January 2005 and December 2024. Eligible studies compared CEM with MTA or calcium hydroxide in VPT. Of 522 studies screened, 8 RCTs met the inclusion criteria. Risk of bias (RoB) was assessed using the Cochrane RoB 2.0 tool. Clinical outcomes were evaluated by postoperative pain (Visual Analog Scale and Numeric Rating Scale), and radiographic outcomes by periapical index. Both CEM and MTA significantly reduced postoperative pain and showed high success rates in direct pulp capping, pulpotomy, and pulpectomy. No significant difference was observed between the two biomaterials. CEM and MTA both demonstrated favorable results in preserving pulp vitality and achieving long term success. While MTA remains widely used due to its proven biocompatibility, CEM offers comparable efficacy with advantages such as easier handling, faster setting, and reduced discoloration. Further long term multicenter RCTs are needed to establish standardized protocols.

INTRODUCTION
Dental caries is a complex, multifactorial disease characterized by the demineralization of enamel, dentin, and cementum in both primary and permanent teeth. The ultimate goal of managing carious lesions is to restore the dentin–pulp complex both structurally and functionally. In contemporary dental practice, various treatment options are available for managing pulpitis in teeth with extensive caries, depending on the remaining dentin thickness. The clinician may opt for direct or indirect pulp capping or perform pulpotomy when the coronal portion of the pulp is involved.
The decision to perform vital pulp therapy (VPT) is influenced by multiple factors, including patient age, medical history, stage of root development, and tooth-related factors such as whether the tooth is primary or permanent, the extent of carious exposure, salivary contamination, previous restorations, or periodontal involvement. VPT has therefore emerged as a reliable treatment modality to preserve the vitality of compromised pulp tissue.
VPT is an umbrella term that includes indirect pulp capping, direct pulp capping, pulpotomy, and apexogenesis, all of which aim to maintain pulp health by enhancing root development and apical closure. Preservation of pulp vitality allows continued dentin deposition and the formation of secondary or reparative dentin, thereby providing a biological seal against microbial infiltration.
For decades, calcium hydroxide (CH) was considered the gold standard for pulp capping. However, its drawbacks – including tunnel defect formation, poor sealing ability, and high solubility – have led to inconsistent long-term outcomes. Consequently, calcium silicate-based cements such as mineral trioxide aggregate (MTA) and calcium-enriched mixture (CEM) have gained prominence in modern endodontics, owing to their superior sealing ability, mechanical strength, and predictable hard tissue formation.
Ford et al. in 1999, first described the use of MTA in VPT. MTA is composed of Portland cement, bismuth oxide, and gypsum, typically in the proportions of 75%, 20%, and 5%, respectively. Portland cement itself contains tricalcium silicate, dicalcium silicate, tricalcium aluminate, and tetracalcium aluminoferrite. On hydration, MTA releases CH, raising the pH to approximately 12.5, which provides antibacterial activity, stimulates pulp cells, and promotes reparative dentin formation. In addition, it facilitates precipitation of a hydroxyapatite-like mineralized layer that chemically bonds to the tooth structure. Despite these advantages, MTA is associated with drawbacks such as difficult handling, potential discoloration, and high cost.
CEM cement is a novel biomaterial that has shown comparable effectiveness to MTA and superior performance to CH in preserving pulp vitality and reducing inflammation in VPT. CEM promotes hard tissue formation through hydroxyapatite deposition and facilitates cementogenesis. Its smaller particle size allows deeper penetration into dentinal tubules, thereby enhancing its sealing ability. Clinical studies also report reduced pulpal inflammation with CEM compared to other pulp-capping agents.
Therefore, this systematic review aims to evaluate and compare the clinical performance of MTA, CEM, and CH as pulp-capping agents. Outcomes assessed include postoperative pain, pulp vitality, sinus tract formation, and radiographic findings such as periapical index (PAI), integrity of lamina dura, apical closure, and pulp canal obliteration. The results of this review are expected to aid clinicians in material selection and highlight areas requiring further research.

MATERIALS AND METHODS
This systematic review has been registered in the PROSPERO database (Registration Number: CRD420251030455). The review was conducted following the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) 2019 guidelines.

Research question
Using the Population, Intervention, Comparison, Outcome framework, the research question was formulated as follows:
“In patients with deep carious lesions (P), does CEM (I) offer superior clinical efficiency and radiographic outcomes (O) compared to MTA (C) in VPT of primary and permanent teeth?”

Eligibility criteria
Type of studies: Clinical trials involving patients with deep carious lesions treated with CEM and compared with MTA or other restorative materials
Population (P): Patients aged 5–70 years with deep caries
The age range of 5–70 years was selected because this reflects the population included in existing randomized controlled trials (RCTs) evaluating VPT
Intervention (I): CEM in VPT
Comparison (C): MTA in VPT
Outcomes (O): Clinical outcomes, including pain, pulp vitality, and sinus tract formation; radiographic outcomes, including PAI and lamina dura during follow-up.

Exclusion criteria
Studies were excluded if they were conducted on artificial teeth or animals, were case–control studies, literature reviews, systematic reviews, case reports, clinical guidelines, letters to the editor, opinion articles, book chapters, conference abstracts, or meetings. Duplicate publications were also excluded from the study.

Search strategy
A systematic search was conducted using Boolean operators “AND” and “OR” in PubMed and similar strategies in other databases. Search terms included: “Dental caries,” “Vital pulp therapy,” “MTA cement,” and “Calcium-Enriched Mixture.”

Study selection
All studies were identified through electronic and manual searches [Figure 1]. Two independent reviewers screened titles and abstracts for relevance, removed duplicates, and excluded studies that did not meet eligibility criteria. Full-text articles were retrieved when title and abstract information were insufficient for eligibility assessment.
PRIMSA flow chart

Data extraction
Data extraction was performed independently by two reviewers using a standardized, predesigned data extraction form developed before the commencement of the review to ensure uniformity in the extraction process. This form included structured fields for collecting all relevant information from each eligible study, such as bibliographic details (author, year, country), study design, sample size, participant characteristics, preoperative pulp diagnosis, details of the intervention and comparator materials, clinical and radiographic outcome measures, follow-up intervals, statistical methods, and risk-of-bias domains (ROB 2.0). Using this uniform template enabled both reviewers to extract data independently consistently and systematically, thereby minimizing subjective interpretation, reducing extraction errors, and enhancing the overall reliability and reproducibility of the synthesized findings.

Data collection method
This systematic review synthesized data from all relevant studies evaluating the effects of CEM and MTA in VPT of molars. A customized data extraction form were developed to standardize the collection process. The clinical and radiographic outcomes following the use of CEM and MTA in VPT were compared across studies.

Data items
For all selected studies meeting the inclusion criteria, data extraction and validity assessment were performed by reviewing the materials, methods, and results sections. Extracted information included.
Year of publication, author(s), and country of origin
Study population and sample size
Inclusion and exclusion criteria
Details of the intervention and control groups
Reported outcomes and assessment methods.

Risk of bias assessment
The methodological quality of the included trials was assessed using the Revised Cochrane RoB Tool for Randomized Trials (ROB 2.0). Two reviewers independently evaluated five domains relevant to individually randomized trials. Each domain was judged as: Low risk – all criteria within the domain were adequately met. Some concerns – at least one criterion raised concern but did not indicate high risk. High risk – at least one criterion indicated high risk.
A standardized template, tailored to the design of randomized trials, was used to assess each domain. An overall RoB for each study was then determined by considering the judgments across all domains, along with the potential direction of bias. Discrepancies between reviewers were resolved through discussion, with consultation of a third reviewer when necessary.

Randomization protocol
All included studies were RCTs, with random allocation sequences generated using computerized random number generators or dedicated randomization software. Allocation concealment was explicitly described in four studies using sealed opaque envelopes or third-party allocation. Four studies did not clearly report their randomization process and were thus categorized as “some concerns” in the randomization bias domain. None of the studies reported baseline imbalances, and most ensured equal distribution of potential confounding variables through proper sequence generation and allocation methods.

RESULTS

Search and study selection
The study selection process is illustrated in the PRISMA flow diagram. The initial database search identified 522 records. After removing 77 duplicates, 455 records were screened based on titles and abstracts. Of these, 200 records were excluded for not meeting the inclusion criteria: 85 were not original studies, 79 were irrelevant, and 36 had missing parameters. The remaining 255 full-text articles were assessed for eligibility, resulting in the inclusion of 8 studies that met the predetermined criteria for this systematic review.

Primary outcomes assessed clinical and radiographic success, whereas secondary outcomes included pain levels, pulp vitality, bleeding control time, and analgesic use. Follow-up duration varied from 7 days to 24 months. Most of the studies reported high retention rates with minimal participant dropout. Analyses employed statistical tests such as ANOVA, Chi-square, and Cohen’s kappa, and strategies for missing data included worst-case assumptions and intention-to-treat principles. Overall, CEM and MTA demonstrated similar high success rates, generally outperforming CH. No significant differences were observed in postoperative pain between CEM and MTA.
We reviewed and summarized the conflict of interest declarations and funding statements reported by the included trials. None of the studies indicated commercial sponsorship that could directly influence treatment outcomes, although incomplete reporting in some trials means that funding-related bias cannot be entirely excluded from the study.

Risk of bias
The RoB in the included RCTs was assessed using the Cochrane Collaboration RoB tool and analyzed with RevMan software [Table 3]. Two independent reviewers evaluated each study, resolving discrepancies through discussion and consultation with a third reviewer when necessary. The assessment covered five domains: selection bias (random sequence generation and allocation concealment), performance bias (blinding of participants and personnel), detection bias (blinding of outcome assessors), attrition bias (incomplete outcome data), and reporting bias (selective reporting). Each domain was graded as low risk, some concerns, or high risk. Across the 40 domains evaluated in the included studies, 38% were rated as low risk, 62% as moderate risk, and none as high risk. Overall, the included trials demonstrated a generally low-to-moderate RoB, reflecting acceptable methodological quality for this systematic review.

Outcome measured

Clinical parameters
Pulp vitality was assessed using electric pulp testing or cold tests. Parameswaran et al. reported that at 18 months, 0% of MTA-treated teeth and 2% of Dycal-treated teeth showed negative vitality, indicating higher pulp survival with MTA and CEM. Asgary et al. observed that after 2 years, 3.6% of MTA and 4.3% of CEM pulpotomies responded to cold tests, whereas responses to electric pulp testing were 19.6% and 44.7%, respectively. Ramteke et al. found that all treated teeth responded positively at 6 months.
Postoperative pain was generally low across all materials, with transient differences at early follow-ups. Parameswaran et al. noted severe pain in 10.5% of Dycal participants versus 2% in MTA at 1 month, which decreased to 0% in all groups by 12–18 months. Asgary and Eghbal and Shafie et al. (2017) reported similar trends, with pain decreasing over time irrespective of material. Sinus tract occurrence was minimal (0.6%–2%) across CEM and MTA groups. Root canal treatment and extractions were rare, with slightly higher incidences in Dycal-treated teeth.
Radiographic Parameters: Periapical outcomes, assessed through the PAI, showed higher success with CEM and MTA compared to Dycal (18-month success: 86.7%, 77.3%, and 57.9%, respectively). Apical closure was achieved in 78.9% of CEM and 81.5% of MTA-treated teeth at 12 months, with no significant differences. External resorption was rare (0.5%–0.6%), and internal resorption was not observed. Partial canal obliteration was noted in 20%–25% of cases at 12–24 months, whereas periodontal ligament widening was reported in only 2% of CEM-treated teeth at 2-year follow-up. Overall, CEM and MTA demonstrated comparable clinical and radiographic outcomes, generally outperforming CH.

DISCUSSION
The reviewed clinical trials consistently demonstrate that bioactive cements – particularly CEM and MTA – provide highly favorable clinical and radiographic outcomes when used in VPT in both children and adults. Clinical success criteria across studies included maintenance of tooth vitality, absence of spontaneous pain, no tenderness on percussion, absence of swelling or sinus tract, and no need for retreatment. Radiographic success was generally defined by the absence of periapical or furcal pathology, no internal or external resorption, and evidence of continued root development in immature teeth.
CEM cement demonstrated comparable, and in some cases slightly superior, clinical success rates compared to MTA in most RCTs. In the studies by Parameswaran et al. and Ramteke et al. CEM achieved an 86.7% success rate, while MTA achieved 77.3%. These differences were statistically significant when compared to CH but were not consistently significant between CEM and MTA. Other trials, including Nosrat et al., Asgary and Eghbal and Sharaan and Ali reported minimal need for endodontic intervention or extraction, indicating that pulpotomy and direct pulp capping with modern bioactive cements can be a reliable alternative to root canal therapy, even in teeth presenting with signs of irreversible pulpitis.
Radiographic assessment, particularly using PAI scoring, was pivotal for evaluating permanent teeth. Lower PAI scores indicated minimal to no periapical inflammation. In immature molars, Nosrat et al. reported that both MTA and CEM achieved over 73% apical closure, a key factor for the long-term success of VPT in young teeth. In mature teeth, Asgary et al. observed radiographic healing in 97.9% of CEM, 100% of MTA, and 98% of RCT-treated cases, demonstrating that pulpotomy can achieve radiographic outcomes comparable to conventional root canal therapy when performed under controlled protocols. Marwa Sharaan and Ali found partial canal obliteration in 25% of CEM and 20% of MTA-treated teeth at 12–24 months.
Both CEM and MTA promote dentinogenesis and pulp healing through the release of calcium and hydroxyl ions, creating an alkaline, antibacterial environment that stimulates differentiation of odontoblast-like cells. This process induces reparative dentin formation and deposition of hydroxyapatite, chemically bonding to the dentin. CEM’s smaller particle size and phosphorus content enhance tubular penetration and mineralization, whereas MTA’s bismuth oxide component contributes to its radiopacity. Both materials exhibit anti-inflammatory effects by reducing inflammatory cell infiltration, modulating cytokine release, and promoting angiogenesis within the pulp tissue. Their biocompatibility supports pulp cell proliferation and differentiation, and the combined effects of bioactivity, antibacterial properties, and tissue compatibility explain the high clinical and radiographic success rates observed in VPT. Notably, CEM may cause less tooth discoloration than MTA while maintaining comparable regenerative potential.
Postoperative pain, while not a primary endpoint in most trials, was similar for CEM and MTA. Asgary and Eghbal and Shafie (2010) reported rapid resolution of pain within 72 h and minimal analgesic requirements, an outcome particularly relevant in pediatric dentistry and emergency endodontics. Emerging materials such as nanohydroxyapatite (NHA) are also being investigated; Haghgoo et al. reported that CEM outperformed NHA in terms of hard tissue barrier formation and pulp inflammation scores, supporting its use in direct pulp capping of primary teeth.
Variations in RCT findings may be influenced by clinical factors such as isolation protocols and patients’ systemic health. Further large-scale, long-term clinical studies are needed to validate the effectiveness, reliability, and comparative performance of CEM. Researchers are also exploring bioactive additives or nanoparticles, such as zinc oxide or silver, to enhance antibacterial and regenerative properties.
It is noteworthy that dentin bridge formation was not assessed in most included trials, and only one study evaluated pulp canal obliteration radiographically. Traditional radiographs provide two-dimensional images of three-dimensional structures, limiting the evaluation of dentin bridge formation, canal calcification, and periapical healing.
Both CEM and MTA show high clinical and radiographic success in VPT due to their bioactive properties, including CH ion release, alkaline environment, and stimulation of odontoblast-like cells, promoting reparative dentin and hydroxyapatite formation. CEM offers advantages over MTA, such as better dentinal tubule penetration and reduced tooth discoloration, while maintaining similar regenerative potential. Their anti-inflammatory effects and biocompatibility further support long-term pulp vitality, making them reliable alternatives to CH, even in teeth with deep carious exposures.
Although the included trials provide meaningful evidence, several limitations should be acknowledged. Considerable heterogeneity existed among the studies in terms of sample size, age range, tooth type, diagnostic criteria, and follow-up duration. Only a few trials reported follow-up of ≥24 months, restricting long-term assessment of dentin bridge maturation and sustained pulp vitality. Publication bias could not be formally evaluated because fewer than ten studies met the inclusion criteria, and funnel plot-based analyses are unreliable with small numbers; therefore, the possibility of selective reporting cannot be ruled out. Variations in operator experience, anatomical complexity, and procedural protocols across studies may also have influenced outcomes. In addition, although conflict-of-interest and funding statements were reviewed, incomplete reporting in some trials means funding-related bias cannot be entirely excluded.
Variations in operator experience and procedural techniques may have influenced the reported outcomes across the included trials. Several studies involved multiple operators or did not specify the level of clinical expertise, which can introduce variability in case selection, execution of VPT procedures, and interpretation of clinical findings. In addition, anatomical differences among treated teeth and the lack of standardized operative protocols across studies may act as potential confounders. These factors should be considered when interpreting the overall findings of this review.
Future research should focus on multicenter randomized trials with standardized protocols, larger sample sizes, and advanced imaging techniques such as cone-beam computed tomography to assess dentin bridge formation, canal calcification. In addition, exploration of bioactive additives or nanoparticles may further enhance the regenerative and antibacterial properties of these materials, guiding optimized clinical application in both pediatric and adult populations.

CONCLUSION
The high success rates of both CEM and MTA in VPT can be attributed to their combined bioactive properties. Both materials promote hydroxyapatite formation, create an alkaline, antibacterial environment, and stimulate pulp cell proliferation and differentiation, supporting reparative dentin formation and long-term pulp vitality. Notably, CEM may offer an additional advantage over MTA by causing less tooth discoloration due to the absence of bismuth oxide, while maintaining comparable regenerative and clinical outcomes. Overall, current evidence supports the use of CEM and MTA as reliable alternatives to CH in VPT, demonstrating favourable clinical and radiographic outcomes with minimal complications.
