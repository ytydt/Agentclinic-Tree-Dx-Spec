Advances in magnetic resonance imaging for the evaluation of colorectal liver metastases in context of individualized precision medicine
Colorectal liver metastases (CRLM) represent a significant clinical challenge, as they are a leading cause of morbidity and mortality in patients with colorectal cancer (CRC). Early detection, accurate diagnosis, and precise treatment planning are crucial for improving patient outcomes. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) has emerged as a cornerstone in evaluating CRLM. This article provides a comprehensive review of recent innovations in MRI for CRLM diagnosis and treatment, with a particular focus on precision surgical models. Additionally, the application of artificial intelligence (AI) and radiomics is explored, highlighting their potential in automating lesion detection, evaluating treatment response, and predicting patient survival. The integration of these advanced imaging techniques and AI-based models holds promise for enhancing clinical decision-making, enabling personalized treatment strategies, and improving patient outcomes in CRLM. As these technologies continue to evolve, they could revolutionize the management of CRLM, offering non-invasive, accurate, and cost-effective solutions for early detection, monitoring, and prognosis prediction in CRC patients.

Introduction
Colorectal cancer (CRC) has become a major global health challenge due to its high incidence and mortality rates. CRC is the third most diagnosed cancer worldwide, with over 1.85 million new cases and approximately 850,000 deaths annually. Notably, CRC is prone to hepatic metastasis, occurring in up to 50% of patients with advanced disease. This propensity for liver metastasis stems from the unique biological features of colorectal tumors, such as the hematogenous spread through the portal venous system, which facilitates the seeding of malignant cells in the liver. As a result, colorectal liver metastases (CRLM) have emerged as a significant challenge in liver disease, alongside chronic liver diseases and primary liver cancer (HCC).
CRLM have traditionally been managed predominantly through systemic therapy. However, in recent years, the emergence of precision surgery has brought about significant changes in the treatment paradigm, precision surgery for CRLM includes optimal selection of both the patient and surgery, which refers to an individualized, targeted approach to surgery based on the unique biological characteristics of the tumor and the patient. It integrates the use of molecular tumor profiling, advanced imaging techniques, and the development of tailored surgical strategies, aiming to achieve the best possible outcomes while minimizing unnecessary treatments. Over the past few decades, the advent of neoadjuvant systemic therapies, such as chemotherapy and targeted therapies, has made initially unresectable CRLM amenable to surgical resection. Studies have demonstrated that surgical resection of CRLM, particularly after systemic therapy, leads to significant survival benefits, with patients often achieving long-term remission. Moreover, a systematic review has shown that precision surgery tailored to the mutational profile of CRLM, such as selecting anatomic resection for RAS-mutated tumors, may further enhance survival outcomes, highlighting the importance of personalized treatment strategies in managing CRLM.
The accurate detection and characterization of colorectal liver metastases are essential for guiding therapeutic decisions and improving clinical outcomes. Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) with its excellent soft tissue contrast, plays a pivotal role in the diagnostic workup of CRLM, which is crucial for assessing the size, number, and location of metastases. Furthermore, MRI provides functional imaging capabilities, such as diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI), dynamic contrast-enhanced MRI, and MRE, which allow for a more comprehensive evaluation of biological characteristics of CRLM. By enabling the differentiation of growth patterns, detection of histopathological features, and identification of genetic mutations, MRI enhances our understanding of the tumor's behavior and prognosis. The ability to correlate MRI findings with molecular characteristics holds great promise for improving the precision of treatment strategies and predicting patient outcomes.
The NCCN 2024 guidelines (https://www.nccn.org/) recommend hepatic MRI with either extracellular contrast agents or hepatobiliary-specific agents (e.g., gadoxetate disodium) as the preferred modality when considering liver-directed therapy or surgery for CRLM. MRI is favored over CT due to its superior lesion characterization and detection of sub-centimeter metastases. The ESMO 2023 clinical practice recommendations for metastatic colorectal cancer, which emphasize MRI's role in treatment planning, particularly in surgical candidacy and assessing disease progression under systemic therapy.
These advanced MRI techniques can provide valuable insights into the tumor's response to treatment, as well as its aggressiveness and potential for recurrence. The use of hepatobiliary-specific contrast agents, such as gadoxetate disodium, has further enhanced the sensitivity and specificity of MRI in detecting CRLM. In addition, MRI allows for precise preoperative staging, facilitating the planning of surgery by assessing the extent of liver involvement and the proximity of lesions to critical vascular structures. This is particularly important in the context of precision surgery, where the exact localization of tumors and their biological characteristics are essential for determining the appropriate surgical approach. As MRI techniques continue to evolve, their role in the non-invasive assessment of CRLM will likely expand, offering new avenues for personalized medicine and improving patient management.
While traditional MRI provides crucial qualitative and semi-quantitative information, the integration of artificial intelligence (AI) and radiomics has opened avenues for extracting complex quantitative features, enabling more precise modeling of tumor behavior and response.
AI holds the potential to revolutionize the clinical management of CRLM by providing faster, more accurate, and more personalized diagnostic and therapeutic strategies. AI technologies are increasingly being used to extract complex features from medical images, which can significantly enhance the detection, characterization, and prognosis of CRLM. These AI models can automatically analyze large datasets of medical images to identify subtle patterns and features that may be overlooked by human radiologists. For example, AI can differentiate CRLM from other focal liver lesions, such as HCC, hepatic hemangioma, or focal nodular hyperplasia, which can be challenging due to overlapping imaging characteristics. AI can also assist in assessing treatment response, predicting the likelihood of recurrence, and even estimating patient prognosis based on tumor characteristics. This capability is particularly valuable in the individualized precision medicine strategy, where an accurate understanding of the tumor's biological behavior is critical for treatment decision. Furthermore, AI can help in the optimization of MRI protocols, improving the efficiency of imaging by reducing scan time while maintaining diagnostic accuracy.

MRI-based precision diagnosis of CRLM
Although contrast-enhanced CT is widely used for initial staging, MRI offers significantly superior sensitivity in detecting small liver metastases, particularly when a hepatic MRI with hepatobiliary gadolinium-based contrast agent is used. According to the NCCN 2024 guidelines, MRI is recommended over CT for evaluating the number and distribution of metastases when liver surgery or ablation is considered. FDG-PET/CT may complement MRI in systemic staging but is less sensitive for small hepatic lesions.
Gadoxetate disodium (Gd-EOB-DTPA) enhanced MRI and DWI is an indispensable tool in the diagnosis and management of CRLM. Gd-EOB-DTPA, a hepatocyte-specific contrast agent, enables comprehensive assessment by combining dynamic vascular imaging with hepatobiliary phase acquisition. This dual capability facilitates the differentiation of metastatic lesions—characterized by the absence of normal hepatocytes—from surrounding liver parenchyma, thereby significantly enhancing lesion conspicuity and diagnostic accuracy. Meanwhile, DWI, which reflects the random motion of water molecules within tissues, offers additional value by identifying metastases through their typically restricted diffusion and correspondingly reduced apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC) values, indicative of high cellular density and tumor aggressiveness. These modalities provide synergistic advantages, improving the detection of small and otherwise inconspicuous lesions, enabling refined lesion characterization, and offering insights into the biological behavior of CRLM. The continuous development of advanced MRI techniques further solidifies its role in improving patient outcomes by enabling earlier detection and more accurate treatment planning.
Recent systematic reviews and meta-analyses have highlighted MRI's exceptional diagnostic performance for detecting liver metastases from colorectal cancer. A meta-analysis incorporating 17 studies revealed that MRI offers pooled sensitivity and specificity rates of 90% and 88%, respectively, with a diagnostic odds ratio (DOR) of 62.19, indicating a strong ability to distinguish between metastatic and non-metastatic lesions. Gd-EOB-DTPA has demonstrated superior accuracy in detecting small liver metastases compared to both conventional MRI and CT scans. A study illustrated that the use of Gd-EOB-DTPA led to significant changes in the treatment strategy in 38% of patients. The accuracy of treatment planning based on Gd-EOB-DTPA was 93%, which was notably higher than the 75% accuracy achieved with CT scans. These findings underscore the utility of Gd-EOB-DTPA in providing critical information that can influence therapeutic decisions, potentially reducing the need for unnecessary reinterventions.
Furthermore, a multicenter, randomized trial comparing Gd-EOB-DTPA, extracellular contrast medium-enhanced MRI, and contrast-enhanced CT for staging CRLM patients demonstrated that Gd-EOB-DTPA required no further imaging in 100% of cases, compared to 17% for extracellular contrast medium-enhanced MRI and 39% for contrast-enhanced CT. This study highlights Gd-EOB-DTPA MRI as the most reliable modality for initial assessment, providing accurate and comprehensive information for treatment planning without necessitating additional diagnostic procedures. The addition of DWI, which measures the diffusion of water molecules within tissues, further enhances the detection of metastases, especially in cases where conventional MRI may fall short. Studies have shown that DWI, in combination with Gd-EOB-DTPA, significantly improves the sensitivity for detecting metastases smaller than 1.5 cm. The superior performance of DWI in detecting both small and larger lesions makes it an invaluable tool in the staging and treatment planning of CRLM.

An accurate assessment of the CRLM's biological characteristics, including distinct growth patterns, histopathological features, and genetic mutations, is crucial for developing individualized treatment strategies. In recent years, MRI-based studies have garnered significant interest among researchers and yielded promising findings in this field (Table 1).

Growth patterns
The growth pattern of CRLM plays a critical role in predicting patient outcomes, including recurrence and survival. MRI is highly effective in identifying different growth patterns, which can be categorized as replacement, pushing, and desmoplastic growth. A study involving 53 chemotherapy-naïve CRLM patients classified tumors into replacement histopathological growth patterns (rHGP) and non-replacement patterns (non-rHGP). The MRI features distinguishing these patterns included rim enhancement and differences in diameter changes between pre- and post-contrast images, with rHGP tumors exhibiting more prominent rim enhancement. Furthermore, the MRI-based differentiation of these patterns was associated with significant prognostic implications, with rHGP tumors showing a higher likelihood of vascular invasion and poorer prognosis. Moreover, The MRI classified focal extranodular protuberant (FEP), and nodular confluent (NC) type of liver metastases were associated with early recurrence, which may facilitate the clinical treatment of CRCLM patients.
Another growth pattern of note is intrabiliary growth, where CRLMs extend into the intrahepatic bile ducts. MRI, particularly through DWI, can visualize restricted diffusion in both intra-hepatic and intrabiliary components, offering crucial information about the tumor's growth trajectory. This growth type is often associated with longitudinal tumor extension, which elevates the risk of recurrence after surgery. These distinctive growth patterns observed on MRI guide clinicians in treatment planning, especially in cases where surgical resection or liver transplantation is considered.

Histopathological features
Histopathological features, including tumor fibrosis and necrosis, can significantly affect the clinical course of CRLMs. MRI is instrumental in detecting these features, which are critical in predicting chemotherapy response and overall survival. A recent study showed that late-phase target tumour enhancement (TTE) of MRI after chemotherapy in CRLM was correlated with tumor fibrosis and post-hepatectomy survival. Strong TTE was associated with improved survival rates, emphasizing the prognostic value of MRI in evaluating post-chemotherapy CRLMs. Furthermore, the presence of intratumoral fat deposition, as detected by dual-echo MRI, has been linked with poor prognosis, highlighting the role of MRI in detecting and characterizing metabolic changes within the tumor. The accumulation of fat within metastases may indicate a less favorable response to chemotherapy, making it an important feature to monitor in preoperative imaging. In addition, MRI biomarkers of vascular function are linked to structural changes in tumor vessels and that antiangiogenic therapy can affect this link, this may provide reference for the selection of anti-vascular targeting drugs. Meanwhile, a significant correlation between apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC) values and histological tumour regression grading of CRLMs were revealed after preoperative chemotherapy.

Genetic mutations
MRI can also provide insights into the genetic landscape of CRLMs, particularly in relation to mutations in key oncogenes. Studies have shown that the presence of mutations in genes such as KRAS and APC correlates with distinct MRI features. For example, CRLMs harboring KRAS mutations exhibit significantly lower ADC values on DWI compared to wild-type KRAS tumors. This observation suggests that lower ADC values may serve as a non-invasive biomarker for KRAS mutation status, which has implications for prognosis and response to therapy. Recent study has demonstrated that integrating clinical, demographic, and MRI data can accurately predict the RAS mutation status in CRLMs, providing essential information for triaging treatment options. Evidence showed the correlation between TTE and APC mutational status of CRLM, which may offer some explanation as to why TTE is associated with overall survival in patients with CRLM, aiding in more personalized treatment strategies. While current evidence on MRI-based prediction of RAS/APC mutations is largely based on retrospective analyses, emerging multicenter prospective trials are validating these findings.

In recent years, a series of advancements have been made in the treatment modalities for CRLM (e.g. local therapy, chemotherapy, targeted therapy, neoadjuvant, transarterial chemoembolization and radioembolization), these have significantly improved the patients' life quality and enhanced survival rates. MRI has demonstrated considerable clinical value in non-invasive efficacy assessment and prognosis prediction (Table 2). FDG-PET/CT is useful in assessing potentially surgically curable M1 disease in selected cases. However, MRI, particularly with hepatobiliary contrast and functional sequences (e.g., DWI, DCE), provides superior anatomical and biological characterization of intrahepatic disease and is more sensitive in detecting early recurrence after therapy.

Local therapy
MRI has shown increasing value in the local treatment of CRLM, including liver resection and radiofrequency ablation (RFA). The ability of MRI to provide detailed insights into tumor characteristics and surrounding liver parenchyma plays a critical role in both treatment decision-making and prognosis assessment. The CAMINO study highlighted the potential clinical value of adding MRI to CT in the preoperative assessment of CRLM. Preliminary results suggest that MRI can identify clinically significant lesions not detected by CT, potentially changing therapeutic management for a subset of patients.
MRI plays a critical role in assessing tumor characteristics that influence long-term prognosis after local therapy. Studies on Gd-EOB-DTPA have shown that signal intensity changes in the liver parenchyma surrounding CRLM can predict pathological vessel invasion and correlate with recurrence-free survival (RFS) and overall survival (OS). Specifically, peripheral bile duct dilatation on MRI has been associated with poor OS, and delayed enhancement of tumors can provide valuable preoperative risk stratification. Notably, strong delayed-phase enhancement on gadoxetate-enhanced MRI has been linked to better overall survival after hepatectomy, offering insight into tumor biology and aiding in the selection of candidates for curative surgery.
In the context of RFA, MRI guidance has been found to provide better local control than CT guidance, with studies demonstrating superior outcomes in terms of local recurrence rates. The combination of MRI with RFA offers enhanced tumor delineation, leading to more precise ablation and reduced risk of treatment failure. Furthermore, post-RFA MRI has proven valuable in monitoring tumor progression, with predictive models based on MRI characteristics showing promise in identifying patients at high risk of local recurrence. These advances underscore the potential of MRI not only in improving treatment planning but also in monitoring the long-term success of local therapies for CRLM.

Chemotherapy
MRI offers significant advantages in predicting the response to chemotherapy in CRLM patients. By leveraging advanced imaging techniques such as radiomics, texture analysis, and histogram analysis. Furthermore, MRI can not only assist in early identification of chemotherapy responders but also guide treatment decisions, ultimately improving patient outcomes.
Machine learning combined with MRI has emerged as a powerful tool for assessing chemotherapy efficacy in patients with CRLM. Radiomic models based on longitudinal MRI data have shown promise in predicting chemotherapy response. For instance, a study involving five different MRI modalities demonstrated that radiomic features extracted from apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC) images, particularly the differences between pre-treatment and early-treatment scans, could accurately predict chemotherapy outcomes. The ensemble model developed using these features achieved a high area under the curve (AUC) of 0.9007 in the training cohort and 0.8958 in the testing cohort, illustrating its potential for clinical application.
Moreover, MRI-based techniques such as Gd-EOB-DTPA enhanced imaging have proven valuable in evaluating chemotherapy responses. In particular, rim enhancement on the hepatobiliary phase (HBP) of pre-treatment Gd-EOB-DTPA MRI was found to be more frequent in responding patients, with quantitative parameters such as signal intensity ratios showing significant correlations with chemotherapy response. The combination of these parameters yielded an AUC of 0.821, emphasizing their utility for early therapeutic evaluation.

Targeted therapy
Targeted therapies have become important in treating CRLM. Functional MRI techniques, especially dynamic contrast-enhanced MRI (DCE-MRI) and DWI, are increasingly utilized to assess the effects of these therapies. Percentage decrease of arterial enhancement area and increase of ADC value significantly differed between lesions of pathologic major or minor response after treated with bevacizumab.
DCE-MRI that utilizing pharmacokinetic modeling parameters such as K_trans (volume transfer constant), V_e (extravascular extracellular volume fraction), and K_ep (rate constant between EES and plasma), offers quantitative insights into tumor perfusion, vascular permeability, and microenvironmental alterations. These parameters collectively provide a robust framework for assessing tumor physiology and hold promise as sensitive biomarkers for monitoring responses to anti-angiogenic therapies and evaluating early treatment efficacy.
Studies have shown that DCE-MRI is more sensitive than intravoxel incoherent motion diffusion-weighted imaging (IVIM-DWI) for detecting anti-angiogenic treatment-induced changes in CRLM. While IVIM-DWI perfusion parameters, such as D∗ and fD∗, had limited sensitivity to treatment effects, DCE-MRI parameters like Ktrans were more strongly correlated with treatment response, providing better insights into therapeutic efficacy. Furthermore, a study investigating mixed-effects modeling of DCE-MRI data have shown that KEnF (a combination of Ktrans and enhancing tumor fraction) can effectively capture the temporal dynamics of treatment response, helping to distinguish between different dosing regimens and schedules in clinical trials. These findings highlight the potential of functional MRI to guide targeted therapy decisions in CRLM patients, providing a more nuanced and early evaluation of therapeutic outcomes than conventional imaging methods.
In assessing the therapeutic effects of bevacizumab, advanced MRI has proven more effective than traditional imaging criteria like RECIST. In a study comparing functional MRI with RECIST in patients receiving bevacizumab-containing chemotherapy, ADC values derived from DWI were found to be superior for predicting OS. Patients with post-treatment ADC values above 1.15 × 10−3 mm2/s showed significantly better OS, whereas RECIST criteria failed to demonstrate a similar prognostic value. This suggests that functional MRI, particularly DWI, offers more accurate assessments of treatment response, which is crucial for personalized therapy and better patient outcomes.

Neoadjuvant and systemic therapy
Neoadjuvant and systemic therapies play a crucial role in the management of CRLM, often aiming to shrink tumors, improve resectability, or enhance survival outcomes. Traditional imaging methods, such as RECIST criteria, primarily assess tumor size changes but may fail to capture the underlying tissue alterations induced by chemotherapy and targeted therapies. As a result, advanced MRI techniques have gained attention as valuable tools for evaluating treatment response.
DWI has emerged as a significant functional imaging modality. A study demonstrated that both pre-treatment and post-treatment ADC values, along with their changes (ΔADC), are reliable biomarkers for assessing tumor response to neoadjuvant chemotherapy. Specifically, post-treatment and ΔADC values were strongly correlated with histological tumor regression grades (TRG), making them useful in predicting therapeutic response. Low ADC values, especially during systemic therapy, have also been linked to improved OS, underscoring the potential of ADC measurements for early treatment evaluation.
Furthermore, hepatobiliary phase (HBP) of Gd-EOB-DTPA has shown promise in predicting the pathological response to systemic therapy. Parameters like relative tumor enhancement (RTE) and the standard deviation ratio (SDR) have demonstrated higher predictive accuracy for strong tumor response compared to traditional RECIST assessments. These findings highlight the ability of advanced MRI techniques to provide more comprehensive, accurate, and early insights into the effectiveness of systemic therapies.

Transarterial chemoembolization and radioembolization
Transarterial chemoembolization (TACE) and radioembolization (TARE) are established treatments for CRLMs, often employed when surgical resection is not feasible. Especially in recent years, the application of Yttrium-90 (Y-90) radioembolization has shown great clinical prospect in the individualized treatment of liver tumors. This therapy combines the use of small radioactive microspheres (containing Y-90) with a catheter that is selectively delivered into the hepatic arteries feeding the liver tumors. However, assessing treatment efficacy and predicting patient outcomes remain challenging.
Magnetic resonance elastography (MRE) has demonstrated potential as a response parameter for TACE. A study on patients undergoing repeated TACE cycles found a significant increase in liver stiffness in metastases following treatment, indicating tumor progression, even when the lesion size decreased. Although the correlation between stiffness and size was weak, MRE provided additional information on the mechanical properties of the tumors, which may be important for evaluating therapeutic response.
For TARE, MR imaging response, based on the qEASL criteria, has been shown to be a more reliable surrogate marker of treatment efficacy compared to vRECIST, particularly in predicting survival outcomes. Another study that analyzed enhancement-based staging markers in CRLMs before intra-arterial therapies showed that enhancing tumor volume and tumor burden were strongly associated with patient survival. These MRI-derived parameters outperformed traditional size-based markers, making them superior prognostic indicators for patients undergoing TACE or TARE.
Additionally, DWI and ADC values have been identified as predictors of survival following selective internal radiation therapy (Y-90). Optimal functional imaging response and hepatic tumor burden were found to be significant independent predictors of overall survival, highlighting the value of DWI in post-treatment surveillance. These findings collectively suggest that advanced MRI techniques, including MRE, DWI, and enhancement-based parameters, offer valuable insights into the treatment response and prognosis of patients with CRLMs undergoing TACE or TARE.

MRI in liver parenchymal assessment and pre-surgical planning
MRI can non-invasively evaluate liver fat fraction and liver volume segmentation, both of which are predictive of hepatic steatosis and regenerative capacity after portal vein embolization (PVE). Additionally, portal vein embolization with stem cell augmentation (PVESA) is an emerging approach for enhancing the growth of the liver segment that will remain after surgery (i.e., future liver remnant, FLR) in patients with liver cancer. Gadoxetate-enhanced MRI can potentially assess segmental liver function, aiding in predicting the efficacy of liver hypertrophy and suitability for resection, as well as for monitoring mesenchymal stem cell therapy, which is being investigated as an adjunct to PVE to accelerate liver growth.

Conclusion of treatment evaluation in CRLM
A comparative understanding of treatment modalities is crucial for tailoring individualized management strategies in patients with CRLM. Liver resection remains the cornerstone for eligible candidates, offering the highest long-term survival rates. However, its application is often restricted by anatomical and functional factors such as resectability and future liver remnant volume. RFA serves as a less invasive and cost-effective alternative, especially for patients with limited and small-sized metastases, though it carries a higher risk of local recurrence compared to surgery. TACE and TARE are largely reserved for unresectable or palliative settings. Notably, TARE, particularly with Y-90, offers favorable progression-free survival but at higher procedural costs and with limited availability. In contrast, systemic therapies, including chemotherapy and targeted agents, are widely accessible but typically yield modest survival improvements alone.
MRI plays a significant role in guiding these therapeutic decisions by providing superior anatomical delineation and functional insights. MRI-guided interventions enhance targeting precision, while techniques such as DCE-MRI and DWI enable early response assessment and evaluation of liver parenchymal reserve. Despite higher initial costs, MRI's diagnostic accuracy ultimately contributes to cost-effectiveness by reducing unnecessary procedures and optimizing treatment pathways.

Radiomics
Radiomics has become a significant tool for the non-invasive evaluation of CRLM, offering a deeper understanding of tumor behavior and therapeutic response. By extracting quantitative features from medical imaging, particularly MRI, radiomics enables the identification of subtle patterns that may not be apparent on conventional imaging. These features can be used to create predictive models for various clinical outcomes, such as differentiation of tumor types, treatment response, and prognosis.
MRI-based radiomics is its ability to integrate multiple imaging modalities and clinical information, providing a comprehensive view of the tumor. For example, in a study aimed at distinguishing intrahepatic mass-forming cholangiocarcinoma (IMCC) from CRLM, a radiomics nomogram combining multiparameter MRI with clinical factors (such as tumor size and serum CEA level) demonstrated superior discrimination compared to clinical models alone. The radiomics model achieved an AUC of 0.92 in the training cohort, outperforming the clinical model (AUC = 0.74).
For the treatment response, radiomics has shown promise as an early predictor of chemotherapy efficacy. A research evaluating the use of MRI-derived radiomic features in predicting local tumor progression after microwave ablation (MWA) revealed that radiomic features from T2-weighted and early arterial-phase T1 fat-suppressed MRI images could predict the likelihood of tumor recurrence. Combined models incorporating clinical data and radiomic features were more effective local tumor progression in CRLM than using either modality alone.

Furthermore, advanced texture analysis techniques, such as diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI), intravoxel incoherent motion (IVIM), and diffusion kurtosis imaging (DKI), have been applied to assess early treatment responses in CRLM patients. Li et al. comparing various diffusion models for predicting chemotherapy response showed that DKI-derived texture parameters performed best, with an AUC of 0.795, offering potential as early biomarkers for clinical outcomes. These findings emphasize the ability of radiomics to provide individualized and early prognostic information, ultimately guiding treatment decisions in CRLM patients (Fig. 4).

Artificial intelligence
The integration of AI into the field of CRLM offers an exciting frontier for enhancing the clinical management of this condition. Particularly machine learning (ML) and deep learning (DL), can analyze complex patterns in medical images, providing more accurate, automated interpretations that can significantly augment clinical decision-making processes.
Recent studies have highlighted the potential of AI in predicting CRLM development in CRC patients. Convolutional neural networks (CNNs), which combine imaging data with clinical variables, have demonstrated remarkable performance in detecting CRLM on CT and MRI. The advances in AI, particularly transformer-based models, have demonstrated superior ability in modeling spatial and temporal dependencies across multi-sequence MRI datasets. Studies have shown that the MRI-endoscopy fusion model based on ResNet-Vision Transformer achieved favorable performance in predicting treatment response to neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy and holds tremendous potential for enabling personalized treatment regimens for locally advanced rectal cancer patients. Furthermore, federated learning frameworks are being explored to develop robust multi-institutional models while preserving data privacy, addressing one of the key barriers to large-scale AI adoption in healthcare, Gunesli et al. illustrated a federated learning approach to tumor detection in colon histology images.
These AI models have been shown to perform at or above the level of expert radiologists, significantly reducing the time and cost involved in diagnosis. Besides, a semi-automated voxel-wise image segmentation algorithm based on DCE-MRI has been developed to define tumour subregions of liver metastases from colorectal cancer, that can be used to extract viable tumour-specific values from quantitative image analysis, and could improve treatment response assessment in clinical practice.
AI technologies are also making strides in predicting treatment responses. For instance, deep learning algorithms trained on pre- and post-chemotherapy MRI scans have successfully predicted pathological tumor response to chemotherapy in CRLM patients, providing more accurate information than traditional criteria such as RECIST. This can help clinicians make better-informed decisions regarding the need for surgery or other interventions. Furthermore, AI models that integrate multiple MRI sequences have been developed to predict 1-year recurrence and recurrence-free survival in patients after CRLM resection, that may enhancing prognostic assessments.

Prospect and summary

Prospect
Looking forward, the integration of advanced imaging techniques such as abbreviated MRI (AMRI) and 68Ga-DOTA-FAPI-04 PET/MR into the diagnostic and management strategies for CRLM holds immense promise for optimizing patient care. The AMRI protocol, which includes ultrafast SE T2-weighted, DWI, and T1-weighted hepatobiliary phase imaging, has demonstrated high sensitivity and lesion detection performance, offering a more efficient, cost-effective alternative to traditional MRI while maintaining diagnostic accuracy. This can significantly reduce scan time, thereby improving patient throughput and making it a valuable tool for CRLM surveillance and post-treatment evaluation.
Moreover, the molecule imaging application of 68Ga-DOTA-FAPI-04 PET/MR imaging provides a highly sensitive and specific modality for assessing CRLM. This technique, surpassing conventional 18F-FDG PET/CT in detecting primary and metastatic lesions, may become a cornerstone in personalized treatment planning.
Additionally, AI continues to enhance imaging capabilities by assisting in lesion detection, therapy response evaluation, and prognosis prediction. AI-driven models, particularly those integrated with MRI data, offer a holistic approach to managing CRLM, from early diagnosis to treatment assessment and long-term monitoring. As these technologies evolve, they promise to further refine precision medicine, improving outcomes and optimizing individualized treatment strategies for patients with CRLM.
In the future, the integration of AI and functional MRI into CRLM management faces challenges including data heterogeneity, lack of interpretability, regulatory hurdles, and integration into clinical workflow. Future directions involve the development of explainable AI models, harmonized multi-center datasets, and prospective validation trials. Collaborative platforms and regulatory frameworks will be critical in translating AI innovations into routine clinical use.

Summary
This review explores the significant role of MRI in the diagnosis, treatment evaluation, and prognosis of CRLM, emphasizing its integration into precision surgery. MRI could non-invasively characterize tumor biology and assess treatment response, particularly through advanced imaging techniques like DWI, DCE, and Gd-EOB-DTPA, enhances the accuracy of preoperative staging and prognosis prediction.
The article highlights how MRI-based radiomics, combined with AI, offers novel insights into tumor behavior, response to therapies, and patient outcomes, providing a more personalized approach to treatment planning. Additionally, in the era of precision surgery, AI and radiomics applications in CRLM improve the detection and characterization of metastatic lesions, thus enabling clinicians to tailor surgical interventions and postoperative management strategies, potentially improving both survival and quality of life for patients.
