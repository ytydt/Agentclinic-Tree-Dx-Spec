Evaluation of antimicrobial efficacy of mineral trioxide aggregate with and without silver nanoparticles: A systematic review and meta-analysis
Mineral trioxide aggregate (MTA) is widely utilized in endodontics due to its excellent sealing properties and biocompatibility. However, its limited antibacterial efficacy, particularly against resistant microorganisms such as Enterococcus faecalis, highlights the need for enhancements in its antimicrobial performance. Recent research has focused on the incorporation of silver nanoparticles (AgNPs) into MTA to improve its antibacterial activity without compromising its physical and biological characteristics. This systematic review evaluates the antibacterial efficacy of AgNP-modified MTA in comparison to conventional MTA. The review protocol was registered in the PROSPERO database (CRD42024555867). A comprehensive literature search was conducted across PubMed, MEDLINE, the Cochrane Library, and Directory of Open Access Journals for the articles published until July 2024. Search strategies employed the PICO framework and Boolean operators, with MeSH terms including MTA, AgNPs, antibacterial activity, and E. faecalis. Two reviewers independently extracted the data, and the quality of the included in vitro studies was assessed using the QUIN tool. Of the 635 records initially identified, seven studies met the inclusion criteria. These studies utilized the various concentrations of AgNPs and employed different methods for antibacterial evaluation. Overall, AgNP-modified MTA demonstrated superior antibacterial activity in a dose-dependent manner, particularly against E. faecalis. The findings support the clinical potential of AgNP-enhanced MTA in improving microbial control during endodontic procedures.

INTRODUCTION
Mineral trioxide aggregate (MTA) has been a transformative material in the field of endodontics since its introduction in the early 1990s by Dr. Mahmoud Torabinejad. Initially developed as a root-end filling material, MTA was designed to provide superior sealing ability, biocompatibility, and regenerative potential compared to conventional materials. Its hydraulic properties enable it to set in the presence of moisture, making it ideal for use in the dynamic oral environment. Chemically, MTA is composed of Portland cement constituents, including tricalcium silicate, tricalcium aluminate, and bismuth oxide, the latter of which imparts radiopacity. Upon hydration, MTA releases calcium hydroxide, resulting in an alkaline pH that promotes antimicrobial activity and facilitates hard tissue formation.
Sadrabad et al. reported that MTA exhibited the lowest cytotoxicity on human dental pulp stem cells within 24 h. Fatima et al. reported that MTA exhibited the highest shear bond strength when bonded after 15 min using a two-step self-etch adhesive. An increase in push-out bond strength with silver nanoparticle (AgNP)-modified MTA was similarly reported by Bichile et al. and Mendonça et al. reported that MTA repair HP exhibited the highest volumetric loss at its extremities when exposed to an acidic environment, indicating reduced stability under simulated inflammatory conditions.
Despite its favorable properties, MTA has certain limitations. Its prolonged setting time, difficult handling characteristics, potential for discoloration, and relatively high cost have prompted the development of modified versions designed to address these drawbacks. Modifying MTA can enhance its properties and overcome the limitations of the conventional material, thereby improving its clinical effectiveness.
One notable modification involves the incorporation of nanoparticles, specifically AgNPs, to enhance the antimicrobial efficacy of MTA.
Silver has a long-standing history as an antimicrobial agent, and its nanoscale form significantly enhances its bioavailability and interaction with microbial cells. AgNPs exert antibacterial effects through multiple mechanisms, including the disruption of cell walls, the generation of reactive oxygen species, and the inhibition of DNA replication. Their incorporation into dental materials has demonstrated promise in reducing microbial contamination without significantly compromising the physical properties of the host material.
The potential of AgNP-enhanced MTA is particularly significant in endodontics, where persistent infections – especially those caused by Enterococcus faecalis – remain a leading cause of treatment failure. By enhancing the antibacterial properties of MTA, AgNPs may contribute to improved clinical outcomes in procedures such as pulp capping, root-end filling, apexification, and perforation repair.
This systematic review evaluates the antibacterial efficacy of AgNP-modified MTA in comparison to conventional MTA, focusing on E. faecalis. The findings are intended to inform clinical decision-making and guide future research aimed at optimizing the antimicrobial potential of endodontic materials.

STRUCTURED QUESTION
Does the incorporation of AgNPs into MTA demonstrates enhanced antibacterial efficacy against E. faecalis when compared to conventional MTA, as evidenced by in vitro studies?

MATERIALS AND METHODS
This systematic review was conducted in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) 2020 guidelines.

PROTOCOL REGISTRATION
The protocol for this systematic review has been registered with the PROSPERO International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews, Registry No. CRD42024555867.

SEARCH STRATEGY
A comprehensive search strategy was developed in accordance with the PICOS criteria outlined in the review protocol and was conducted following the PRISMA 2020 guidelines. Two independent reviewers screened the titles and abstracts of all retrieved studies, and any disagreements were resolved through discussion with a third reviewer.
The electronic databases searched included the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials, Scopus, MEDLINE, the Directory of Open Access Journals, and PubMed Central. The search was conducted up to July 31, 2024, with no language restrictions applied.
Both controlled vocabulary (MeSH terms) and free-text keywords were utilized, combined with Boolean operators (AND, OR).
Pubmed search strategy: ([“Enterococcus faecalis”(MeSH) OR “E. faecalis”(tiab) OR “endodontic pathogens”(tiab)] AND [(“Mineral Trioxide Aggregate”[MeSH] OR “MTA”[tiab]) AND (“Metal Nanoparticles”[MeSH] OR “silver nanoparticles”[tiab])] AND [“Control Groups”(MeSH) OR “unmodified MTA”(tiab)] AND [“Anti-Bacterial Agents”(MeSH) OR “antimicrobial efficacy”(tiab)] AND [“In Vitro Techniques”(MeSH) OR “in vitro”(tiab)]) NOT (“Review”[Publication Type] OR “Case Reports”[MeSH] OR “Animals”[MeSH]).
Scopus search strategy: TITLE-ABS-KEY (“Mineral Trioxide Aggregate” OR “MTA”) AND TITLE-ABS-KEY (“silver nanoparticles” OR “AgNP” OR “Ag NPs” OR “nano-silver”) AND TITLE-ABS-KEY (“antibacterial” OR “antimicrobial” OR “anti-bacterial”) AND (TITLE-ABS-KEY [“Enterococcus faecalis” OR “E. faecalis” OR “endodontic pathogens”]) AND (LIMIT-TO [DOCTYPE, “ar”] OR LIMIT-TO [DOCTYPE, “re”]) # Articles and reviews only.
In addition, manual searches of reference lists from the relevant articles were conducted to identify any studies that may have been overlooked in the electronic search.

SELECTION CRITERIA
Studies were selected based on their relevance to the study design, population, intervention, comparison, and outcomes. In vitro studies evaluating the antibacterial efficacy of MTA modified with AgNPs were included. Eligible studies utilized AgNPs as the modifying agent, compared against conventional MTA, and assessed antimicrobial activity, particularly against E. faecalis. Studies employing agar diffusion, colony-forming unit (CFU) counts, or other validated microbial assays were considered. Excluded from this review were reviews, case reports, case series, animal studies, randomized or nonrandomized clinical trials, and studies lacking antimicrobial evaluation or complete data. DATA EXTRACTION
Data extraction was conducted independently by two reviewers using a standardized form to ensure accuracy and consistency. The extracted data included the study title, authors, year of publication, country, study design, sample type and size, type and concentration of AgNPs used, details of the intervention and comparison groups, target microorganisms (particularly *Enterococcus faecalis*), methods of antimicrobial assessment, outcome measures, and key findings. Any discrepancies between the reviewers were resolved through discussion and consensus. Additional information, such as funding sources and potential conflicts of interest, was also recorded when available.

RISK OF BIAS ASSESSMENT
The risk of bias for all included in vitro studies was assessed using the QUIN (Quality Assessment Tool for In vitro Studies) tool. This tool evaluates methodological quality across key domains, including the clarity of objectives, sample size calculation, sampling technique, description of intervention and control groups, operator blinding, randomization, outcome measurement methods, and statistical analysis. Each domain was scored as adequately specified, inadequately specified, or not specified. A cumulative score was calculated to categorize each study as having a low, medium, or high risk of bias. Discrepancies in assessment were resolved through discussions between the two independent reviewers.

META-ANALYSIS
A meta-analysis was conducted on studies that provided comparable quantitative data regarding the antibacterial efficacy of MTA with and without AgNPs, specifically against E. faecalis. Eligible studies included those that reported outcomes using standardized measures, such as inhibition zone diameters or CFU counts. Effect sizes were calculated as mean differences between the intervention and control groups. Due to anticipated variability in experimental conditions, a random-effects model was employed to account for heterogeneity. Statistical heterogeneity was assessed using the I² statistic, with values interpreted according to the Cochrane guidelines. A high I² value indicated substantial variation across studies, warranting cautious interpretation of the pooled results. The meta-analysis aimed to determine whether the incorporation of AgNPs into MTA significantly enhanced its antibacterial properties compared to conventional MTA.

RESULTS

Search and selection
The initial electronic database search identified a total of 635 records. After removing 496 duplicate entries, 139 studies were screened based on their titles and abstracts. Of these, 67 were excluded for not meeting the inclusion criteria. The full texts of 72 articles were assessed for eligibility, of which 55 were excluded due to irrelevant interventions, outcomes, or incomplete data. Ultimately, 7 in vitro studies met the predefined inclusion criteria and were included in the systematic review and meta-analysis. The study selection process was conducted in accordance with the PRISMA 2020 guidelines and is illustrated in the PRISMA [Figure 1].

Description of included studies
A total of seven in vitro studies were included in this systematic review. These studies, published between 2013 and 2023, were conducted in various countries, including Iran, Egypt, Mexico, and South Korea. All included studies investigated the antibacterial efficacy of MTA modified with AgNPs in comparison to conventional MTA. The sample types varied and included extracted human teeth, dentin blocks, and microbial cultures.
The concentration of AgNPs used ranged from 0.1 mg/mL to 200 ppm. Various methods were employed to assess antibacterial activity, including agar diffusion assays, broth dilution techniques, membrane-enclosed immersion tests, and colorimetric assays. E. faecalis was the most commonly studied microorganism; however, some studies also included other endodontic pathogens such as Porphyromonas gingivalis, Staphylococcus aureus, and Pseudomonas aeruginosa.
While all studies demonstrated enhanced antimicrobial activity of AgNP-modified MTA compared to conventional MTA, the degree of efficacy varied based on nanoparticle concentration and the testing methods employed. The general characteristics of the included studies are summarized in Table 2.

Risk of bias assessment
The methodological quality of the seven included in vitro studies was evaluated using the QUIN tool. Among these studies, two were rated as having a low risk of bias, while the remaining five were classified as having a moderate risk. The common limitations included inadequate reporting of randomization procedures, the absence of blinding for both operators and outcome assessors, and a lack of justification for sample sizes. Most studies clearly articulated their objectives, intervention and control groups, and methods for measuring outcomes. However, incomplete reporting in areas such as operator expertise, randomization, and statistical methodology contributed to variability in quality. Despite these shortcomings, all included studies met at least 50% of the assessed quality criteria, justifying their inclusion in the final analysis.

Meta-analysis
Out of the seven studies included two provided sufficient and comparable quantitative data for inclusion in the meta-analysis, which focused on the antibacterial efficacy of MTA modified with AgNPs against E. faecalis. The effect sizes were calculated as the mean difference in inhibition zones between the AgNP-modified MTA and the conventional MTA groups.
The pooled analysis revealed a mean difference of 1.41 mm in favor of AgNP-modified MTA, with a 95% confidence interval ranging from −1.30 to 4.11. However, this result was not statistically significant (P > 0.05), as the confidence interval included zero. The heterogeneity among the studies was substantial, with an I2 value of 99%, indicating considerable variability likely attributable to the differences in nanoparticle concentrations, microbial strains, and experimental protocols.
Despite the absence of statistical significance, a trend indicating enhanced antibacterial activity with the use of AgNPs was observed. These findings underscore the necessity for more standardized and high-quality studies to validate the clinical relevance of these materials. DISCUSSION
The findings of this systematic review and meta-analysis underscore the enhanced antimicrobial potential of MTA when combined with AgNPs, in comparison to conventional MTA. An analysis of seven in vitro studies revealed key variables influencing outcomes, including targeted bacterial strains, AgNP concentrations, antimicrobial assessment methods, and the types of control groups employed. These factors collectively enhance our understanding of the effectiveness of AgNPs in improving the antibacterial properties of MTA for endodontic applications.
The included studies targeted a variety of bacterial strains, reflecting the microbial diversity associated with endodontic infections. E. faecalis was the most frequently examined organism, appearing in five of the seven studies, which underscores its clinical significance in persistent and recurrent root canal infections due to its resistance to conventional disinfection methods. This bacterium is recognized for its resilience to harsh conditions and its ability to thrive in the complex environment of the root canal system, even after standard treatment.
The finding that MTA modified with AgNPs demonstrated enhanced antibacterial activity against E. faecalis in multiple studies is significant. The incorporation of AgNPs into MTA appears particularly advantageous for managing infections caused by E. faecalis, a bacterium often associated with treatment-resistant endodontic cases. This enhancement may lead to improved clinical outcomes and a reduced need for retreatment. However, other pathogens commonly implicated in endodontic-periodontal infections – such as P. gingivalis, Prevotella intermedia, and Aggregatibacter actinomycetemcomitans – were evaluated in fewer studies. This variability in microbial targets limits the generalizability of the findings and prevents definitive conclusions regarding the broad-spectrum antimicrobial potential of AgNP-modified MTA.
The inclusion of diverse bacterial strains underscores the widespread interest in assessing the antimicrobial potential of AgNP-modified MTA, but it also introduces variability that hinders direct comparisons. Due to the resistance of E. faecalis to conventional treatments, this bacterium serves as an ideal model organism. Standardizing target strains in future studies would enhance data comparability and strengthen conclusions regarding the efficacy of AgNP-enhanced MTA.
One of the key factors influencing the outcomes across the included studies was the concentration of AgNPs incorporated into MTA, which ranged from 0.1 mg/mL to 200 ppm. This wide variation reflects the lack of standardized guidelines for optimal dosing. Several studies including those by Bahador et al. and Waly and Medicine reported a dose-dependent increase in antibacterial efficacy, particularly against anaerobic pathogens. However, the dose-response relationship was not consistently investigated, limiting conclusions about the ideal concentration for clinical use. While higher concentrations of AgNPs may enhance antimicrobial action, they may also pose a risk of cytotoxicity. Notably, none of the included studies assessed biocompatibility, underscoring the need for future research to determine safe and effective AgNP concentrations for clinical application.
The included studies employed a variety of methods to assess the antibacterial activity of MTA with and without AgNPs. These methods included agar diffusion, disc diffusion, broth dilution, and colorimetric assays. Agar diffusion, the most widely used technique, is a standard method for measuring inhibition zones; however, it cannot differentiate between bacteriostatic and bactericidal effects. In contrast, broth dilution methods, as demonstrated by Bahador et al., provide more precise insights into bacterial growth inhibition over time. This variability in testing approaches contributes to inconsistencies in results and complicates direct comparisons across studies.
The colorimetric assays employed by Lee et al. provided a more quantitative assessment of bacterial viability compared to traditional diffusion methods, allowing for a clearer evaluation of the antimicrobial effects of AgNP-modified MTA. These assays yield precise measurements of bacterial inhibition or death, thereby contributing to a deeper understanding of the underlying mechanisms. However, their limited application in endodontic research diminishes comparability across studies. Standardizing antimicrobial assessment methods by integrating both qualitative and quantitative approaches would enhance the reliability and consistency of future findings.
The included studies utilized various control groups to assess the efficacy of AgNP-modified MTA. Some studies employed conventional MTA without nanoparticles, while others used distilled water as a placebo control. The latter approach is less meaningful for comparison, as it does not consider the baseline antimicrobial properties of MTA. Research conducted by Samiei et al. and Loyola-Rodríguez et al., which directly compared MTA with and without AgNPs, provides stronger evidence of the additive antimicrobial benefits of nanoparticles. These findings underscore the potential of AgNPs to enhance the effectiveness of MTA, particularly in the management of persistent endodontic infections. Furthermore, variability in the selection of control groups across the included studies introduces a potential source of bias. While some studies utilized MTA without AgNPs as a control, others employed distilled water, which may overestimate the efficacy of AgNP-modified MTA due to its lack of inherent antimicrobial properties. Comparisons using conventional MTA provide a more accurate assessment of the added value of AgNPs. For example, studies by Samiei et al. and Loyola-Rodríguez et al. demonstrated significantly enhanced antibacterial efficacy with AgNPs when directly compared to unmodified MTA. To improve consistency and reliability, future studies should standardize the use of MTA without nanoparticles as the control group.
The risk of bias assessment using the QUIN tool indicated that two studies had a low risk of bias, while five were classified as having a medium risk. The most common methodological limitations included the absence of detailed sample size calculations, lack of randomization, and insufficient reporting of operator blinding. These factors raise the concerns regarding the statistical power and objectivity of the results, particularly in studies that rely on subjective assessments of inhibition zones. Nonetheless, all included studies met or exceeded 50% of the QUIN criteria, supporting their inclusion. Future research should prioritize rigorous methodological standards, including clear descriptions of randomization, operator training, blinding, and power analysis, to strengthen the evidence base and reduce bias.
Despite these limitations, the collective evidence supports the enhanced antimicrobial efficacy of MTA when modified with AgNPs. Notably, Bahador et al. were among the first to demonstrate a dose-dependent antibacterial effect of AgNP-modified MTA, with higher concentrations exhibiting greater inhibition of P. gingivalis and other anaerobic pathogens. These findings are particularly significant given the role of P. gingivalis in chronic periodontitis, which is often associated with endodontic-periodontal lesions. Such results provide a robust foundation for the continued exploration of AgNP-enhanced MTA, especially in cases where conventional materials may prove inadequate.
Similarly, Samiei et al. evaluated the antimicrobial effect of AgNP-modified MTA against a broader range of bacterial strains, including E. faecalis and S. aureus. Their findings align with those of Bahador et al., confirming that the addition of AgNPs significantly enhances the antibacterial properties of MTA, particularly against E. faecalis, a key pathogen in treatment-resistant endodontic infections. Waly and Medicine and Farahat et al. also reported increased antibacterial efficacy with AgNP incorporation, utilizing methodologies such as the modified Kirby-Bauer disc-diffusion test to demonstrate enhanced inhibition of both aerobic and anaerobic bacteria. Loyola-Rodríguez et al. and Lee et al. provided further evidence, showing that MTA combined with AgNPs significantly reduces bacterial viability, especially in formulations intended for endodontic sealers or retrograde fillings. These results suggest that AgNP-enhanced MTA has promising potential to improve infection control in endodontic treatments.
This systematic review and meta-analysis synthesize evidence from in vitro studies evaluating the antibacterial efficacy of AgNP-modified MTA. According to the Oxford Centre for Evidence-based Medicine hierarchy, the included studies represent Level 3 evidence (nonrandomized experimental studies, including controlled laboratory investigations).
However, several limitations must be considered when interpreting these findings. The included studies exhibited a high degree of heterogeneity regarding the bacterial strains tested, AgNP concentrations, and antimicrobial assessment methods. This lack of standardization presents the challenges for synthesizing the data and drawing definitive conclusions. While most studies concentrated on *E. faecalis*, fewer examined other clinically relevant pathogens, such as *Fusobacterium nucleatum* and *Aggregatibacter actinomycetemcomitans*. Consequently, the broad-spectrum efficacy of AgNP-modified MTA remains uncertain. Standardizing future study designs, including microbial targets and testing protocols, will be essential for gaining a clearer understanding of the full potential and clinical applicability of this promising material.
While the findings of this review are promising, several limitations must be acknowledged. A major concern is the high degree of heterogeneity among the included studies, characterized by variations in bacterial strains, AgNP concentrations, and antimicrobial assessment methods, which complicate the data synthesis. Most studies focused on *Enterococcus faecalis*, while fewer examined other clinically relevant pathogens, such as *Fusobacterium nucleatum* or *Aggregatibacter actinomycetemcomitans*, thereby limiting the generalizability of the results to all endodontic infections. In addition, the wide range of AgNP concentrations used from 0.1 mg/mL to 200 ppm highlights the lack of standardization, making it challenging to determine the optimal dosage for clinical use. None of the studies assessed long-term biocompatibility or cytotoxicity, which is an essential consideration, given that higher concentrations of AgNPs may pose risks to periapical tissues. Future research should aim to establish the standardized protocols for bacterial testing and nanoparticle concentration while also evaluating the safety profile of AgNP-modified MTA in the clinical settings.
This review is limited by its focus on in vitro studies, which, while valuable, do not fully capture the complexities of the clinical environment where the factors such as saliva, immune response, and tissue interactions significantly influence the outcomes. The lack of clinical trials restricts the real-world applicability of AgNP-enhanced MTA, highlighting the necessity for in vivo validation. Furthermore, most studies exhibited a moderate risk of bias due to incomplete reporting of randomization, blinding, and sample size calculations. Future research should concentrate on identifying the optimal AgNP concentration that ensures efficacy without compromising biocompatibility. In addition, studies should evaluate long-term safety, include a broader range of clinically relevant pathogens beyond E. faecalis, and adopt standardized antimicrobial testing methods to enhance comparability. Such efforts are crucial for elucidating the clinical potential of AgNP-modified MTA in endodontic therapy.
Beyond antimicrobial efficacy, future studies should evaluate how AgNPs influence the physical properties of MTA, including setting time, compressive strength, and sealing ability–factors critical for the clinical success of procedures such as root-end fillings and perforation repairs. While this review emphasizes the antimicrobial advantages of AgNP-enhanced MTA, it is equally important to confirm its mechanical integrity. Furthermore, clinical trials are necessary to validate in vitro findings in real-world conditions, where biological factors can significantly impact treatment outcomes. Research should concentrate on infection control, healing rates, and overall effectiveness in scenarios where conventional MTA may be inadequate. With a comprehensive assessment of safety, mechanical performance, and clinical efficacy, AgNP-modified MTA demonstrates considerable potential to improve endodontic therapy.

CONCLUSION
Based on the analysis of seven in vitro studies, this systematic review suggests that AgNP-modified MTA demonstrates enhanced antibacterial activity against E. faecalis compared to conventional MTA, although meta-analysis of two studies showed nonsignificant results (P > 0.05) with high heterogeneity (I = 99%). While these findings support the potential of AgNP-MTA for improving endodontic disinfection, the evidence remains limited by methodological variations in nanoparticle concentrations (0.1–200 ppm), testing protocols, and lack of clinical or biocompatibility data. Future research should focus on standardized in vitro models, optimal AgNP dosing, and translational studies to determine clinical applicability while ensuring safety for periapical tissues.
