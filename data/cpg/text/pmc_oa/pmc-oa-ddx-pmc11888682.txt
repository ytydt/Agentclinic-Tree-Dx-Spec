A Holistic Approach to Postendodontic Pain Management: A Narrative Review
ABSTRACT
Pain is a major symptom in dental visits, especially postendodontic procedures. Prevention and treatment are crucial, requiring clinicians to integrate various pain management strategies to promote physical healing, emotional, and psychological well-being, emphasizing the importance of holistic pain management. The purpose of this narrative review was to summarize the importance of holistic pain management strategies after endodontics. A comprehensive literature search was conducted on various databases including PubMed, Science Direct, Scopus and Google Scholar, focusing on the holistic approach to endodontic pain management and postendodontic pain management, following the PRISMA 2020 Statement protocols. Although numerous review publications on endodontic pain management have emerged, only three of them have delved into both pharmacological and nonpharmacological approaches to treating endodontic pain. These studies illustrated the effectiveness of nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) in lowering the incidence of adverse events and endodontic postoperative pain. Only one study illustrated the effectiveness of behavioral therapies in treating individuals with dental anxiety and phobia. Holistic pain management strategies postendodontic procedures promote physical recovery, emotional wellness, trust, and treatment outcomes, enhancing patient experience and fostering trust in dental settings.

INTRODUCTION
Pain management plays a pivotal role in patient care, as pain is considered the primary trigger for dental anxiety. Pain is defined as an uncomfortable experience, both viscerally and emotionally, associated with actual or prospective tissue damage, or a sensation expressed in connection with such an injury. A complex process is involved in generating and experiencing odontogenic pain. The tissue damage generates an action potential that targets the central nervous system while also inducing inflammatory reactions at the site of injury. The intensity of perceived pain can vary, ranging from dull sensations (mediated by C nerve fibers) to constant, sharp pains (mediated by A nerve fibers) or throbbing sensations.
Patients who have undergone postendodontic treatment procedures commonly experience pain, with a reported prevalence of 2.5% to nearly 60%. The pain experience typically exhibits an upward trend between 6- and 12-hour posttreatment and reaching to the maximum peaking within 24 hours before diminishing to 11% in the subsequent week. Factors influencing the postendodontic pain experience can be broadly classified into three categories. The first category deals with the patient-related factors such as gender and age, whereas the second category comprises of tooth-related factors including preoperative pulp condition. Finally, the third group includes treatment-related factors such as primary root canal therapy or retreatment. All the abovementioned categories significantly influence postoperative endodontic pain.
Essential elements of endodontic care include reducing and managing postendodontic pain. It results from a wide range of intricate circumstances that are linked to the acute inflammation in periapical area arising due to localized host–microbial interaction, and chemical, or mechanical injury occurred during the endodontic procedures. By educating and providing information about the postendodontic pain along with prescribing appropriate drugs to control pain may increase the patient’s confidence in their dental practitioner. In addition, it will increase their ability to tolerate pain and improve their attitude about getting dental treatment in the future. A number of strategies have been suggested to address posttreatment endodontic pain. These encompass technical considerations and protocols that are expected to be carried out during endodontic treatment, as well as occlusal adjustments. Furthermore, researchers have proposed numerous strategies such as adjunctive therapies including low-level laser therapy, phototherapy, cryotherapy, application of topical medications, and systemic pharmacotherapy. Hence, the current study aims to present a narrative review about the holistic approach for the postendodontic pain management.
After endodontic treatments, a holistic approach to the pain management places an emphasis on all-encompassing care that takes the patient’s physical, emotional, and psychological health into account. It is possible to efficiently control pain and discomfort associated with endodontic procedures, such as root canal therapy, by combining both pharmacological and nonpharmacological approaches. Studies that illustrate the treatment interventions used and carried out using holistic approaches are rarely available. The results from a systematic review established the effectiveness of both pharmaceutical and nonpharmacological approaches in managing dental pain intensity and anxiety in pediatrics and adolescent populations during dental procedures. Therefore, research is required to fully evaluate the efficacy and safety of using holistic techniques in therapies.

MATERIALS AND METHODS
Owing to the absence of existing studies evaluating the efficacy of a holistic approach to managing endodontic pain, the present study attempts to conduct a narrative review instead of a systematic review. Previous studies have predominantly focused on assessing the effectiveness of individual components of holistic health separately. Whenever feasible, the current review has referenced higher levels of clinical evidence, such as systematic reviews. To streamline the discussion, the present review has categorized the diverse components of a holistic approach into common themes reflected in the current literature.
A comprehensive literature search was conducted to identify relevant studies. Various databases including PubMed, ScienceDirect, Scopus, and Google Scholar were systematically searched using the search term “holistic approach to endodontic pain management” or “management of postendodontic pain” or “postendodontic pain, adhering to the guidelines outlined in the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis (PRISMA) 2020 Statement. Articles were screened based on their relevance to a guided holistic approach to endodontic pain management, considering their titles. Only English-language publications were included in the review, and only original articles, systematic reviews, case series, and case reports were deemed eligible for consideration. Interim reports, abstracts, letters, short communications, and textbook chapters were excluded.
After importing all identified studies into an Endnote library, duplicates were systematically removed. Subsequently, articles meeting the eligibility criteria were selected, and their titles and abstracts were independently evaluated by two independent reviewers. In cases of disagreement, a third reviewer was consulted for resolution. After initial screening, the full texts of the chosen articles were independently analyzed by the first and second reviewer to determine inclusion in the review, with the assistance of the third reviewer in resolving any disputes. Data extraction into a uniform format was conducted by the same two reviewers. DISCUSSION

Postoperative pharmacological interventions are administered to manage postendodontic pain
In the past decades, numerous pharmacological approaches for pain management have surfaced. Among the strategies used to alleviate endodontic pain is the prescription of opioid analgesics, steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (SAIDs), and NSAIDs. Research has shown that, depending on the sort and quantity of drugs administered during surgery, SAIDS can lessen the severity of pain within the first 24 hours after a primary root canal procedure. The analgesic efficacy of SAIDs may be impacted by the form and dosage of the drug; however, there was no apparent difference between betamethasone at 2 mg and dexamethasone at 4 mg.
NSAIDs became the most often prescribed oral painkillers after surgery, with ibuprofen receiving special attention due to its considerable research. Research has shown that when given 6 hours after the procedure, ibuprofen (600 mg) and acetaminophen (1000 mg) together were significantly effective in controlling postoperative endodontic pain, indicating their potential as preferred initial treatments during the immediate postendodontic period. Although there is only low to moderate evidence to support such recommendations, emerging data suggest that combining ketoprofen (50 mg) and naproxen (500 mg) may be even more effective than ibuprofen (600 mg) alone at the same postoperative interval. In addition, although precise procedures are still pending, multiple-dose regimens seem to provide better pain control than single-dose regimens.
There is general agreement regarding the usefulness of corticosteroids as a pain reliever during endodontic procedures due to their anti-inflammatory and immune-suppressive qualities, which have been demonstrated in a few studies. According to a recent systematic review and meta-analysis, in postoperative endodontic patients, corticosteroids have been shown to effectively reduce the pain. This review highlighted, among other things, how different treatment regimens can affect the extent of this effect at different times. Methylprednisolone has been well studied in the treatment of pain because of its strong anti-inflammatory qualities. For pain alleviation in cases of acute pulpitis, intraosseous methylprednisolone injection was compared with pulpotomy during a 7-day observation period in a randomized clinical trial examining the effectiveness of a minimally invasive intervention. The results showed that, after 7 days, methylprednisolone injection for acute pulpitis provided better results than pulpotomy, saving important dental resources such as endodontic supplies and equipment as well as dentists’ time.

Nonpharmacological interventions to manage postendodontic pain
Two groups are identified by psychological techniques and physical therapy strategies for nonpharmacological interventions. These interventions may be used in addition to pharmacological treatments such as dental anxiety, nutritional support, music therapy, and cryotherapy. The primary goal of these interventions is to alleviate psychological distress experienced by patients before and during dental procedures. In addition, indirectly by mitigating mental distress, these interventions may contribute to reducing pain and promoting recovery posttreatment. This is crucial, as mental distress has been linked to decreased success rates in postoperative outcomes after surgical, endodontic, or other dental procedures.

Treatment of dental anxiety
Despite advances in oral anesthesia making treatments essentially painless, dental procedures are still often viewed as unpleasant, frightening, and puzzling. Hence, a lot of patients feel scared or anxious, not just before invasive operations. A variety of stimuli, including perceived loss of control and predictability, injections, dental equipment, sights, odors, and sounds, related with the dental environment, and expectation of pain, influence patients’ psychological distress. Although clinically serious dental phobia is reported to affect approximately 4% of the population, up to one in four people report experiencing dental concerns.
Researchers believe that conventional dentists can effectively treat those with mild to moderate types of dental anxiety, although treatment for severe dental anxiety or even dental phobia often requires more specialist interventions, including psychotherapy. Researchers have recently developed several nonpharmacological techniques to effectively treat anxious patients before and during dental procedures. The categories of current approaches include music therapy, hypnosis, cognitive–behavioral therapy (CBT), improved information, and strategies for relaxation. Improved information uses the patient’s cognitive level to communicate procedural and/or sensory information before, during, and after a dental procedure.
Cognitive–behavioral therapy is widely acknowledged as the most effective psychological intervention for addressing anxiety related to specific objects and situations. A broad term covering behavioral and cognitive therapies, which encompass techniques such as exposure, systematic desensitization, and cognitive restructuring. Clinicians often integrate these interventions in clinical settings. The objective of CBT techniques is to alleviate dental anxiety by using strategies such as systematic desensitization, positive reinforcement, distraction, and sensory focusing. A relaxation technique is a way of teaching patients’ techniques like regulating their breathing, progressive muscle relaxation, imagery guidance, autogenic training, or other techniques with the goal of promoting comfort and relaxation. In addition, it was determined by the systematic review and meta-analysis that CBT therapies are effective in treating dental anxiety and fear.
In various medical domains, music interventions have been used to address patients’ physical, psychological, spiritual, and social requirements. Patients recognize their ability for the intrinsic components of music to impact their physiological and psycho–emotional reactions, such as triggering memories and associations, fostering imagery, eliciting emotions, and facilitating distraction and relaxation. Gardner and Licklider proposed the idea of “audio analgesia,” which is the use of sound and music to reduce pain. To regulate the auditory stimulation, patients are outfitted with control boxes and headphones. The patient can turn up the music to a maximum of 116 decibels above 0.0002 microbar during the therapy session if they feel any pain or discomfort. This causes a calming waterfall-like sound to be produced, which encourages relaxation. As a result, this music promotes relaxation by reducing the conditioned fear that is frequently connected to the sound of a dental drill.

Nutritional support in managing endodontic pain
Recognizing the importance of proper nutrition both before and after the procedure is crucial. Lowering inflammation and hastening healing can be achieved by eating a diet high in fruits and vegetables, whole grains, healthy fats, and anti-inflammatory nutrients. In addition, adequate hydration is important for maintaining oral health and reducing pain. The primary focus of any anti-inflammatory diet revolves around limiting calorie intake. Reduced systemic cellular oxidative stress is the outcome of cutting back on excess calorie consumption. Among the many methods that have been researched, calorie restriction has proven to be the most successful therapeutic intervention for improving health span, which is defined as longevity minus years of disability. For healthy overweight and normal weight people who followed calorie restriction, several CALERIE (Comprehensive Assessment of the Long-Term Effects of Reducing Intake of Energy) studies have shown notable improvements in metabolism.
The success of lifelong calorie restraint relies on the capacity of the diet to promote satiety. In this context, ensuring satisfactory protein intake at each meal is considered a crucial initial step, a concept referred to as protein leveraging. Potential mechanisms behind protein leveraging may involve elevation of glucagon levels to stabilize blood glucose levels, as well as increased secretion of satiety hormones like PYY and GLP-1 from the gut. Furthermore, incorporating fermentable fibre into a calorie-restricted diet is vital for producing short-chain fatty acids (SCFA), which amplify the signaling effectiveness of PYY and GLP-1 induced by protein consumption during a mealtime.

Application of cryotherapy to managing endodontic pain
Considerable attention has been garnered by cryotherapy, a well-established technique extensively used in managing pain as well as follow-up treatment for surgical operations and sports-related injuries. When applied briefly before various orthopedics, abdominal, gynecological, and hernia procedures, it has demonstrated efficacy in lowering oedema, pain, inflammation, and recovery time. As a result, researchers have studied cryotherapy’s local physiological effects and methods of action. In essence, applying cold causes tissues to lose heat, which lowers body temperature. Vasoconstriction results from a decline in temperature, which prevents the production of oedema. Moreover, vasoconstriction lowers the rate of cellular metabolism, which lowers the oxygen requirement of cells and restricts the generation of free radicals in tissues. In addition, scientists have found that inflammatory enzymes rise with warmth.
In dentistry, cryotherapy is commonly used after intraoral surgical procedures to manage postoperative pain. A saline solution that had been refrigerated to 2.5°C was subjected to EndoVac, a specialized negative pressure equipment made especially for root canal irrigation, for 5 minutes. By using the well-established advantages of cold therapy in the treatment of traumatic injuries and in aiding recuperation in a range of surgical situations, this strategy sought to reduce the temperature at the root surface. In comparison to a typical control group, the study data showed that applying 2.5°C cold saline as the final irritant greatly reduced postoperative discomfort. As a result, cryotherapy has been shown to be a successful, economical, and secure way to reduce postoperative pain after a single root canal procedure.

CONCLUSIONS
This narrative review has offered a concise summary of holistic approaches for postendodontic pain management. The current review has delineated the impact of pharmacological strategies, encompassing opioid analgesics, SAIDs, and NSAIDs, as well as nonpharmacological approaches, such as CBT, nutritional support, music therapy, and cryotherapy. By integrating these holistic approaches into pain management strategies after endodontic procedures, dental professionals can provide patients with comprehensive care that promotes not only physical healing but also emotional and psychological well-being. This patient-centered approach fosters trust, enhances treatment outcomes, and contributes to a positive overall dental experience.
