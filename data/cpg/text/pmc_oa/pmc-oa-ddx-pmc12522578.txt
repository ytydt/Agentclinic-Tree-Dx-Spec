Organizational resilience in nursing: a concept analysis using Rodgers’ evolutionary approach
Aim
We sought to distinguish, clarify and define the development process, concepts, attributes, types, prerequisites and consequences of organizational resilience, ultimately with the aim of improving relevant nursing strategies.
Design
Rodgers and Knafl’s evolutionary concept was applied to the concept analysis.
Methods
Five databases (MEDLINE, CINAHL, Web of Science, PubMed and Embase) were searched from 1973 to 2023 to identify relevant sources. Thematic analysis was used to form the conceptual connotation of organizational resilience.
Results
A total of 45 articles were included for synthesis. Alternative and related terms (mentioned in the literature) that share attributes with organizational resilience include crisis management and change management. Six antecedents of organizational resilience were identified: Resource, Communication, Social Networks, Infrastructure, Organizational Structure and People. Nine attributes of organizational resilience include: robustness, redundancy, rapidity, resourcefulness, diversity, adaption, self-regulation, flexibility and recoverability. Consequences of organizational resilience were separated into three themes: organization, employee and customer.
Conclusions
Organizational resilience delineates a coherent set of antecedents, attributes and consequences that can guide healthcare systems in calibrating their structures, processes and resources to context-specific demands. By internationalizing these qualities, organizations can systematically pursue resilience objectives, thereby sustaining adaptive capacity under conditions of stress and change. Our conceptual analysis of organizational resilience gives a complete comprehension of this phenomenon and a rationale for instrument development, future research and intervention.
Impact
This study defines the concept, antecedents, attributes and consequences of organizational resilience. By strengthening these elements, organizations can enhance their adaptability and long-term sustainability. The findings offer evidence-based guidance that healthcare organizations can apply to build resilience and effectively respond to future challenges.

Introduction
In recent years, pandemics, climate change, armed conflict and public health emergencies have converged, generating systemic risks with profound social and economic consequences. Accelerating globalization and an evolving geopolitical order are rendering the environments in which organizations operate increasingly volatile. Crises and disasters have become recurrent phenomena that organizations must anticipate and navigate. To survive and develop under such conditions—especially during major shocks – organizations must cultivate greater adaptive capacity. In 2020, the World Health Organization re-issued Strengthening the resilience of health systems: key concepts and strategies, asserting that the resilience of health systems is pivotal to managing catastrophic events. In addition, the World Health Organization has integrated resilience into the formulation of the 2030 Sustainable Development Goals. Thus, as a result, the importance and impact of resilience in international disaster-related research and national policies have increased in recent years.
Coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) has again exposed organizations’ capacity to absorb health shocks while sustaining core functions, prompting intensified interest – among both governments and institutions – in cultivating organizational resilience. Although scholarship and policy initiatives have proliferated, definitional and analytical consensus remains elusive; competing conceptualizations continue to circulate. In nursing, in particular, the construct remains notably under-theorized.

Background
Resilience – originally denoting toughness, elasticity and the ability to recover – refers to the capacity of a material to return rapidly to its initial state after deformation by external forces. It first appeared in the field of materials science. The construct emerged in materials science and has since evolved through three principal developmental phases. The first phase began in 1973, when C.S. Holling applied complex adaptive systems theory to ecological contexts, demonstrating that ecosystems reorganize in response to shifting environmental imperatives. Holling developed the concept of ecological resilience, defined as the magnitude of disturbance a system can absorb before shifting to an alternative stability domain. The concept has been applied to all fields of complex systems science (economics, politics, etc.) and featured prominently in the Resilience Alliance series, which sought to elucidate how systems reconfigure in response to perturbations. Since then, the concept of resilience has been widely used and popularized. Later, to adapt to sudden disasters and environmental changes, a combination of social, managerial, economic, psychological and other multidisciplinary content was gradually introduced into the field of social science, evolving into ecological social resilience, also known as evolutionary resilience. With the rapid change in the economic environment and the increasingly fierce competition, management scholars introduced the concept of resilience into the field of management and put forward the topic of organizational resilience.
In the wake of the 2014–2016 Ebola outbreak in West Africa, the WHO called on all countries to “build resilient and integrated systems capable of responding to and actively responding to any future threats”, and the concept of resilience is gradually being extended to health systems. Despite the growing significance of this field, academic research has seldom ventured beyond defining concepts and establishing frameworks. Furthermore, while numerous resilience measures for health systems are documented in the literature, there appears to be a gap in elucidating the relationship between resilience definitions and measurement methodologies. Therefore, it is urgent to establish a practical, evidence-based and comprehensive conceptual framework that can support cross-national comparisons, facilitate the implementation of plans for universal health coverage and ensure a systematic response to emergencies from a healthcare perspective.
Concept analysis is a systematic literature review method designed to clarify concepts that are theoretically significant in academic discourse but lack definitional clarity, carefully assessing the internal composition of complex concepts and identifying their components to improve their exploratory capabilities. Concept analysis helps to identify common meanings used in a discipline and provides a basis for educational, practical and theoretical development. Thus, to fill this knowledge gap, our study aimed to differentiate, clarify and identify the development, concept, attributes, types, prerequisites and consequences of organizational resilience, ultimately to contribute to improved strategies.

Methods

Study design
Resilience is an evolving concept, and Rodgers and Knafl’s (2000) evolutionary concept analysis approach accounts for the impact of dynamic changes such as temporal and contextual shifts on concepts, thereby effectively clarifying concepts with ambiguous meanings. Consequently, it was used to conduct this concept analysis because the emphasis is on inductive inquiry and robust analysis, which involves four stages: (1) identifying the intended concepts and their associated statements and alternative terms; (2) identifying and selecting an appropriate scope for data collection; (3) collecting data related to conceptual characteristics and aligned with contextual changes, including social, cultural, interdisciplinary and temporal changes (antecedents and consequences of the concept); and (4) taking into account the characteristics of the data analysis of the concept, identifying suitable examples related to the concept and identifying the assumptions and implications of the analysis to further develop the concept.

Data sources
Search terms included “resilience” or “resilient” or “resilienc*” and “organizations” or “organization” or “institution” or “system” or “organization*” in MEDLINE, CINAHL, Web of Science, PubMed and Embase. There was a limit from 1973 to 2023.
Inclusion criteria consisted of the following: (1) relevance to conceptually defined elements: Priority shall be given to studies offering unique insights into attributes, antecedents, consequences or alternative terminology about organizational resilience. This screening criterion narrows the research scope to studies providing substantive and nuanced perspectives on the core elements of this concept; (2) factors associated with organizational resilience; (3) primary research findings on theoretical or conceptual models or frameworks of organizational resilience; and (4) studies published in English.
Exclusion criteria of the following: (1) articles that lack references, authors and full text; (2) duplicate articles published by the same author in different journals; (3) focus on psychological, ecological, personal or other agent of resilience rather than organizational resilience; and (4) non-English publications.

Data analysis process
The analytical workflow comprised three sequential phases. First, Y.L.O., X.L. and C.N.W. performed a full-text review and imported the extracted data into a structured Excel matrix. Second, Y.P. and H.H.H. reviewed again for methodical information assortment and to guarantee the legitimacy of the coding and use point investigation to indicate attributes, antecedents and consequences. Third, Y.P. and H.H.H. utilized diagrams and networks, analysed every one of these classifications independently to distinguish key and thorough subjects, then sorted the discoveries into attributes, antecedents, consequences, related concepts, alternative terms and definitions.
Inside each segment, the information is explored on different occasions to empower specialists to delve into it and concentrate on central issues and names to give an unmistakable clarification of different parts of the idea. Finally, the data on the concept of organizational resilience are summarized and analysed, and the theme is determined. The data unit includes words and sentences that answer the question: What are the specific attributes of organizational resilience? How to define organizational resilience? What are the signs of organizational resilience? What factors are related to organizational resilience? What are the consequences of organizational resilience?

Related concepts

Crisis management
Crisis management is a ubiquitous concept often related to health-systems resilience, with a large number of reports, guidelines and strategic recommendations depending on context, type of crisis, geographic area, etc.. A resilient organization ought to know how to oversee emergencies. Endeavouring to make it resilient will require the creation and use of crisis management tools. Nevertheless, organizational resilience in a systemic way seems to be something wider, and crisis management is only one of the many instruments to achieve it. Disarray and shortcomings emerge especially when versatility is viewed as a program to be executed: Emergency management is one piece of what we ought to do, while resilience is what we ought to need.

Change management
Organizations are persistently exposed to dynamic forces – shifting climates, strategies, technologies, markets and stakeholder demands – whose consequences necessitate continuous adaptation. Change management, therefore, denotes the structured capacity to respond to such forces. Rather than being merely an adjacent construct, it functions as the mediating mechanism that links resilience to healthcare systems: Resilience becomes the intended outcome of change management, and the health system is the entity that must transform to attain it.

Antecedents
Antecedent variables describe the conditions or phenomena that precede, and are causally linked to, the emergence of a concept. In this study, the antecedents of organizational resilience were grouped into six domains: Resource, Communication, Social Networks, Infrastructure, Organizational Structure and People.

Resource-related factors
Resources constitute the material basis for organizational resilience in the face of shocks. They encompass sustained service-delivery capacity, economic strength, technology and expertise, and stockpiles of anti-epidemic and emergency supplies. Their accessibility is viewed as a key empowering influence of organizational resilience. Organizations need to create and shift resources to cope with unexpected situations. At the point when material resources are utilized in an intelligent way, organizations can defeat disturbance. Monetary resources are likewise viewed as important to prepare other required assets during a crisis; good cash flow helps organizations to maintain normal operations when a crisis comes.

Information-related factors
Organizational resilience is contingent upon the systematic management and utilization of information. Timely and accurate information has been identified as a critical determinant of how effectively organizations adapt to challenges. Ensuring the reliability, security, and flexibility of information and communication systems is of paramount importance. Organizations can distinguish the early warning flags that precede an emergency by checking the inside and outside conditions. At the same time, effective communication mechanisms ensure transparency and the smooth flow of information, enabling organizations to respond swiftly and adapt to environmental changes.

Social networks-related factors
The extent to which organizations can establish, maintain and leverage their social networks determines their resilience to both routine challenges and acute shocks. Social networks provide channels for the accelerated mobilization and transfer of information, as well as for the diffusion of innovations, thereby amplifying overall system resilience. Collaboration among organizations in a networked environment likewise extends assets that can be drawn on, the capacity to learn and its ability to respond, enhancing the efficiency and timeliness of information exchange between organizations. When organizations find themselves in adversity and require assistance, they can fully leverage their existing social network relationships to help navigate crises and restore operations.

Infrastructure-related factors
Infrastructure encompasses the physical and technical systems upon which an organization depends to sustain core operations and to bolster both adaptability and resilience. It comprises three inter-related domains: (1) built-environment design (safety, layout, other related channels, etc.); (2) utility networks (power, water supply, drainage, communication, firefighting, energy, etc.) and (3) strategic stockpiles of emergency materials. The construction and maintenance of infrastructure constitute the essential hardware facilities that ensure an organization’s continued operation in the face of diverse challenges and changes.

Organizational structure-related factors
An organizational structure constitutes the core guarantee of an effective emergency-response system; it denotes the capacity to detect, adapt to and resolve unforeseen events and disruptions. This capacity encompasses shock absorption, adaptation to interference, operational reconfiguration, innovation and continual change. Only through sustained executive attention, meticulous preparation, and iterative drills and after-action reviews can an organization build the resilience required to play a decisive role in disaster prevention, response and recovery.

People-related factors
Human capital theory emphasizes the importance of individuals’ educational background and experience as assets brought into organizations. As the key subject and important element of organizational resilience, people are the core driving force for successful responses to impact events. This includes the labour force and leadership. Labour force and its fundamental capabilities form the essential foundation of resilience, playing a crucial role in building an organization’s resilience to both routine challenges and severe disruptions. The significance of administration practices to the resilience of organizations is a repetitive subject across the chosen papers in both wellbeing and different areas, and for both intense and regular difficulties, leadership provides organizations with clear direction and support, fostering a positive organizational culture that encourages employees to embrace challenges and change, thereby promoting organizational innovation and self-improvement.

Attributes
Determining the critical traits of an idea is a significant stage in its explanation and refinement, which brings about a profound comprehension of the idea and its separation from different ideas. Attributes are the constituent elements that shape a concept. This study integrates conceptual attributes by identifying synonyms and clustering related items. (1) Robustness, which is the capacity to withstand stress without being affected by it, is used to describe resource intensity and is represented by a low probability of resource deterioration; (2) Redundancy refers to the degree to which elements are substitutable when resources are disrupted or depleted. A condition related to redundancy is resource diversity. Organizations that rely on resources within a narrow range are less able to inside changes in resource exhaustion, a state sometimes referred to as resource dependence; (3) Rapidity is the capability of an organization to achieve objectives on time, control damage, avoid disruptions, react quickly and recover; (4) Resourcefulness refers to the ability to access and mobilize resources when a situation threatens the organization, including external social resources as well as internal human, material and other resources to meet emergency needs; (5) Diversity means that the organization relies on a large number of people with different work abilities and qualities and a wealth of resources to provide high-quality services that meet people’s needs; (6) Adaptation is the ability of an organization to change its ways, to respond to crises and changing circumstances, to make rapid changes to improve its functioning and to assess its performance and improve it. Any organization that is adaptive should improve its performance in the short term to help build long-term resilience; (7) Self-regulation means that the organization does not depend on the government and outside help; through the organization’s internal network, rapid organization of self-help and mutual aid is facilitated; (8) Flexibility refers to the ability of an organization to adapt quickly to changes, challenges and crises in the internal and external environment, including the coordination of the functions of the infrastructure and the flexible trade-offs of management systems; (9) Recoverability is the ability of an organization to quickly return to normal operations after a shock or crisis, or even to a stronger state than before the crisis, and is a core component of organizational resilience.

Consequences and prevention
Consequences are the events or situations that follow from the emergence or application of a concept. Although the antecedents and attributes of organizational resilience have been extensively examined, the desired outcomes remain underspecified. Within the organizational resilience literature, consequences are typically grouped into three thematic domains:

Impact on the organization
Firstly, better crisis response capability: Organizations with resilience have a better crisis response capability and are able to respond to emergencies and crises more effectively, adapt quickly to changes and reduce negative impacts. Enhanced change management capability: Organizational resilience promotes better change management, enabling organizations to pass the transition period smoothly and seize the opportunities brought by change. Enhance organizational effectiveness: Through effective resource management and optimized work processes, organizational resilience can improve overall effectiveness. Secondly, enhance competitive advantage: Organizations that can quickly adapt to environmental changes and challenges are more likely to stay ahead of the competition. Thirdly, improved innovation: resilient organizations are more inclined to encourage innovative thinking and experimentation, which can help drive the development of services and solutions. Improve organizational reputation and image: Organizations that are able to respond effectively to crises and challenges usually earn public respect and trust, which enhances their organizational image. Fourthly, promote continuous learning and growth: Organizational resilience promotes a culture of continuous learning, which helps the organization and its employees learn and grow in the face of difficulties and failures. Fifth, long-term stability and sustainable development: Resilient organizations are better able to cope with long-term challenges and achieve sustainable development.

Impact on employee
Employees in resilient organizations experience higher job satisfaction and perceive greater job security, resulting in improved retention. Moreover, the process of collectively surmounting challenges and attaining objectives deepens their engagement with, and commitment to, the organization’s mission and strategic goals.

Impact on customer
Enhancement of customer satisfaction and wellbeing: Resilient organizations usually provide a variety of adaptive services and activities to ensure service quality and supply stability and at the same time are able to meet the service needs of customers, thus enhancing customer satisfaction and wellbeing. Collectively, the findings indicate that organizational resilience yields positive outcomes for the organization, its employees and its customers.

Model case
Rodgers’ evolutionary approach encourages the identification of model cases that utilize all the essential attributes described in the literature being analysed. Model cases depict practical demonstrations of concepts analysed in real life. None of the included studies possessed all of the attributes of the conceptual components of organizational resilience proposed in the included studies. The following is a typical case based on the first author’s experience.
Qinggang Nursing Home is the first hospital-affiliated elderly care institution approved by the National Development and Reform Commission – a pilot project for the construction of the national basic elderly care service system in 2009. It is the first large-scale public hospital-sponsored elderly care institution in China. It proclaimed a “condition of caution” and took on a progression of versatile procedures when the coronavirus pandemic was first announced. During the COVID-19 outbreak, the organization immediately declared a condition of readiness and embraced a progression of resilience strategies, starting with leaders immediately activating emergency plans, setting up emergency management teams and unifying the organization. In terms of infrastructure, the organization separated the working area, the living area and the isolation area by dividing the major partitions and establishing a buffer zone to block the spread of the virus from the space aspect. In terms of information, the organization continuously collected data to respond to repeated changes and shocks through the monitoring and recording of health data of people within the organization, as well as lessons learned and changes in external data. In terms of resources, the organization immediately mobilized the available resources within the organization and rationally planned their use. Moreover, it set up a receiving and testing point for external drugs, express bundles and vehicles to guarantee the inventory of materials during the epidemic. In terms of social networks, the organization has built a close social network with relevant medical institutions. When there is a lack of material resources in the organization, the medical institutions support them. At the same time, the medical institutions also play an important role in guaranteeing a change in the elderly’s condition in the organization, and once the condition changes, they will be transferred to the medical institutions for treatment. In terms of people, the epidemiological detection, monitoring and prevention of all employees should be conducted according to the infectious characteristics of COVID-19, and online training should be conducted for employees, such as basic knowledge of COVID-19, hand hygiene, wearing of medical masks, disinfection, etc. At the same time, leaders also paid attention to changes in employees’ workload, timely appeased their negative emotions and provided support, such as psychological counselling. When the epidemic situation was stable, organization leaders did not relax their vigilance, but helped the organization quickly recover from the epidemic state, and operate normally. Finally, leaders drew lessons from this outbreak to make up for infrastructure and resource deficiencies and updated and improved emergency plans to help organizations stay in good shape when the next shock hits. Qinggang Nursing Home had good resilience during the impact period; therefore, the organization was not affected by the impact but still operates well, had zero infections among 335 residents and 276 healthcare personnel, and maintained good vitality during the impact.

Discussion
Frequent public health events in recent years, especially COVID-19, which had a wide negative radiation effect, have made the public realize that only by placing an organization in the context of a major emergency and seeking actual improvement of the organization’s comprehensive capacity with the help of resilient governance strategies can an organization achieve a forward-looking transformation from its vulnerability to resilience. By establishing organizational resilience governance mechanisms that integrate with major disaster response systems, organizations can overcome practical challenges encountered during disaster management and response—such as inherent vulnerabilities and surges in medical demand—to enhance their capacity for effective disaster mitigation and recovery.
Until now, a large part of the writing that explicitly references organizational resilience has zeroed in on high-level attributes, as opposed to recognizing explicit limits that the organization should be adaptable to in response to unavoidable illness flare-ups and regular risks. The concept of organizational resilience adds dynamism and resilience to the long-standing work of the organization, fortifying and offering an opportunity to gain from different areas. This study pointed toward giving a clearer depiction of organizational resilience by indicating various components of organizational resilience and recognizing it from comparable concepts and phrasings. Our study analyses the conceptual evolution, attributes, characteristics, related concepts, influencing factors, endings and measurement indexes of organizational resilience through Rodgers’ evolutionary concept analysis, which helps to deepen the public’s understanding of organizational resilience, and pay attention to resilience construction and enhancement, to effectively respond to emergencies.
Although different organizations face different risk probabilities of natural event threats and social event threats, organizational resilience could provide a new observation perspective for internal management and external governance of organizations, and is conducive to building a normalized risk treatment platform for organizations in dynamic uncertainty. The overall direction for what organizational resilience involves and its overall qualities could assist an organization with focusing on its activities and systems within its unique circumstance and arrive at its resilience objective, accordingly adding to resilience worldwide. Our study found that organizational resilience is described mainly in terms of resources, communication, social networks, infrastructure, organizational structure and people. The connections among the blocks make up a resilient organization. The linkages among the blocks, including the role of people as actors who drive the system forward, are fundamental to understanding a resilient organization. This provides the theoretical basis for the development of relevant assessment tools.
We have to admit that there are some limitations to our study. Owing to the fact that there is not enough research on organizational resilience in nursing and articles from other fields are widely included, there may be some differences in organizational resilience in nursing. Therefore, further selection, modification or validation of reliable measures is needed before they can be used in empirical studies. Further consideration by experts is required before they can be developed into a user-friendly instrument. The measures in the framework are still presented in the form of a checklist with no clear evaluation criteria or benchmarks. This is because the existing evidence base is insufficient to identify capacity or process measures that are relevant to the desired outcomes or at a level that is considered appropriate. In addition, the research methodology of the conceptual analysis itself has limitations in terms of the research process and the researcher’s perspective that may lead to bias. In the future, empirical results from the survey will be needed to further test the feasibility and validity of the measures and to further develop them into evaluation measurement tools (e.g. self-report questionnaires). Detailed information about the average or desired level of competence or performance of the pilot area hospitals could then be provided explicitly. In the future, the authors will continue to explore the application of organizational resilience in nursing and conduct a time study to delve into the factors influencing organizational resilience in nursing, develop a rational assessment tool, and promote the improvement of the quality of nursing services.

Conclusions
A conceptual understanding of organizational resilience is critical and could provide the intellectual basis for an integrated approach to systems development. To date, there is no agreed framework for organizations to form the basis for resilience measurement. This study (1) defines organizational resilience; (2) constructs a conceptual framework (including key domains); (3) proposes a comprehensive measure for potential assessment inclusion; and (4) develops a critical issues matrix to enhance resilience against future disasters. Findings from this concept analysis contribute to the understanding and clarity of organizational resilience in the context of crisis, providing a theoretical basis for the evaluation and quantification of organizational resilience.
