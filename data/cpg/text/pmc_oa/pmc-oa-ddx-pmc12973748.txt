Quantitative evaluation of computational fluid dynamics application development in the cardiovascular field through literature retrieval and bibliometric analysis
Background and objective
The application of computational fluid dynamics (CFD) in the cardiovascular field has been increasingly observed to analyze hemodynamic conditions within intravascular lumens. This study aims to quantitatively elucidate the development of CFD technology on hemodynamics over the past two decades through literature retrieval and bibliometric analysis.
Methods
The literature retrieval is conducted using the Web of Science database, where all academic articles concerning hemodynamic analysis using CFD technology in the past 20 years are included. The retrieval strategy was primarily based on three aspects: time, cardiovascular anatomical parts, and cardiovascular diseases.
Results
Over the past two decades, the publication of CFD-focused articles in the cardiovascular field has grown steadily at an average annual rate of 10.19%, with a stable distribution across anatomical parts. A similar overall trend is observed for research on cardiovascular diseases (11.89% annual growth). However, in recent years, the growth rates for publications on individual diseases have begun to diverge significantly.
Conclusions
The quantitative evidence from literature retrieval and bibliometric analysis shows the continuous development of CFD technology in the cardiovascular field over the past two decades. The consistent distribution of research across different cardiovascular anatomical parts suggests a balanced development process. However, the development of CFD technology on specific cardiovascular diseases might perform distinctively in the coming years.

Introduction
Computational fluid dynamics (CFD) is progressively utilized for the analysis of the hemodynamic condition in the cardiovascular system. Moreover, this technology is widely applied in the clinical management of cardiovascular diseases, extending its utility from diagnosis to prognostic prediction (e.g., forecasting aneurysm growth and thrombus formation) and interventional optimization. In the past few decades, an abundance of related academic studies have been published, encompassing various aspects of the cardiovascular system, including the heart, aorta, artery, and vein. Diverse cardiovascular diseases, such as coronary artery disease (CAD), dissection, aneurysms, and atherosclerosis also have been comprehensively analyzed for the exploration of pathological mechanisms through CFD simulations. Additionally, there are specific CFD studies focused on investigating the hemodynamic changes resulting from stenting, offering valuable insights for surgeons in surgical management. Hemodynamic parameters obtained from CFD analysis, such as pressure, velocity, wall shear stress (WSS), oscillating shear index (OSI), relative residence time (RRT), and fractional flow reserve (FFR) have been extensively investigated to elucidate the pathology and clinical diagnosis of associated cardiovascular diseases. In recent years, additional advanced indices, including endothelial cell activation potential (ECAP) and flow helicity, have been increasingly employed to provide deeper insights into complex flow phenomena and their biological impacts in the cardiovascular-related studies. Nevertheless, despite the widespread use of CFD technology in cardiovascular research and its increasing adoption in clinical practice, a comprehensive quantitative assessment of its development in the field over the past few decades remains undisclosed. Furthermore, considering the broad application scope across different anatomical parts of the cardiovascular system and various cardiovascular diseases, it is unclear which particular subdivision within the cardiovascular field garners the most attention in CFD research due to the absence of related researches. Therefore, conducting bibliometric analyses of relevant academic studies becomes imperative to gain an overall understanding of the historical development trends and current state of CFD utilization in the cardiovascular field, thereby facilitating decision-making processes for research institutes and commercial entities.

Methods
We conducted a comprehensive literature retrieval of academic articles published within the last two decades (from January 1, 2005, to December 31, 2024) using various categories, for instance different cardiovascular anatomical parts and diseases in the Web of Science (WoS) in the current study. The number of academical articles corresponding to each specific category searched in the WoS database was documented and arranged. These results provide an intuitive representation of the evolving landscape of academic CFD studies within the cardiovascular field in the past two decades, enabling us to make predictions regarding future development trends. Additionally, by analyzing the discrepancies in article counts across different categories, we could identify which areas of the cardiovascular system and types of cardiovascular diseases garner greater emphasis in CFD simulations.
As an online accessible web search engine that indexes scholarly literature from various publishing formats and disciplines, the WoS database provides bibliographic information and information analysis resources that allow the evaluation and analysis of research tendency. To ensure the robustness of our trend analysis, we initially performed parallel searches across three major academic databases: Web of Science (WoS), Google Scholar, and PubMed. This multi-database comparison aimed to validate whether the observed growth in CFD-related cardiovascular literature was a consistent phenomenon, independent of the indexing characteristics of any single source. The high degree of consistency in the temporal trends obtained from all three databases (Fig. 2) confirmed the reliability of the observed developmental trajectory. However, for the subsequent detailed bibliometric analysis, including keyword co-occurrence and clustering, the WoS Core Collection was selected as the primary database. This choice aligns with standard practices in bibliometric research, as WoS provides a curated, high-quality citation index that is widely used and validated for scientometric studies. Its comprehensive coverage of peer-reviewed journals across multiple disciplines makes it a suitable and authoritative database for analyzing publication trends within a well-defined field such as cardiovascular CFD.
In the present investigation, the focus was placed on three main aspects to understand the overall application development of computational fluid dynamics in the cardiovascular field: time, cardiovascular anatomical parts, and cardiovascular diseases. To achieve this goal, a systematic keyword search strategy was designed, and bibliometric keywords analysis and visualization are accomplished by CiteSpace.

The number of academic articles retrieved from each year, ranging from 2005.01.01 to 2024.12.31, was accumulated and the retrieval data collection was accomplished from 2025.05.01 to 2025.08.30. It is worth noting that the counting not only includes studies specifically focused on CFD simulations of the heart, but also includes studies that have considered or mentioned both CFD and the heart at the same time in these researches. By considering a wide range of studies related to CFD and the cardiovascular system, the present study aims to provide a comprehensive understanding of the overall situation of CFD technology development tends in the cardiovascular field in the past two decades.
The application of CFD analysis on cardiovascular diseases has also been frequently investigated nowadays. Since the concept of FFR was proposed in 1996 for the quantitative evaluation of CAD and the range between 0.75 and 0.80 is considered clinical stenosis significance by reviews of clinical trials, the combination of computed tomography (CT) scan images and CFD simulation is considered as another reliable approach to obtain FFR (FFRCT) instead of coronary catheterization. Another widely investigated application of CFD is to reveal the relationship between the hemodynamic parameters (such as WSS and OSI) obtained from CFD simulations and the formation, development, and rupture of aneurysms. Furthermore, the associated researches of CFD analysis have involved multiple cardiovascular diseases, which include dissection, atherosclerosis, and thrombosis. We selected several mostly common or concerned diseases representative of multifarious cardiovascular diseases which are investigated by CFD technology and aneurysm, dissection, coronary artery disease (CAD), atherosclerosis, thrombosis, mitral regurgitation (MR), and stenosis.

Discussion
The progressive development of CFD technology in the cardiovascular field in the past two decades has been quantitatively confirmed by the retrieved data in the present investigation. Actually such a tendency has already been noticed by the academic community and can also be indirectly confirmed by the growth of related commercial entities. However, it seems researchers in the field face both opportunities and challenges when conducting hemodynamic analyses through CFD simulations. It is important to understand the development trends of CFD application in the cardiovascular field over time and be aware of the research directions that have received the most attention in recent years.

CFD technology in cardiovascular field over time
The number of retrieved articles for all major anatomical cardiovascular parts has increased progressively from 71 in 2005 to 449 in 2024, reaching the average annual growth rate of 10.19% further supports the sustained development of CFD applications in the past 20 years. Data obtained on diverse cardiovascular diseases exhibit a comparable trend and the average annual growth rate in the past two decades has reached 12.01%. The steady climb in CFD-related cardiovascular literature signals the technology's expanding influence and esteemed role in the field. These sustained growth trajectories over 20 years highlight the increasing adoption of CFD as a noninvasive and reliable approach for hemodynamic analysis. The growing accessibility of computationally intensive CFD simulations to research and commercial entities is primarily driven by advances in computing power and numerical algorithms, facilitating the digital reconstruction of intraluminal hemodynamics. Concurrently, the improvement of high-quality medical imaging, for instance magnetic resonance imaging (MRI)—a key theme identified by CiteSpace keyword analysis (Fig. 5) and computed tomography angiography (CTA), is critical for obtaining precise morphological information of cardiovascular system. Progress in imaging technology has facilitated the accurate reconstruction of geometry during the pre-processing phase, thereby further propelling the application of CFD in cardiovascular research.
Furthermore, modern imaging extends beyond static anatomy to directly inform the hemodynamic simulation itself. Techniques such as phase-contrast MRI can provide patient-specific in vivo flow velocity data, which serve as crucial boundary conditions or validation benchmarks for CFD models. Another medical imaging technology, electrocardiogram (ECG)-gated multi-phase CT enables the capture of dynamic vascular geometry changes throughout the cardiac cycle, allowing for more physiologically accurate, time-resolved simulations. This dual role of imaging, which provides both accurate anatomy and dynamic physiological data, is fundamental to enhancing the fidelity and clinical relevance of cardiovascular CFD studies.
Meanwhile, the convergence of CFD and Artificial Intelligence (AI) has attracted increasing scholarly interest in cardiovascular hemodynamics in recent years, despite the absence of keyword "AI" in Fig. 5. This integration is poised to fundamentally transform the field by enabling key advancements such as physics-informed surrogate models for real-time clinical decision support and the automation of complex workflows from medical imaging to simulation. These developments might shift the paradigm from retrospective analysis to predictive, personalized hemodynamic assessment, thereby facilitating the discovery of novel biomarkers and optimized therapeutic planning for cardiovascular diseases and further accelerating the adoption of CFD in clinical medicine. Nevertheless, scientific questions regarding the validity and reliability of AI-driven hemodynamic analyses persist. It is therefore imperative that these results be treated with caution in the context of clinical application.
In summary, the data collected indicate a positive outlook for the future of CFD technology in cardiovascular research. The increasing number of academic articles and the continuous growth trend suggest that CFD will continue to play a significant role in advancing our understanding of cardiovascular fluid dynamics in coming years.

Application of CFD on different cardiovascular parts
Figure 1 shows the proportion of academic articles that have focused on or mentioned CFD and distinctive cardiovascular parts in the past two decades. The area of the circles representing each cardiovascular part is proportional to the number of retrieved articles, providing an intuitive impression of the distribution of CFD-based hemodynamic analysis. The publication data, categorized by cardiovascular anatomical parts, facilitates a granular analysis of the expanding scope of CFD applications. Over the past 20 years, research using CFD simulations has been overwhelmingly focused on the heart and arteries. Together, studies on these two areas constituted nearly 80% (79.27%) of all publications pertaining to the six major parts of the cardiovascular system (Fig. 4), with individual shares of 40.27% and 39.00%, respectively. The concentration of CFD research on the heart and arteries is a typical outcome driven by clinical needs and supported by technical feasibility. These areas bear the heaviest disease burden, and their pathological processes are most directly linked to hemodynamics. At the same time, current medical imaging technologies are best suited to support precise CFD modeling of these regions. This high degree of focus reflects how CFD, as an engineering tool, evolves in close alignment with the fundamental goal of addressing critical medical challenges.
To investigate the preference of CFD-based analysis on different cardiovascular parts at different periods, we equally split the past 20 years into four periods. The proportions of retrieved researches focused on different cardiovascular parts in every 5 years are illustrated in Fig. 4. Interestingly, the distributions of related research proportions are relatively consistent, with no significant variation in the study preference in the four divided periods. This remarkable consistency leads us to speculate that the current distribution of CFD applications across cardiovascular anatomies will remain unchanged for years to come.
The arranged data show that the increased tendency in the overall number of articles also corresponds to the academic research numbers for each specific cardiovascular part. The average annual growth rates for the heart, aorta, artery, vein, and vena cava over the past 20 years are 10.61%, 12.12%, 10.40%, 10.29%, and 16.43% separately, which are relatively close to the average annual growth rate of academic researches for all the major anatomical parts of cardiovascular system obtained from Fig. 2. These bibliometric results suggest that the growing application of CFD in the cardiovascular field is a generalized trend, not limited to specific anatomical regions. This widespread adoption signifies that CFD has achieved considerable acceptance and recognition within the medical community.
Blood capillary represents a notable exception, with significantly less application of CFD technology, as evidenced by the retrieval of only a single related article. This scarcity is primarily due to the challenges of geometric reconstruction, a fundamental prerequisite for CFD. The acquisition of accurate three-dimensional capillary geometry is difficult because of their minute dimensions and intricate architecture. These features, combined with the unique fluid dynamics present at the micro-scale, render accurate hemodynamic modeling and simulation highly challenging. Nevertheless, investigating capillaries is critically important due to their essential role in nutrient and waste exchange. Further research could yield valuable insights into microcirculatory function and its impact on cardiovascular health.
To investigate research focus in greater detail, major cardiovascular anatomical parts like the heart and arteries were segmented into subcategories. For the heart, this included the atria, ventricles, and cardiac valves (mitral, tricuspid, aortic, and pulmonary). Our retrieval yielded related articles for each specific subcategory. The analysis reveals a disparity in research attention: ventricles are mentioned more frequently than atria in CFD studies, and the aortic valve receives significantly more focus than the other valves. Meanwhile, retrieved academic investigations using CFD demonstrates that coronary and carotid arteries are more prominent than other arterial sites, though studies on other different arterial regions have also received considerable attention. The widespread application of CFD across cardiovascular parts reflects the field's consensus on the critical importance of hemodynamics, solidifying CFD as an indispensable analytical tool.

Application of CFD on different cardiovascular diseases
One of the major concerns of retrieved articles focused on the CFD analysis for the understanding of related cardiovascular diseases, such as atherosclerosis, coronary artery disease (CAD), aneurysm based on clustering analysis (shown in Fig. 5). We plotted the related academic article numbers for the selected cardiovascular diseases over time (shown in Fig. 6). The development tendencies for the researches on different phenotypes of cardiovascular diseases studied by CFD also show a high correspondence with the illustrated curves for different cardiovascular parts, which indicate the application of CFD on different cardiovascular diseases is also continuously developing during the past 20 years. The annual average growth rates for aneurysm, CAD, atherosclerosis, thrombosis, and stenosis from 2005 to 2024 are 11.36%, 18.21%, 7.90%, 11.79%, and 12.88%, respectively (shown in Table 1). In recent years, the numerical analyses of hemodynamics based on CFD are more likely be utilized on dissection and the average annual growth rates in the past 3 and 5 years have reached to 21.54% and 16.65%, even though there are no academic articles retrieved 20 years ago. In contrast, the growth rate of academic CFD studies considered or mentioned aneurysm performs a slowdown and the average annual growth rates in the past 3 and 5 years are −5.47% and 1.20%, respectively.
Bibliometric clustering analysis of CFD research on aneurysms and dissections is presented in Fig. 7. The keyword clustering analysis results (Fig. 7a) for aneurysm category shows a concentration of terms like wall shear stress (WSS) and cerebral aneurysms on the early segment of the timeline (pre- or circa 2005). The absence of major new keywords in recent years, with the exception of fluid–structure interaction (FSI), suggests a stagnation in conceptual innovation within this sub-field. The declining annual growth rate in aneurysm research in recent years (Fig. 6), despite its continued dominance in publication volume, may be attributable to this intellectual stagnation. In contrast, the timeline for aortic dissection research (Fig. 7b) shows a more dispersed and dynamic keyword distribution. The two primary clusters, #0 thoracic endovascular aortic repair and #1 aortic dissection, not only represent the historical focus of the field, but also indicate critical directions for future investigative efforts.
Overall, the retrieved data support the notion that CFD technology is being increasingly applied to various parts of the cardiovascular system and different phenotypes of cardiovascular diseases. This further emphasizes the growing acceptance and recognition of CFD's value in cardiovascular research. Such a tendency also reflect on the research status for each specific subdivision category. However, future development trends are likely to diverge among different cardiovascular diseases based on the demonstrated results.

Limitations
Several limitations should be acknowledged in this study. First, the bibliometric analysis was conducted exclusively through the Web of Science (WoS) database, which was considered to contain sufficient and representative literature for tracking technology development in this field. While WoS provides a well-curated and widely recognized corpus for scholarly evaluation, it is important to note that certain proprietary, regional, or newly published content may not be fully indexed, and further validation using additional databases (e.g., Scopus, PubMed) could strengthen the generalizability of our findings. Second, our retrieval strategy relied on keyword-based search queries centered on the term "computational fluid dynamics". Although this approach ensures a focused analysis on studies explicitly employing or referencing CFD methodology, it may not capture all relevant investigations that use synonymous or related terminology without explicitly mentioning CFD. Furthermore, the retrieved article counts primarily reflect the co-occurrence frequency between CFD and selected anatomical or disease terms, rather than detailing the specific methodological emphasis or clinical context of each study. Therefore, while the trends identified are indicative of broader research activity, readers should interpret these quantitative results with an understanding of these inherent constraints in keyword-based bibliometric analysis.

Conclusion
In the present investigation, the literature retrieval and statistical analysis results based on the WoS database quantitatively indicate the application of CFD technology in the cardiovascular field has progressively developed in the past 20 years. The stable proportional research focus on various cardiovascular components across different periods suggests a maturing, balanced field, rather than concentrated growth in isolated areas, and the heart and artery have received the most attention. We also quantified a continuous increase in CFD publications addressing cardiovascular diseases. Nonetheless, future development trends for CFD applications are anticipated to diverge across different disease categories.
