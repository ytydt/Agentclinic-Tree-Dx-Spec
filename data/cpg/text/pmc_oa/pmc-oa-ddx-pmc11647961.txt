Global research trends in the teacher evaluation of medical college: A bibliometric and visualized study
Background
The evaluation of education in colleges and universities is not only an essential means to promote teachers' professional development and ensure high-quality development of education but also a strong support for the overall development of colleges and universities. In this study, to understand the development trends and predict the future of teacher evaluation in medical colleges from 2005 to 2023, we conducted a comprehensive bibliometric and visualized study using Web of Science.
Methods
The articles on teacher evaluation in medical colleges were extracted from the "Core Collection" of the WoS database. VOSviewer software, CiteSpace and R-Bibliometrix Package were employed to visually analyze countries/regions, journals, authors, keywords, institutions, and highly cited articles in this field.
Results
The number of articles on teacher evaluation in medical colleges has gradually increased. The United States has the most significant number of publications in this field. The most frequently used keywords were "medical education," "education," "teaching," "assessment," and "curriculum." BMC Medical Education was the leading journal. The leading institutions were Pontificia Universidad Católica de Chile and the University of Toronto. Furthermore, Singh T, Roberts C, Riquelme A, Raupach T, and Padilla O published the most papers.
Conclusion
This study's results indicate that teacher evaluation in medical colleges remains a significant area of research worldwide. The findings will contribute to the ongoing study of teacher evaluation in medical colleges.

Introduction
Shinkfield and Stufflebeam (1995) define teacher evaluation as the systematic assessment of a teacher's performance and qualifications about the teacher's defined professional role and the school district mission. The evaluation of teachers represents a fundamental aspect of the assessment of educational institutions. The evaluation of medical education in medical colleges is closely related to the development of medical education. Teachers in colleges and universities constitute an essential component of professional and technical talent and play a pivotal role in advancing education and cultivating high-level talent. The content of the evaluation determines the value orientation of teachers' work and the quality of education. Consequently, numerous medical colleges across the globe are striving to implement a rigorous and efficacious teacher evaluation framework to guarantee the formation of a robust teaching corps, enhance the caliber of talent development, and facilitate the advancement of medical education. In particular, the Chinese government is intensifying its efforts to eliminate the unscientific orientation of teacher evaluation with a policy commonly called "scores-oriented, graduation-oriented, certification-oriented, publication-oriented, and title-oriented evaluation." Furthermore, many colleges and universities worldwide are attempting to implement a highly diverse and interactive evaluation system. Currently, the teacher evaluation in medical colleges is a subject of intense research interest among scholars in the field of medical education.
Bibliometrics is a quantitative discipline in literary research that employs statistical and visual analysis techniques. The discipline of bibliometrics can be traced back to the early 20th century. In 1917, philologists F. J. Cole and Nellie B. Eales employed the methodology of literary statistical analysis to statistically analyze the comparative anatomy literature published from the 1600s to the 1860s. The term "bibliometrics" was first used by Pritchard in 1969, defined as "the application of mathematical and statistical methods to books and other media of communication". Subsequently, the term "bibliometrics" has been widely adopted and refined by researchers. Bibliometrics is generally regarded as a quantitative citation and content analysis method for scholarly journals and books. At present, bibliometrics is employed to describe the quantity, quality, distribution, and development trend of published literature in a specific field, thereby identifying the most active research areas and anticipating future research directions. In particular, with the development of document analysis and visualization software, including VOSviewer, CiteSpace, BICOMB, bibexcel, and R, bibliometrics has facilitated the widespread use of bibliometrics in the research of library science, medicine, higher education, laws, environmental science, sports science, and other fields.
Despite the plethora of studies and review articles on teacher evaluation in medical colleges, a dearth of articles has examined the relationship among research or summarized the global research trends in this field. The objective of this study was to utilize VOSviewer, R, and Microsoft Excel to conduct a comprehensive bibliometric visual analysis of the research literature on the teacher evaluation in medical colleges. This study presents a comprehensive analysis of the characteristics and evolution of the research literature on the teacher evaluation in medical colleges. It describes the growth in the number of publications, national and international collaborations, institutions, journals, authors, and keyword co-occurrence. The findings will provide new insights and inspiration for future research.

Material and methods

Material and search strategy
Flowchart of data search strategy.
Fig. 1
The bibliographical data used in this study were obtained from the Web of Science (WoS) Core Collection database. WoS is a robust multidisciplinary database by Clarivate that supports global academic research across sciences, social sciences, and arts and humanities. Literature search was conducted on January 2, 2024 without language restriction. Fig. 1 illustrates the study strategy employed in this study. The search formula included the following terms: topic=("medical college" AND "teacher evaluation") or topic= ("medical college" AND "teachers assessment") or topic= ("medical college" AND "teacher appraisal"). The search formula included the following terms: topic=("medical university" AND "teacher evaluation") topic= ("medical university" AND "teachers assessment"), topic= ("medical university" AND "teacher appraisal"), topic= ("medical university" AND "evaluation of teachers"), topic= ("medical college" AND "evaluation of teachers"), topic= ("medical schools" AND "evaluation of teachers"), topic= ("medical schools" AND "teachers assessment"), topic= ("medical schools" AND "teachers appraisal"), and topic= ("medical schools" AND "teachers evaluation"). All the retrieved records were exported in a plain text file format, with the record content comprising the entire record and cited references. Subsequently, all files were added to a zip file and imported into bibliometric tools for further analysis.

Data analysis and visualization software
All downloaded data were imported into VOSviewer (version 1.6.18), CiteSpace (version 6.3R3), R (version 4.2.1), and Microsoft Excel 2010 for bibliometric analysis. VOSviewer is a software tool designed to construct and visualize bibliometric networks. VOSviewer enables the construction of networks of authors or journals based on co-citation data or keywords based on co-occurrence data. In VOSviewer's network visualization maps, the circle and label form an element, with the element's size dependent on several factors, including the degree of the node, the strength of the line, the number of citations, and so forth. Furthermore, the various clusters are represented by different colors. In this study, VOSviewer is employed to generate bibliographic coupling maps of organizations and co-citation maps of articles. CiteSpace, leveraging quantitative analysis and visualization technologies, empowers researchers to delve into and comprehend the intricate knowledge structures and evolving trends within academic disciplines. Its robust capabilities enable seamless processing of scientific literature data sourced from diverse databases, transforming these vast information sets into intuitive, visually compelling graphs and diagrams. R is a free software environment for statistical computing and graphics. It can compile and run on a wide variety of UNIX platforms, including Windows and MacOS. The bibliometrix package is an R-tool for comprehensive science mapping analysis. The bibliometric R-package offers quantitative research tools for bibliometrics and scientometrics. The bibliometric R-package was employed to analyze countries and regions, core sources, author keywords and keywords plus, and institutions. Microsoft Excel was employed primarily for constructing tables and displaying annual national publications and citation trends.

This result indicated that the regression equation accurately described the correlation between the year and the number of publications. Furthermore, there was a growing trend in the number of publications from 2005 to 2023. As illustrated in Fig. 2C, the number of citations exhibited a gradual increase over time, reaching its peak in 2023 (n = 1688). In contrast, the number of publications reached its highest in 2021 and 2022 (n = 77).

Discussion
Teacher evaluation in medical colleges is a process in which evaluators collect the evaluation materials according to specific evaluation standards and make value judgments about medical teachers' performance and abilities. Theoretically, the purpose of teacher evaluation is to serve as a professional development process and quality assurance mechanism. Scientific and objective evaluation of teachers' work plays a vital role in teachers' personal growth and development, as well as in school and teacher management. It has attracted considerable attention from college teachers, students’ parents, school leaders and social media. Public schools in the United States have undergone widespread reform in recent decades to redesign teacher evaluation systems. Teacher evaluation in medical colleges is one of the core contents of medical school evaluation and has become a research “hotspot” for medical education scholars.
In this study, we conducted a bibliometric analysis to analyze the global research trends in the teacher evaluation of medical colleges on the outputs, countries, keywords, journals, institutions, authors, and citation of the publication. We found that teacher evaluation in medical colleges has been widely researched. Eight hundred sixty-seven publications were published from 2005 to 2023, and the number of publications per year showed a clear upward trend. In particular, the number of publications increased rapidly, especially from 2019 to 2023. This may indicate that the evaluation of medical college teachers has received more attention over the past decade.
In terms of national or regional influence, the United States published the most articles (191 articles), accounting for 22.03 % of the total number of documents published, followed by China (75 articles, 8.65 %) and Germany (75 articles, 8.65 %). The United States also ranked first with 3926 citations, followed by Germany (1575) and the United Kingdom (1367). These results indicate that the research level of the United States has been at the forefront in this field. It is worth noting that the average number of citations in the Netherlands is as high as 26.60, higher than that in the United States (20.60). The articles published by the United Kingdom had a more significant academic impact. International collaboration is an essential feature of scientific research. The degree of cooperation can reflect mutual penetration and overlap of research fields as well as the quality and quantity of studies in research fields. The United States competently cooperates with other countries, such as Canada and the United Kingdom. China mainly cooperated with the United States and Australia, and their cooperation has been more intense. These results show that China should strengthen international cooperation. Keywords are an essential research topic, indicating the core content of a literature. Through the bibliometric analysis of the author's keywords and keywords plus, we accurately revealed the “hotspots” and overall development trend of teacher evaluation in medical college. Our study showed that "medical education", "education", and "medical students" appeared in both author keywords and keywords plus. This may indicate that the above keywords were the hot topics regarding teacher evaluation in medical colleges from 2005 to 2023. We also summarized the co-occurrence network of author keywords. The author keywords "medical education", "evaluation" and "curriculum" had the highest total link strength. These results indicate that the above three keywords were the hot topics regarding teacher evaluation in medical colleges from 2005 to 2023.
In terms of journal influence, our results showed that the top three prolific journals were "BMC Medical Education" (impact factor = 3.651), "Medical Teacher" (impact factor = 4.763), and "Academic Medicine" (impact factor = 7.498). It can be seen that the impact factor of "Medical Teacher" and "BMC Medical Education" is less than 5 in 2023. This may indicate that the research results were mainly published in the journal with moderate academic impact. In addition, the most frequently cited article was published in "Academic Medicine" (impact factor = 7.498). This article is an essential milestone in evaluating medical schoolteachers and has important clinical implications.
Regarding institutional influence, 'Pontificia Universidad Católica de Chil' and 'University of Toronto' have the most significant number of publications (n = 37), followed by 'University of California San Francisco' (n = 33). These universities are from Chile, Canada, and the United States. Interestingly, 'University of Michigan' (n = 483) and 'University of California San Francisco' (n = 387) are the two institutions with the highest number of citations. These universities are all from the United States. These results suggest that 'University of California, San Francisco' has higher academic performance. In terms of author influence, Roberts C made the most outstanding contribution to medical school faculty evaluation, who has published five articles since 2011, with the highest h-index (n = 4) and g-index (n = 5). Among the top 20 authors, 'Medical education' was the most frequently used author keyword, with seven incoming flow counts.
Literature co‐citation analysis reflects the knowledge base of research hotspots and frontiers. The analysis results showed that this research field has constructed a high-density network composed of 1111 nodes gathered in 15 clusters. The results further confirm the high degree of aggregation, uniqueness, and consistency of each cluster in the network around specific research topics or perspectives. It is particularly noteworthy that the cluster of "COVID-19 pandemic" not only highlights the profound impact of the global pandemic on teacher evaluation research, but also strongly calls for a re-examination and adjustment of evaluation criteria of medical teacher. The display of the timeline chart reveals the dynamic evolution trajectory of research topics over time, including the persistence of COVID-19-related research and medical student education clusters, further emphasizing the long-term value and profound significance of research in these fields. Looking to the future, research should focus on deepening the analysis of how the pandemic has reshaped teacher evaluation standards, continuing to track innovative practices in the field of medical education evaluation, strengthening interdisciplinary collaboration to build a more comprehensive and scientific evaluation system, and keenly capturing emerging topics to lead the innovation and development of research directions.
The findings of our comprehensive study offer several profound insights that can guide future research endeavors in teacher evaluation. Firstly, it is proposed that the potential of leveraging emerging technologies, in particular artificial intelligence, should be taken advantage of in the field of teacher assessment. AI-driven tools can provide objective, data-driven insights into teachers' performance, thereby facilitating the identification of areas for improvement and excellence recognition. Secondly, we advocate a paradigm shift in teacher evaluation to better capture their multifaceted effectiveness. Traditional methods focus solely on student performance, overlooking teachers' broader contributions. We suggest blending qualitative and quantitative methods, with qualitative data offering deeper insights into teachers' practices and perspectives. Moreover, amidst global health challenges and the internationalization of medical education, international cooperation in teacher evaluation is vital. This collaboration will intensify, fostering cross-border research to disseminate best practices and jointly address evaluation challenges. Finally, we emphasize the need for continuous professional development and feedback loops in teacher evaluation. Teachers should be provided with opportunities for growth and improvement, and the evaluation process should serve as a tool for reflection and self-improvement. By implementing these recommendations, we can move towards a more comprehensive and effective teacher evaluation system that recognizes the diverse contributions of teachers and supports their continued growth and development.
We can identify several notable strengths of this study. Firstly, it presents, for the first time, the research “hotspots” and trends in faculty evaluation in medical schools and universities over the past 18 years. Secondly, bibliometric analysis provides a more comprehensive and intuitive research approach than traditional literature reviews. However, bibliometric studies also have some inherent limitations. Firstly, some publications may be omitted because the data are retrieved entirely from the WoSCC database. Nevertheless, the WoSCC database is often used to analyze scientific metrics data. Secondly, our search strategy only includes specific terms for the primary target objects and conditions. Thus, the retrieved publications may contain potential false positives and false negatives, as no search query can achieve 100 % accuracy. In addition, this study uses both the R-Bibliometrics package, CiteSpace and VOSviewer, so the search algorithms may need improvement, leading to some bias. Despite these limitations, our research findings still provide a solid global perspective on faculty evaluation in medical colleges and universities over the past eighteen years, offering valuable insights for current research and directions for future studies in this area.

Conclusions
A bibliometric and visual analysis was conducted on global research progress and trends in teacher evaluation of medical colleges from 2005 to 2023. The bibliometric analysis involved the investigation of relationships among countries/regions, authors, keywords, institutions, and citations. The current study presented a comprehensive analysis of the characteristics and evolution of the research literature on the teacher evaluation of medical colleges. It will also serve as an important reference for predicting the future direction of research on the teacher evaluation of medical colleges. Future research directions might include the integration of technologies such as artificial intelligence, machine learning, which will trigger a revolution and collectively form a promising research topic in the field of medical education.
