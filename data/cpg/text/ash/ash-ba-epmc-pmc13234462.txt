Key Points 
• 
The all-oral, MRD-driven, time-limited regimen duvelisib plus venetoclax is active in R/R CLL and RT, including in high-risk patients. 
• 
Typical toxicities of PI3K and BCL-2 inhibitors were observed, which required active management.

Abstract 
In this phase 1/2 study we evaluated duvelisib plus venetoclax in patients with relapsed/refractory (R/R) chronic lymphocytic leukemia/small lymphocytic lymphoma (CLL/SLL) and Richter transformation (RT). Venetoclax was added after 1 week of duvelisib. After 1 year of therapy, patients with a complete response (CR) and undetectable minimal residual disease (uMRD) could discontinue therapy; patients with detectable MRD continued venetoclax monotherapy until 2 separate occasions of uMRD. Thirty five patients with CLL/SLL and 9 with RT enrolled. Among patients with CLL, 77% had unmutated immunoglobulin heavy chain variable region, 46% had TP53 aberrancy, and patients had a median of 2 prior therapies, including 60% with prior Bruton tyrosine kinase inhibitor. In phase 1 the maximum tolerated dose was not reached, and the recommended phase 2 dose was 25mg twice daily duvelisib plus 400mg daily venetoclax. 91% of patients experienced ≥grade 3 neutropenia, with febrile neutropenia in 6%. Common nonhematologic toxicities were diarrhea (63%, 14% ≥grade 3) and elevated aspartate aminotransferase (51%, 9% ≥grade 3). The best CR and overall response rates were 60% and 91%, respectively. At cycle 13, peripheral blood and bone marrow uMRD at 10 −4 were 43% and 40%, respectively. The median progression-free survival (PFS) for patients with CLL/SLL was 46 months, and the 3-year PFS was 67% (95% confidence interval, 0.51-0.86). Four patients with RS achieved a response (3 CRs). Overall, duvelisib plus venetoclax was active in R/R CLL/SLL and RT, although serious adverse events occurred, including immune-mediated toxicities. This trial was registered at www.clinicaltrials.gov as NCT03534323 . 
Introduction 
Despite a growing number of treatment options, patients with chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL) and small lymphocytic lymphoma (SLL) continue to experience relapse after initial therapy and are in need of novel treatment strategies. 1 Transformation of CLL to diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, known as Richter transformation (RT), is also typically resistant to therapy and associated with a dismal prognosis. 2 , 3 The use of combination targeted therapy approaches has the potential to deepen response, with higher rates of undetectable minimal residual disease (uMRD) than had been achievable in the past with chemoimmunotherapy. 4 Furthermore, combination targeted therapies offer a potential advantage over single-agent continuous therapy, as time-limited strategies can provide a treatment-free interval that can limit toxicities and cost. 
Although the phase 3 MURANO trial demonstrated that venetoclax, an orally available B-cell leukemia/lymphoma 2 (BCL-2) inhibitor, plus rituximab is an effective time-limited regimen for relapsed/refractory (R/R) CLL and SLL, it includes 2 years of treatment and an infusional component. 5 , 6 This study also found that patients who had uMRD at the end of treatment had an improvement in overall survival (OS) as compared with patients with detectable MRD at the end of therapy, demonstrating the prognostic value of MRD in this setting. 7 Furthermore, although venetoclax monotherapy can be used in high-risk patients, treatment is typically indefinite in this setting. 8 , 9 
Duvelisib is an oral inhibitor of PI3K-δ/γ that is approved by the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for patients with CLL/SLL after at least 2 prior therapies. 10 As duvelisib and venetoclax both target pathways fundamental to CLL biology but have distinct mechanisms of action, they offer promise as dual therapy. Preclinical data further support this combination, as ex vivo-cultured CLL cells from patients treated with duvelisib demonstrated increased BCL-2 protein expression and enhanced apoptosis after venetoclax exposure as compared with baseline samples. 11 Using BH3 profiling, a functional assay that measures anti-apoptotic dependencies, we also previously reported that inhibiting PI3K enhances the dependence of CLL cells on BCL-2 and increases sensitivity to BCL-2 inhibition. 12 Similarly, BH3 profiling revealed that PI3K inhibition increases BCL-2 dependency in other non-Hodgkin lymphoma subtypes as well. 13 Duvelisib plus venetoclax was also found to be synergistic in RT patient-derived xenograft models, suggesting a potential role for this combination in RT as well. 14 
Therefore, we hypothesized that duvelisib plus venetoclax would achieve deep remissions in patients with R/R CLL/SLL and RT that would allow for an all-oral, MRD-guided, time-limited therapy. Here, we report the results of the phase 1/2 study of this combination in R/R CLL/SLL and RT. 
Methods 
Patients 
This multicenter, investigator-sponsored, phase 1/2 study was performed at 4 US academic medical centers. The trial ( NCT03534323 ) was approved by the institutional review board at each trial site and performed according to Good Clinical Practice guidelines and the Declaration of Helsinki. Patients were eligible if they had a confirmed diagnosis of CLL/SLL and required treatment per 2008 International Workshop on CLL (iwCLL) criteria or had biopsy-proven RT. 15 Patients in the CLL/SLL cohort required at least 1 prior line of therapy. No prior therapy was required for the RT cohort. Eligible patients were aged ≥18 years; had an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status of ˂2; and had adequate hematologic, hepatic, and renal function. Key exclusion criteria were that patients could not have active gastrointestinal disease or confirmed central nervous system involvement. In the CLL/SLL cohort, patients with prior treatment with venetoclax or duvelisib were excluded, but in the RT cohort, patients could have received prior venetoclax as long as it was finished >1 year before trial enrollment. 
Study design 
In the phase 1 portion of the study, patients were treated with duvelisib 25 mg twice daily for 7 days. A traditional 3 + 3 design was used to accrue patients to 1 of 3 dose levels of venetoclax (100, 200, and 400 mg), which started on day 8 in combination with duvelisib. Due to the potential for drug-drug interaction, patients were started on 10 mg of venetoclax for 1 week, followed by the weekly ramp-up per the venetoclax label. Patients were admitted for the venetoclax ramp-up for close monitoring for tumor lysis syndrome (TLS). The primary end point was the maximum tolerated dose and the recommended phase 2 dose (RP2D) for duvelisib plus venetoclax. Once established, patients in the lower dose venetoclax cohorts had the option to escalate to the RP2D. Toxicity was evaluated by Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events Version 4 (CTCAEv4) and 2008 iwCLL criteria and reported descriptively. 
In the phase 2 portion of the study, a 10% complete response (CR)/CR with incomplete count recovery (CRi) rate was considered not promising, a 30% CR/CRi rate was considered promising, and the probabilities of a type I error and type II error were set at 0.1 and 0.1, respectively. 16 Patients in the phase 1 portion of the study treated at the RP2D were counted in the phase 2 study. For the RT cohort, we planned to enroll 20 patients, where a CR rate of 20% was considered not promising, and a CR rate of 45% was promising, and with a type 1 and type 2 error of 0.09 and 0.1, respectively. 17 Patients started duvelisib monotherapy for 1 week and added venetoclax using a weekly dose ramp-up to the maximum tolerated dose on day 8. Because there was no TLS in the phase 1 portion of the study, patients in the phase 2 portion had the option of starting with 20 mg of venetoclax, with inpatient treatment for the first 2 doses. Patients who were low or medium risk for TLS also had the option of starting with 10 mg in the outpatient setting. Patients with RT were treated with accelerated daily venetoclax escalation in the inpatient setting, starting at 20 mg and ramping up to 400 mg over 5 days. 18 Patients were treated with duvelisib plus venetoclax for a total of 12 28-day cycles ( Figure 1 ), at which point those with a CR and uMRD had the option to discontinue all therapy. Patients with detectable MRD after cycle 12 remained on venetoclax monotherapy until they achieved uMRD in the peripheral blood (PB) on at least 2 consecutive occasions. Patients with a partial response and uMRD had the option of discontinuing therapy after at least 2 years of treatment. Patients with recurrent MRD could restart venetoclax monotherapy. The primary end point was the best CR rate as defined by iwCLL criteria and Lugano criteria for CLL/SLL and RT, respectively. Secondary end points included rates of uMRD in the PB and bone marrow (BM), defined as <0.01% by 4- to 8-color flow cytometry (sensitivity 10 −4 ), toxicity, progression-free survival (PFS), and OS. MRD analyses were performed in an intention-to-treat style, such that patients with missing data at a time point were considered to have detectable MRD at that time point. The Kaplan-Meier method was used to estimate PFS and OS, with rates summarized as percentages with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). The median follow-up was calculated using the reverse Kaplan-Meier method with the death event censored. Data cutoff was 10 April 2024. Prophylaxis against Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia (PJP) and varicella-zoster virus was required. Monitoring for cytomegalovirus occurred at study entry and monthly thereafter.

Results 
A total of 44 patients were enrolled between January 2019 and June 2023, including 35 patients with CLL/SLL and 9 patients with RT. Baseline characteristics are presented in Table 1 . Accrual to the RT cohort was discontinued before completion of planned enrollment because of study closure. The median age for the CLL/SLL cohort was 69 years (range 50-79). Of the patients, 69% (24/35) were male. Immunoglobulin heavy chain variable (IGHV) was unmutated in 77% (27/35). Fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) results demonstrated that 29% (10/35) had del(17p), 23% (8/35) had trisomy 12, and 11% (4/35) had del(11q). Furthermore, 31% of the patients (11/35) had a complex karyotype (defined as ≥3 abnormalities), and 43% (15/35) and 23% (8/35) had TP53 and NOTCH1 mutations, respectively. The median number of prior therapies was 2 (range 1-6), and 60% (21/35) had previously been treated with a covalent Bruton tyrosine kinase (BTK) inhibitor (cBTKi). In these cBTKi-exposed patients, 13 of the 21 (62%) discontinued cBTKi because of disease progression, 7 of the 21 (33%) because of cBTKi intolerance, and for 1 patient, the reason for discontinuation was not available.

Among patients with RT, the median age was 64 years (range 55-72), and 7 of the 9 patients were male. With regard to the CLL prognostic markers for these RT patients, 4 patients had unmutated IGHV, FISH demonstrated trisomy 12 in 3 patients, del(17p) in 1 patient, and a complex karyotype was observed in 3 patients. Furthermore, 3 and 2 patients had NOTCH1 and TP53 mutations, respectively. The median number of prior therapies for both CLL and RT was 2 (range 1-4) and 1 for RT alone (range 0-2). One patient had previously been treated with BTKi, and 2 patients had previously been treated with venetoclax. 
Safety 
In the phase 1 portion of the study (n = 12), there were no dose-limiting toxicities, and the RP2D was duvelisib 25 mg twice daily and venetoclax 400 mg daily. Key adverse events for the entire study are summarized in Figure 2 . In the patients with CLL/SLL, all-grade hematologic toxicity included the following: neutropenia (97%, 91% ≥grade 3), thrombocytopenia (74%, 26% ≥grade 3), and anemia (63%, 14% grade 3). The most common nonhematologic toxicities included the following: fatigue (69%, 3% grade 3), diarrhea (63%, 14% ≥grade 3), increased alkaline phosphatase (57%, all grade 1/2), hypocalcemia (57%, 20% grade 3), hyperglycemia (51%, 9% grade 3), elevated aspartate aminotransferase (51%, 9% ≥grade 3), and nausea (49%, 3% grade 3). Serious adverse events occurred in 49% of the patients. Infections of any grade occurred in 46% of the patients, with 11% patients (4/35) experiencing a ≥grade 3 infection (including 1 case of grade 4 sepsis and 1 case of grade 5 COVID-19 pneumonia). Febrile neutropenia occurred in 6% of the patients. There was 1 case of PJP in a patient who had inadvertently not been taking prophylaxis. There were 4 cases of laboratory TLS. One patient had clinical TLS after receiving their first starting dose of 20 mg of venetoclax during the ramp-up and developed acute kidney injury, transiently requiring dialysis. The renal function eventually normalized, and the patient came off dialysis. The patient resumed venetoclax therapy without further complications. One patient experienced perforation of a gastric ulcer while on high-dose steroids for ∼4 weeks to manage grade 3 diarrhea and discontinued study treatment. Adverse events of special interest included diarrhea (63%, 14% ≥grade 3), colitis (9%, 3% ≥grade 3), elevated aspartate aminotransferase (51%, 9% ≥grade 3), elevated alanine aminotransferase increased (31%, 3% ≥grade 3), and rash (37%, 9% ≥grade 3). There were no cases of pneumonitis. Furthermore, 60% (21/35) required dose reductions because of toxicity, including 54% (19/35) for duvelisib and 26% (9/35) for venetoclax; 20% (7/35) of patients required dose reduction of both duvelisib and venetoclax. The most common reasons for drug holds or reductions included diarrhea (n = 6), TLS (n = 4), rash (n = 3), and elevated amylase and/or lipase (n = 3).

Among patients with RT, hematologic toxicity included neutropenia (56%, 33% ≥grade 3), anemia (56%, 11% grade 3), and thrombocytopenia (11%, 11% grade 4). Common nonhematologic toxicity included diarrhea (56%, 11% grade 3), fatigue (56%, all grade 1/2), nausea (56%, all grade 1/2), hypertension (44%, all grade 1/2), elevated alanine aminotransferase (33%, all grade 1/2), and elevated creatinine (33%, all grade 1/2). Notable serious adverse events among patients with RS included 1 patient each with febrile neutropenia, atrial fibrillation, grade 3 diarrhea, and colitis. One patient with biopsy-proven RT involving the liver developed grade 5 hepatic failure in the setting of progressive RT in the liver. 
Pharmacokinetics 
Pharmacokinetic studies demonstrated that venetoclax plasma levels increased proportionally with higher doses when coadministered with duvelisib and were similar to previously published concentrations with venetoclax administered as monotherapy ( supplemental Figure 1 A). 19 Duvelisib plasma levels were also similar when coadministered with doses of 10, 20, 50, 100, 200, and 400 mg of venetoclax daily ( supplemental Figure 1 B). 
Efficacy 
Among patients with CLL/SLL, the best CR/CRi rate was 60% (21/35; 95% CI, 0.42-0.76) and the best overall response rate (ORR) was 91% (32/35; 95% CI, 0.77-0.98; Figure 3 A). At cycle 13, day 1 (C13D1), the CR/CRi was 43% (15/35; 95% CI, 0.26-0.61) and the ORR was 69% (24/35; 95% CI, 0.51-0.83), with 10 patients who discontinued therapy before the C13D1 response assessment because of toxicity counted as nonresponders at the time point. In patients with TP53 -aberrant disease, the CR/CRi rate was 44% (7/16; 95% CI, 0.20-0.70) at C13D1, and the ORR was 56% (9/16; 95% CI, 0.30-0.80). Among patients with any prior covalent BTKi exposure, the CR/CRi rate at C13D1 was 38% (8/21; 95% CI, 0.18-0.62), and the ORR was 57% (12/21; 95% CI, 0.34-0.78). In addition, 11 of the 13 patients with refractory disease to cBTKi achieved a response, including 6 with CR/CRi as their best response.

Among all patients with CLL/SLL, 40% (14/35; 95% CI, 0.24-0.58) achieved BM uMRD, and 43% (15/35; 95% CI, 0.26-0.61) had PB uMRD at C13D1 ( Figure 3 B). In those with MRD results available at C13, the rates of uMRD in the BM and PB were 64% (14/22; 95% CI, 0.41-0.83) and 68% (15/22; 95% CI, 0.45-0.86), respectively. The best BM- and PB-uMRD rates in all patients were 57% (20/35; 95% CI, 0.39-0.74; 6 unknown) and 69% (24/35; 95% CI, 0.51-0.83; 2 unknown), respectively. 
At a median follow-up on study of 40 months (range 0-62), 16 patients had come off the study. Reasons for discontinuation included the following: progressive CLL (n = 4), treatment-related toxicity (n = 3; including 1 death because of COVID-19), death unrelated to study treatment (n = 3; myelodysplastic syndrome, acute leukemia, and traumatic fall), progression with RT (n = 2), electively proceeding to allogeneic stem cell transplant (n = 2), and 1 patient each for initiating treatment for a solid tumor and lost to follow-up. 
The median PFS for patients with CLL/SLL was 46 months, and the 3-year PFS was 67% (95% CI, 0.51-0.86; Figure 4 A). The median OS was not reached, and the 3-year OS was 90% (95% CI, 0.80-1.0; Figure 4 B). Furthermore, 12 patients discontinued therapy in the setting of uMRD. Two patients who achieved a CR with uMRD after 1 year of combination therapy and subsequently discontinued therapy in remission were found to have recurrent PB MRD without any clinical progression at the cycle 25 restaging visit. Both patients restarted venetoclax monotherapy, and 1 achieved uMRD again.

Discussion 
We found that duvelisib plus venetoclax, an all oral, MRD-guided, time-limited treatment, is active in R/R CLL/SLL and RT, although significant toxicities requiring active management did occur. We also demonstrated that it is feasible to combine duvelisib and venetoclax at the full doses that are approved by the US FDA, and no major drug-drug interactions were found. The adverse events observed in the study were similar to those found with PI3K and BCL-2 inhibitor monotherapy and, in most cases, were manageable. Although there were high rates of neutropenia with this regimen, rates of febrile neutropenia and serious infections were similar to other venetoclax-based regimens. Tumor lysis syndrome was found in a small subset of patients but was reversible with supportive interventions. 
Although there have been safety concerns regarding the class of PI3K inhibitors in B-cell malignancies, duvelisib has retained its full FDA approval in R/R CLL, making it an available treatment option for patients in clinical practice. However, because of the cumulative toxicity risks when giving duvelisib as a continuous therapy, it has been challenging to use the drug more widely in practice as a long-term therapy. In our study, the use of duvelisib was limited to 1 year, allowing most patients to complete the fixed duration of combination therapy, although treatment still required prophylaxis against PJP and varicella-zoster virus and routine cytomegalovirus monitoring. Unlike venetoclax plus CD20-based therapies for R/R CLL, which require 2 years of therapy including 6 months of IV infusions, or venetoclax monotherapy, which is typically used indefinitely, duvelisib plus venetoclax has the advantage of being an all oral, time-limited approach with only a 1-year duration of combination therapy and without a BTKi. 
Our study also demonstrated that duvelisib plus venetoclax is a highly active regimen for patients with R/R CLL/SLL, with a CR/CRi rate of 60%, a 43% uMRD rate in the PB, respectively, and a 3-year PFS of 67%. This compares favorably to other regimens for R/R CLL/SLL, including venetoclax plus rituximab (the MURANO regimen), which had a CR rate of 8.2%, uMRD rate of 62.4% in the PB, and 3-year and 5-year PFS of 71% and 38%, respectively, among less heavily pretreated patients. 5 , 7 , 20 It is especially notable that our study was also enriched for patients with high-risk biology (46% with TP53 aberration), and unlike MURANO, more than half of the patients in our study were postcovalent BTKi. Among patients who had recurrent MRD after discontinuation of therapy, resumption of venetoclax resulted in a decline or elimination of MRD, demonstrating a proof-of-principle for the potential role of retreatment, although more data are needed here. 
BTKis are an active, oral therapy available for patients in need of initial treatment or for treatment of R/R CLL. As BTKis are often a preferred first-line treatment strategy, patients with R/R disease require therapies that inhibit alternative targets. In addition, although BTKis have proven to be active in combination with venetoclax, they can be challenging to give to some patients because of cardiac risk factors or increased bleeding risk, such as patients already on systemic anticoagulation. 21 , 22 Although the recent approval of the noncovalent BTKi pirtobrutinib does offer an appealing option for these patients, the median PFS for patients on the BRUIN-321 trial treated with pirtobrutinib was only 14 months. 23 Venetoclax plus pirtobrutinib has been studied in a time-limited, all-oral fashion 24 and is being investigated in combination with rituximab in an ongoing phase 3 trial ( NCT04965493 ). However, a combination with pirtobrutinib may not be an option for patients intolerant of BTKis or for the growing number of patients with CLL refractory to noncovalent BTKis. Venetoclax plus duvelisib offers an alternative, non–BTKi-based oral combination treatment strategy for these patients. Of note, a triplet approach using the PI3K inhibitor umbralisib with venetoclax and the CD20 monoclonal antibody ublituximab (U2-Ven) has also been explored in patients with R/R CLL. 25 Although this combination also saw promising activity, with an intention-to-treat ORR of 98% and 42% CR rate, the regimen included an infusional component, and further development of umbralisib has been discontinued for patients with CLL/SLL. 25 
Complete responses were also found in a small cohort of patients with RT, a disease known to be highly refractory to chemotherapy and associated with a poor prognosis. 26 One responder was also able to proceed with allogeneic stem cell transplant, a proof-of-principle that this combination could be used as a bridging strategy for some patients. The median PFS for patients with RT in our study was short at 2.6 months, which is similar to other available therapies, such as noncovalent BTKis, in this setting. 27 However, the small number of patients in this cohort makes interpretations difficult. 
In summary, duvelisib + venetoclax was active in patients with R/R CLL/SLL, including those with high-risk disease biology such as TP53 aberrancy, and in patients with progressive disease on BTKis and patients with RT. Typical toxicities of PI3K and BCL-2 inhibitors were observed and required active management. Given the availability of both approved drugs, our data support the potential use of duvelisib plus venetoclax in a carefully selected group of patients monitored closely for toxicity and given antimicrobial prophylaxis while on therapy.
